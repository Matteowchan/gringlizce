Prepositions of movement describe the movement and direction of a subject from one point to another: to (towards a destination), into (to the inside), onto (onto a surface), through (passing inside), across (from one side to the other), along (alongside), towards (in the direction of). The Turkish -e/-a (dative) and -den/-dan (ablative) suffixes do this job, but not with a single suffix — with different words. The most critical distinction is this: for a static position in / on / at is used, whereas if there is movement towards that position, into / onto / to is used. This page covers the seven main prepositions with their contexts, traps and plenty of examples; then it builds a bridge to Conjunctions.

Prepositions of movement are small but powerful words that describe how a person or object goes from one point to another. In English, the answer to a sentence's 'where to' and 'in which direction' questions is mostly given by these prepositions. She walked to the shop the word that carries the real information is to; if you remove it, the goal of the movement is lost.

This topic exists because movement is a basic need of every language: to go, to enter, to exit, to pass, to cross. English encodes these fine directional differences not with a single suffix but with separate words. While Turkish handles 'eve girdim' with a single -e suffix and the meaning of the verb, English carries the idea of 'inside' with into, the idea of 'on top' with onto, and the idea of 'through' with through — separate words.

"Prepositions of movement are the sentence's GPS: to shows the destination, into the entry inside, through the passage through; changing the preposition changes the route.

The Turkish equivalent

In Turkish, movement and direction are mostly expressed with case suffixes: the dative case -e/-a (ev-e, okul-a), the ablative case -den/-dan (ev-den, okul-dan). English's prepositions of movement largely correspond to this -e/-a logic: to the house = ev-e, into the house = ev-in içine, onto the roof = çatı-nın üstüne. So the intuition is familiar for Turkish learners; the difficulty is, instead of a single suffix, choosing the right one among several words.

Why does it seem hard?

  • The single Turkish -e/-a suffix splits in English into several options like to, into, onto, towards; the student has to decide which one to choose.
  • Static position (in/on/at) and movement (into/onto/to) are confused because they are often rendered by the same Turkish sentence: English draws the difference between 'odada' and 'odaya' sharply with in / into.
  • across and throughThey are both translated as 'to pass/cross', but one describes crossing over a surface (two dimensions), the other passing through an environment (three dimensions).
  • to and towards can both be translated as 'towards', but to indicates arrival, towards indicates only the direction; this difference is easily hidden in Turkish.
Key Point
If you see a movement verb (go, walk, run, drive, fly, jump, swim, move, throw...) in a sentence, expect a movement preposition, not a position preposition. Think 'if the verb moves, let the preposition move too': be in but go into.
  • to = bir hedefe doğru gitmek; en genel varış edatı: go to work (işe gitmek).
  • into = bir şeyin içine girmek (dışarıdan içeriye): walk into the room.
  • onto = bir yüzeyin üstüne çıkmak: climb onto the roof.
  • through = bir şeyin içinden geçerek (tünel, orman, kalabalık): go through the door.
  • across = bir yüzeyin bir yanından öbür yanına: walk across the street.
  • along = uzun bir şeyin boyunca ilerlemek: drive along the road.
  • towards / toward = bir yerin yönünde (varış garantisi yok): run towards the exit.

  • Bu edatlar neredeyse her zaman bir hareket fiiliyle birlikte gelir; edatı fiilden ayrı düşünmeyin.
  • to = going towards a destination; the most general arrival preposition: go to work (to go to work).
  • into = going into something (from outside to inside): walk into the room.
  • onto = getting onto a surface: climb onto the roof.
  • through = passing through something (tunnel, forest, crowd): go through the door.
  • across = from one side of a surface to the other: walk across the street.
  • along = moving along a long thing: drive along the road.
  • towards / toward = in the direction of a place (no guarantee of arrival): run towards the exit.
  • Additional direction prepositions: up (up), down (down), over (over, crossing above), past (past, going by), round/around (around, circling), off (off, leaving a surface), out of (out of, from inside).
  • Golden rule: static position = in / on / at (see Prepositions of Place); movement to that position = into / onto / to.

The basic structure of a phrase with a movement preposition is very regular. The preposition comes after the movement verb and before the place noun. These prepositions are not conjugated by subject (they stay the same for every subject) and are independent of verb tense.

Affirmative structure

Subject + movement verb + movement preposition + place  →  They / walked / into / the office.

The children ran into the garden.
Çocuklar bahçeye (içeri) koştu.
into = movement from outside into the garden.
We drove across the bridge at sunset.
Gün batımında köprüden karşıya geçtik.
across = from one end of the bridge to the other.
He climbed onto the wall to see better.
Daha iyi görmek için duvarın üstüne çıktı.
onto = movement onto the surface of the wall.

Negative structure

The negation is made not on the preposition but on the verb (don't / doesn't / didn't + verb). The preposition stays the same.

Don't walk into the street without looking.
Bakmadan yola (caddeye) girme.
Negation with 'Don't'; 'into' does not change.
She didn't go through the main gate; she used the side door.
Ana kapıdan geçmedi; yan kapıyı kullandı.
didn't go through = the passage did not happen.

Question structure

A question is formed by fronting the auxiliary; the preposition stays at the end or in place. For the 'where to' question, Where is used but the preposition is often hidden; for 'in which direction/which way' the preposition is asked explicitly.

3) onto — bir yüzeyin üstüne

Statik on konumu, onto ise o konuma çıkışı anlatır: be on the stage (sahnede olmak) vs. walk onto the stage (sahneye çıkmak). Günlük dilde çoğu zaman onto yerine on da duyulur; ama net bir 'üstüne hareket' vurgusu için onto tercih edilir.

The cat jumped onto the windowsill.
Kedi pencere pervazının üstüne atladı.
onto = yüzeye çıkışla biten hareket.
The band walked onto the stage to loud applause.
Grup gür alkışlarla sahneye çıktı.
onto the stage = sahne yüzeyine hareket.
Load the boxes onto the truck, please.
Kutuları kamyona (üstüne) yükleyin lütfen.
İş bağlamı; yüzeye/platforma yükleme.
Hold onto the railing as you go down.
İnerken korkuluğa (sıkıca) tutun.
'hold onto' = tutunmak; sabitlenme mecazı.

4) through — içinden geçerek

through, öznenin bir şeyin içinden, onu çevreleyen bir ortamdan geçtiğini anlatır. Tünel, orman, kalabalık, kapı, boru gibi üç boyutlu ya da çevreleyen ortamlarda kullanılır. Türkçedeki '-in içinden geçmek' ifadesinin tam karşılığıdır. Mecazi olarak zor bir süreçten geçmeyi de anlatır: get through a difficult year.

The train went through several tunnels.
Tren birkaç tünelin içinden geçti.
through = çevreleyen tünelin içinden.
We pushed our way through the crowd.
Kalabalığın arasından ite kaka geçtik.
Kalabalık üç boyutlu bir 'ortam' gibi düşünülür.
Sunlight streamed through the window.
Güneş ışığı pencereden içeri süzüldü.
Işığın cam yüzeyinden geçişi.
She got through a tough time with her family's support.
Ailesinin desteğiyle zor bir dönemi atlattı.
Mecazi through = süreçten geçip çıkmak.

5) across — bir yandan öbür yana

across, bir yüzeyin ya da alanın bir kenarından karşı kenarına geçişi anlatır; through'un aksine iki boyutlu, düz bir yüzeyi düşünürüz: cadde, nehir, oda, tarla. 'Karşıdan karşıya' fikri buradadır. Nehri 'across' geçmek yüzeyini kat etmektir; 'through' geçmek ise suyun içinden ilerlemeyi vurgular.

Be careful when you walk across the road.
Caddeyi (karşıdan karşıya) geçerken dikkatli ol.
across = yüzeyin bir yanından öbür yanına.
He rowed across the lake in twenty minutes.
Gölü yirmi dakikada kürek çekerek geçti.
Göl yüzeyinin karşı kıyısına.
Her office is just across the hall from mine.
Ofisi benimkinin tam karşısında, koridorun öbür tarafında.
Statik 'across' de olur: karşı tarafta konumlanma.
The news spread across the whole country.
Haber tüm ülkeye yayıldı.
Mecazi 'across' = bir alanın her yerine.

6) along — boyunca ilerlemek

along, öznenin uzun ve çizgisel bir şeyi (yol, nehir, sahil, koridor, duvar) izleyerek ilerlediğini anlatır. Hareket bu çizginin üstünde, ona paralel devam eder. Türkçedeki '-boyunca / -in kenarında ilerlemek' anlamına gelir.

We walked along the river until it got dark.
Karanlık çökene kadar nehir boyunca yürüdük.
along = nehrin uzunluğunca ilerleme.
Drive along this street and turn right at the lights.
Bu cadde boyunca sür ve ışıklarda sağa dön.
Yol tarifi bağlamında çok yaygın.
Flowers grew all along the fence.
Çit boyunca her yerde çiçekler açmıştı.
Çizgisel bir hat boyunca dağılım.

7) towards / toward — yönünde (varış garantisi yok)

towards (Amerikan yazımı toward), hareketin bir yerin yönünde olduğunu söyler ama oraya ulaşıldığını söylemez. 'to' varışı, 'towards' yalnızca yönü verir. Bu ayrım Türkçede 'kapıya doğru yürüdü' ('doğru' = towards) ile 'kapıya yürüdü/vardı' arasındaki farkla aynıdır. İngiliz İngilizcesinde çoğunlukla towards, Amerikan İngilizcesinde toward tercih edilir; anlam aynıdır.

She stood up and walked towards the window.
Ayağa kalktı ve pencereye doğru yürüdü.
Yön belirtir; pencereye ulaşıp ulaşmadığı önemli değil.
The storm is moving toward the coast.
Fırtına kıyıya doğru ilerliyor. (Amerikan yazımı)
toward = varış değil, gidiş yönü.
We're saving money towards a new car.
Yeni bir araba için para biriktiriyoruz.
Mecazi 'towards' = bir hedefe yönelik ama henüz ulaşılmamış.

Ek yön edatları (up, down, over, past, round, off, out of)

Bu yedi ana edatın yanında sık kullanılan yön sözcükleri de vardır: up (yukarı), down (aşağı), over (üstünden aşarak), past (yanından geçip), round/around (etrafından/dolanarak), off (üstünden ayrılarak), out of (içinden dışarı — into'nun tersi).

The cat climbed up the tree and wouldn't come down.
Kedi ağaca tırmandı ve aşağı inmedi.
up / down = dikey yön çifti.
The horse jumped over the fence.
At çitin üzerinden atladı.
over = bir engelin üstünden aşarak geçiş.
We drove past the school without stopping.
Okulun yanından durmadan geçtik.
past = yanından geçip geride bırakma.
She got out of the taxi and ran off.
Taksiden indi ve koşup uzaklaştı.
out of = into'nun tersi; off = üstünden/oradan ayrılma.

Now let's go through the seven main prepositions one by one, from the most common to the most specific. For each we give its meaning, when it is chosen, and the close neighbour it can be confused with.

to

Movement towards a destination: go to school, walk to the door.

into

From outside into something: walk into the room, jump into the pool.

through

Passing through something: walk through the forest, drive through a tunnel.

1) to — towards a destination (most common)

to says the movement is directed at a destination. It is the most direct answer to the 'Where to?' question and the closest equivalent of the Turkish -e/-a suffix. When no detail like going inside or onto is emphasised, the default choice is to. Important exception: the word home does not take 'to' — we say go home.

I go to the gym three times a week.
Haftada üç kez spor salonuna giderim.
General arrival; no need for an inside/onto detail.
The delegation flew to Ankara for the summit.
Heyet zirve için Ankara'ya uçtu.
Work/formal context; 'to' + city.
Turn left and walk to the end of the corridor.
Sola dön ve koridorun sonuna kadar yürü.
Movement up to a point.
She's going home now, so don't wait.
Şimdi eve gidiyor, o yüzden bekleme.
'to' is NOT used with 'home': go home.

2) into — into something (from outside to inside)

into says the subject crosses a boundary and goes into an area; at the end of the movement the subject is inside that container. The difference from static in is exactly this: be in the room is static, go into the room is movement. into is also used in the sense of colliding: crash into a wall.

He walked into the meeting ten minutes late.
Toplantıya on dakika geç girdi.
into = passing into the room/meeting.
Pour the milk into the bowl slowly.
Sütü yavaşça kâsenin içine dök.
Kitchen/everyday context; the target is a container.
The car skidded and crashed into the barrier.
Araba kaydı ve bariyere çarptı.
into = collision; movement ending in contact.
Translate this sentence into English.
Bu cümleyi İngilizceye çevir.
Figurative 'into': transformation from one state/language into another.

3) onto — onto a surface

onto says the movement ends on a surface; in the end the subject touches that surface. The static on is the position, onto describes the movement onto that position: be on the stage vs. walk onto the stage. In everyday speech, onto is often heard as on too; but for a clear 'movement onto' emphasis, onto is preferred.

The cat jumped onto the windowsill.
Kedi pencere pervazının üstüne atladı.
onto = movement ending in getting onto a surface.
The band walked onto the stage to loud applause.
Grup gür alkışlarla sahneye çıktı.
onto the stage = movement onto the stage surface.
Load the boxes onto the truck, please.
Kutuları kamyona (üstüne) yükleyin lütfen.
Work context; loading onto a surface/platform.
Hold onto the railing as you go down.
İnerken korkuluğa (sıkıca) tutun.
'hold onto' = to hold on; a figure of holding fast.

4) through — passing through

through says the subject passes through something, through an environment that surrounds it. It is used for three-dimensional or surrounding environments like a tunnel, forest, crowd, door, pipe. It is the exact equivalent of the Turkish 'to pass through the inside of'. Figuratively it also describes getting through a difficult process: get through a difficult year.

The train went through several tunnels.
Tren birkaç tünelin içinden geçti.
through = through the surrounding tunnel.
We pushed our way through the crowd.
Kalabalığın arasından ite kaka geçtik.
A crowd is thought of as a three-dimensional 'environment'.
Sunlight streamed through the window.
Güneş ışığı pencereden içeri süzüldü.
Light passing through a glass surface.
She got through a tough time with her family's support.
Ailesinin desteğiyle zor bir dönemi atlattı.
Figurative through = getting through a process.

5) across — from one side to the other

across describes crossing from one edge of a surface or area to the opposite edge; unlike through, we picture a two-dimensional, flat surface: a street, river, room, field. The idea of 'from one side to the other' lives here. To cross a river 'across' is to traverse its surface; to cross it 'through' emphasises moving inside the water.

Be careful when you walk across the road.
Caddeyi (karşıdan karşıya) geçerken dikkatli ol.
across = from one side of the surface to the other.
He rowed across the lake in twenty minutes.
Gölü yirmi dakikada kürek çekerek geçti.
To the far shore across the lake's surface.
Her office is just across the hall from mine.
Ofisi benimkinin tam karşısında, koridorun öbür tarafında.
Static 'across' also exists: being positioned on the other side.
The news spread across the whole country.
Haber tüm ülkeye yayıldı.
Figurative 'across' = all over an area.

6) along — moving along

along describes the subject moving following a long and linear thing (road, river, coast, corridor, wall). The movement continues on and parallel to this line. It means the Turkish 'to move along / beside'.

We walked along the river until it got dark.
Karanlık çökene kadar nehir boyunca yürüdük.
along = moving along the length of the river.
Drive along this street and turn right at the lights.
Bu cadde boyunca sür ve ışıklarda sağa dön.
Very common in giving directions.
Flowers grew all along the fence.
Çit boyunca her yerde çiçekler açmıştı.
Distribution along a linear line.

7) towards / toward — in the direction of (no guarantee of arrival)

towards (American spelling toward), says the movement is in the direction of a place but does not say it was reached. 'to' gives arrival, 'towards' gives only the direction. This distinction is the same as the Turkish difference between 'kapıya doğru yürüdü' ('doğru' = towards) and 'kapıya yürüdü/vardı'. British English mostly prefers towards, American English prefers toward; the meaning is the same.

She stood up and walked towards the window.
Ayağa kalktı ve pencereye doğru yürüdü.
Indicates direction; whether the window is reached doesn't matter.
The storm is moving toward the coast.
Fırtına kıyıya doğru ilerliyor. (Amerikan yazımı)
toward = not arrival, but the direction of travel.
We're saving money towards a new car.
Yeni bir araba için para biriktiriyoruz.
Figurative 'towards' = aimed at a goal but not yet reached.

Additional direction prepositions (up, down, over, past, round, off, out of)

Alongside these seven main prepositions there are also frequently used direction words: up (up), down (down), over (over, crossing above), past (past, going by), round/around (around, circling), off (off, leaving a surface), out of (out of, from inside — the opposite of into).

The cat climbed up the tree and wouldn't come down.
Kedi ağaca tırmandı ve aşağı inmedi.
up / down = the vertical direction pair.
The horse jumped over the fence.
At çitin üzerinden atladı.
over = crossing above an obstacle.
We drove past the school without stopping.
Okulun yanından durmadan geçtik.
past = going by and leaving behind.
She got out of the taxi and ran off.
Taksiden indi ve koşup uzaklaştı.
out of = the opposite of into; off = leaving from a surface/place.

Aynı hareketi farklı edatlarla anlatmak, bakış açısını ve vurguyu değiştirir. Doğru edat, konuşanın neyi öne çıkardığını gösterir: varışı mı, süreci mi, yönü mü, yoksa yüzeyi mi?

into vs. to: içeriye vurgu mu, sadece varış mı?

to yalnızca hedefi söyler; into ise 'içine girme' eylemini vurgular. Go to the room pek doğal değildir; oda bir 'içine girilen' yer olduğu için into daha yaygındır. Ama go to the party doğaldır çünkü parti bir olay/etkinliktir, fiziksel bir kap değildir.

He went into the kitchen to make tea.
Çay yapmak için mutfağa girdi.
Mutfak fiziksel kap → into daha doğal.
We went to the concert last night.
Dün gece konsere gittik.
Konser bir etkinlik → to doğru seçim.

through vs. across: üç boyut mu, iki boyut mu?

Sizi çevreleyen bir ortamdan geçiyorsanız through (orman, kalabalık, tünel); düz bir yüzeyin karşısına geçiyorsanız across (cadde, nehir yüzeyi, tarla). Aynı nehir için ikisi de mümkündür ama vurgu değişir: swim across the river karşı kıyıya ulaşmaya, swim through the river ise suyun içinden ilerlemeye odaklanır (ikincisi daha nadirdir).

We walked across the field to the farmhouse.
Çiftlik evine ulaşmak için tarlayı karşıdan karşıya geçtik.
Tarla düz yüzey → across.
We walked through the forest for hours.
Saatlerce ormanın içinden yürüdük.
Orman bizi çevreliyor → through.

to vs. towards: kesinlik ve niyet

to varışın gerçekleştiğini ima eder (kesinlik yüksek); towards yalnızca yönü verir, sonucu belirsiz bırakır. Bir hikâyede gerilimi korumak için towards güçlü bir araçtır: He walked towards the door (ama açtı mı, çıktı mı bilmiyoruz).

The child ran to her mother and hugged her.
Çocuk annesine koştu ve sarıldı.
to = anneye ulaştı (varış kesin).
A stranger was walking towards us in the fog.
Siste bir yabancı bize doğru yürüyordu.
towards = yön; henüz yanımıza gelmedi.
Akılda Tutma
Boyut kuralı: düz yüzey iki boyut → across; çevreleyen ortam üç boyut → through. 'Üstünde mi yürüyorum, içinden mi?' diye sorun. Cadde üstünde → across; tünel içinden → through.

Describing the same movement with different prepositions changes the perspective and the emphasis. The right preposition shows what the speaker foregrounds: arrival, process, direction, or surface?

into vs. to: emphasis on going inside, or just arrival?

to states only the destination; into emphasises the act of 'going inside'. Go to the room is not very natural; because a room is a place one 'goes into', into is more common. But go to the party is natural because a party is an event, not a physical container.

He went into the kitchen to make tea.
Çay yapmak için mutfağa girdi.
The kitchen is a physical container → into is more natural.
We went to the concert last night.
Dün gece konsere gittik.
A concert is an event → to is the right choice.

through vs. across: three dimensions or two?

If you are passing through an environment that surrounds you, use through (forest, crowd, tunnel); if you are crossing to the other side of a flat surface, use across (street, river surface, field). Both are possible for the same river, but the emphasis changes: swim across the river focuses on reaching the far shore, swim through the river focuses on moving through the water (the second is rarer).

We walked across the field to the farmhouse.
Çiftlik evine ulaşmak için tarlayı karşıdan karşıya geçtik.
A field is a flat surface → across.
We walked through the forest for hours.
Saatlerce ormanın içinden yürüdük.
A forest surrounds us → through.

to vs. towards: certainty and intention

to implies that arrival happened (high certainty); towards gives only the direction and leaves the outcome uncertain. To keep tension in a story, towards is a powerful tool: He walked towards the door (but we don't know if he opened it or went out).

The child ran to her mother and hugged her.
Çocuk annesine koştu ve sarıldı.
to = reached the mother (arrival certain).
A stranger was walking towards us in the fog.
Siste bir yabancı bize doğru yürüyordu.
towards = direction; hasn't reached us yet.
Memory Aid
The dimension rule: a flat surface is two dimensions → across; a surrounding environment is three dimensions → through. Ask 'Am I walking on it, or through it?' On a street → across; through a tunnel → through.
İpucu

Tek "-e/-a" ekini parçalara ayır: salt hedef → to; içine → into; üstüne → onto; -e doğru → towards; içinden → through. Türkçedeki tek ek, İngilizcede bütün bir yelpazeye açılır.

Türkçe hareketi ve yönü büyük ölçüde hâl ekleriyle anlatır ve tek ek çoğu zaman İngilizcenin birden çok edatını karşılar. Bu 'birden bire' genişleme, Türk öğrencinin en sık takıldığı yerdir: tek -e/-a ekinin altında İngilizcenin to, into, onto, towards seçenekleri yatar.

  • Türkçe -e/-a (yönelme) → İngilizce to / into / onto / towards (hangi detay vurgulanıyorsa).
  • Türkçe -den/-dan (çıkma) → İngilizce out of / off / from (içinden / üstünden / -den).
  • Türkçe '-in içinden geçmek' → İngilizce through; '-i karşıdan karşıya geçmek' → across.
  • Türkçe fiiller yönü kendi içine gömer (gir-mek, çık-mak); İngilizce bunu genelde fiil + edat ile yapar (go in, go out, go up).
Yanlış I went to inside the house.
Doğru I went into the house.
Eve girdim.
Türkçedeki 'içeri/içine' fikri İngilizcede tek kelimeyle, 'into' ile karşılanır. 'to inside' diye ikili bir çeviri İngilizcede yoktur.
Yanlış He jumped to the table.
Doğru He jumped onto the table.
Masanın üstüne atladı.
Türkçe 'masaya atladı' derken 'üstüne' fikri gizlidir. İngilizce bir yüzeye çıkışı 'onto' ile açıkça belirtir; 'to' yalnız 'masaya doğru' anlamı verir, üstüne çıkmayı söylemez.
Yanlış We passed from the tunnel.
Doğru We went through the tunnel.
Tünelden geçtik.
Türkçedeki '-den geçmek' kalıbı öğrenciyi 'from'a iter ama İngilizcede bir ortamın içinden geçiş 'through' ile anlatılır. 'from' burada 'kaynak/başlangıç' demek olurdu, geçiş değil.
Yanlış She is going to home.
Doğru She is going home.
Eve gidiyor.
Türkçe 'ev-e' der ve öğrenci otomatik 'to' ekler. Ancak 'home' zarf gibi davranır ve 'to' almaz: go home, come home, get home.
Tip

Break the single "-e/-a" suffix into parts: pure destination → to; inside → into; onto → onto; towards → towards; through → through. The single Turkish suffix opens into a whole range in English.

Turkish expresses movement and direction largely with case suffixes and a single suffix often corresponds to several English prepositions. This 'sudden' expansion is where Turkish learners get stuck most: under the single -e/-a suffix lie English's to, into, onto, towards options.

  • Turkish -e/-a (dative) → English to / into / onto / towards (whichever detail is emphasised).
  • Turkish -den/-dan (ablative) → English out of / off / from (from inside / off / from).
  • Turkish 'to pass through the inside of' → English through; 'to cross from one side to the other' → across.
  • Turkish verbs embed the direction inside themselves (gir-mek, çık-mak); English usually does this with verb + preposition (go in, go out, go up).
Wrong I went to inside the house.
Correct I went into the house.
Eve girdim.

En Sık Tek Hata
Öğrencilerin bir numaralı hatası, hareket varken statik edat kullanmaktır: go in the room yerine go into the room. 'go/come/run/walk + in/on' gördüğünüzde durun ve 'into/onto' olması gerekip gerekmediğini düşünün.

Prepositions of movement are most confused with prepositions of position (see Prepositions of Place). The distinction is simple but critical: a fixed place, or movement to it? The table below places these pairs side by side.

Another area of confusion is prepositions of time (see Prepositions of Time); but they answer the 'when' question, while movement prepositions answer 'where to/in which direction'; their functions are entirely different. The Single Most Common Mistake
The number-one mistake is using a static preposition when there is movement: go in the room instead of go into the room. When you see 'go/come/run/walk + in/on', stop and consider whether it should be 'into/onto'.

meaning distinction How do the two sentences in each pair carry different meanings? Explain.
  1. (a) He ran to the door. (b) He ran towards the door.
    (a) reached the door (arrival certain). (b) ran in the direction of the door but whether he reached it is unclear. 'towards' gives only the direction.
  2. (a) The cat is on the table. (b) The cat jumped onto the table.
    (a) static position: the cat is already on the table. (b) movement: the cat got onto the table. on = position, onto = movement to that position.
  3. (a) We walked across the forest. (b) We walked through the forest.
    (b) is the natural one: a forest surrounds us, we pass through it (through). (a) is odd; 'across' suits a flat surface (field, street).
  4. (a) She went to home. (b) She went home.
    (b) is correct: 'home' behaves like an adverb, takes no 'to'. (a) is a grammar mistake.
  5. (a) Get in the car. (b) Get on the bus.
    Both are correct but depend on the vehicle: small vehicle (car) → get in/into; large vehicle (bus) → get on. The size of the vehicle determines the preposition.

İlk derste tek bir ayrımla başlayın: statik in/on vs. hareketli into/onto. Öğrencilere sınıfta somut gösterin: 'I am in the classroom' (yerinizde durun) — 'I go into the corridor' (kapıdan çıkın). Kavram kontrolü için soru dizisi: 'Am I moving or staying still?' (into mı in mi?), 'Is it a flat surface or something around me?' (across mı through mü?), 'Did I reach it or just head there?' (to mu towards mu?). Bu üç soru, öğrencinin doğru edata kendi kendine ulaşmasını sağlar.

Where to start?
In the first lesson, start with a single distinction: static in/on vs. dynamic into/onto. Show it concretely in class: 'I am in the classroom' (stand still) — 'I go into the corridor' (walk out the door). This physical demonstration makes the 'movement' meaning of the preposition stick. Then add the other prepositions one by one; giving them all at once creates confusion.

For concept-checking, a question sequence: 'Am I moving or staying still?' (into or in?), 'Is it a flat surface or something around me?' (across or through?), 'Did I reach it or just head there?' (to or towards?). These three questions let the student arrive at the right preposition on their own.

Activity suggestion: 'Blindfold directions' — blindfold one student, and the others guide them around the class using only movement prepositions ('walk towards the window, go around the desk, walk to the door'). Physical, fun, and reinforces the prepositions instantly. For Turkish learners' most common mistakes, 'go to home' and 'arrive to', put up a separate 'no to' list (home, here, there, arrive at).

  • Prepositions of movement describe a subject's movement and direction from one point to another; they are the versatile equivalent of the Turkish -e/-a suffix.
  • to = general arrival; into = into; onto = onto; through = through (surrounding environment); across = across (flat surface); along = along; towards = in the direction of (no guarantee of arrival).
  • Golden rule: static position = in/on/at (see Prepositions of Place); movement to that position = into/onto/to.
  • through is for a three-dimensional/surrounding environment, across is for a two-dimensional flat surface.
  • to indicates arrival, towards indicates only the direction.
  • Exceptions: home, here, there, abroad take no 'to'; arrive at/in; enter takes no preposition; large vehicle get on/off, small vehicle get into/out of.
  • Classic traps for Turkish learners: go to home, arrive to, pass from, and, when there is movement, the static go in the room.
