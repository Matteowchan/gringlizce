Essay TypesDeneme Türleri

Deneme (essay) türleri ve yapıları: opinion/argumentative (tez + gerekçeler), discussion (iki görüş + kendi görüşün), problem-solution, cause-effect, advantages-disadvantages, compare-contrast. Her tür farklı bir soru kalıbına ve paragraf planına karşılık gelir. IELTS Task 2 soru tipini doğru okumak (Do you agree? / Discuss both views / What problems...) doğru yapıyı seçmenin anahtarıdır; yanlış tür = düşük puan.

Essay types and structures: opinion/argumentative (thesis + reasons), discussion (two views + your own view), problem-solution, cause-effect, advantages-disadvantages, compare-contrast. Each type corresponds to a different question pattern and paragraph plan. Reading the IELTS Task 2 question type correctly (Do you agree? / Discuss both views / What problems...) is the key to choosing the right structure; the wrong type = a low score.

An essay is not just "writing in English"; every essay type has its own question, a plan of thought and a paragraph architecture. This topic teaches six basic types: opinion/argumentative (defend a thesis), discussion (discuss two views + your own), problem-solution, cause-effect, advantages-disadvantages and compare-contrast. Choosing the right structure depends on reading the question correctly: "Do you agree or disagree?" and "Discuss both views" demand completely different plans. Choosing the wrong type lowers your score even if your English is flawless. Closely related topics: thesis statement, paragraph structure and argumentation.

Türkçede "kompozisyon" deyince aklımıza çoğu zaman tek bir şey gelir: bir konu hakkında güzel cümlelerle yazı yazmak. Oysa akademik İngilizcede essay (deneme) bir tür ailesidir. Her tür farklı bir soruya cevap verir, farklı bir düşünce planı ister ve farklı bir paragraf mimarisiyle inşa edilir. Bir mühendisin köprü mü bina mı yaptığına göre farklı plan çizmesi gibi, iyi bir yazar da önce "Bu soru benden hangi türü istiyor?" diye sorar.

Bu konu tam olarak şunu öğretir: bir sınav sorusuna veya bir yazı görevine baktığında hangi essay türünün istendiğini teşhis etmek ve o türe uygun iskeleti kurmak. Altı temel tür vardır: görüş (opinion/argumentative), tartışma (discussion), problem-çözüm, neden-sonuç, avantaj-dezavantaj ve karşılaştırma. Bunlar özellikle IELTS Writing Task 2, TOEFL Independent Writing, üniversite sınav yazıları ve akademik ödevlerin bel kemiğidir.

When we say "composition" in Turkish, one thing usually comes to mind: writing about a topic with nice sentences. In academic English, however, an essay is a family of types. Each type answers a different question, requires a different plan of thought and is built with a different paragraph architecture. Just as an engineer draws a different plan depending on whether they are building a bridge or a building, a good writer first asks, "Which type is this question asking me for?"

This topic teaches exactly this: when you look at an exam question or a writing task, to diagnose which essay type is wanted and to build the skeleton suited to that type. There are six basic types: opinion/argumentative, discussion, problem-solution, cause-effect, advantages-disadvantages and comparison. These are the backbone especially of IELTS Writing Task 2, TOEFL Independent Writing, university exam essays and academic assignments.

Why does it exist? Because academic writing is a way of thinking. Defending an idea, weighing two views, solving a problem or comparing two things are different mental operations; the language too is shaped to reflect that operation. The right type gives the reader the message "here is exactly what I am doing" from the very first paragraph.

The biggest parallel for the Turkish student: the "introduction-development-conclusion" pattern we remember from school. That is a correct start but insufficient; because the inside of the "development" is filled completely differently depending on the type:

In an opinion essay

development = reasons

In a discussion essay

development = two sides

In problem-solution

development = problems and solutions

Why is it hard?
The source of the difficulty is not the language but reading the question. Students who know English well lose points by writing only their own view in response to a "Discuss both views and give your opinion" question. So the error is not in grammar but in misdiagnosing the task type. That is why the heart of this topic is not vocabulary but strategy.
  • Essay = tür ailesi: tek bir kalıp değil; altı ana tür vardır ve her biri farklı bir soruya cevap verir.
  • Önce soruyu oku, sonra planla: yapı seçimi her zaman soru kalıbından çıkar (Do you agree? / Discuss both / What problems?).
  • Görüş (opinion): net bir tez al, 2-3 gerekçeyle savun. Kararsız kalma.
  • Tartışma (discussion): iki görüşü de dengeli sun, sonra kendi tarafını belirt. Sadece kendi görüşünü yazma!
  • Problem-çözüm: her soruna somut, uygulanabilir bir çözüm eşle.
  • Neden-sonuç: nedenleri ve etkileri ayrı ayrı, mantık zinciriyle bağla.
  • Avantaj-dezavantaj: artıları/eksileri dengele; "outweigh" sorusunda hangisinin ağır bastığına karar ver.
  • Karşılaştırma: benzerlik ve farkları sistematik kıyasla; rastgele sıçrama yapma.
  • Her türde iskelet aynı kalır: giriş (paraphrase + tez) → gövde paragrafları → sonuç. Değişen, gövdenin içeriğidir.
  • Yanlış tür = düşük puan: İngilizcen kusursuz olsa bile görev tipini yanlış okursan Task Response puanın düşer.

  • Essay = a family of types: not a single pattern; there are six main types and each answers a different question.
  • Read the question first, then plan: the choice of structure always comes from the question pattern (Do you agree? / Discuss both / What problems?).
  • Opinion: take a clear thesis, defend it with 2-3 reasons. Do not sit on the fence.
  • Discussion: present both views in a balanced way, then state your own side. Do not write only your own view!
  • Problem-solution: match each problem with a concrete, workable solution.
  • Cause-effect: link the causes and the effects separately, with a chain of logic.
  • Advantages-disadvantages: balance the pros/cons; in an "outweigh" question, decide which one weighs more.
  • Comparison: compare similarities and differences systematically; do not jump around randomly.
  • The skeleton stays the same in every type: introduction (paraphrase + thesis) → body paragraphs → conclusion. What changes is the content of the body.

Every essay type shares the same three-part spine: Introduction → Body paragraphs → Conclusion. The only thing that changes is the content of the body. Let us first build the common skeleton, then see how it is filled according to the type.

The common skeleton (all types)

Formula: Introduction = [paraphrase the question] + [thesis/general approach]. Body = [topic sentence + explanation + example] × 2-3. Conclusion = [restate the thesis] + [final evaluation].

Introduction: It is often argued that governments should fund public transport rather than build new roads. This essay agrees with that view for two compelling reasons.

Conclusion: In conclusion, prioritising public transport is the wiser policy, chiefly because it protects the environment and eases congestion.

The thesis is restated + the two reasons are summarised. No new idea is added.

Keep in Mind: 'Tell – Tell – Tell'
The classic way to remember the three-part spine: "Tell them what you will say → say it → tell them what you said." That is, the Introduction announces to the reader what you will do (paraphrase + thesis), the Body does the actual work (reason + example), and the Conclusion gathers up what was done (adding no new idea). For each body paragraph there is also a short rule of thumb: T-E-E → Topic sentence → Explanation → Example. Without a topic sentence the paragraph is 'headless', and without an example it is 'evidence-less'.

1) Görüş / Argümantatif (Opinion / Argumentative)

Soru kalıbı: "Do you agree or disagree?", "To what extent do you agree?", "What is your opinion?". Görev: Net bir pozisyon al ve onu 2-3 gerekçeyle savun. En sık hata: kararsız kalmak. Bir tarafı seç ve sonuna kadar arkasında dur. "To what extent" (ne ölçüde) sorusunda kısmi katılım da mümkündür ("largely agree but...").

I strongly agree that plastic packaging should be banned, primarily because of its devastating impact on marine life.
Plastik ambalajın yasaklanması gerektiğine kesinlikle katılıyorum; öncelikle deniz yaşamı üzerindeki yıkıcı etkisi nedeniyle.
Net tez (strongly agree) + öncelikli gerekçenin sinyali (primarily because).
While I largely support online learning, I would argue that it cannot fully replace face-to-face interaction.
Çevrimiçi öğrenmeyi büyük ölçüde desteklesem de, yüz yüze etkileşimin yerini tamamen alamayacağını savunuyorum.
'To what extent' sorusuna uygun kısmi/nüanslı tez (largely... but not fully).
There is no doubt in my mind that space exploration deserves public funding, as the scientific spin-offs benefit everyone.
Uzay araştırmalarının kamu fonu hak ettiğine zerre şüphem yok; çünkü bilimsel yan ürünler herkese yarar sağlar.
Güçlü kesinlik ifadesi + gerekçe (spin-offs).

2) Tartışma (Discussion — 'Discuss both views')

Soru kalıbı: "Discuss both these views and give your own opinion." Görev: İki karşıt görüşü de dengeli ve adil biçimde sun, sonra kendi görüşünü belirt. En sık hata: sadece kendi görüşünü yazıp karşı tarafı ihmal etmek. Bir gövde paragrafı A görüşüne, bir gövde paragrafı B görüşüne ayrılır; kendi görüşün girişte belirtilir ve sonuçta pekiştirilir.

On the one hand, supporters of homework claim it reinforces classroom learning and builds discipline.
Bir yandan, ödev savunucuları onun sınıf öğrenimini pekiştirdiğini ve disiplin kazandırdığını iddia eder.
Görüş A'yı adil sunan kalıp (On the one hand, supporters claim).
On the other hand, critics contend that excessive homework causes stress and erodes family time.
Öte yandan, eleştirmenler aşırı ödevin strese yol açtığını ve aile zamanını aşındırdığını savunur.
Görüş B'yi dengeleyen kalıp (On the other hand, critics contend).
Having weighed both arguments, I am inclined to believe that moderate, purposeful homework is beneficial.
Her iki argümanı tarttıktan sonra, ölçülü ve amaçlı ödevin yararlı olduğuna meylediyorum.
Kendi görüşünü tartıştıktan sonra veren olgun kalıp (Having weighed... I am inclined to believe).

3) Problem-Çözüm (Problem-Solution)

Soru kalıbı: "What problems does this cause and what solutions can you suggest?" Görev: Bir sorunu tanımla ve ona somut, uygulanabilir çözümler eşle. En sık hata: soyut çözümler ("people should be more careful"). Her çözüm belirli bir aktörle (hükümet, okullar, bireyler) ve mekanizmayla bağlanmalıdır.

A pressing problem is that rapid urbanisation has overwhelmed public housing.
Acil bir sorun, hızlı kentleşmenin kamu konutlarını çökertmiş olmasıdır.
Sorunu net tanımlayan konu cümlesi (A pressing problem is that...).
One effective solution would be for city councils to offer tax incentives to developers who build affordable flats.
Etkili bir çözüm, şehir meclislerinin uygun fiyatlı daire yapan müteahhitlere vergi teşviki sunması olurdu.
Somut çözüm: aktör (city councils) + mekanizma (tax incentives). Soyut değil.
This measure would, in turn, ease the housing shortage without inflating rents.
Bu önlem, karşılığında, kiraları şişirmeden konut sıkıntısını hafifletirdi.
Çözümün sonucunu gösteren bağlama (would, in turn, ease).

4) Neden-Sonuç (Cause-Effect)

Soru kalıbı: "What are the causes of X and what effects does it have?" Görev: Bir olgunun nedenlerini ve etkilerini mantık zinciriyle ayrı ayrı açıkla. En sık hata: neden ile sonucu karıştırmak. Nedenler "neden oluyor?", etkiler "ne olur?" sorusuna cevap verir.

The main cause of childhood obesity is the widespread availability of cheap, calorie-dense fast food.
Çocukluk çağı obezitesinin başlıca nedeni, ucuz ve yüksek kalorili fast-food'un yaygın erişilebilirliğidir.
Nedeni net veren kalıp (The main cause of X is...).
As a direct consequence, rates of type 2 diabetes among adolescents have risen sharply.
Doğrudan bir sonuç olarak, ergenlerde tip 2 diyabet oranları keskin biçimde arttı.
Etkiyi neden-sonuç bağıyla veren kalıp (As a direct consequence).
This, in turn, places an enormous strain on already stretched health services.
Bu da, karşılığında, zaten zorlanan sağlık hizmetlerine muazzam bir yük bindirir.
Zincirin ikinci halkası (This, in turn, places strain) — etkinin etkisi.

5) Avantaj-Dezavantaj (Advantages-Disadvantages)

Soru kalıbı: "What are the advantages and disadvantages?" veya "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?" Görev: Artıları ve eksileri dengeli sun. Dikkat: "outweigh" (ağır basmak) sorusunda karar vermelisin — hangi taraf daha ağır? Sadece listelemek yetmez; tartıp bir yargıya varmalısın.

One clear advantage of remote work is the elimination of stressful daily commutes.
Uzaktan çalışmanın açık bir avantajı, stresli günlük yolculukların ortadan kalkmasıdır.
Avantajı net veren kalıp (One clear advantage of X is...).
Nevertheless, a significant drawback is the blurring of boundaries between work and home life.
Yine de, önemli bir sakınca, iş ile ev yaşamı arasındaki sınırların bulanıklaşmasıdır.
Dezavantajı karşıtlıkla veren kalıp (Nevertheless, a significant drawback is...).
On balance, the flexibility of remote work outweighs its isolating tendencies.
Genel olarak, uzaktan çalışmanın esnekliği, yalnızlaştırıcı eğilimlerine ağır basar.
'Outweigh' sorusuna net karar veren kalıp (On balance... outweighs...). Kararsız kalma!

6) Karşılaştır-Karşıtla (Compare-Contrast)

Soru kalıbı: İki seçenek/dönem/yaklaşım kıyaslanır (ör. "Compare traditional and online education"). Görev: Benzerlikleri ve farkları sistematik sun. İki düzen vardır: blok düzeni (önce A'nın hepsi, sonra B'nin hepsi) veya nokta-nokta düzeni (her paragrafta bir ölçüt üzerinden A ve B). En sık hata: rastgele sıçrayarak kıyaslamak.

Both traditional and online classrooms aim to transfer knowledge, yet they differ markedly in flexibility.
Hem geleneksel hem çevrimiçi sınıflar bilgi aktarmayı amaçlar, ancak esneklik bakımından belirgin farklıdır.
Benzerlik + fark ölçütünü aynı cümlede kuran kalıp (Both... yet they differ in...).
Whereas a lecture hall demands physical presence, an online course can be accessed at any hour.
Bir amfi fiziksel varlık talep ederken, çevrimiçi bir kurs her saatte erişilebilir.
Karşıtlık bağlacıyla nokta-nokta kıyas (Whereas A..., B...).
Similarly, both formats increasingly rely on digital assessment tools.
Benzer biçimde, her iki format da giderek dijital değerlendirme araçlarına dayanır.
Benzerliği vurgulayan geçiş (Similarly, both...).
Püf Noktası: Tür teşhisi kısayolları
İki somut parmak kuralı türü saniyede yakalatır. 1) 'Discuss'ta iki S var → iki taraf. Discuss both views gördüğünde kelimedeki çift S'i "iki görüş" diye hatırla: bir gövde paragrafı bir tarafa, bir gövde paragrafı ötekine. 2) 'Outweigh' = terazi. TIP

Before you touch the pen, mark the key word in the prompt: agree/disagree means one side, discuss both two sides, problems/solutions problem-solution. Diagnosing the type is half of the right plan.

1) Opinion / Argumentative

Question pattern: "Do you agree or disagree?", "To what extent do you agree?", "What is your opinion?". Task: Take a clear position and defend it with 2-3 reasons. The most common error: sitting on the fence. Choose a side and stand behind it all the way. In a "To what extent" question a partial agreement is also possible ("largely agree but...").

I strongly agree that plastic packaging should be banned, primarily because of its devastating impact on marine life.
I strongly agree that plastic packaging should be banned, primarily because of its devastating impact on marine life.
A clear thesis (strongly agree) + the signal of the primary reason (primarily because).
While I largely support online learning, I would argue that it cannot fully replace face-to-face interaction.
While I largely support online learning, I would argue that it cannot fully replace face-to-face interaction.
A partial/nuanced thesis suited to a 'To what extent' question (largely... but not fully).
There is no doubt in my mind that space exploration deserves public funding, as the scientific spin-offs benefit everyone.
There is no doubt in my mind that space exploration deserves public funding, as the scientific spin-offs benefit everyone.
A strong expression of certainty + a reason (spin-offs).

2) Discussion ('Discuss both views')

Question pattern: "Discuss both these views and give your own opinion." Task: Present both opposing views fairly and in a balanced way, then state your own view. The most common error: writing only your own view and neglecting the other side. One body paragraph is devoted to view A, one body paragraph to view B; your own view is stated in the introduction and reinforced in the conclusion.

On the one hand, supporters of homework claim it reinforces classroom learning and builds discipline.
On the one hand, supporters of homework claim it reinforces classroom learning and builds discipline.
The pattern that presents view A fairly (On the one hand, supporters claim).
On the other hand, critics contend that excessive homework causes stress and erodes family time.
On the other hand, critics contend that excessive homework causes stress and erodes family time.
The pattern that balances view B (On the other hand, critics contend).
Having weighed both arguments, I am inclined to believe that moderate, purposeful homework is beneficial.
Having weighed both arguments, I am inclined to believe that moderate, purposeful homework is beneficial.
A mature pattern that gives your own view after weighing (Having weighed... I am inclined to believe).

3) Problem-Solution

Question pattern: "What problems does this cause and what solutions can you suggest?" Task: Define a problem and match it with concrete, workable solutions. The most common error: abstract solutions ("people should be more careful"). Each solution must be linked to a specific actor (government, schools, individuals) and a mechanism.

A pressing problem is that rapid urbanisation has overwhelmed public housing.
A pressing problem is that rapid urbanisation has overwhelmed public housing.
A topic sentence that clearly defines the problem (A pressing problem is that...).
One effective solution would be for city councils to offer tax incentives to developers who build affordable flats.
One effective solution would be for city councils to offer tax incentives to developers who build affordable flats.
A concrete solution: actor (city councils) + mechanism (tax incentives). Not abstract.
This measure would, in turn, ease the housing shortage without inflating rents.
This measure would, in turn, ease the housing shortage without inflating rents.
A link showing the result of the solution (would, in turn, ease).

4) Cause-Effect

Question pattern: "What are the causes of X and what effects does it have?" Task: Explain the causes and the effects of a phenomenon separately, with a chain of logic. The most common error: confusing cause with effect. Causes answer "why does it happen?", effects answer "what happens?".

The main cause of childhood obesity is the widespread availability of cheap, calorie-dense fast food.

A pattern that states the cause clearly (The main cause of X is...).
As a direct consequence, rates of type 2 diabetes among adolescents have risen sharply.

A pattern that gives the effect with a cause-effect link (As a direct consequence).
This, in turn, places an enormous strain on already stretched health services.
This, in turn, places an enormous strain on already stretched health services.
The second link of the chain (This, in turn, places strain) — the effect of the effect.

5) Advantages-Disadvantages

Question pattern: "What are the advantages and disadvantages?" or "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?" Task: Present the pros and cons in a balanced way. Note: in an "outweigh" question you must decide — which side is heavier? Merely listing is not enough; you must weigh them and reach a judgement.

One clear advantage of remote work is the elimination of stressful daily commutes.
One clear advantage of remote work is the elimination of stressful daily commutes.
A pattern that states the advantage clearly (One clear advantage of X is...).
Nevertheless, a significant drawback is the blurring of boundaries between work and home life.
Nevertheless, a significant drawback is the blurring of boundaries between work and home life.
A pattern that gives the disadvantage with a contrast (Nevertheless, a significant drawback is...).
On balance, the flexibility of remote work outweighs its isolating tendencies.
On balance, the flexibility of remote work outweighs its isolating tendencies.
A pattern that decides clearly on an 'outweigh' question (On balance... outweighs...). Do not sit on the fence!

6) Compare-Contrast

Question pattern: Two options/periods/approaches are compared (e.g. "Compare traditional and online education"). Task: Present similarities and differences systematically. There are two arrangements: the block arrangement (first all of A, then all of B) or the point-by-point arrangement (A and B on one criterion per paragraph). The most common error: comparing by jumping around randomly.

Both traditional and online classrooms aim to transfer knowledge, yet they differ markedly in flexibility.
Both traditional and online classrooms aim to transfer knowledge, yet they differ markedly in flexibility.
A pattern that sets up a similarity + a difference criterion in one sentence (Both... yet they differ in...).
Whereas a lecture hall demands physical presence, an online course can be accessed at any hour.
Whereas a lecture hall demands physical presence, an online course can be accessed at any hour.
A point-by-point comparison with a contrast conjunction (Whereas A..., B...).
Similarly, both formats increasingly rely on digital assessment tools.
Similarly, both formats increasingly rely on digital assessment tools.
A transition emphasising the similarity (Similarly, both...).
Key Point: type-diagnosis shortcuts
Two concrete rules of thumb catch the type in a second. 1) 'Discuss' has a double S → two sides. When you see Discuss both views, remember the double S in the word as "two views": one body paragraph to one side, one body paragraph to the other. 2) 'Outweigh' = a balance scale. When you see this word, picture a balance scale in your mind; one pan must weigh more — if you leave it balanced, you have not answered the question. Short formula: agree/disagree → one side; discuss both → two sides + you; outweigh → weigh and decide.

Clarity of stance. Sitting on the fence is a weakness in an opinion essay; but taking sides too early upsets the balance in a discussion essay. So the advice "be clear" depends on the type: a clear thesis in opinion, a balanced presentation + a clear conclusion in discussion.

Emphasis and ordering by importance. Starting your reasons from the strongest (or saving the strongest for last) is a rhetorical choice. Telling the reader which argument is the heavy one with expressions like "The most compelling reason..." is a way of managing your case.

Tips & Tricks: tuning certainty (hedging)
For a high band, soften absolute words: always → often / tends to, completely → largely / significantly, everyone → many / most. If a sentence of yours is so overstated it cannot be refuted ("X always leads to Y"), the reader finds an exception in their mind and your claim weakens. Reflex: after writing a strong claim, ask "is there an exception to this?"; if there is, scale it with may, can, in most cases, arguably. This fine-tuning is also a signal of maturity in the speaking exam. (Warning: this is a stylistic tip, not a rigid rule; in an opinion essay that needs a clear thesis, excessive softening is also counted as a weakness.)

Most of the traps a Turkish student falls into when writing an essay come not from grammar but from Turkish writing culture. The Turkish academic/literary tradition favours a more circular, associative structure that lengthens the opening sentence; English academic writing, by contrast, is direct, thesis-first and linear.

Turkish writing culture

Circular and associative; it saves the thesis for the end, lengthens the introduction, and rewards the ornate sentence.

↔
English academic writing

Linear and thesis-first; it gives the idea in the introduction, advances with example/reason, and writes transition words explicitly.

  • Delaying the thesis. In Turkish, saving the idea for the end can be considered elegant; in English the thesis is stated clearly in the introduction. The reader wants to know what you are arguing from the very first paragraph.
  • Evidence instead of ornament. In a Turkish composition, beautiful/literary sentences can earn points; in an English essay, points come not from vague beauty but from concrete examples and reasons.
  • The 'I' emphasis. In Turkish 'bence' (in my opinion) can occur very often; in English academic writing 'In my opinion' is not overused, it is positioned clearly once.
  • Lack of connectors. Turkish clauses are linked with suffixes; in English the transition words (however, therefore, moreover) must be written explicitly.

Whenever students sit down to write an essay, their instinct is to start producing sentences immediately. This instinct is exactly what separates a mediocre essay from a strong one, because the single most important decision comes before the first word: identifying what type of essay the question actually demands. Consider a typical prompt: "Some people believe that governments should invest in space exploration, while others feel this money should be spent on solving problems on Earth. Discuss both views and give your own opinion." A hurried writer sees the word "space" and begins arguing passionately for or against space programmes. Yet the crucial instruction is buried in the final sentence: discuss both views. This is a discussion essay, not an opinion essay, and the two require completely different structures. In a discussion essay, one body paragraph must present the case for space exploration fairly—the scientific breakthroughs, the technological spin-offs, the inspiration it provides—while a second body paragraph gives equal weight to the opposing view: that hunger, disease, and poverty are more urgent claims on public money. Only after both sides have been treated honestly does the writer reveal a personal position, usually in the conclusion. Compare this with an opinion prompt: "To what extent do you agree that space exploration is a waste of money?" Here the task flips. The writer must take a clear stance from the outset and defend it with two or three well-developed reasons, perhaps acknowledging a counter-argument only to refute it. The lesson is simple but easily forgotten under exam pressure: read the instruction words before you plan, because they silently dictate the entire architecture of your essay. A brilliant vocabulary and flawless grammar cannot rescue an essay that answers the wrong question. Master the art of diagnosing the task type, and half the battle is already won.

  2. "Some think fame brings happiness; others disagree. Discuss both views and give your opinion."

  3. "What problems does traffic congestion cause and how can they be solved?"

  4. "Do the advantages of studying abroad outweigh the disadvantages?"
    Advantages-disadvantages (outweigh). Plan: advantages + disadvantages + a CLEAR decision on which one weighs more.
  5. "What are the causes of rising obesity and what effects does it have on society?"
    Cause-effect. Plan: paragraph(s) of causes + paragraph(s) of effects. Do not confuse cause with effect.

    Correct: Sitting on the fence and not choosing a side
    An opinion essay demands a clear position. Saying 'both sides have a point' is failing to do the task.

    Correct: Decide which one weighs more
    'Outweigh' demands a judgement; merely listing is not enough, you must say which side weighs more.

    Correct: People should be more careful
    This is abstract and unmeasurable. A good solution contains a specific actor and a concrete mechanism; the other three options do this.
  5. What should the FIRST sentence of the introduction be?
    Correct: Paraphrasing the question
    A good introduction does not copy the question; it re-expresses it in your own words (paraphrase). Copying earns no points.

    Correct: It gives the effect/definition instead of the cause
    'People become unhealthy' is not the cause of obesity but obesity itself/its effect. A cause must answer 'why does it happen?'.

    Correct: The introduction-body-conclusion skeleton
    The three-part spine is the same in all types; only how the body is filled changes.

    Correct: An advantages-disadvantages / weighing conclusion
    'On balance' is used when weighing two sides and reaching a judgement; it suits the conclusion of outweigh questions very well.

    Correct: Nuance and hedging (measured certainty)

    Correct: You skip half the task, and the score drops
    All parts of a hybrid question must be answered; skipping half directly lowers the Task Response/Achievement score.

    Correct: Add a completely new argument
    The conclusion gathers up the existing argument; adding a new idea surprises the reader and breaks the structure.

Where to start
Teach the student reading the question first, not writing. In the first lesson have them write nothing; just put 8-10 different questions on the table and have them diagnose which type each one wants. Do not move to paragraph writing until students can confidently distinguish the types. Because the whole building rests on this diagnosis; if the diagnosis is wrong, grammar correction is meaningless.

Questions for concept checking: 'Does this question ask you to choose a side, or to present both sides?' / 'What does the word "outweigh" here force you to do?' / 'Is this a solution, or just a wish (people should...)?' These questions test not the rule but understanding.

Activity suggestion: 'Type-matching cards' — question patterns on one side, type names on the other; students match them. Then a 'plan race': whoever draws the correct skeleton fastest for a given question wins. The writing itself is given as homework; class time is devoted to diagnosis and planning, because that is the real skill.

The single most common error
Students' number-one error: writing a one-sided opinion essay in response to a 'Discuss both views' question. This single error is the main reason even students who write good English get stuck at 6.0 in IELTS. For every discussion question, have them check 'Is there another side too?' — until it becomes a reflex.
