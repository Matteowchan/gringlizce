These two question types must be solved very quickly but are the ones most often missed by rushing; the rule is always to read the goal first.

Rhetorical Synthesis, Digital SAT Reading & Writing bölümünün en mekanik ama en çok puan sızdıran alt başlığıdır. Sana bir dizi not (bullet points) ve belirli bir retorik hedef verilir; görevin, dört seçenek arasından o hedefe en etkili şekilde ulaşan cümleyi bulmaktır. İşin püf noktası şudur: seçeneklerin çoğu notlardaki bilgilerle gerçektir, ama yalnızca biri sana verilen görevi yerine getirir. Buna biz doğru-ama-alakasız (true but irrelevant) tuzağı diyoruz.

Rhetorical Synthesis is the most mechanical but most point-leaking sub-topic of the Digital SAT Reading & Writing section. You are given a series of notes (bullet points) and a specific rhetorical goal; your task is to find, among four choices, the sentence that reaches that goal most effectively. The key point is this: most of the choices are, by the information in the notes, true, but only one carries out the task you were given. We call this the true but irrelevant trap.

Aynı beceri ailesinin ikinci yarısı Transitions (geçiş kelimeleri) sorularıdır: iki cümle arasındaki mantık ilişkisini (zıtlık, sonuç, örnek, ekleme...) doğru okuyup uygun bağlacı seçmen istenir. Bu sayfada iki tipi de sıfırdan, gerçek SAT zorluğunda örneklerle işliyoruz. Geçiş kelimelerinin gramer temeli için gramer-linking-words sayfasına da göz atabilirsin.

The Digital SAT Reading & Writing section is divided into four main content areas. Rhetorical Synthesis and Transitions fall under the Expression of Ideas area. This area measures how effectively and logically a text conveys information — that is, not grammatical correctness but communicative effectiveness.

Expression of Ideas questions ask you to revise a text to improve the effectiveness of written expression and to meet a specific rhetorical goal.
Bu sorular, bir metni belirli bir retorik hedefe ulaşacak ve anlatımı daha etkili olacak şekilde revize etmeni ister. "Doğru cümle" değil, "görevi yapan cümle" ararsın.

By the numbers:

  • Each R&W module contains 27 questions; two modules in total (Module 1 and the adaptive Module 2).
  • Expression of Ideas accounts for about 3-4 questions per module; within that, Rhetorical Synthesis is usually 1-2, and Transitions is 1-2 questions.
  • Across the whole test you'll see on average around 6-8 Transitions + Synthesis questions. It looks small, but because it's a fast and guaranteed-points area, it is strategically very valuable.
Teacher's Note
Rhetorical Synthesis questions are the most learnable part of the Reading & Writing section. Unlike reading-comprehension questions, there is no "interpretation" here; there is a clear goal and a clear correct answer. A student who once internalizes the method almost never misses these questions. So this page is one of the highest-return investments.

Bu beceri altında iki ayrı format vardır. Ayırt etmeyi öğrenmek, hangi refleksi kullanacağını bilmek demektir.

1) Rhetorical Synthesis (notlardan hedef cümle)

Soru, önce bir not listesi verir, ardından hep aynı kalıptaki yönergeyi kullanır:

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
• ...
• ...

The student wants to [belirli bir hedef]. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
"Öğrenci şu hedefi gerçekleştirmek istiyor. Notlardaki ilgili bilgiyi bu hedefe en etkili şekilde kullanan seçenek hangisi?" Buradaki kritik ifadeler: most effectively (en etkili) ve accomplish this goal (bu hedefi gerçekleştir). Yani ölçüt "doğruluk" değil, "hedefe uygunluk".

Sık görülen hedef fiilleri: emphasize (vurgula), introduce ... to an audience unfamiliar with (tanımayan bir okura tanıt), compare / contrast (karşılaştır/zıtla), present a finding / conclusion (bir bulgu/sonuç sun), explain the purpose / method (amaç/yöntem açıkla), describe (betimle).

2) Transitions (geçiş kelimeleri)

Kısa bir pasaj verilir, bir cümlenin başında boşluk vardır ve dört geçiş ifadesi arasından mantığa uygun olanı seçmen istenir:

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?
"Metni en mantıklı geçiş ifadesiyle tamamlayan seçenek hangisi?" Burada gramer değil, iki cümle arasındaki mantık yönü test edilir: ekleme mi, zıtlık mı, sonuç mu, örnek mi?
İpucu
İki tipi hemen ayırt et: Not listesi + "the student wants to..." görürsen bu Synthesis; boşluklu tek pasaj + "most logical transition" görürsen bu Transitions. Refleksin farklı olmalı.

There are two separate formats under this skill. Learning to tell them apart means knowing which reflex to use.

1) Rhetorical Synthesis (goal sentence from notes)

The question first gives a note list, then always uses a prompt in the same pattern:

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
• ...
• ...

The student wants to [belirli bir hedef]. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
"Öğrenci şu hedefi gerçekleştirmek istiyor. Notlardaki ilgili bilgiyi bu hedefe en etkili şekilde kullanan seçenek hangisi?" Buradaki kritik ifadeler: most effectively (en etkili) ve accomplish this goal (bu hedefi gerçekleştir). Yani ölçüt "doğruluk" değil, "hedefe uygunluk".

Common goal verbs: emphasize (stress), introduce ... to an audience unfamiliar with (introduce to a reader unfamiliar with it), compare / contrast (compare/contrast), present a finding / conclusion (present a finding/conclusion), explain the purpose / method (explain the purpose/method), describe (describe).

2) Transitions (transition words)

A short passage is given, there is a blank at the start of a sentence, and you are asked to choose the logically appropriate one among four transition expressions:

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?
"Metni en mantıklı geçiş ifadesiyle tamamlayan seçenek hangisi?" Burada gramer değil, iki cümle arasındaki mantık yönü test edilir: ekleme mi, zıtlık mı, sonuç mu, örnek mi?
Tip
Tell the two types apart at once: if you see a note list + "the student wants to...", this is Synthesis; if you see a single passage with a blank + "most logical transition", this is Transitions. Your reflex should be different.

Rhetorical Synthesis için 4 adım

  1. Önce hedefi oku, notları sonra. Çoğu öğrenci notları baştan sona okuyarak zaman kaybeder. Halbuki notlar sadece bir bilgi havuzu; önemli olan hedefin ne istediği. Yönergedeki fiili (emphasize / compare / introduce...) daire içine al.
  2. Hedefi tek cümlelik bir kritere çevir. Örnek: hedef "emphasize the sophistication relative to later devices" ise kriterin: "Seçenek, cihazın sonraki cihazlara kıyasla gelişmişliğini öne çıkarıyor mu?" Bu kriter, doğru cevabın filtresidir.
  3. Her seçeneği kritere karşı test et, notlara değil. Seçeneğin notlarda geçmesi (doğru olması) yeterli DEĞİL. Soruyu her seçenek için sor: "Bu, hedefi yapıyor mu?" Yapmıyorsa ele — bilgi doğru olsa bile.
  4. Kalan tek seçeneği doğrula. Genelde hedefteki anahtar kelimeyi (comparison, difference, growth...) somutlayan tek seçenek kalır.

Transitions için 3 adım

  1. Geçişi kapat, iki cümleyi çıplak oku. Boşluktan önceki cümle ile sonraki cümlenin ilişkisini kendi kelimenle adlandır: "ikinci cümle birinciyle çelişiyor" (zıtlık), "birincinin sonucu" (sonuç), "örneği" (örnek), "ekstra bilgi" (ekleme).
  2. İlişkiyi bir kategoriye yerleştir. Yukarıdaki Hızlı Başvuru tablosundaki kategorilerden birine oturt. Bu adım, seçenekleri okumadan yapılmalı ki çeldiriciler seni yönlendirmesin.
  3. Seçeneklerden o kategoriye ait olanı seç. Çoğu zaman dört seçenek dört farklı kategoriye aittir (bir zıtlık, bir sonuç, bir ekleme, bir örnek). Kategoriyi doğru koyduysan cevap kendini gösterir.
Dikkat
Transitions sorusunda sadece boşluğa komşu cümleye bakma yeterli olmayabilir; bazen ilişkiyi anlamak için bir önceki cümleyi de okuman gerekir. Özellikle "however" mi "in fact" mi ikileminde, ikinci cümlenin ilkini çeldiği mi yoksa güçlendirdiği mi ancak bağlamla anlaşılır.

4 steps for Rhetorical Synthesis

  1. Read the goal first, the notes later. Most students waste time reading the notes from start to finish. But the notes are just an information pool; what matters is what the goal asks for. Circle the verb in the prompt (emphasize / compare / introduce...).
  2. Turn the goal into a one-sentence criterion. Example: if the goal is "emphasize the sophistication relative to later devices," your criterion is: "Does the choice foreground the device's sophistication relative to later devices?" This criterion is the filter for the correct answer.
  3. Test each choice against the criterion, not the notes. It is NOT enough for a choice to appear in the notes (to be true). Ask for each choice: "Does this do the goal?" If not, eliminate it — even if the information is true.
  4. Verify the one remaining choice. Usually only one choice remains that makes the key word in the goal (comparison, difference, growth...) concrete.

3 steps for Transitions

  1. Cover the transition, read the two sentences bare. Name in your own words the relationship between the sentence before the blank and the sentence after: "the second sentence contradicts the first" (contrast), "the result of the first" (result), "an example of it" (example), "extra information" (addition).
  2. Place the relationship into a category. Fit it into one of the categories in the Quick Reference table above. This step should be done without reading the choices, so the distractors don't steer you.
  3. Choose the option that belongs to that category. Most of the time the four choices belong to four different categories (one contrast, one result, one addition, one example). If you placed the category correctly, the answer reveals itself.
Caution
In a Transitions question, looking only at the sentence next to the blank may not be enough; sometimes you must also read the sentence before to understand the relationship. Especially in the "however" vs. "in fact" dilemma, whether the second sentence contradicts or strengthens the first can only be understood from context.

A few invariable rules govern these two question types. Memorize them.

#RuleWhy
1In Synthesis, truth isn't enough, the task is essential.All four choices can be true from the notes; the only thing that distinguishes them is fit to the goal.
2Read the goal before the notes.The notes are an unfiltered pile of information; the goal gives you your filter.
3In a "compare/contrast" goal, the choice must include both sides.A choice describing only one side does not make a comparison.
4In an "introduce to an unfamiliar audience" goal, the choice must give basic identifying information.Not an expert detail, but a sentence that introduces the topic is needed.
5In Transitions, the connective is chosen by logic, not grammar.All four choices fit grammatically; what distinguishes them is the direction of the logic.
6however ≠ therefore ≠ in fact: contrast / result / emphasis are different things.The most frequently confused trio; each points in a different direction.

Advanced
in fact / indeed is not a contrast connective; it strengthens or intensifies the previous idea. Example: "The bridge was old. In fact, it had not been repaired in eighty years." Here the second sentence does not contradict the first, it intensifies it. 700+ students never confuse "however" with "in fact": one changes direction, the other deepens in the same direction.

Rhetorical Synthesis tuzakları

  • Doğru-ama-alakasız (true but irrelevant): Notlardan tam doğru bir bilgi verir, ama hedefle ilgisizdir. En sık ve en tehlikeli tuzak. Örnek: hedef "başarıyı vurgula" iken sana türün ağırlığını söyler — doğru ama görevle alakasız.
  • Tek not tekrarı: Sadece bir notu aynen tekrarlar; hedef ise iki notu ilişkilendirmeni ister (ör. karşılaştırma). Tek taraflı kalır.
  • Kapsam kaydıran (scope shift): Hedefin sorduğundan daha geniş veya daha dar bir şey söyler. "Sonraki cihazlara kıyasla" isteniyorsa, sadece "ilk cihazdı" demek kapsamı kaydırır.
  • Karşıt hedef: Hedefin tam tersini yapar. "Başarıyı vurgula" isteniyorsa, düşüşü (fewer than 50 remained) anlatan seçenek karşıt yöndedir.

Transitions tuzakları

  • Yanlış yön: İki cümle zıt iken "therefore" (sonuç) veya "similarly" (benzerlik) koyar.
  • Sahte sebep-sonuç: İki cümle sadece sıralanmışken "consequently" ile zorla nedensellik uydurur.
  • Vurgu / zıtlık karışımı: "in fact" (güçlendirme) ile "however" (zıtlık) arasında seni şaşkına çevirir.
  • Örnek yerine ekleme: İkinci cümle bir örnek iken "moreover" (ekleme) verir; ikisi yakın ama özdeş değil.
Sık Hata
Öğrenciler Synthesis'te "en doğru görünen" veya "en çok bilgi içeren" seçeneği işaretler. SAT bunu bilir ve en uzun, en "bilgi dolu" seçeneği bilerek yanıltıcı yapar. Uzunluk = doğruluk değildir. Tek ölçüt: hedef fiili yapıyor mu?

The SAT's distractors in these questions are not random; they are produced in specific patterns. If you recognize the patterns, you'll see what the wrong choices are trying to do as you read them.

Reflex

For each Rhetorical Synthesis choice, ask a single question: "Does this sentence carry out the goal?" Information that is true but does not serve the goal is the most tempting distractor.

Rhetorical Synthesis traps

  • True-but-irrelevant: Gives a perfectly true piece of information from the notes, but it is unrelated to the goal. The most common and most dangerous trap. Example: when the goal is "emphasize the success," it tells you the species' weight — true but irrelevant to the task.
  • Single-note repetition: Merely repeats a single note; but the goal asks you to relate two notes (e.g. comparison). It stays one-sided.
  • Scope-shifting (scope shift): Says something broader or narrower than the goal asks. If "relative to later devices" is required, saying only "it was the first device" shifts the scope.
  • Opposite goal: Does the exact opposite of the goal. If "emphasize the success" is required, a choice describing the decline (fewer than 50 remained) is in the opposite direction.

Transitions traps

  • Wrong direction: When the two sentences are contrasting, it puts "therefore" (result) or "similarly" (comparison).
  • False cause-effect: When the two sentences are merely sequential, it forces causality with "consequently."
  • Emphasis / contrast mix-up: Confuses you between "in fact" (strengthening) and "however" (contrast).
  • Addition instead of example: When the second sentence is an example, it gives "moreover" (addition); the two are close but not identical.
Common Mistake
In Synthesis, students mark the "most correct-looking" or "most information-packed" choice. The SAT knows this and deliberately makes the longest, most "information-dense" choice misleading. Length ≠ correctness. The only criterion: does it do the goal verb?
While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
• The Antikythera mechanism is an ancient Greek device recovered from a shipwreck in 1901.
• It is made of bronze and contains at least 30 interlocking gears.
• Scholars believe it was used to predict astronomical positions and eclipses decades in advance.
• Some historians call it the first known analog computer.
• Its complexity was not matched by any surviving device for over a thousand years.

The student wants to emphasize the mechanism's technological sophistication relative to later devices. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

A) The Antikythera mechanism, made of bronze, was recovered from a shipwreck in 1901.
B) Containing at least 30 interlocking gears, the Antikythera mechanism was used to predict astronomical positions.
C) The Antikythera mechanism was so sophisticated that no surviving device matched its complexity for over a thousand years.
D) Some historians call the Antikythera mechanism, an ancient Greek device, the first known analog computer.

A) The Antikythera mechanism, made of bronze, was recovered from a shipwreck in 1901.
B) Containing at least 30 interlocking gears, the Antikythera mechanism was used to predict astronomical positions.
C) The Antikythera mechanism was so sophisticated that no surviving device matched its complexity for over a thousand years.
D) Some historians call the Antikythera mechanism, an ancient Greek device, the first known analog computer.
Hedefi çözelim: Anahtar ifade "sophistication relative to later devices" — yani gelişmişliği sonraki cihazlarla kıyaslayarak vurgula. Kriterimiz: seçenek, cihazın gelişmişliğini zaman içinde/sonraki cihazlara kıyasla öne çıkarıyor mu?

Correct answer: C. "so sophisticated that no surviving device matched its complexity for over a thousand years" combines both the word sophistication and the comparison with later devices (not surpassed for over a thousand years) in a single sentence. Exactly the heart of the goal.

Distractor analysis
A — True-but-irrelevant: that it is bronze and was found in 1901 is true, but has nothing to do with sophistication or comparison. A classic trap.
B — True information (30 gears, astronomical prediction) but the comparison dimension is missing; it only says what it does, the "relative to later devices" comparison is absent. Half-right.
D — The strongest distractor. "First known analog computer" implies sophistication and looks tempting. But the goal wants superiority relative to later devices; D, however, is a naming/priority claim (being first), and makes no comparison with later ones. It shifts scope. C beats D precisely because it explicitly establishes this temporal comparison.

B) However,

B) However,
C) For instance,
D) In fact,
İlişkiyi kuralım: İlk cümle bir beklenti kuruyor: ekonomistler istihdamın düşeceğini varsaydı. İkinci cümle ise bir bulgu veriyor: istihdam düşmedi, hafifçe yükseldi. Yani ikinci cümle birinci ile çelişiyor. Aradığımız ilişki: zıtlık.

Correct answer: B (However). The expectation (jobs fall) and what actually happened (jobs rose slightly) run in opposite directions; "However" establishes this unexpected contrast exactly.

Distractor analysis
A) As a result — False cause-effect. The second sentence is not the result of the first; on the contrary, it is a finding that overturns its expectation. Wrong direction.
C) For instance — Example connective. The second sentence is not an example of the claim in the first; it is data that refutes it. Wrong category.
D) In fact — The subtlest distractor. "In fact" strengthens/makes concretethe previous idea; but here the second sentence does not strengthen the first, it contradicts it. A student may drift to D with a "striking fact" feeling, but "in fact" does not change direction — so it is wrong. Where contrast is needed, it is never "in fact."
Tip
When you see an "assumed / expected / most believed"-type expectation word, predict that the next sentence will often break that expectation — that is, be ready for a contrast transition (however, but, yet, instead).

Sık Hata
Concession ile contrastı karıştırmak. "Admittedly, the method was expensive; however, it saved time." Burada iki geçiş de var: "admittedly" bir ödün (karşıya hak ver), "however" asıl dönüşü yapar. Boşluk ödün kısmındaysa "admittedly/granted", asıl zıtlık kısmındaysa "however/nevertheless" gerekir. Cümledeki hangi yarı boşlukta, ona bak.
Wrong Reading the notes carefully from start to finish and then looking at the goal. Time is lost, and without the goal's filter all the notes look "important."
Correct Read the goal sentence first, mark its verb; return to the notes only with that filter. You won't use most of the notes at all.
Wrong Marking true information, thinking "this choice appears in the notes, so it might be right."

Wrong In a comparison goal, choosing a choice that describes only one side ("Study A used geolocators").

Common Mistake
Concession confused with contrast. "Admittedly, the method was expensive; however, it saved time." Here there are two transitions: "admittedly" is a concession (grant the other side), "however" makes the real turn. If the blank is in the concession part, you need "admittedly/granted"; if in the actual contrast part, "however/nevertheless." Which half of the sentence the blank is in — look at that.

The eight questions below are of real SAT difficulty. Solve each with the method, then press Check.

1) While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
• The kakapo is a flightless, nocturnal parrot native to New Zealand.
• Weighing up to 4 kilograms, it is the world's heaviest parrot.
• By the 1990s, fewer than 50 individuals remained in the wild.
• Conservationists relocated the survivors to predator-free islands.
• The population has since grown to around 250.

The student wants to emphasize the success of the conservation effort. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct: C. The goal is "emphasize the success." Only C, by showing a change/increase (from under 50 to around 250), makes the success concrete. A and B are true but irrelevant kinds of facts (nocturnal, weight). D only describes the decline — the opposite direction, not success.

2) The new titanium-aluminum alloy is remarkably lightweight, a property that makes it highly attractive for reducing fuel consumption in commercial aircraft. ______ its tendency to become brittle at the low temperatures of high-altitude flight has so far prevented engineers from adopting it for such uses.

Correct: B. The first sentence is an advantage (lightweight, attractive), the second a disadvantage (brittleness, couldn't be used) — contrast. "Therefore" gives result, "For example" example, "Likewise" comparison; all the wrong direction.

3) While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
• Two research teams independently studied songbird migration.
• Team A tracked 200 warblers using lightweight geolocators; Team B tracked 150 thrushes using satellite tags.
• Team A published its work in 2018; Team B in 2020.
• Both teams concluded that the birds relied on Earth's magnetic field to navigate.

The student wants to present the shared conclusion of the two teams. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct: C. The goal is to present the shared conclusion. Only C says the two teams reached the same conclusion. A and D, on the contrary, emphasize the differences (opposite goal). B is only the publication dates — true-but-irrelevant.

4) Dr. Alvarez had expected the samples from the hyperarid desert to be almost entirely lifeless, since standard models predict that such soils cannot support microbial communities. ______ her analysis revealed a startling diversity of drought-resistant bacteria thriving beneath the surface crust.

Correct: B. The expectation (almost lifeless) and what actually happened (astonishing diversity) are diametrically opposed; "Instead" overturns the expectation and puts the reality in its place — the strongest choice. "Consequently" false result, "For instance" example, "In addition" addition; all the wrong direction. "However" would also work, but among the choices "Instead" most clearly conveys the overturning of expectation.

5) While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
• Kintsugi is a traditional Japanese art of repairing broken pottery.
• The cracks are filled with lacquer mixed with powdered gold.
• Rather than hiding the damage, the technique highlights it.
• The practice dates back to the 15th century.
• Some collectors deliberately break ceramics to have them repaired this way.

The student wants to introduce kintsugi to an audience unfamiliar with it. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct: B. The introduce goal gives a reader who knows nothing about the topic the basic definition: only B answers "kintsugi — what is it?" (a traditional Japanese art of repairing pottery). C and D give detail/advanced information and assume the reader already knows what kintsugi is (missing information). A, meanwhile, is an expert/marginal detail that does not introduce the topic (irrelevant to the goal).

6) Many desert plants have evolved remarkable strategies for surviving long stretches without rain. ______ the saguaro cactus can store hundreds of liters of water in its pleated stem, which visibly expands after a downpour.

Correct: B. The second sentence is, of the general claim in the first sentence (water-storage strategies), a concrete example — the saguaro cactus. "For example" establishes exactly this relationship. "Nevertheless" and "In contrast" require contrast, but there is no contradiction here (wrong direction); "Therefore" invents a false cause-effect.

7) While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
• Johann Sebastian Bach and George Frideric Handel were both born in Germany in 1685.
• Bach spent his entire career in Germany; Handel eventually settled in England.
• Bach was known mainly for church and instrumental music; Handel for operas and oratorios.
• Both composers went blind late in life.

The student wants to emphasize a similarity between the two composers. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct: C. The similarity goal asks for a shared trait; only C gives the aspects the two composers shared (same year/country, both went blind). A and B, with "whereas/while," on the contrary emphasize the differences (opposite goal). D is a one-sided detail that describes only Handel (missing information, no comparison).

8) A century of exposure to salt air had badly corroded the suspension bridge's original iron cables, and inspectors found several load-bearing strands close to snapping. ______ the city closed the span to all traffic until a complete replacement could be installed.

Correct: B. The cables reaching the point of snapping is a cause, and closing the bridge is its direct result; "Consequently" establishes this real causality. The trap here is the "everything is contrast" reflex: "However" and "Nevertheless" require contrast, but the two sentences don't contradict (wrong direction), and there is no example relationship for "For instance."

Doğru ama hedefe hizmet etmeyen cümle — bu tipin en pahalı tuzağıdır. Seçenek notlardan birebir doğru bir bilgi verir, dolayısıyla "yanlış" diyecek bir gerekçe bulamazsın ve elini kaldırırsın. Oysa doğruluk bir ön koşuldur, ölçüt değildir. Ölçüt tek: hedefteki fiili yapıyor mu? "Benzerliği vurgula" denen bir soruda iki cismin farkını anlatan seçenek kusursuz derecede doğru olabilir — ama karşıt hedefe hizmet ettiği için yanlıştır. 750+ öğrenci, "bu doğru" hissini bir kenara bırakıp "bu görevi yapıyor mu?" sorusuna geçmeyi otomatikleştirmiştir.

İki-üç notu birleştiren cümlelerin gücü

Doğru cevap çoğu zaman tek bir notu tekrarlayan değil, iki veya üç notu tek cümlede ilişkilendiren seçenektir. Bunun nedeni basittir: hedefler genellikle bir ilişki ister — karşılaştırma, sebep-sonuç, değişim, ortak sonuç. Tek not, ilişkinin yalnızca bir ucunu taşır; ilişkiyi ise ancak notları birbirine bağlayan bir cümle kurabilir.

Örneğin "koruma çabasının başarısını vurgula" hedefinde, "populasyon 50'nin altındaydı" (bir not) ile "şimdi 250'ye çıktı" (başka bir not) tek başına yetersizdir; ikisini birleştiren "50'nin altından 250'ye yükseldi" cümlesi başarıyı — yani değişimi — somutlar. Bu yüzden bir seçeneğin birden çok notu ustaca birleştirdiğini gördüğünde, o genellikle güçlü bir adaydır: sadece doğru olmakla kalmaz, hedefin istediği ilişkiyi de kurar.

İpucu
Bir seçenek yalnızca tek notu aynen tekrarlıyorsa şüphelen: hedef bir ilişki istiyorsa (karşılaştırma, değişim, ortak sonuç) tek-not cümlesi neredeyse her zaman eksik kalır. İki notu bağlayan seçeneği önce test et.

After you've internalized the method, what really increases your speed is knowing what you're looking for the moment you see the verb in the goal sentence. The SAT generates Rhetorical Synthesis goals from a limited number of patterns; once you recognize these patterns, before reading the choices you already know what shape the correct answer must have.

Common goal patterns and what each looks for

The table below summarizes the goal verbs you see most often and what form the sentence that genuinely meets that goal must take. Mark the verb, recall the row, weigh the choices against that criterion.

Goal patternWhat it looks forForm of the winning sentence
emphasize a contrast (emphasize a contrast)Foregrounding the difference between two thingsA sentence that names both sides and establishes the opposition between them (whereas / unlike / by contrast)
emphasize a similarity (emphasize a similarity)Foregrounding the shared aspect of two thingsA sentence that covers both subjects and states the trait they share (both / like / similarly)
introduce ... to an unfamiliar audience (introduce)For a reader who knows nothing about the topic, a basic definition"X — what is it?" — the sentence answering this, which assumes no prior knowledge
generalize (generalize)From individual examples, a broad inferenceAn umbrella / general-principle sentence that covers the specific data
specify / illustrate (specify / illustrate)Supporting the general claim with a concrete detail A specific sentence containing a number, name, or single case

Your reflex: underline the goal first, then return to the notes

The most reliable shortcut for this skill is a reading order: read the goal sentence before the notes and underline its verb. The notes are an unfiltered pile of information; diving into them without reading the goal means trying to memorize all five lines without knowing which information will be useful to you. After you mark the verb and return to the notes, you'll read most notes by eliminating them — you stop only at the one or two notes that can serve the goal.

Practical tip: turn the verb into a criterion ("emphasize a similarity" → "is there a shared trait?") and ask this criterion of each of the four choices one by one. A choice that fails the criterion is eliminated, no matter how true its information.

Advanced
A sentence that is true but does not serve the goal — this is the costliest trap of this type. The choice gives an exactly true piece of information from the notes, so you can't find grounds to call it "wrong," and you raise your hand. But truth is a precondition, not the criterion. The criterion is single: does it do the goal verb? In a question that says "emphasize a similarity," a choice describing the difference between two objects can be flawlessly true — but it is wrong because it serves the opposite goal. The 750+ student has automated moving past the "this is true" feeling to the question "does this do the task?"

The power of sentences that combine two or three notes

The correct answer is often not the one that repeats a single note, but the one that relates two or three notes in a single sentence. The reason is simple: goals usually ask for a relationship — comparison, cause-effect, change, shared conclusion. A single note carries only one end of the relationship; only a sentence that links the notes together can build the relationship.

For example, in the goal "emphasize the success of the conservation effort," "the population was under 50" (one note) and "it has now risen to 250" (another note) are insufficient alone; the sentence that combines the two, "it rose from under 50 to 250," makes the success — that is, the change — concrete. So when you see a choice skillfully combining several notes, it is usually a strong candidate: it is not only true, it also builds the relationship that the goal wants.

Tip
Be suspicious if a choice merely repeats a single note verbatim: if the goal wants a relationship (comparison, change, shared conclusion), a single-note sentence almost always falls short. Test the choice that links two notes first.

Rhetorical Synthesis ve Transitions, Digital SAT R&W'nin en öğrenilebilir, en hızlı puan veren alanıdır. Özetle:

  • Synthesis: Önce hedefi oku, fiilini işaretle, hedefi tek cümlelik bir kritere çevir. Her seçeneği o kritere karşı test et. Doğruluk yetmez, görev şarttır.
  • En büyük tuzak: doğru-ama-alakasız seçenek. Bilgi notlardan gerçek olabilir ama hedefi yapmıyorsa yanlıştır.
  • Karşılaştırma hedefinde iki tarafı da içeren; tanıtım hedefinde temel tanımı veren seçenek doğrudur.
  • Transitions: geçişi kapat, iki cümlenin ilişkisini adlandır (zıtlık/sonuç/örnek/ekleme), o kategoriye ait seçeneği bul. however ≠ therefore ≠ in fact.
  • Bu soruları 30-45 saniyede bitirip zamanı zor sorulara aktar.

Rhetorical Synthesis and Transitions are the most learnable, fastest-scoring area of the Digital SAT R&W. In summary:

  • Synthesis: Read the goal first, mark its verb, turn the goal into a one-sentence criterion. Test each choice against that criterion. Truth isn't enough, the task is essential.
  • The biggest trap: the true-but-irrelevant choice. The information may be true from the notes, but if it doesn't do the goal, it's wrong.
  • In a comparison goal, the one that includes both sides; in an introduction goal, the one that gives the basic definition is correct.
  • Transitions: cover the transition, name the relationship of the two sentences (contrast/result/example/addition), find the choice belonging to that category. however ≠ therefore ≠ in fact.
  • Finish these questions in 30-45 seconds and transfer the time to hard questions.
