Sınav Hakkında

Bu ara sınav, Ünite 1-10'da öğrendiğin her şeyi C2 seviyesinde, senin yaşına uygun çocuk dostu örneklerle ölçer: register ve üslup aralığı; öne çekme (fronting) ve tematik konum; end-weight ve end-focus; cleft yapılarının ustalığı; retorik tercih olarak devrik yapılar (inversion); hedging, boosting ve stance (duruş); nominalizasyon ve dilbilgisel eğretileme; bağdaşıklık (gönderim/yerine koyma/eksilti); söylem belirteçleri ve metin örgütlenmesi; ve modalitede ince ayrım. Speaking hariç dört beceri test edilir. Toplam süre 100 dakikadır ve tek bir sayaçla ilerler — süreyi bölümler arasında kendin yönetirsin.

Reading (2 metin · 20 soru)

Suggested time: 25 minutes. (Önerilen süre: 25 dakika.) Read both texts, then answer the questions. (İki metni oku, soruları cevapla.)

Text 1 · The Busy World of Bees

Every time you eat a crunchy apple, a sweet strawberry or a spoonful of honey, there is a tiny insect you ought to thank: the honeybee. Bees may look small and unimportant as they buzz from flower to flower, yet the quiet work they do keeps a huge part of our food growing. Without them, our meals would be far less colourful, and far less sweet.

The secret lies in something called pollination. As a bee lands on a flower to drink its sugary nectar, a fine yellow dust called pollen sticks to its furry body. When the bee flies on to the next flower, some of that pollen rubs off, and this is exactly what the plant needs in order to make seeds and fruit. In this way, a single bee can help hundreds of flowers in a single morning.

Life inside a beehive is wonderfully organised. Thousands of bees live together, and each one has a job to do. Some clean the hive, some guard the entrance, and some fly out to search for flowers. When a bee finds a good patch of blossom, it does not keep the news to itself; instead, it performs a special 'waggle dance' that tells the other bees exactly where to go.

Sadly, bees today face a number of serious problems. Farmers sometimes use strong chemicals that can harm them, and the wild meadows where bees once found plenty of flowers are slowly disappearing. When there are fewer flowers, there is less food, and hungry bees find it much harder to survive the long winter.

The good news is that everyone can help, even children. Planting a few flowers in a garden or on a balcony gives bees somewhere to feed, and leaving a shallow dish of water outside offers them a safe place to drink. These may seem like tiny actions, but if enough people take them, they could make an enormous difference to the future of these remarkable little insects.

Text 2 · The Old Treehouse

Leo had not climbed into the old treehouse for two whole years, and as he stood at the bottom of the garden that afternoon, he could not quite say why he had stayed away so long. He told his friends he had simply grown too busy with football and homework; but the real reason, he suspected, was closer to worry — the worry that the little wooden house he remembered so fondly might have fallen apart, or might never have been quite as wonderful as he recalled.

The rope ladder still hung from the lowest branch, a little greener with moss than before. Leo climbed it slowly, half expecting the whole thing to wobble and creak beneath him. It did creak, but it held. When he pushed open the small door at the top, he found the treehouse exactly where it had always been, waiting quietly among the leaves.

Inside, everything was smaller than he remembered. The shelf where he had once kept his comics was still there, though the comics themselves were gone. It was the smell, though, rather than any of these sights, that took him by surprise — the warm, dusty scent of old wood, which carried him straight back to long summer afternoons spent hiding from the rain. On the wall, a faded drawing of a pirate ship, made when he was seven, was still pinned in place.

Leo sat down on the wooden floor and looked out through the little window at the garden below. He did not feel sad, as he had feared he might. Instead, he felt oddly grateful. The treehouse had not changed at all; it was he who had grown taller, older and busier. Somehow, knowing that his favourite hiding place was still here, still standing, made the whole world feel a little safer.

When he finally climbed back down, the afternoon light was turning golden. Leo promised himself that he would not leave it another two years before returning. Some places, he realised as he walked back towards the house, are worth visiting again and again — not because they have changed, but precisely because they have not.

Writing (2 görev)

Suggested time: 35 minutes — Task 1: 13 min, Task 2: 22 min. (Önerilen süre: 35 dakika — Görev 1: 13 dk, Görev 2: 22 dk.) Öğretmenin değerlendirecek; örnek cevap yoktur.

Task 1 · Opinion Email (120-150 words)

Your school has suggested turning half of the playground into a vegetable garden that all the classes would look after together. The school newsletter has asked pupils for their views. Write a formal email to the newsletter giving your opinion. (Okulun, bahçenin yarısını tüm sınıfların birlikte bakacağı bir sebze bahçesine çevirmeyi önerdi. Okul bülteni öğrencilerin görüşünü soruyor. Görüşünü belirten resmî bir e-posta yaz.)

  • Maintain a consistent formal register throughout. (baştan sona tutarlı resmî register)
  • Use at least one cleft or inversion for emphasis ("It is the noise that..."; "Not only would this..."). (vurgu için en az bir cleft ya da devrik yapı)
  • Hedge or boost at least one claim ("The proposal may well..."; "There is no doubt that..."). (en az bir iddiada hedging/boosting)
  • Use at least 3 discourse markers: however, nevertheless, moreover, therefore. (en az 3 söylem belirteci)
  • 120-150 words. (120-150 kelime)
E-POSTA YAPISI
  • Açılış: "Dear Editor," + neden yazdığın ("I am writing to share my view on the garden proposal...").
  • Gövde: görüşün + en az iki gerekçe; bir cleft/devrik yapıyla vurgu ver.
  • Kapanış: görüşünü özetle + "Yours faithfully," + adın.
0 kelime

Task 2 · For-and-Against Paragraph (160-200 words)

Some people believe that children should be given homework every single day. Others strongly disagree. Discuss the arguments for and against this view, and then give your own opinion. (Bazıları çocuklara her gün ödev verilmesi gerektiğini düşünür; bazıları buna kesinlikle katılmaz. Bu görüşün lehindeki ve aleyhindeki argümanları tartış, sonra kendi görüşünü ver.)

  • Introduce the topic, then give one paragraph FOR and one AGAINST. (biri lehte, biri aleyhte)
  • Use at least one nominalisation ("the rapid rise in...") and one cleft/inversion. (bir nominalizasyon + bir cleft/devrik)
  • Signal your stance with hedging or boosting ("It could be argued..."; "There is little doubt..."). (hedging/boosting ile duruş)
  • Use at least 5 discourse markers: however, whereas, nevertheless, moreover, therefore. (en az 5 söylem belirteci)
  • End with a clear opinion. (net bir görüşle bitir)
  • 160-200 words. (160-200 kelime)
