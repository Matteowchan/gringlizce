Academic Task 1 gives you a visual (a graph, table, map or process diagram) and asks you to describe it in at least 150 words, in roughly 20 minutes. You do not give an opinion; you select, summarise and compare the data. This guide covers every visual type one by one, step by step, with template sentences, data language and band 5–9 comparisons.

1. Task 1 nedir ve senden tam olarak ne isteniyor?

IELTS Writing sınavı iki bölümden oluşur ve toplam süre 60 dakikadır. Task 1 için önerilen süre 20 dakika, Task 2 için 40 dakikadır. Task 1 puanının Task 2'ye göre ağırlığı daha düşüktür (kabaca 1/3), bu yüzden Task 1'e 20 dakikadan fazla harcamak stratejik bir hatadır. Ama düşük ağırlık "önemsiz" demek değildir: zayıf bir Task 1, genel Writing bandını yarım tam puan aşağı çekebilir.

Academic Task 1'de görevin bir görsel bilgiyi (visual information) kendi cümlelerinle özetlemek ve öne çıkan özellikleri raporlamaktır. Sana bir soru cümlesi (prompt) ve bir görsel verilir. Klasik yönerge şudur:

The chart below shows the percentage of households in owned and rented accommodation in England and Wales between 1918 and 2011. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Bu yönergedeki üç anahtar fiil senin görevini tanımlar: summarise (özetle — her sayıyı değil, önemli olanı), select and report the main features (ana özellikleri seç ve raporla) ve make comparisons (karşılaştır). Görüş, sebep, tahmin ya da öneri istenmez.

"Task 1 rapor ister, yorum değil: gördüğünü seç, özetle ve karşılaştır — sebebini asla tahmin etme.

Academic Task 1'de görebileceğin görsel tipleri

  • Line graph — zaman içinde değişen bir ya da birkaç eğri

1. What is Task 1 and exactly what is being asked of you?

The IELTS Writing test has two parts and lasts 60 minutes in total. The recommended time for Task 1 is 20 minutes, and 40 minutes for Task 2. Task 1 carries less weight than Task 2 (roughly one third), so spending more than 20 minutes on it is a strategic mistake. But low weight does not mean "unimportant": a weak Task 1 can pull your overall Writing band down by half a whole point.

In Academic Task 1 your job is to summarise visual information in your own words and report the most striking features. You are given a prompt and a visual. The classic instruction is:

The chart below shows the percentage of households in owned and rented accommodation in England and Wales between 1918 and 2011. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

The three key verbs in this instruction define your task: summarise (summarise — not every number, only what matters), select and report the main features and make comparisons. You are not asked for opinions, causes, predictions or recommendations.

"Task 1 asks for a report, not commentary: select, summarise and compare what you see — never guess the reason.

The visual types you may see in Academic Task 1

  • Line graph — one or more curves changing over time
  • Bar chart — comparison across categories (vertical/horizontal bars)
  • Pie chart — percentage slices of a whole (sometimes a comparison of 2 pies)
  • Table — raw figures in rows and columns
  • Map — usually two periods: a place in the past vs the future/present
  • Process / diagram — the stages of a manufacturing or natural cycle
  • Mixed question — e.g. a line graph + a table on the same page

The General Training Task 1 difference (a short note)

In the General Training module, Task 1 is not a graph but a letter (complaint, request, invitation, apology, etc.). Tone (formal / semi-formal / informal) and letter structure (salutation, body, closing) are essential. This page focuses on the Academic visual task; the GT letter type is a separate topic and is covered on its own guide page. The data language and band logic below do not apply to GT — there the language criteria are politeness and clarity of purpose.

2. Assessment criteria: how is your score calculated?

Task 1 is scored on four criteria, each weighted 25%. The final Task 1 band is the average of these four. Knowing what each criterion measures tells you which sentence to spend energy on.

CriterionWhat does it measure?Its concrete equivalent in Task 1
Task Achievement (TA)Did you complete the task fully: 150+ words, accurate data, a clear overview, appropriate selectionIs there an overview sentence? Are the numbers correct? Did you select the main trends or list every figure?
Coherence & Cohesion (CC)Logical flow and linking: paragraphing, connectors, reference (this, such)A clear 4-paragraph structure; correct use of connectors like while, whereas, in contrast, meanwhile
Lexical Resource (LR)Range and accuracy of vocabulary; paraphrasing power, collocationNot repeating the same verb: rise / climb / surge / soar; collocations like a sharp increase
Grammatical Range & Accuracy (GRA)Variety of sentence structures and grammatical accuracyComparison structures, relative clauses, the passive (in processes), correct tense (past/present/future), error-free sentences
The critical takeaway for a Turkish student

Half of TA depends on one thing: the overview. Without an overview, TA can almost never exceed 6 — meaning that however beautifully you write, your overall band is capped. So your first investment should not be fancy vocabulary but a clear overview.

3. Dört paragraflık altın yapı

Görsel tipi ne olursa olsun (süreç hariç, ona 5. bölümde değineceğiz) güvenli iskelet dört paragraftır. Bu yapı hem CC puanını korur hem de yazarken düşünmeyi kolaylaştırır.

  1. Giriş (paraphrase): Soru cümlesini kendi kelimelerinle yeniden yaz. Kopyalama; eş anlamlı + yapı değişimi kullan. 1–2 cümle yeter.
  2. Overview (genel eğilimler): Sayı vermeden en çarpıcı 2–3 özelliği söyle: en yüksek/düşük, genel yön, en büyük değişim. Bandın kalbi burası.
  3. Detay paragrafı 1: Verinin bir grubunu somut sayılarla anlat (ör. artan seriler, ya da ilk dönem).
  4. Detay paragrafı 2: Kalan grubu sayılarla anlat (ör. azalan seriler, ya da ikinci dönem). Karşılaştırmayı burada yoğunlaştır.

Giriş (paraphrase) için kalıp

The line graph illustrates how the proportion of households that owned versus rented their homes in England and Wales shifted over a period of almost one hundred years, from 1918 to 2011.

Parafraz araç kutusu: shows → illustrates / depicts / presents / gives information about; the number of → the figure for / the quantity of; percentage → proportion / share; from 1918 to 2011 → over a 93-year period / between 1918 and 2011.

Overview için kalıp (sayı YOK)

Overall, home ownership rose to become the dominant form of tenure for most of the period, whereas renting followed the opposite trend, although the gap narrowed sharply in the final years.

Overview başlatıcıları: Overall, / In general, / It is clear that / A striking feature is that… Overview'da kesin rakam vermezsin; onları detay paragraflarına saklarsın.

3. The golden four-paragraph structure

Whatever the visual type (except a process, which we cover in section 5), the safe skeleton is four paragraphs. This structure both protects your CC score and makes thinking easier while you write.

  1. Introduction (paraphrase): Rewrite the prompt sentence in your own words. Do not copy; use synonyms + a change of structure. 1–2 sentences are enough.
  2. Overview (general trends): State the 2–3 most striking features without giving numbers: the highest/lowest, the general direction, the biggest change. This is the heart of the band.
  3. Detail paragraph 1: Describe one group of the data with concrete figures (e.g. the rising series, or the first period).
  4. Detail paragraph 2: Describe the remaining group with figures (e.g. the falling series, or the second period). Concentrate the comparisons here.

A template for the introduction (paraphrase)

The line graph illustrates how the proportion of households that owned versus rented their homes in England and Wales shifted over a period of almost one hundred years, from 1918 to 2011.

Paraphrase toolkit: shows → illustrates / depicts / presents / gives information about; the number of → the figure for / the quantity of; percentage → proportion / share; from 1918 to 2011 → over a 93-year period / between 1918 and 2011.

A template for the overview (NO numbers)

Overall, home ownership rose to become the dominant form of tenure for most of the period, whereas renting followed the opposite trend, although the gap narrowed sharply in the final years.

Overview openers: Overall, / In general, / It is clear that / A striking feature is that… In the overview you do not give exact figures; you save them for the detail paragraphs.

4. Grafik tipleri — her biri derinlemesine

Aşağıda her görsel tipi için üç şey var: nasıl okunur, hangi dil, ve model overview + model detay cümlesi.

4.1 · Line graph (çizgi grafik)

Nasıl okunur: Yatay eksen genellikle zamandır (yıl, ay). Dikey eksen ölçülen değerdir. Her çizgi bir seridir. Önce genel yönü (yükseliyor/düşüyor/dalgalı) yakala, sonra başlangıç–bitiş, en yüksek/düşük nokta ve çizgilerin kesiştiği yılları not al.

Hangi dil: Zaman geçmişse past simple (rose, fell). Değişim fiilleri + derece zarfları: rose sharply, fell steadily, fluctuated. Zaman ifadeleri: in 1990, over the next decade, by 2011.

Overview: Overall, the two figures moved in opposite directions: ownership climbed for most of the century while rented accommodation declined, before both trends levelled off near the end.
Detay: The proportion of owned homes stood at just under 25% in 1918 and rose steadily to a peak of around 69% in 2001, before dipping slightly to 64% by 2011.

4.2 · Bar chart (çubuk grafik)

Nasıl okunur: Zaman olmayabilir — çubuklar kategoriler (ülkeler, yaş grupları) arası karşılaştırmadır. En uzun ve en kısa çubuğu, gruplaşmaları (benzer yükseklikte olanlar) ve varsa dönem farkını (aynı kategoride 2 çubuk) yakala.

Hangi dil: Karşılaştırma ağır basar: the highest / the lowest / the second most, more than twice as many, roughly equal to, far fewer than. Zaman yoksa present simple kullanılabilir.

Overview: Overall, spending on leisure was highest among the oldest group, whereas the youngest respondents allocated the largest share to technology.
Detay: At approximately 45%, holidays accounted for the biggest expense among over-60s — more than double the figure recorded for those under 30.

4.3 · Pie chart (pasta grafik)

Nasıl okunur: Her dilim bir bütünün yüzdesidir; toplam %100'dür. En büyük ve en küçük dilimi bul. Sık formatta iki pasta verilir (ör. 2000 vs 2020) — o zaman görev, dilimlerin dönemler arası değişimini karşılaştırmaktır.

Hangi dil: Oran/parça dili: accounts for, makes up, represents, constitutes; the largest share, a quarter, just over half, a fifth. İki pasta varsa değişim fiilleri de girer.

Overview: Overall, cars were the most common means of commuting in both years, yet their dominance weakened as public transport gained ground over the two decades.
Detay: In 2000, private cars accounted for exactly half of all journeys, but this share fell to 38% by 2020, while train use rose from a fifth to almost a third.

4.4 · Table (tablo)

Nasıl okunur: Tablo en çok ham veri içeren ve en kolay "her sayıyı listeleme" tuzağına düşülen tiptir. Her hücreyi yazmazsın. Satır/sütun bazında en yüksek, en düşük ve dikkat çekici örüntüyü seç. Toplam/ortalama satırı varsa oradan başla.

Hangi dil: Seçme ve karşılaştırma dili: the highest figure was recorded for…, in terms of…, when it comes to…, by comparison. Yaklaşıklık dili sık gerekir (yuvarlanmamış sayılar için).

Overview: Overall, Germany consistently reported the highest figures across all three categories, while Japan showed the most noticeable growth over the period.
Detay: In terms of car ownership, Germany led with around 570 vehicles per 1,000 people, whereas the figure for India remained below 30 — the lowest in the table.

4.5 · Map (harita — değişim)

Nasıl okunur: Genellikle bir yerin iki (bazen üç) dönemi verilir: 1990 vs bugün, ya da "şimdi" vs "planlanan". Görev, neyin eklendiğini, kaldırıldığını, taşındığını, dönüştürüldüğünü, genişletildiğini raporlamaktır. Yön (kuzey/güney/doğu/batı) ve konum ifadeleri esastır.

Hangi dil: Ağırlıkla edilgen (passive) ve konum dili: was built / was demolished / was replaced by / was converted into / was relocated to the north; in the north-eastern corner, adjacent to, on the site of the former…. Zaman: geçmiş→şimdi ise past + present; şimdi→gelecek ise will be / is going to be.

Overview: Overall, the town changed from a largely rural settlement into a developed residential area, with green spaces giving way to housing and new transport links.
Detay: The woodland in the north was cleared to make way for a housing estate, while the small farm to the east was replaced by a supermarket and a car park.

4.6 · Process / diagram (süreç şeması)

Nasıl okunur: Sayı yoktur; aşamalar (stages) vardır. İki tür: man-made (üretim: tuğla, kâğıt, çikolata) ve natural cycle (yaşam döngüsü, su döngüsü). Önce kaç aşama olduğunu ve başlangıç–bitiş noktasını say; döngüyse "sürekli tekrar eden" olduğunu belirt.

Hangi dil: Sıra bağlaçları + edilgen present simple: First, / Next, / After that, / Subsequently, / Finally; the beans are harvested, the mixture is heated, once it has cooled…. Overview burada "kaç aşama / doğal mı yapay mı / girdi-çıktı" der.

Overview: Overall, the process consists of seven distinct stages, beginning with the harvesting of raw cacao and ending with the packaging of finished chocolate bars.
Detay: In the first stage, the ripe pods are harvested by hand. After that, the beans are removed and left to ferment, before being dried under the sun.

4. Graph types — each one in depth

For each visual type below there are three things: how to read it, which language to use, and a model overview + model detail sentence.

4.1 · Line graph

How to read it: The horizontal axis is usually time (year, month). The vertical axis is the measured value. Each line is a series. First catch the general direction (rising/falling/fluctuating), then note the start–end, the highest/lowest point and the years where the lines cross.

Which language: If time is in the past, use the past simple (rose, fell). Change verbs + degree adverbs: rose sharply, fell steadily, fluctuated. Time expressions: in 1990, over the next decade, by 2011.

Overview: Overall, the two figures moved in opposite directions: ownership climbed for most of the century while rented accommodation declined, before both trends levelled off near the end.
Detail: The proportion of owned homes stood at just under 25% in 1918 and rose steadily to a peak of around 69% in 2001, before dipping slightly to 64% by 2011.

4.2 · Bar chart

How to read it: There may not be time — the bars compare categories (countries, age groups). Catch the longest and shortest bar, the groupings (those of similar height) and, if present, the difference between periods (two bars in the same category).

Which language: Comparison dominates: the highest / the lowest / the second most, more than twice as many, roughly equal to, far fewer than. If there is no time, the present simple can be used.

Overview: Overall, spending on leisure was highest among the oldest group, whereas the youngest respondents allocated the largest share to technology.
Detail: At approximately 45%, holidays accounted for the biggest expense among over-60s — more than double the figure recorded for those under 30.

4.3 · Pie chart

How to read it: Each slice is a percentage of a whole; the total is 100%. Find the largest and smallest slice. A common format gives two pies (e.g. 2000 vs 2020) — in that case the task is to compare how the slices change between periods.

Which language: Proportion/part language: accounts for, makes up, represents, constitutes; the largest share, a quarter, just over half, a fifth. If there are two pies, change verbs come in as well.

Overview: Overall, cars were the most common means of commuting in both years, yet their dominance weakened as public transport gained ground over the two decades.
Detail: In 2000, private cars accounted for exactly half of all journeys, but this share fell to 38% by 2020, while train use rose from a fifth to almost a third.

4.4 · Table

How to read it: The table holds the most raw data and is the type where it is easiest to fall into the "listing every number" trap. You do not write out every cell. Select the highest, the lowest and the striking pattern by row/column. If there is a total/average row, start there.

Which language: Selecting and comparing language: the highest figure was recorded for…, in terms of…, when it comes to…, by comparison. Approximation language is often needed (for un-rounded numbers).

Overview: Overall, Germany consistently reported the highest figures across all three categories, while Japan showed the most noticeable growth over the period.
Detail: In terms of car ownership, Germany led with around 570 vehicles per 1,000 people, whereas the figure for India remained below 30 — the lowest in the table.

4.5 · Map (change)

How to read it: Usually two (sometimes three) periods of a place are given: 1990 vs today, or "now" vs "planned". The task is to report what was added, removed, relocated, converted or expanded. Direction (north/south/east/west) and location expressions are essential.

Which language: Mostly the passive and location language: was built / was demolished / was replaced by / was converted into / was relocated to the north; in the north-eastern corner, adjacent to, on the site of the former…. Tense: if past→present, use past + present; if present→future, use will be / is going to be.

Overview: Overall, the town changed from a largely rural settlement into a developed residential area, with green spaces giving way to housing and new transport links.
Detail: The woodland in the north was cleared to make way for a housing estate, while the small farm to the east was replaced by a supermarket and a car park.

4.6 · Process / diagram

How to read it: There are no numbers; there are stages. Two kinds: man-made (manufacturing: brick, paper, chocolate) and natural cycle (life cycle, water cycle). First count how many stages there are and identify the start–end point; if it is a cycle, state that it "repeats continuously".

Which language: Sequencing connectors + the passive present simple: First, / Next, / After that, / Subsequently, / Finally; the beans are harvested, the mixture is heated, once it has cooled…. Here the overview states "how many stages / natural or man-made / input-output".

Overview: Overall, the process consists of seven distinct stages, beginning with the harvesting of raw cacao and ending with the packaging of finished chocolate bars.
Detail: In the first stage, the ripe pods are harvested by hand. After that, the beans are removed and left to ferment, before being dried under the sun.

5.3 · Karşılaştırma dili

  • compared with / in comparison with the previous year — önceki yıla kıyasla
  • ownership rose, whereas / while renting fell — zıtlık (iki seri karşıtsa)
  • twice as many men as women; three times higher than… — kat karşılaştırması
  • the figure for A was far higher than / slightly lower than that for B
  • In contrast, / By contrast, / On the other hand, — cümle başı zıtlık
  • A and B followed a similar pattern / moved in line with each other — benzerlik
In 2011, there were almost twice as many owner-occupied homes as rented ones, whereas the reverse had been true in 1918.

5.4 · Yaklaşıklık — sayıyı tam vermek zorunda değilsin

Grafikten okunan değer nadiren tam sayıdır; yaklaşıklık dili hem daha doğal hem daha güvenlidir.

  • approximately / around / roughly / about 40%
  • just under 50% (49-ish), just over 50% (51-ish)
  • well over 60%, nearly / almost 70%
  • a little more than a quarter, close to half, the vast majority
The figure stood at just under a fifth in 1918 and reached well over two-thirds by 2001.

Common error: adjective instead of adverb, adverb instead of adjective. ✗ It rose sharp. → ✓ It rose sharply. / ✗ a rise sharply → ✓ a sharp rise.

5.3 · Comparison language

  • compared with / in comparison with the previous year — compared with the previous year
  • ownership rose, whereas / while renting fell — contrast (when two series are opposed)
  • twice as many men as women; three times higher than… — multiple comparison
  • the figure for A was far higher than / slightly lower than that for B
  • In contrast, / By contrast, / On the other hand, — sentence-initial contrast
  • A and B followed a similar pattern / moved in line with each other — similarity
In 2011, there were almost twice as many owner-occupied homes as rented ones, whereas the reverse had been true in 1918.

5.4 · Approximation — you don't have to give the exact number

The value read off a graph is rarely a whole number; approximation language is both more natural and safer.

  • approximately / around / roughly / about 40%
  • just under 50% (49-ish), just over 50% (51-ish)
  • well over 60%, nearly / almost 70%
  • a little more than a quarter, close to half, the vast majority
The figure stood at just under a fifth in 1918 and reached well over two-thirds by 2001.

6. Same data, three bands: 5 vs 7 vs 9

"The same observation can be written at any band; what makes the difference is not the data but vocabulary, pattern and accuracy.

Below, a single real observation — "home ownership rose from 23% in 1918 to 64% in 2011" — is written at three different band levels. The difference lies in vocabulary, pattern and accuracy.

BandExample sentenceWhy this band?
Band 5 In 1918 the owned home is 23%. In 2011 it go up to 64%. It is more big now. Wrong tense (is/go up), weak vocabulary (more big), repeated pattern, no approximation.
Band 7 The proportion of owned homes increased significantly, from 23% in 1918 to 64% in 2011. Correct tense and pattern, appropriate vocabulary (proportion, significantly), clear figures. Clean but plain.
Band 9 Having stood at just under a quarter in 1918, the share of owner-occupied housing climbed steadily to peak at almost two-thirds by 2011 — a near-threefold rise. Advanced structure (having + p.p.), natural approximation, collocation (owner-occupied), multiple comparison, fluent and flawless.

What changes on the band ladder?

  • 5→6: tense errors decrease, an overview appears.
  • 6→7: the overview becomes clear, data is selected (not every number), collocation enters, sentence repetition decreases.
  • 7→8: complex sentences flow without error, approximation and comparison feel natural, vocabulary is precisely placed.
  • 8→9: every sentence is purposeful; nothing is forced; the most apt data is selected and the language is effortless.

9. Sık yapılan hatalar (Türk öğrenci odaklı)

1) Görüş / yorum eklemek

Task 1 rapor ister, tartışma değil. ✗ "I think this is because the economy grew…" Sebep tahmini yasak. Sadece görselde görüneni yaz.

2) Her sayıyı listelemek

Özellikle tabloda. Her hücreyi çevirmek "mekanik listeleme"dir ve TA'yı düşürür. Seç: en yüksek, en düşük, en büyük değişim.

3) Overview'ı unutmak

En pahalı hata. Overview yoksa TA 6'da tavanlanır. Ayrı paragraf yap ve Overall ile başlat.

4) Zaman karışıklığı

Geçmiş veri → past simple. Tarihsiz/genel tablo → present. Harita/süreçte edilgen. ✗ "In 1990 sales increase".

5) Soruyu kopyalamak

Giriş cümlesini olduğu gibi almak kelime sayısına sayılmaz ve LR'ı düşürür. Mutlaka parafraz et.

6) 150 kelimenin altında kalmak

Otomatik ceza. Ama 200+ da gereksiz risktir (hata + zaman). Hedef: 160–190 kelime.

7) Zarf/sıfat karışması

✗ rose sharp / a increase sharply. Fiille zarf (sharply), isimle sıfat (a sharp rise).

8) Tek monoton kalıp

Sürekli "X rose to Y" demek. Kalıpları döndür: there was a rise / saw an increase / witnessed growth.

9. Common mistakes (Turkish-student focused)

1) Adding opinion / commentary

Task 1 asks for a report, not an argument. ✗ "I think this is because the economy grew…" Guessing a cause is forbidden. Write only what is visible in the graph.

2) Listing every number

Especially in a table. Translating every cell is "mechanical listing" and lowers TA. Select: the highest, the lowest, the biggest change.

3) Forgetting the overview

The most expensive mistake. Without an overview, TA is capped at 6. Make it a separate paragraph and start it with Overall.

4) Tense confusion

Past data → past simple. A dateless/general table → present. Passive in maps/processes. ✗ "In 1990 sales increase".

5) Copying the question

Taking the introduction sentence as it is does not count towards the word count and lowers LR. Always paraphrase.

6) Staying under 150 words

An automatic penalty. But 200+ is also an unnecessary risk (errors + time). Target: 160–190 words.

7) Confusing adverb/adjective

✗ rose sharp / a increase sharply. Adverb with a verb (sharply), adjective with a noun (a sharp rise).

8) A single monotonous pattern

Constantly saying "X rose to Y". Rotate patterns: there was a rise / saw an increase / witnessed growth.

16–20CheckIs the tense consistent? is there an overview? is it 150+ words? are adverbs/adjectives, singular-plural and the percent sign correct?

Final checklist (do not leave without checking)

  • ☐ Is there a separate Overview paragraph? (Does it start with Overall?)
  • ☐ Did I paraphrase the question, not copy it?
  • ☐ Did I select the main features rather than every number?
  • ☐ Did I make at least one clear comparison?
  • ☐ Is the tense consistent? (passive in maps/processes)
  • ☐ Is it 150+ words? (160–190 is ideal)
