Two-part question (bazen direct question ya da double question denir), IELTS Writing Task 2'de aynı konu hakkında iki farklı soru yönelten soru tipidir. Opinion (agree/disagree) veya discussion (both views) tiplerinden farkı budur: burada tek bir tez tartışılmaz; sana açıkça sorulmuş iki soruya da ayrı ayrı cevap vermen beklenir.

En sık görülen kalıp şudur: bir "Neden?" sorusu artı bir değerlendirme sorusu. Örneğin: "Nowadays many people choose to live alone. Why is this happening? Is it a positive or negative development?" Burada birinci soru sebep, ikinci soru senin görüşünü ister. Başka varyasyonlar da vardır: "What are the causes? What can be done?" ya da "Why...? What effect does it have on...?"

Ayırt etme ipucu: Soruda iki ayrı soru işareti veya iki emir cümlesi görüyorsan, bu bir two-part sorudur. "To what extent do you agree?" tek soru sorar; two-part ise iki kez soru sorar. Bu farkı görmezsen sorunun yarısını cevaplarsın ve Task Response puanın çakılır.

Yapı her zaman 4 paragraftır ve mantık çok nettir:

  • Introduction: Konuyu paraphrase et, iki soruyu da olacağını haber ver ve (ikinci soru görüş istiyorsa) pozisyonunu kısaca belirt.
  • Body 1: Sadece birinci soruyu cevapla (ör. neden oluyor). İDDİA → GEREKÇE → ÖRNEK → SONUÇ.
  • Body 2: Sadece ikinci soruyu cevapla (ör. pozitif mi negatif mi). Aynı iç mantık; pozisyon burada nettir.
  • Conclusion: İki sorunun cevabını tek cümlede özetle ve YARGInı tekrarla.
Altın kural: Bir gövde = bir soru. Body 1'de ikinci sorunun cevabına girme, Body 2'de birinci soruya geri dönme. Bu ayrım, okuyucuya (examiner'a) her iki soruyu da fully addressed ettiğini gösterir.

Diğer tiplerle karışmasın: Opinion sorusu ("To what extent do you agree?") tek tez tartıştırır; discussion sorusu ("Discuss both views and give your opinion") iki karşıt görüşü ele aldırır. Two-part ise iki karşıt görüş değil, aynı konuda iki ayrı soru içerir; bu yüzden gövdeler "karşı taraf" değil, "ayrı soru" mantığıyla kurulur. Bu ince farkı görmek, doğru yapıyı seçmenin ilk adımıdır.

Two-part question (sometimes referred to as direct question or double question) is a type of question in IELTS Writing Task 2 that poses two different questions on the same topic. This differs from opinion (agree/disagree) or discussion (both views) types: here, a single thesis is not debated; you are expected to provide separate answers to the two questions that have been explicitly asked.

The most common pattern is as follows: a "Why?" question plus an evaluation question. For example: "Nowadays many people choose to live alone. Why is this happening? Is it a positive or negative development?" Here, the first question asks for a reason, and the second question asks for your opinion. There are other variations as well: "What are the causes? What can be done?" or "Why...? What effect does it have on...?"

Distinction tip: If you see two separate question marks or two imperative sentences in the question, this is a two-part question. "To what extent do you agree?" asks a single question; a two-part question asks twice. If you miss this distinction, you will only answer half of the question, and your Task Response score will drop.

The structure is always 4 paragraphs and the logic is very clear:

  • Introduction: Paraphrase the topic, indicate that there will be two questions, and briefly state your position if the second question asks for an opinion.
  • Body 1: Answer only the first question (e.g., why it is happening). CLAIM → REASON → EXAMPLE → CONCLUSION.
  • Body 2: Answer only the second question (e.g., is it positive or negative). The same internal logic applies; the position is clear here.
  • Conclusion: Summarize the answers to both questions in a single sentence and restate your JUDGMENT.
Golden rule: One body = one question. Do not address the answer to the second question in Body 1, and do not return to the first question in Body 2. This distinction shows the reader (the examiner) that you have fully addressed both questions.

Do not confuse with other types: An Opinion question ("To what extent do you agree?") debates a single thesis; a discussion question ("Discuss both views and give your opinion") addresses two opposing views. A two-part question contains two separate questions on the same topic, so the bodies are constructed based on the logic of "separate questions" rather than "opposing sides." Recognizing this subtle difference is the first step in choosing the correct structure.

Soru. In many countries, people are now living longer than ever before. Why is this happening, and is it a positive or negative development?

Model cevap (281 kelime)

Introduction. In many parts of the world, life expectancy has risen dramatically and people are living longer than any previous generation. This essay will first explain why this is occurring and then argue that, despite some challenges, it is fundamentally a positive development.
Body 1 · Why. Two main factors explain rising longevity. The first is the enormous progress made in medicine: diseases that were once fatal, such as many infections, can now be prevented by vaccination or cured with antibiotics. The second is the general improvement in living standards, including cleaner water, better nutrition and safer working conditions. Public-health campaigns that promote exercise and discourage smoking have reinforced these gains still further. In wealthy countries, for example, a child born today can expect to live decades longer than one born a century ago, largely because of these combined advances.
Body 2 · Positive or negative. Although longer lives place pressure on pension systems and healthcare, I believe the development is overwhelmingly positive. Living longer means more years with family and more time to contribute to society, and many people now remain active and productive well into their seventies. Societies that regard their older members merely as a burden overlook the experience, stability and unpaid care that these citizens provide. The strain on public finances, while real, is a problem of success that can be managed through gradual reforms such as flexible retirement ages, rather than a reason to regret longer life itself.
Conclusion. In conclusion, people are living longer chiefly because of medical progress and higher living standards. Despite the economic challenges this creates, the ability to enjoy more years of healthy, active life is unquestionably a development to be welcomed rather than feared, and one that reflects centuries of human achievement.
Neden yüksek band: Soru iki sey soruyor: ikisi de cevaplandi (Body 1 = neden, Body 2 = pozitif mi). Pozisyon ikinci soruda net. Conclusion iki cevabi da baglar.

Two-part'ta en çok puan kaybettiren şey, ikinci soruyu unutup sadece birine yüklenmektir. Bunu önlemenin en güvenli yolu, yazmaya başlamadan önce soruyu iki parçaya fiziksel olarak ayırmaktır.

  1. Soruyu ayrıştır. Soru cümlesindeki iki soruyu işaretle ve kenara yaz: Soru A = ? , Soru B = ? Örneğin "Why do people move to cities?" (A) ve "Is this positive or negative?" (B).
  2. Her body'yi bir soruya kilitle. Body 1 yalnızca Soru A'yı, Body 2 yalnızca Soru B'yi cevaplar. Beyin fırtınası yaparken her soru için 1 net iddia + 1 gerekçe + 1 örnek çıkar.
  3. İkinci soruda pozisyonu netleştir. Değerlendirme sorusu (positive/negative, advantage/disadvantage) varsa, Body 2'nin ilk cümlesinde tarafını açıkça söyle: "In my view, this is a largely positive development." Kararsız ("it depends") kalma.
  4. Introduction'da iki soruyu da haber ver. Giriş cümlenin sonunda "This essay will explain why... and argue that..." diyerek her iki soruyu da ele alacağını göster.
  5. Conclusion'da ikisini birden özetle. Son paragrafta hem sebebi hem yargıyı tek cümlede topla; yeni bilgi ekleme.
Uyarı: Değerlendirme sorusunda "hem pozitif hem negatif" deyip ortada kalırsan bu discussion tipine kayar ve soruyu tam cevaplamamış sayılırsın. Bir tarafa net ağırlık ver; karşı tarafı sadece bir cümlelik concession olarak an, sonra kendi görüşünle bitir.

Zaman planı: 40 dakikanın ilk 5 dakikasını bu ayrıştırmaya ayır. İki soruyu doğru böldüğünde, geri kalan yazım neredeyse kendiliğinden akar. Kalan zamanı da yaklaşık 5 dk giriş, 12'şer dk gövdeler, 3 dk sonuç ve 3 dk kontrol olarak dağıt.

The biggest score loss in a two-part essay comes from forgetting the second question and focusing only on one. The safest way to prevent this is to physically separate the question into two parts before you start writing.

  1. Break down the question. Mark the two questions in the question sentence and write them down: Question A = ? , Question B = ? For example, "Why do people move to cities?" (A) and "Is this positive or negative?" (B).
  2. Lock each body to a question. Body 1 answers only Question A, Body 2 answers only Question B. Brainstorm one clear claim + one reason + one example for each question.
  3. Clarify your position in the second question. If there is an evaluation question (positive/negative, advantage/disadvantage), state your side clearly in the first sentence of Body 2: "In my view, this is a largely positive development." Do not remain undecided ("it depends").
  4. Indicate both questions in the introduction. At the end of the introduction sentence, say "This essay will explain why... and argue that..." to show that you will address both questions.
  5. Summarize both in the conclusion. In the last paragraph, combine both the reason and the judgment into a single sentence; do not add new information.
Warning: If you say "both positive and negative" in the evaluation question and remain undecided, this shifts to a discussion type and you will be considered to have not fully answered the question. Clearly lean towards one side; mention the opposing side only as a one-sentence concession, then finish with your own opinion.

Mini example breakdown: "Many young people today spend a lot of time on social media. Why is this? Do you think it is a positive or negative trend?" Break this into two: Question A = Why do young people spend so much time on social media? (Body 1: accessibility + peer influence + entertainment, example: a student’s constant notification flow). Question B = Is this positive or negative? (Body 2: clear position — largely negative; reason distraction and sleep issues; mention connection benefits as a concession). This two-line plan is the skeleton of the entire essay.

Time plan: Spend the first 5 minutes of the 40 minutes on this breakdown. When you correctly divide the two questions, the remaining writing flows almost naturally. Distribute the remaining time as approximately 5 minutes for the introduction, 12 minutes for each body, 3 minutes for the conclusion, and 3 minutes for review.

Nuance: Saying "There are several reasons" and then providing only one reason appears weak. If you say "several," provide the main reason, then add a second reason with "In addition". The promise of the template must match the content.

Soru: In many countries, people are choosing to have children later in life. Why is this happening? Is this a positive or negative development?

Model cevap (287 kelime, 4 paragraf, iki soru da cevaplı):

[Introduction] It is increasingly common for adults in numerous nations to postpone parenthood until their thirties or even forties. This essay will examine the reasons behind this trend before arguing that, on balance, it is a positive development.

[Body 1 — Neden?] There are several reasons why couples are choosing to delay having children. This is primarily because young people today prioritise education and career advancement, both of which demand years of sustained commitment. A recent graduate, for instance, may spend her twenties completing a master's degree and establishing herself professionally, leaving little time or money for a family until she is well into her thirties. In addition, the soaring cost of housing means that many couples feel financially unprepared to raise a child, and they prefer to wait until they own a home. Consequently, parenthood is naturally pushed to a later stage of life.

[Body 2 — Pozitif mi Negatif mi?] In my opinion, this is largely a positive development. Older parents tend to be more emotionally mature and financially secure, which allows them to provide a stable and nurturing environment for their offspring. Moreover, mature parents are often better equipped to guide their children through life's challenges. Someone who has already built a solid career, for example, can afford quality childcare and dedicate greater attention to a child's upbringing without the constant stress of financial hardship. Although later pregnancies can carry certain health risks, advances in modern medicine have significantly reduced these concerns. Therefore, the benefits of postponing parenthood clearly outweigh the drawbacks.

[Conclusion] In conclusion, the tendency to have children later stems mainly from the pursuit of education and financial security, and I firmly believe this shift benefits both parents and children by ensuring a more stable upbringing.

1. Answering only one question. The deadliest mistake. If you write pages on the "Why?" question and skip the "positive or negative?" part, your Task Response will automatically drop to Band 5. Mark both questions before writing.

2. Not providing a position. Saying "it can be both good and bad" in the evaluation question and not making a decision. This does not directly answer the question. Clearly lean towards one side.

3. Body without examples. Saying "Older parents are more mature" remains abstract. Include a concrete example ("a recent graduate...", "someone with a solid career...") in each body; examples elevate the claim from Band 6 to Band 7-8.

4. Writing five paragraphs. Opening two separate bodies for the second question or dedicating a separate paragraph to the opposing view disrupts the structure. The opposing view should remain a single-sentence concession within Body 2. Structure: 1 introduction + 2 bodies + 1 conclusion.

5. Mixing the two questions. While explaining the reason in Body 1, slipping into evaluation with "and this is good because...". The reason body should remain neutral; the judgment belongs to Body 2.

6. Copying the question verbatim. Writing the question sentence exactly in the introduction lowers the Lexical score; always paraphrase.
