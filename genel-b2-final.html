B2 Final — Tüm Üniteler (1-19) (Genel Sınav)

Read the rules, then start the exam. (Kuralları oku, sonra sınavı başlat.)

Sınav Hakkında

Bu final, B2 kurundaki 19 ünitenin tamamını ölçer: Perfect zamanların gözden geçirilmesi; Future Continuous & Future Perfect; Third Conditional; Mixed Conditionals; Wish & If Only; şimdiki ve geçmişe dönük modal çıkarım (must / can't / might (have)); ileri düzey edilgen (Advanced Passive); rapor yapıları (is said/believed to be); ettirgen yapı (have/get something done); ileri dolaylı anlatım (advise / deny / suggest kalıpları); ileri ilgi cümlecikleri (edat + whom/which, reduced); ortaç cümlecikleri (having done, being...); ileri gerund/infinitive anlam farkları (remember / stop / try / regret); ileri bağlaçlar ve zıtlık (despite / whereas / nevertheless); vurgu için devrik yapı (Inversion: never / not until / hardly); yarık cümleler (Cleft: It-cleft / What-cleft); ileri artikel ve belirteçler (Advanced Articles & Determiners); çok kelimeli fiiller ve deyimler (Phrasal Verbs & Idioms). Speaking hariç dört beceri test edilir. Toplam süre 100 dakikadır ve tek bir sayaçla ilerler — süreyi bölümler arasında kendin yönetirsin.

B2 Final Sınavı · Bölüm 4/6

Reading (2 metin · 20 soru)

Suggested time: 25 minutes. (Önerilen süre: 25 dakika.) Text 1 bir bilim insanının geçmiş mücadelesini, Text 2 bir doğa projesinin geleceğini anlatır. (Text 1 tells a scientist's past struggle; Text 2 is about the future of a rewilding project.)

Text 1 · The Reluctant Cartographer

For most of her early career, Elena Voss was told that the ocean floor was flat and featureless — a claim she never quite believed. Having studied geology at a time when few women were admitted to the field, she had grown used to being underestimated. In the 1950s, denied permission to join research voyages because she was a woman, she was left ashore to analyse the data that her male colleagues collected at sea.

It was precisely this restriction that led to her greatest discovery. While plotting thousands of depth measurements by hand, Elena noticed a deep valley running down the middle of the Atlantic. Had the pattern appeared only once, she might have dismissed it; but it recurred in every profile she drew. The valley, she argued, was evidence that the seafloor was slowly splitting apart — an idea most scientists rejected at the time.

Her conclusions were dismissed as "girl talk" by one senior researcher. Yet within a decade the theory she had defended was widely accepted, and it is now regarded as a cornerstone of modern geology. Elena rarely spoke about the recognition she had been denied. "If I had been allowed on those ships," she once remarked, "I might never have sat still long enough to see the pattern."

Text 2 · Letting the Land Return

A controversial experiment is under way in the highlands, where a large estate is being deliberately returned to the wild. For over a century the land had been used for intensive sheep farming, which, according to ecologists, had stripped the hills of their native forests. The owners have now decided that nature, rather than machinery, should be allowed to do the work of restoration.

Under the scheme, which is known as rewilding, animals that disappeared from the region hundreds of years ago are gradually being reintroduced. Beavers, whose dams slow the flow of rivers, have already been released, and it is hoped that their activity will reduce the flooding that has repeatedly damaged towns downstream. If the beavers had not been brought back, supporters argue, these floods would only have grown worse.

Not everyone welcomes the project. Some farmers, whose livelihoods depend on the same land, fear that predators will eventually be introduced too. Others worry that tourists, attracted by the returning wildlife, will disturb the very ecosystems the scheme is meant to protect. The estate's managers insist that local residents will be consulted before any large animal is released. "Nothing will be decided," the director promised, "without the community having been asked first."

B2 Final Sınavı · Bölüm 5/6

Writing (2 görev)

Suggested time: 35 minutes — Task 1: 13 min, Task 2: 22 min. (Önerilen süre: 35 dakika — Görev 1: 13 dk, Görev 2: 22 dk.) Öğretmenin değerlendirecek; örnek cevap yoktur.

Task 1 · Formal Email: A Course That Was Not as Described (130-170 words)

You recently paid for a professional online course, but it did not match the description you were given when you enrolled. Write a formal email to the course provider. Explain what was promised and what actually happened, describe the impact this has had on you, and state clearly what action you expect them to take. (Kısa süre önce mesleki bir çevrimiçi kursa ücret ödedin, ama kurs kayıt sırasında sana verilen açıklamayla örtüşmedi. Kurs sağlayıcısına resmi bir e-posta yaz: ne vaat edildiğini ve gerçekte ne olduğunu açıkla, bunun seni nasıl etkilediğini anlat ve ne yapmalarını beklediğini net şekilde belirt.)

  • Use a formal opening and closing: "Dear Sir / Madam," ... "Yours faithfully," / "Yours sincerely,". (resmi açılış ve kapanış)
  • Use at least one passive / reporting structure: "I was assured that...", "It was stated that the course would...". (edilgen / rapor yapısı)
  • Include reported speech: "The website claimed that...". (dolaylı anlatım)
  • Make a firm but polite request with would / could: "I would therefore be grateful if you could...". (kesin ama kibar rica)
  • 130-170 words, formal register throughout. (130-170 kelime)
E-POSTA YAPISI
  • Açılış: "Dear Sir / Madam," + yazma sebebin (I am writing to complain about / regarding...).
  • Gövde: vaat edilen (passive + reported speech) → gerçekte olan → etkisi → beklediğin işlem (would/could + refund/repeat).
  • Kapanış: "I look forward to your prompt response." + "Yours faithfully," + adın.
0 kelime

Task 2 · Argument Essay: Space Exploration vs Problems on Earth (200-260 words)

Some people argue that governments should invest heavily in space exploration, while others believe the money would be better spent solving problems here on Earth. Discuss both views and give your own opinion, supporting it with clear reasons and examples. (Bazıları hükümetlerin uzay araştırmalarına büyük yatırım yapması gerektiğini savunurken, bazıları bu paranın Dünya'daki sorunları çözmeye harcanmasının daha iyi olacağına inanıyor. Her iki görüşü tartış ve kendi görüşünü net gerekçeler ve örneklerle destekle.)

  • Discuss both views before giving your own opinion. (kendi görüşünden önce iki görüşü de tartış)
  • Use at least three concession/contrast linkers: whereas, despite, nevertheless, although, however. (en az üç zıtlık bağlacı)
  • Include one conditional (Third or Mixed): "If governments had invested more in..., we would...". (bir koşul cümlesi)
  • Use passive and reporting structures (it is argued that..., is believed to be...). (edilgen ve rapor yapıları)
  • 200-260 words, organised into clear paragraphs. (200-260 kelime)
