The most feared aspect of the old paper SAT was the esoteric words you would almost never meet in daily life (obfuscate, perspicacious, pulchritude...). Students memorised lists of thousands of words and, in the exam, wrestled with these "SAT words" whose meaning was asked in isolation. On the Digital SAT this era is over.

"What earns points now is not knowing a word, but reading the functional meaning it takes in the sentence.

The new exam no longer tests rare words in a vacuum. Instead it measures high-utility academic vocabulary in context (Words in Context): the word is usually one you know, but you must read the functional meaning it takes in the sentence.

In other words, what really earns points is not knowing a word but reading the context.

The practical upshot is this: the goal of vocabulary study is not list-memorisation but building three things — (1) a core of high-return academic vocabulary, (2) sensitivity to connotation/register and (3) the reflex of decoding an unknown word from its root and prefix. This page builds exactly these three.

Teacher's Note
Students still look for a 5000-word "SAT list". Yet on the Digital SAT the reason for missing a single context question is almost never "I didn't know the word at all"; the reason is that you did not read the word's secondary meaning or its connotation in context. The strategy must shift from memorising to reading.

Bir bağlam sorusunda puanı aşağı çeken tek alışkanlık: önce şıklara bakmak. Doğru refleks tam tersidir. Kelimeyi bir öğrenme aracı olarak kullanmak istiyorsan, bağlamı kendi kelimenle doldurmayı bir alışkanlık haline getir.

  1. Boşluğu / kelimeyi kapat. Şıkları görmeden sadece cümleyi oku.
  2. Kendi kelimeni üret (prediction). Boşluğun yerine kendi kelimeni koy — "olumlu bir şey", "sınırlama fiili" kadar kaba bile olsa yeter.
  3. Bağlam ipucunu işaretle. Zıtlık mı (but, however, unlike)? Devam mı (and, because)? Tanım mı (iki nokta, tire)? Bu, tahminin yönünü kilitler.
  4. Connotation ve tonu belirle. Cümle olumlu mu, olumsuz mu, nötr mü?
  5. Şıkları tahminine göre ele. Yön ve ton olarak uymayanları çiz; kalanı register ve collocation ile seç.

The novelist's late works are ______: sprawling, digressive, and resistant to any single interpretation.
İki nokta (colon) bir tanım/açıklama ipucudur. Boşluk = "sprawling, digressive, tek yoruma dirençli" demek. Kendi tahminimiz: complex / unruly (karmaşık). Şıklar bu tahmine göre elenir.

The one habit that lowers your score on a context question: looking at the options first. The right reflex is the opposite. If you want to use vocabulary as a learning tool, make it a habit to fill in the context with your own word.

  1. Cover the blank / the word. Read only the sentence, without seeing the options.
  2. Produce your own word (prediction). Put your own word in place of the blank — even something as rough as "a positive thing" or "a limiting verb" will do.
  3. Mark the context clue. Is it a contrast (but, however, unlike)? A continuation (and, because)? A definition (colon, dash)? This locks the direction of your prediction.
  4. Determine connotation and tone. Is the sentence positive, negative or neutral?
  5. Eliminate options against your prediction. Cross out those that don't match in direction and tone; choose among the rest by register and collocation.

Words in Context'te doğru kelimeyi bulmanın anahtarı, cümlenin sana zaten verdiği ipucunu görmektir. Boşluğun yönünü ve tonunu belirleyen bu sinyaller genellikle altı türden birine oturur. Her tür farklı bir "işaret kelimesi" ailesiyle gelir; bunları tanımak, tahminini saniyeler içinde kilitler. Aşağıda her tür ayrı ayrı, örneğiyle.

1) Tanım / açıklama ipucu

Cümle, boşluğun anlamını doğrudan tanımlar ya da açar. Sinyaller: iki nokta (:), tire (—), that is, in other words, meaning, which is. Boşluğun karşılığı çoğu zaman kelimesi kelimesine cümlenin içindedir; sadece eş anlamlısını bulman gerekir. Bu, altı sinyal içinde en cömert olanıdır: cevabın eş anlamlısı cümlenin içine gizlendiği için, sözlükte hiç bilmediğin bir kelime bile buradan çözülebilir. Türk öğrencinin tek riski, iki noktayı bir "eşittir" işareti gibi okumak yerine şıklara erken atlamaktır; doğru refleks, iki noktadan sonrasını kendi kelimenle özetleyip o özetin karşılığını şıklarda aramaktır. Sınavda bu tür genellikle en hızlı çözülen sorudur.

The committee praised the report for its ______: it left no question unanswered and no source unchecked.
İki nokta bir tanım ipucudur. "Yanıtsız soru bırakmadı, kaynak atlamadı" = eksiksizlik / titizlik. Tahmin: thoroughness. Şıklar bu tanıma göre elenir.

2) Zıtlık ipucu

Cümle iki fikri karşı karşıya koyar; boşluk, komşusunun tersi yöndedir. Sinyaller: but, however, yet, unlike, whereas, despite, in contrast, rather than, on the contrary. Zıtlık gördüğünde tahminin yönünü çevir. Bu tür aynı zamanda SAT'ın en sık kurduğu tuzağın zeminidir: çeldirici çoğu zaman cümlenin öteki yarısındaki kelimeyle aynı yöne bakan bir seçenektir, yani zıtlık sinyalini görmezden gelirsen "tanıdık" göründüğü için onu seçersin. Bu yüzden but, however, unlike, whereas gördüğün an, tahminini komşu cümlenin tam tersi kutbuna sabitle; ancak ondan sonra şıklara bak.

Unlike her voluble co-author, who filled every silence with talk, Mara was notably ______.
Unlike = zıtlık. Konuşkan (voluble) yardımcının tersi → suskun / az konuşan. Tahmin: reticent / taciturn.

3) Örnek ipucu

Cümle, boşluğu bir ya da birkaç örnekle somutlar. Örneklerin ortak özelliği, boşluğun anlamını verir. Sinyaller: for example, for instance, such as, including, like. Buradaki hüner, tek tek örneklere değil, hepsinin ortak paydasına bakmaktır: Roma sikkesi, ortaçağ el yazması ve modern fotoğraf ayrı ayrı bir şey söylemez, ama birlikte "çok çeşitli" derler. Öğrenciler sık sık son örneğe kilitlenip boşluğu ona göre daraltır; oysa doğru kelime tüm örnekleri birden kapsayacak kadar geniş olmalıdır.

The museum's ______ collection ranged from Roman coins and medieval manuscripts to modern photography.
Örnekler (Roma sikkeleri, ortaçağ el yazmaları, modern fotoğraf) çok çeşitli → boşluk = çeşitli / geniş kapsamlı. Tahmin: eclectic / diverse.

4) Sebep-sonuç ipucu

Bir yan cümle, boşluğun nedenini ya da sonucunu verir. Sinyaller: because, since, as, so, therefore, consequently, as a result, thus. Sonucun mantığı boşluğu belirler. Bu türde ipucu "yön"den çok bir mantık zinciridir: önce nedeni netçe anla, sonra ondan doğal olarak çıkan sonucu tahmin et. Tek ve tekrarlanmamış bir deneyden (neden) ancak temkinli bir yargı (sonuç) çıkar; bu bağı kurmadan şık seçmek, doğru yönü bulsan bile yanlış tonu seçmene yol açar. because, since, therefore, thus gördüğünde kendine "bu neden bizi hangi sonuca zorluyor?" diye sor.

Because the evidence came from a single, unrepeated trial, the researchers described their conclusion as ______.
Because tek, tekrarlanmamış deney → sonuç olarak vardıkları yargı temkinli / kesin olmayan olmalı. Tahmin: tentative / provisional.

5) Eş anlam yeniden ifade (restatement)

Cümle aynı fikri, çoğu zaman bir ara söz (apposition) ile yeniden ifade eder. Sinyaller: virgül arası açıklama, in other words, namely, or ("yani" anlamında). Boşluğun karşılığı bu yeniden ifadenin içindedir. Yeniden ifade, tanım ipucunun daha örtük akrabasıdır: cevap yine cümlenin içindedir, ama bu kez iki nokta yerine bir virgül arası açıklama ya da "yani" anlamında bir or ile verilir. Riskli nokta şu: "keskin, alaycı, sessizce acımasız" gibi bir yeniden ifadeyi görünce öğrenciler en çarpıcı sözcüğe (cruel) kilitlenir; oysa boşluk üçünün bileşkesini — iğneleyici bir ton — karşılamalıdır.

The critic's tone was ______ — in other words, sharp, mocking, and quietly cruel.
In other words bir yeniden ifadedir. "Keskin, alaycı, sessizce acımasız" → boşluk = iğneleyici. Tahmin: caustic / acerbic.

6) Genel ton ipucu

Bazen tek bir işaret kelimesi yoktur; ipucu, cümlenin ya da paragrafın genel tutumundan (yazarın onaylayan/eleştiren, umutlu/karamsar tavrı) okunur. Övgü sözcükleri olumlu ucu, yergi sözcükleri olumsuz ucu ister. Bu, altı türün en inceliklisidir; çünkü tek bir "sinyal kelime" yoktur, yönü yazarın sözcük seçimlerinin toplamından sezersin. Uygulamada iş basittir: cümledeki değer yüklü kelimeleri (celebrated, warmth, honesty gibi) işaretle, hepsinin artı mı eksi mi olduğuna karar ver, sonra aynı yükü taşımayan tüm şıkları hemen ele. Türk öğrenci burada sık sık kelimenin sözlük anlamına takılıp çağrışım yükünü atlar — oysa bu türde kazandıran tam olarak yük okumaktır.

Reviewers celebrated the debut for its warmth and honesty, calling its young author remarkably ______.
Genel ton olumlu ("celebrated", "warmth", "honesty"). Boşluk da olumlu bir nitelik olmalı → olgun / içten. Tahmin: mature / sincere. Olumsuz çağrışımlı hiçbir şık uymaz.
İpucu
Bir cümlede birden çok ipucu türü aynı anda bulunabilir (hem zıtlık hem tanım). İlk işini, "bu ipucu boşluğu komşusuyla aynı yöne mi, zıt yöne mi çekiyor?" sorusuna cevap vermek olarak düşün. Yön kilitlenince, ton ve register kalan iki şıkkı ayıklar.

The key to finding the right word in Words in Context is seeing the clue the sentence has already given you. These signals, which set the direction and tone of the blank, usually fall into one of six types. Each type comes with a different family of "signal words"; recognising them locks your prediction within seconds. Below is each type, one by one, with its example.

1) Definition / explanation clue

The sentence directly defines or unpacks the meaning of the blank. Signals: colon (:), dash (—), that is, in other words, meaning, which is. The equivalent of the blank is often word for word inside the sentence; you just need to find its synonym. This is the most generous of the six signals: because the synonym of the answer is hidden inside the sentence, even a word you have never seen in a dictionary can be decoded from here. The Turkish student's only risk is jumping to the options early instead of reading a colon like an "equals" sign; the right reflex is to summarise what follows the colon in your own word and look for its equivalent among the options. In the exam this type is usually the fastest to solve.

The committee praised the report for its ______: it left no question unanswered and no source unchecked.
The colon is a definition clue. "Left no question unanswered, skipped no source" = completeness / thoroughness. Prediction: thoroughness. The options are eliminated against this definition.

2) Contrast clue

The sentence sets two ideas against each other; the blank points in the opposite direction to its neighbour. Signals: but, however, yet, unlike, whereas, despite, in contrast, rather than, on the contrary. When you see a contrast, flip the direction of your prediction. This type is also the ground for SAT's most frequent trap: the distractor is often an option that faces the same way as the word in the other half of the sentence, so if you ignore the contrast signal you pick it because it "looks familiar". So the moment you see but, however, unlike, whereas, fix your prediction to the exact opposite pole of the neighbouring clause; only then look at the options.

Unlike her voluble co-author, who filled every silence with talk, Mara was notably ______.
Unlike = contrast. The opposite of a talkative (voluble) co-author → quiet / says little. Prediction: reticent / taciturn.

3) Example clue

The sentence makes the blank concrete with one or more examples. The common feature of the examples gives the meaning of the blank. Signals: for example, for instance, such as, including, like. The trick here is to look not at the individual examples but at their common denominator: a Roman coin, a medieval manuscript and modern photography say nothing separately, but together they say "very varied". Students often fixate on the last example and narrow the blank to it; whereas the right word must be broad enough to cover all the examples at once.

The museum's ______ collection ranged from Roman coins and medieval manuscripts to modern photography.
The examples (Roman coins, medieval manuscripts, modern photography) are very varied → the blank = varied / wide-ranging. Prediction: eclectic / diverse.

4) Cause-effect clue

A subordinate clause gives the cause or effect of the blank. Signals: because, since, as, so, therefore, consequently, as a result, thus. The logic of the result determines the blank. In this type the clue is less a "direction" than a chain of logic: first understand the cause clearly, then predict the effect that naturally follows. From a single, unrepeated trial (cause) only a cautious judgement (effect) can follow; picking an option without building this link leads you to the wrong tone even when you have the right direction. When you see because, since, therefore, thus, ask yourself "what result is this cause forcing us towards?"

Because the evidence came from a single, unrepeated trial, the researchers described their conclusion as ______.
Because a single, unrepeated trial → the judgement they reach as a result must be cautious / not certain. Prediction: tentative / provisional.

5) Synonym restatement

The sentence restates the same idea, often through an apposition. Signals: an explanation between commas, in other words, namely, or (in the sense of "that is"). The equivalent of the blank is inside this restatement. Restatement is the more implicit cousin of the definition clue: the answer is again inside the sentence, but this time it is given not by a colon but by a comma-set explanation or an or meaning "that is". The risky point: seeing a restatement like "sharp, mocking, quietly cruel", students lock onto the most striking word (cruel); yet the blank must cover the resultant of all three — a stinging tone.

The critic's tone was ______ — in other words, sharp, mocking, and quietly cruel.
In other words is a restatement. "Sharp, mocking, quietly cruel" → the blank = stinging. Prediction: caustic / acerbic.

6) General tone clue

Sometimes there is no single signal word; the clue is read from the overall attitude of the sentence or paragraph (the writer's approving/critical, hopeful/pessimistic stance). Words of praise call for the positive pole, words of censure the negative. This is the subtlest of the six types, because there is no single "signal word"; you sense the direction from the sum of the writer's word choices. In practice it is simple: mark the value-laden words in the sentence (celebrated, warmth, honesty), decide whether they are all plus or minus, then immediately eliminate every option that does not carry the same charge. The Turkish student often gets stuck on the dictionary meaning of the word and skips the connotation load — yet in this type it is precisely reading the load that wins.

Reviewers celebrated the debut for its warmth and honesty, calling its young author remarkably ______.
The overall tone is positive ("celebrated", "warmth", "honesty"). The blank must also be a positive quality → mature / sincere. Prediction: mature / sincere. No negatively-charged option fits.
Tip
A single sentence may contain more than one clue type at once (both contrast and definition). Think of your first job as answering the question: "does this clue pull the blank in the same direction as its neighbour, or the opposite?" Once direction is locked, tone and register weed out the last two options.

Örnek 1 — zıtlık ipucu

Although the museum's earlier exhibitions had been criticized as staid and predictable, its latest show struck visitors as strikingly ______, juxtaposing Renaissance portraiture with digital installations.
Soru kökü: Boşluğa en mantıklı ve isabetli kelime hangisi? İpucu: Although + "staid and predictable" (durgun, öngörülebilir) → boşluk bunun tersi olmalı; ayrıca "Rönesans portresini dijital enstalasyonla yan yana koyan" = beklenmedik/yenilikçi. Tahmin: yenilikçi / cüretkâr.
  • A) inventive — Doğru. "Durgun ve öngörülebilir"in doğrudan tersi olan bu kelime, Although'un kurduğu zıtlığı tam karşılar. İpucu cümlenin sonundadır: Rönesans portresini dijital enstalasyonla yan yana koymak beklenmedik, yaratıcı bir kürasyondur ve "strikingly" (çarpıcı biçimde) zarfı bu olumlu şaşkınlığı pekiştirir. Yön, ton ve kapsam üçü de oturur.
  • B) conventional — Yanlış — tam bir yön hatası. conventional (geleneksel) "predictable" ile aynı yöne bakar, oysa Although bize tersini arattırıyor. Bu, çeldiricinin klasik "karşıt hipotez" tuzağıdır: cümlenin ilk yarısındaki kelimeyi boşluğa taşıyıp zıtlık sinyalini görmezden gelmeni umar. Türk öğrenci burada çoğu zaman anlamı bildiği için erken seçim yapıp yakalanır.
  • C) restrained — Yanlış. restrained "ölçülü, çekingen, tutuk" demektir; bu da yine öngörülebilirlik ucuna düşer, dolayısıyla yönü zıtlık sinyaliyle çatışır. Ayrıca çağlar arası cüretkâr bir yan yana koymayı "ölçülü" diye adlandırmak, cümledeki somut örnekle (Rönesans + dijital) çelişir.
  • D) chaotic — Yanlış — ve en sinsi çeldirici. Yön olarak doğru tarafta (beklenmedik) durduğu için acele edeni yakalar, ama chaotic (karmakarışık) olumsuz çağrışımlıdır; oysa cümlenin tonu olumludur, ziyaretçiler serginin etkisinden övgüyle söz ediyor. Kapsam da fazla: yaratıcı bir kürasyonu "kaos" diye nitelemek anlamı abartır. Doğru yön + yanlış ton = klasik connotation tuzağı.

Örnek 2 — tanım / açıklama ipucu

The senator was known for her ______: she refused gifts from lobbyists, published her full schedule, and answered even hostile questions directly.
Soru kökü: Boşluğa en isabetli kelime? İpucu: İki nokta bir tanım ipucudur. Hediye reddetme, programı yayımlama, düşmanca sorulara dürüst cevap = dürüstlük / şeffaflık. Tahmin: integrity.
  • A) ambition — Yanlış. ambition (hırs), tanımlanan üç davranışla — hediye reddetmek, programı yayımlamak, düşmanca soruya bile dürüst cevap vermek — hiçbir mantıksal bağ kurmaz. İki noktadan sonra gelen liste bir "dürüstlük" portresi çizer; hırs bu portrenin dışında kalır, yani kapsam dışıdır.
  • B) integrity — Doğru. İki nokta bir tanım ipucudur: ardından gelen üç davranışın ortak paydası boşluğu tanımlar. Çıkar ilişkisini reddetmek, şeffaf olmak ve zor sorudan kaçmamak — hepsi tek bir niteliğe, ahlaki bütünlüğe (dürüstlük + ilkelilik) işaret eder. integrity bu üçünü aynı anda kapsayan tek kelimedir.
  • C) charisma — Yanlış. charisma (karizma) bir insanın çekiciliği ve etkileyiciliğiyle ilgilidir, ahlaki davranışıyla değil; biri son derece karizmatik ama hiç şeffaf olmayabilir. Liste davranışların dürüstlüğünü anlatıyor, çekiciliğini değil — bu yüzden bu şık, kulağa yakın gelen ama bağlamı tanımlamayan bir kapsam kaymasıdır.
  • D) reticence — Yanlış ve tam zıt. reticence "suskunluk, ağzını açmama" demektir; oysa senatör programını herkese açıyor ve düşmanca sorulara bile doğrudan cevap veriyor — bu açıklık, suskunluğun tam karşıtıdır. Kelimeyi belli belirsiz "ölçülülük" diye hatırlayan öğrenci burada yanılır; bağlam sinyali (açıkça cevap vermek) yönü net biçimde ters çevirir.

Örnek 3 — ikincil anlam (temper)

As used in the passage, "temper" most nearly means to ______. (Context: "Aware that her sweeping generalizations might invite objection, the author sought to temper them with careful qualifications.")
Soru kökü: "temper" bağlamda hangi anlama en yakın? İpucu: "sweeping generalizations" (aşırı genellemeler) + "with careful qualifications" (temkinli kayıtlar) → genellemeleri yumuşatmak. En bilinen anlam (metali tavlamak / öfke) bir tuzaktır.
  • A) intensify — Yanlış ve zıt. intensify "yoğunlaştırmak, güçlendirmek" demek; oysa "with careful qualifications" (temkinli kayıtlarla) öbeği yazarın iddialarını gevşettiğini, yani tam tersini yaptığını söyler. Aşırı genellemelerine itiraz gelebileceğinin farkında olan biri onları körüklemez, frenler.
  • B) moderate — Doğru. Bağlamın anahtar öbeği "careful qualifications" (dikkatli kayıtlar/çekinceler): genellemelere kayıt koymak onları ılımlaştırmaktır. temper'ın burada istenen mecazi anlamı tam budur — "yumuşatmak, dozunu düşürmek". Kelimenin sözlükteki daha bilinen anlamları (metali tavlamak, öfke) bilinçli olarak çeldiriciye dönüştürülmüştür.
  • C) harden — Yanlış. harden (sertleştirmek) yön olarak zıttır: yazar iddialarını katılaştırmıyor, esnetiyor. Bu şık ayrıca bir çağrışım tuzağıdır — temper fiilinin metalürjideki "çeliği tavlayıp sertleştirmek" anlamını hatırlayan öğrenciyi somut anlama çeker. Oysa bağlam soyut bir tartışmadan söz ediyor, metal işlemekten değil.
  • D) conceal — Yanlış. conceal (gizlemek) bağlamla uyuşmaz: yazar genellemelerini saklamıyor, tam tersine açıkça ortaya koyup yanına çekinceler ekliyor. "Sınırlamak" ile "gizlemek" yakın görünse de farklıdır — biri iddiayı görünür kılıp yumuşatır, öteki büsbütün ortadan kaldırır. Bu bir kapsam hatasıdır.

Örnek 4 — connotation / ton

Colleagues admired how she managed the department's tight budget: never wasteful, yet never denying her team the tools it truly needed. Her approach was best described as ______.
Soru kökü: En isabetli kelime? İpucu: Ton olumlu ("admired"). Ölçülü harcama ama ekibi mahrum bırakmama = tutumlu ama cömert değil, ölçülü/akıllıca tasarruflu. Olumlu çağrışımlı bir kelime gerekir.
  • A) stingy — Yanlış ton. stingy (cimri) olumsuz çağrışımlıdır, oysa cümle "admired" (hayranlık duyulan) ile açıkça olumlu bir tablo çiziyor. Üstelik "ekibini gerçekten ihtiyaç duyduğu araçlardan mahrum etmeden" öbeği cimriliği doğrudan yalanlar. Aynı "tutumluluk" fikrini paylaşan doğru cevapla bu çeldirici arasındaki tek fark, çağrışım yüküdür.
  • B) prudent — Doğru. prudent "tedbirli, basiretli" demektir ve olumlu çağrışımlıdır — tam da "admired" tonunun istediği yük. Kelime, cümledeki dengeyi (asla savurgan değil, ama asla cimri de değil) tek başına taşır: akıllıca, ölçülü ama ihtiyacı gözeten bir yönetim. Yön, ton ve kapsam aynı anda oturur.
  • C) reckless — Yanlış ve zıt. reckless (pervasız, savurgan) sıkı bir bütçeyi başarıyla yöneten biriyle taban tabana çelişir; "never wasteful" (asla savurgan değil) öbeği bu şıkkı doğrudan dışlar. Ton da olumsuz — övgüyle anlatılan bir yöneticiye yakışmaz.
  • D) extravagant — Yanlış. extravagant (müsrif, savurgan) "tight budget" (sıkı bütçe) bağlamıyla çelişir; parayı ölçülü harcayan biri müsrif olamaz. Bu şık, cümledeki "wasteful" fikrini boşluğa taşıyarak zıt kutbu doğruymuş gibi göstermeye çalışan bir yön tuzağıdır.

Örnek 5 — kök / önek ipucu

Skeptical readers found the memoir's tidy resolutions ______: the author's own diaries, published later, contradicted nearly every claim.
Soru kökü: En isabetli kelime? İpucu: Günlükler neredeyse her iddiayı çürütüyor → anlatı inandırıcı değil. "cred" (inanmak) kökü doğru yöne götürür. Tahmin: not credible.
  • A) implausible — Doğru. implausible = im- (olumsuz) + plaus ("alkışlanır", yani inandırıcı) — "akla yatkın olmayan, inandırıcılıktan uzak". Anlatının kendi günlükleriyle çürütülmesi tam olarak bunu gerektirir. Kök-önek çözümü doğru yönü (olumsuz) baştan verir, bağlam da bunu onaylar.
  • B) poignant — Yanlış. poignant (dokunaklı, hüzün verici) bir duygu niteliğidir; oysa boşluk bir doğruluk/inandırıcılık yargısı istiyor. Bir metin hem dokunaklı hem yalan olabilir — yani bu kelime cümledeki mantık eksenini ıskalar, kapsam dışıdır. "Skeptical readers" ve "contradicted" sinyalleri duyguyu değil, güvenilirliği tartışıyor.
  • C) convincing — Yanlış ve zıt. convincing (ikna edici) olumlu yöndedir; oysa günlükler neredeyse her iddiayı çürütüyor, dolayısıyla anlatı ikna edici değildir. Bu, inandırıcılık ekseninin yanlış ucudur ve "contradicted" sinyaliyle çelişir. Öneki olmayan olumlu kelimeyi seçmek, cümledeki çürütmeyi görmezden gelmektir.
  • D) verbose — Yanlış. verbose (laf kalabalığı olan, gereğinden uzun) bir üslup özelliğidir, doğrulukla ilgisi yoktur; bir anlatı hem kısa hem yalan, hem uzun hem doğru olabilir. Bağlam metnin uzunluğundan değil, günlüklerle çelişen iddialarının güvenilirliğinden söz ettiği için bu şık eksen dışıdır.

Örnek 6 — register / collocation ipucu

In their peer-reviewed article, the authors were careful to note that the data merely ______ a correlation, stopping well short of claiming cause.
Soru kökü: En isabetli fiil? İpucu: Akademik, hakemli makale register'ı + "nedeni iddia etmekten kaçınarak" → ölçülü bir fiil gerekir. Akademik eşdizim: the data suggest / indicate.
  • A) suggested — Doğru. Hakemli bir makalenin register'ı ölçülü, ihtiyatlı bir fiil ister; "the data suggest" akademik yazının yerleşik eşdizimidir. "stopping well short of claiming cause" (nedeni iddia etmekten özenle kaçınarak) öbeği de tam bu ihtiyatı vurgular: veri bir korelasyonu ima eder, dayatmaz. Ton + collocation birlikte doğrular.
  • B) screamed — Yanlış register. screamed (bağırdı) gündelik, abartılı ve duygusal bir fiildir; hakemli bir makalenin ölçülü tonuna taban tabana aykırıdır. "Veri bir korelasyonu bağırdı" mantıken de abartılı — metin tam tersine temkini vurguluyor. Register uyumsuzluğu tek başına bu şıkkı eler.
  • C) proved — Yanlış. proved (kanıtladı) fazla güçlü bir iddiadır: bir şeyi kanıtlamak nedeni ortaya koymak demektir, oysa cümle "stopping well short of claiming cause" (nedeni iddia etmekten özenle kaçınarak) diyerek bunun tersini söyler. Bilimsel dilde "correlation" ile "cause" arasındaki fark kritiktir — bu şık o ayrımı çiğner. Anlamca çekici ama bağlamla çelişen bir kapsam aşımı.
  • D) guessed — Yanlış. guessed (tahmin etti) veriyi olduğundan değersiz gösterir; hakemli bir çalışmada toplanan veri "tahmin" yürütmez. Üstelik "the data guessed" doğal bir eşdizim değildir — collocation kulağı bunu hemen yabancılar. Register hem fazla gündelik hem de mantıken uyumsuz.
Öğretmen Notu
Bu altı örnekteki elemenin ortak dili: her yanlış şıkkı bir kategoriyle etiketle — "zıt yön", "kapsam dışı", "yanlış ton/register", "en bilinen anlam tuzağı". Bir süre sonra dört şıkkı görür görmez üçünü bu etiketlerle eleyip doğruya varırsın. İsabet, hatanın türünü tanımaktır.

The examples below are the heart of this page. Each contains an original short passage of genuine SAT difficulty, a question stem and four options; then you see, one by one, why each option is right or wrong. First cover the blank, produce your own word; then read the solution. When eliminating options, use the trio of direction, tone and scope.

Example 1 — contrast clue

Although the museum's earlier exhibitions had been criticized as staid and predictable, its latest show struck visitors as strikingly ______, juxtaposing Renaissance portraiture with digital installations.
Question stem: Which is the most logical and precise word for the blank? Clue: Although + "staid and predictable" → the blank must be the opposite; also "juxtaposing Renaissance portraiture with digital installations" = unexpected/innovative. Prediction: innovative / bold.
  • A) inventive — Correct. As the direct opposite of "staid and predictable", this word fully answers the contrast set up by Although. The clue is at the end of the sentence: putting Renaissance portraiture beside digital installations is an unexpected, creative curation, and the adverb "strikingly" reinforces this positive surprise. Direction, tone and scope all fit.
  • B) conventional — Wrong — a clear direction error. conventional faces the same way as "predictable", whereas Although makes us look for the opposite. This is the distractor's classic "opposite hypothesis" trap: it hopes you will carry a word from the first half of the sentence into the blank and ignore the contrast signal. The Turkish student, knowing the meaning, often chooses early here and gets caught.
  • C) restrained — Wrong. restrained means "measured, reserved, held back"; this again falls on the predictability pole, so its direction clashes with the contrast signal. Also, calling a bold cross-era juxtaposition "restrained" contradicts the concrete example in the sentence (Renaissance + digital).
  • D) chaotic — Wrong — and the sneakiest distractor. Because it stands on the right side directionally (unexpected), it catches the hasty; but chaotic is negatively charged, whereas the sentence's tone is positive — visitors speak of the show's effect with praise. The scope is also excessive: calling a creative curation "chaos" overstates the meaning. Right direction + wrong tone = a classic connotation trap.

Example 2 — definition / explanation clue

The senator was known for her ______: she refused gifts from lobbyists, published her full schedule, and answered even hostile questions directly.
Question stem: The most precise word for the blank? Clue: The colon is a definition clue. Refusing gifts, publishing her schedule, answering even hostile questions honestly = honesty / transparency. Prediction: integrity.
  • A) ambition — Wrong. ambition has no logical link with the three behaviours defined — refusing gifts, publishing her schedule, answering even hostile questions honestly. The list after the colon paints a portrait of "honesty"; ambition falls outside this portrait, i.e. it is out of scope.
  • B) integrity — Correct. The colon is a definition clue: the common denominator of the three behaviours that follow defines the blank. Refusing conflicts of interest, being transparent and not dodging hard questions — all point to a single quality, moral integrity (honesty + principle). integrity is the only word that covers all three at once.
  • C) charisma — Wrong. charisma is about a person's charm and appeal, not their moral conduct; someone can be extremely charismatic yet not at all transparent. The list describes the honesty of the behaviours, not their appeal — so this option is a scope slip that sounds close but does not define the context.
  • D) reticence — Wrong and the exact opposite. reticence means "silence, holding back"; yet the senator opens her schedule to everyone and answers even hostile questions directly — that openness is the very opposite of reticence. A student who vaguely remembers the word as "reserve" errs here; the context signal (answering openly) clearly flips the direction.

Example 3 — secondary meaning (temper)

As used in the passage, "temper" most nearly means to ______. (Context: "Aware that her sweeping generalizations might invite objection, the author sought to temper them with careful qualifications.")
Question stem: Which meaning is "temper" closest to in context? Clue: "sweeping generalizations" + "with careful qualifications" → to soften the generalisations. The best-known meaning (to temper metal / anger) is a trap.
  • A) intensify — Wrong and opposite. intensify means "to strengthen, heighten"; but "with careful qualifications" says the author is loosening her claims, i.e. doing the opposite. Someone aware that her sweeping generalisations might draw objection does not stoke them but reins them in.
  • B) moderate — Correct. The key phrase of the context is "careful qualifications": placing qualifications on generalisations is to moderate them. This is exactly the intended figurative meaning of temper — "to soften, tone down". The word's better-known meanings (to temper metal, anger) have been deliberately turned into distractors.
  • C) harden — Wrong. harden is directionally opposite: the author is not stiffening her claims but bending them. This option is also a connotation trap — it pulls the student who remembers temper's metallurgical sense ("to temper and harden steel") towards the concrete meaning. But the context is about an abstract discussion, not metalwork.
  • D) conceal — Wrong. conceal does not fit the context: the author is not hiding her generalisations but, on the contrary, stating them openly and adding qualifications. "To limit" and "to conceal" may look close but differ — one makes the claim visible and softens it, the other removes it entirely. This is a scope error.

Example 4 — connotation / tone

Colleagues admired how she managed the department's tight budget: never wasteful, yet never denying her team the tools it truly needed. Her approach was best described as ______.
Question stem: The most precise word? Clue: The tone is positive ("admired"). Measured spending but not depriving the team = thrifty but not stingy, measured/wisely economical. A positively-charged word is needed.
  • A) stingy — Wrong tone. stingy is negatively charged, whereas the sentence clearly paints a positive picture with "admired". Moreover, the phrase "without denying her team the tools it truly needed" directly refutes stinginess. The only difference between the correct answer, which shares the "thrift" idea, and this distractor is the connotation load.
  • B) prudent — Correct. prudent means "careful, wise" and is positively charged — exactly the load the "admired" tone calls for. The word alone carries the balance in the sentence (never wasteful, but never stingy): a wise, measured management that still meets needs. Direction, tone and scope all fit at once.
  • C) reckless — Wrong and opposite. reckless flatly contradicts someone successfully managing a tight budget; the phrase "never wasteful" directly excludes this option. The tone is also negative — unbecoming of a manager described with praise.
  • D) extravagant — Wrong. extravagant clashes with the "tight budget" context; someone who spends measuredly cannot be extravagant. This option is a direction trap that carries the "wasteful" idea of the sentence into the blank to make the opposite pole look correct.

Example 5 — root / prefix clue

Skeptical readers found the memoir's tidy resolutions ______: the author's own diaries, published later, contradicted nearly every claim.
Question stem: The most precise word? Clue: The diaries contradict nearly every claim → the narrative is not credible. The "cred" (believe) root points the right way. Prediction: not credible.
  • A) implausible — Correct. implausible = im- (negative) + plaus ("worthy of applause", i.e. believable) — "not credible, far from convincing". The narrative being refuted by its own diaries requires exactly this. Root-prefix decoding gives the right direction (negative) from the start, and the context confirms it.
  • B) poignant — Wrong. poignant (moving, touching) is a quality of emotion; the blank calls for a judgement of truth/credibility. A text can be both moving and false — so this word misses the logical axis of the sentence and is out of scope. The signals "Skeptical readers" and "contradicted" discuss reliability, not emotion.
  • C) convincing — Wrong and opposite. convincing is positive in direction; but the diaries refute nearly every claim, so the narrative is not convincing. This is the wrong end of the credibility axis and clashes with the "contradicted" signal. Choosing the un-prefixed positive word is to ignore the refutation in the sentence.
  • D) verbose — Wrong. verbose (wordy, over-long) is a feature of style, unrelated to truth; a narrative can be short and false, or long and true. Because the context speaks not of the text's length but of the credibility of its claims, which the diaries contradict, this option is off-axis.

Example 6 — register / collocation clue

In their peer-reviewed article, the authors were careful to note that the data merely ______ a correlation, stopping well short of claiming cause.
Question stem: The most precise verb? Clue: A peer-reviewed academic register + "avoiding any claim of cause" → a measured verb is needed. Academic collocation: the data suggest / indicate.
  • A) suggested — Correct. The register of a peer-reviewed article calls for a measured, cautious verb; "the data suggest" is the established collocation of academic writing. The phrase "stopping well short of claiming cause" underlines exactly this caution: the data imply a correlation, they do not impose it. Tone + collocation confirm it together.
  • B) screamed — Wrong register. screamed is an everyday, exaggerated and emotional verb; it is flatly at odds with the measured tone of a peer-reviewed article. "The data screamed a correlation" is also logically overblown — the text stresses caution, the opposite. The register mismatch alone eliminates this option.
  • C) proved — Wrong. proved is too strong a claim: to prove something is to establish cause, whereas the sentence says "stopping well short of claiming cause" — the opposite. In scientific language the distinction between "correlation" and "cause" is critical, and this option violates it. Attractive in meaning but a scope overreach that contradicts the context.
  • D) guessed — Wrong. guessed makes the data sound worthless; data gathered in a peer-reviewed study do not "guess". Moreover "the data guessed" is not a natural collocation — the ear rejects it at once. The register is both too casual and logically inconsistent.
Teacher's Note
The shared language of elimination in these six examples: label every wrong option with a category — "wrong direction", "out of scope", "wrong tone/register", "best-known-meaning trap". After a while you will eliminate three of the four options with these labels the moment you see them and arrive at the right answer. Precision is recognising the type of the error.

"Word charge": her kelimenin bir yükü var

Bir kelimeyi öğrenirken anlamının yanına bir de yük işareti (word charge) kaydet: (+) olumlu, (−) olumsuz, (0) nötr. Aynı "kaba" fikri paylaşan üç kelime çoğu zaman üç farklı yük taşır. SAT tam olarak bu farkı test eder: cümlenin tonu hangi yükü istiyorsa, doğru şık odur.

Olumlu yük (+)

meticulous, resolute, candid, thrifty, inquisitive, confident, innovative — övgü tonlu cümleler ister.

↔
Olumsuz yük (−)

fussy, obstinate, blunt, stingy, nosy, arrogant, erratic — eleştiri/yergi tonlu cümleler ister.

Dikkat
Cümlenin tonu yazarın tutumundan okunur. "praising", "admired", "celebrated" gibi kelimeler olumlu ucu; "dismissed", "criticized", "lamented" olumsuz ucu ister. İki eş-anlamlı seçenek kaldığında, tonu esas al: cümle övüyorsa economical, yeriyorsa stingy.

The three columns of the table are not an empty symmetry but a distinction that works directly in the exam. Take the determined → steadfast → stubborn row: all three describe someone who does not give up easily, but steadfast carries praise, stubborn censure; if the sentence admires the person the first is correct, if it criticises them the second is. In the talkative → articulate → garrulous row, articulate is a positive ability ("expressing one's thoughts fluently and clearly") while garrulous is a negative flaw ("talking too much, to no purpose") — if both are memorised as "talkative", the tone question is missed. The cautious → prudent → timid row is the subtlest: prudent praises caution as a virtue, while timid belittles the same caution as "fearfulness". When you memorise a word, write beside it not just its meaning but this charge marker.

"Word charge": every word has a charge

When you learn a word, record beside its meaning a charge marker (word charge): (+) positive, (−) negative, (0) neutral. Three words sharing the same "rough" idea often carry three different charges. SAT tests exactly this difference: whichever charge the sentence's tone calls for is the correct option.

Positive charge (+)

meticulous, resolute, candid, thrifty, inquisitive, confident, innovative — call for praise-toned sentences.

↔
Negative charge (−)

fussy, obstinate, blunt, stingy, nosy, arrogant, erratic — call for criticism/censure-toned sentences.

Caution
The tone of a sentence is read from the writer's attitude. Words like "praising", "admired", "celebrated" call for the positive end; "dismissed", "criticized", "lamented" the negative. When two synonymous options remain, decide by tone: if the sentence praises, economical; if it censures, stingy.

Every text has a register (level of formality): scientific, literary, historical, everyday. The correct word almost always matches the text's register. In a scientific passage "the data suggest a correlation" is natural; "the data brag about a correlation" may be grammatical but is absurd — because brag is everyday and human, at odds with the measured academic tone.

All the contrasts in the table rest on the same principle: the correct word must belong to the text not only in meaning but also in tone. "a marked increase" and "a gigantic increase" say almost the same thing, but marked is measured-academic while gigantic is casual-exaggerated; in a research text the latter grates on the ear. The Turkish student's reflex here is often "the meaning is right, so it works"; but SAT counts as correct the one that fits the register among two synonyms. A practical test: place your chosen word beside the text's own sentences — if it does not sound like it "came from the same mouth", eliminate it.

Collocation is the final eliminator

Some words are "settled" with particular neighbours; this is called collocation. An increase in English is "a marked increase" or "a sharp increase"; "a gigantic increase" is not a natural collocation. Likewise "draw a distinction", "raise a concern", "pose a threat" are established. Of two synonymous options, the one that is the correct collocation is the one that is right.

Advanced
When you study vocabulary, learn a word as a chunk: "a marked increase", "cast doubt on", "pose a threat". Memorising the established collocation rather than the single word lets you instantly find the "sounds right" option both in Writing and in context questions.

The most tested and highest-return vocabulary group on the SAT is not single academic words; it is the transition and logic words that build the relationship between sentences and ideas (transitions). In Digital SAT R&W, several questions per module are directly of the form "which transition word fits here?". This is the highest-return vocabulary investment specific to the SAT.

Meaning relationshipTransition wordsWhat it does
Contrast / oppositionhowever, nevertheless, in contrast, on the other hand, converselySets two ideas against each other; the second reverses the first
Result / cause-effecttherefore, consequently, thus, as a result, henceThe second idea is the result of the first
Addition / reinforcementmoreover, furthermore, in addition, alsoAdds one more idea in the same direction
Examplefor instance, for example, namely, such asMakes a general statement concrete with an example
Similaritysimilarly, likewise, in the same waySays the two cases are parallel

What matters is not memorising these six relationships but distinguishing them, because SAT options often offer one transition from each of several families and only one names the correct relationship. The most expensive confusion is between contrast and result: however turns the second idea against the first, while therefore makes it the first's natural output — mixing these two turns the sentence's logic upside down. The second frequent error is confusing addition and example: moreover adds new, independent evidence in the same direction, while for instance makes an existing idea concrete with an example; addition brings new information, an example pictures what is already there. Third is the concession family (admittedly, granted): these herald conceding the opposing view for a moment and immediately rebutting it, so they are usually followed by a contrast.

Caution
The method for a transition question: delete the transition word, name the logical relationship between the previous sentence and the next in your own words ("this is a contrast", "this is a result"). Then pick the transition that fits that relationship. Confusing however (contrast) with therefore (result) is the most expensive error.

The logic of a transitions question

In a transition question the options are often four transitions belonging to different meaning relationships; so choosing the one that "sounds nice" does not work. The right method has three steps: (1) ignore the transition word, read the two sentences on their own; (2) name the relationship between them in your own words ("this is a contrast", "this is an example", "this is a result"); (3) pick the transition from that relationship's family. Since the options usually include one example from each family, once you name the relationship correctly three options fall away by themselves.

  1. Delete the transition, read the relationship. Do the two sentences go the same way or opposite ways?
  2. Name the relationship. Contrast / result / addition / example / similarity / concession?
  3. Choose from the family. Mark the transition belonging to the relationship you named; the others are eliminated automatically.
Early critics dismissed the technique as a gimmick. ______, within a decade it had become a standard tool taught in every studio.
First sentence: it was dismissed. Second sentence: within a decade it became standard. Relationship = contrast (the opposite of what was expected). Right family: However / Nevertheless. Therefore (result), For example (example), Similarly (similarity) are eliminated for naming the relationship wrongly.
Common Error
Turkish students often confuse the pairs moreover (addition) with however (contrast), and thus (result) with still (nevertheless, contrast). Rather than translating the transition into Turkish and saying "this fits here", first name the relationship between the two sentences.

The prefix usually gives the direction (positive/negative), the suffix the part of speech (noun/adjective/verb). Reading the two together decodes at once both the sign of an unknown word's meaning and its job in the sentence.

  1. Take the direction from the prefix. If you see in-, un-, dis-, mis-, anti-, the word is probably negative.
  2. Take the core meaning from the root. cred (believe), luc (light), ten (hold)...
  3. Take the part of speech from the suffix. -ous/-able adjective, -tion/-ism noun, -fy/-ate verb.
  4. Verify with context. Compare the guess from the parts with the direction of the sentence; if it fits, lock it in.

Step-by-step root decoding: three examples

Below we unpack, on three words, the reasoning that should run through your mind when you meet a word you have never seen. The aim is not to memorise the word but to see the parsing reflex.

  1. intractable → in- (negative) + tract (to pull) + -able (capability). The parts say "unable to be pulled"; for a person or a problem it settles into "unmanageable, unyielding, hard to deal with". If the context says "an intractable dispute" (a dispute that cannot be resolved), the prefix has already given you the direction (negative), so you eliminate the positive options instantly.
  2. circumspect → circum- (around) + spect (to look). "One who looks around" = someone who checks every direction and takes precautions, i.e. "cautious, wary". The same spect root also lives in retrospective (looking back) and conspicuous (standing out); recognising one root opens a whole word family.
  3. benevolent → bene- (good) + vol (to wish) + -ent (adjective). "Wishing good" = well-intentioned, charitable. You produce its opposite by the same method: mal- (bad) + vol → malevolent (ill-intentioned). Knowing the prefix pair (bene-/mal-) tells you whether a word is positive or negative even without looking at the context.
Caution
Root decoding is a powerful guessing tool but not an exact dictionary: some words have drifted unexpectedly from their root (manufacture no longer means "to make by hand"). So never skip the fourth step — verifying with context. The root gives you the direction, the context gives the verdict.

SAT distractors are not random. When you study vocabulary, recognise these three trap categories; most of your errors fall into one of them.

The most expensive reflex

Knowing a word very well pushes you to automatically pick its most familiar meaning. If the context wants the secondary meaning, this reflex loses points directly; first stop, read what the sentence really wants.

1) The most-familiar-meaning trap

The word's most common dictionary meaning is placed as a distractor; the context wants the secondary meaning. novel = not "a book" but "new/original"; reserved = not "booked" but "reticent"; in words like novel/qualify/check the first meaning is often the trap. The workings of this trap are psychological: the better you know a word, the faster, almost automatically, you pick the meaning you use most — and that very speed misleads you. Because SAT knows this reflex, it deliberately builds the context around the secondary meaning. The defence is simple but disciplined: when you see a familiar word among the options, pause for a second and ask "could an unusual meaning be wanted here?" and go back to the sentence.

2) The sound-similarity trap

A word similar in spelling/sound but unrelated in meaning: flatten (to make flat) vs flatter (to praise insincerely); adverse (unfavourable) vs averse (unwilling); eminent (distinguished) vs imminent (near). This trap hits the Turkish student especially, because we often recognise words not by eye but "by ear"; two similar-sounding words merge into a single blurred entry in the mind. adverse (unfavourable conditions) and averse (an unwilling person) differ by a single letter but one describes a situation, the other an attitude. The solution is to fix the confused pairs separately, each with an example sentence — so that a similar-sounding distractor cannot pull you off course in the exam.

3) The polysemous-verb trap

If a verb has both a concrete and a figurative meaning, SAT overwhelmingly tests the figurative one; the concrete/physical meaning is the distractor. The reason is that SAT passages discuss abstract ideas far more than concrete actions: a writer "checks" (check) a claim, "tempers" (temper) a generalisation, or "tables" (table) a proposal — none of these is a physical movement. So when you see a polysemous verb, before choosing its physical meaning, ask: "Is the sentence really speaking of a tangible action, or of a thought?" If the answer is "a thought", the concrete option is almost certainly the distractor.

How to study

  • Learn in chunks: not the single word but the established collocation (cast doubt on, a marked increase, pose a threat).
  • See it in context: use every new word in a sentence of your own; record its meaning together with its connotation.
  • Active recall: not passive reading but spaced repetition + self-testing. Word on the front of the card, meaning + example + antonym on the back.

How spaced repetition works

The brain stores a word for good not at first sight but as you review it just before you fully forget it. This is the logic of spaced repetition: push a card you know well to the back, bring one you know poorly to the front. This way you spend most of your time on the words that have not yet settled.

  1. Day 1: see the new words with a context sentence; do the first review the same day.
  2. Days 2-3: self-test yesterday's words; mark the ones you know, bring the rest forward.
  3. Day 7 and Day 14: scan the same words again; those still hesitated over move to the "hard deck".
  4. Hard deck: work more often, separately, only with the words persistently forgotten.

Front of the card

The word alone: tenuous. Try to recall the meaning, then flip.

Back of the card

Why it works

Meaning + charge + example + antonym together; the word is coded not alone but with its network.

The questions below are in the Words in Context style and at genuine SAT difficulty. First cover the blank/word, produce your own word, then mark your answer.

The scientist's findings were initially met with ______; only after repeated independent replication did the broader community accept them.

Explanation: "only after repeated replication did the community accept them" → at first they were not accepted, they were met with doubt → B) skepticism. A) acclaim (praise) is the wrong direction (opposite hypothesis). C) indifference is out of scope: the community was not indifferent but unconvinced. D) certainty is the wrong direction.

Rather than settling the question, the new evidence only served to ______ the controversy, giving each side fresh ammunition.

Explanation: "Rather than settling" (contrast) + "fresh ammunition" → to inflame the controversy → A) intensify. B) resolve is exactly what the sentence rejects ("rather than settling"). C) summarize is out of scope. D) postpone is wrong: the controversy is live now, not postponed.

As used in the text, "temper" most nearly means to ______. (Context: "The editor urged the author to temper her most sweeping claims, softening them with qualifications.")

Explanation: "softening them with qualifications" is a definition clue → "temper" = to soften/moderate → D) moderate. B) heat is the concrete/most-familiar-meaning trap (to temper metal). A) harden is the wrong direction. C) ignore is out of scope.

The prefix in "incoherent" signals negation, so the word describes an argument that ______.

Explanation: "in-" is a negation prefix, "coherent" = consistent/well-ordered; incoherent = inconsistent → C) fails to hang together. A) flows logically is the exact opposite (coherent itself). B) persuades easily is out of scope. D) repeats itself is a different flaw (not the root/prefix logic).

Although often described as ______, the policy in fact drew support from across the political spectrum, uniting rather than dividing.

Explanation: "Although" (contrast) + "uniting rather than dividing" → the blank must be the opposite, i.e. "divisive" → A) divisive. B) popular sets up no contrast; it would face the same way as "drew support" (opposite hypothesis). C) moderate and D) temporary are out of scope.

The root "cred" means "believe," so a witness whose account is not ______ will struggle to convince a jury.

Explanation: the "cred" = believe root → believable → B) credible. The "convince a jury" context also points to believability. A) audible (able to be heard) is a sound-similarity/out-of-scope trap. C) lengthy and D) recent are unrelated to the context.

Her prose is admirably ______: every sentence is stripped to its essentials, with not a word wasted.

In academic writing, one would expect a researcher to note a "______ increase" in the data rather than a "gigantic" one.

Explanation: a register/collocation trap. In academic text "a marked increase" is established and measured → C) marked. A) insane and B) awesome belong to everyday register, at odds with the academic tone. D) massive, like "gigantic", is exaggerated/casual; the sentence already wants a measured word instead of "gigantic".
