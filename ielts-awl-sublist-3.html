What this page is for

Academic Word List (AWL) is divided into 10 tiers (sublists). This page works through Sublist 3 from start to finish: the correct forms of 53 academic word families, how they are used in an IELTS sentence, their collocations and the mistakes Turkish candidates make most often. The aim is not to memorise the raw list but to become able to actively produce these words in Writing and Speaking.

The AWL is a list of words that recur constantly in academic texts but appear rarely in everyday speech. The words are divided into 10 sub-lists by frequency: Sublist 1 holds the most frequent families, Sublist 10 the rarest. Sublist 3 is still in the high-frequency zone on this map; that is, these 53 families are words you will need often in real academic texts and in your own essays. Once you have automated Sublist 1 and 2, this is the next stop with the highest return.

The character of Sublist 3 is this: the words are concrete and functional. To demonstrate an idea, to illustrate a result, to justify an opinion, to work within a framework, to speak of a proportion — these are the backbone of every Task 2 essay and every academic passage. That is why Sublist 3 words fall exactly under the Lexical Resource criterion's exact definition of “topic-appropriate, less-common words”: not everyday, but not forced either.

“Knowing” a word is different from being able to “use” it. In the exam you often need to produce not the verb form but the noun (demonstration) or the adjective (demonstrable) form. That is why in the table below we give each headword not on its own but with its family: when you learn a family, learn all its forms together.

“Sublist 3 does not mean “medium difficulty” but “medium-high frequency”. These 53 families are productive words that help in every essay the moment you know them; automate them at reflex level, not just recognition.

Family logic

The AWL counts not “words” but “word families”. rely Knowing the family means knowing not only the verb but also reliance (trust/reliance), reliable (dependable) and reliability (reliability) forms as well. A candidate who learns a family this broadly can flexibly use a single concept in the noun, adjective and verb role of a sentence — this is the essence of Band 7+ Lexical Resource.

Kelimeyi listede görmek yetmez; onu akademik bir cümle içinde çalışırken görmek gerekir. Aşağıdaki 10 özgün cümle, Sublist 3 kelimelerini doğal bir IELTS bağlamında (Task 2 ve akademik pasaj tonu) gösterir. Her cümlenin altında Türkçe açıklaması var; altı çizili kelimeler Sublist 3'e aittir. Bu cümleleri yüksek sesle oku ve kendi konularınla yeniden yaz — üretim ancak taklitten geçer.

1

The government must ensure that any new scheme does not exclude low-income families.

Hükümet, yeni bir programın (scheme) düşük gelirli aileleri dışlamamasını (exclude) güvence altına almalı (ensure).

2

A considerable proportion of the workforce now relies on digital technology to work from home.

İş gücünün hatırı sayılır bir oranı (considerable proportion) artık evden çalışmak için dijital teknolojiye dayanıyor (rely on).

3

The report aims to demonstrate how these components interact within a single framework.

Rapor, bu bileşenlerin (components) tek bir çerçeve (framework) içinde nasıl etkileştiğini (interact) göstermeyi (demonstrate) amaçlıyor.

4

Initial findings imply that corporate investment will contribute to regional growth.

Başlangıçtaki (initial) bulgular, kurumsal (corporate) yatırımın bölgesel büyümeye katkıda bulunacağını (contribute to) ima ediyor (imply).

5

Researchers must specify the criteria they use before they publish their results.

Araştırmacılar sonuçlarını yayımlamadan (publish) önce kullandıkları ölçütleri (criteria) açıkça belirtmelidir (specify).

6

The core problem is that funding alone cannot justify such a constant rise in costs.

Asıl (core) sorun, tek başına finansmanın (funding) maliyetlerdeki bu denli sürekli (constant) bir artışı haklı çıkaramamasıdır (justify).

7

Without parental consent, the school cannot register a minor for the overseas trip.

Ebeveyn onayı (consent) olmadan okul, reşit olmayan bir öğrenciyi (minor) yurtdışı gezisine kaydedemez (register).

8

Financial constraints dominate the debate over how to coordinate public spending.

Mali kısıtlar (constraints), kamu harcamalarının nasıl koordine edileceği (coordinate) tartışmasına hâkim (dominate).

9

These figures correspond closely to earlier data and illustrate a clear shift in behaviour.

Bu rakamlar önceki verilerle yakından örtüşüyor (correspond) ve davranıştaki net bir değişimi (shift) örnekliyor (illustrate).

10

The outcome of the task depends on whether the technique remains valid at a larger volume.

Görevin (task) sonucu (outcome), tekniğin (technique) daha büyük bir hacimde (volume) geçerli (valid) kalıp kalmadığına bağlıdır.

The biggest score leaks in Sublist 3 are not word choice but confusing its form, preposition and near-synonym. The mistakes below appear again and again among Turkish candidates; learning each one correctly once saves more than one sentence in the exam.

The three most critical traps

1) criteria is plural — its singular is criterion. 2) considerable (large amount) and considerate (thoughtful) are not the same word; they shift the meaning entirely. 3) imply (the writer implies) and infer (the reader infers) are opposite in direction. These three alone lower many candidates' Lexical Resource score.

Preposition mistakes (collocation)

Sublist 3 verbs come with fixed prepositions, and prepositions are a matter of memorisation, not reasoning: contribute to, rely on, comment on, an alternative to, place emphasis on. A wrong preposition makes even the right word look amateurish. Memorise these with an example sentence, do not look for a rule.

Self-check list
