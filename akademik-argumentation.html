ArgumentationArgümantasyon (Argumentation)

Argümantasyon: bir iddiayı (claim) gerekçe (reason) ve kanıtla (evidence) destekleyip karşı görüşü (counter-argument) kabul edip çürüterek (rebuttal/concession) ikna edici biçimde kurma. Toulmin modeli (claim-data-warrant). "It is true that... however..." kalıbıyla ödün verip karşı çıkma. IELTS Task 2 ve akademik denemenin özü; tek yanlı, kanıtsız iddia zayıf sayılır.

Argumentation: building a claim persuasively by supporting it with reasons and evidence, and by acknowledging and refuting the counter-argument (rebuttal/concession). The Toulmin model (claim-data-warrant). Conceding and objecting with the "It is true that... however..." pattern. The essence of IELTS Task 2 and the academic essay; a one-sided, unsupported claim is considered weak.

Argümantasyon, bir iddiayı (claim) yalnızca söylemek değil, onu gerekçe (reason) ve kanıtla (evidence) destekleyip, karşı görüşü (counter-argument) de kabul edip çürüterek (concession + rebuttal) ikna edici bir bütün hâline getirme sanatıdır. Temel formül basittir: iddia → çünkü (gerekçe) → örneğin (kanıt) → doğrudur ki... ancak... (ödün + çürütme). Klasik Toulmin modeli bunu üç parçaya indirger: claim (iddia), data (veri/kanıt) ve ikisi arasındaki gizli köprü olan warrant (gerekçelendirme mantığı). Tek yanlı, kanıtsız bir iddia akademik yazıda zayıf sayılır; asıl olgunluk, karşı tarafın en güçlü noktasını görüp onunla hesaplaşmaktır. Argümantasyon, tez cümlesi ile açılan, deneme türlerinin belkemiğini oluşturan ve yönlendirici ifadelerle okura yol gösteren akademik yazının kalbidir. IELTS Task 2, TOEFL Independent Writing ve üniversite denemelerinin tümü bu beceriyle ölçülür.

Argumentation is the art of not merely stating a claim, but making it into a persuasive whole by supporting it with a reason and evidence, and by also acknowledging and refuting the counter-argument (concession + rebuttal). The basic formula is simple: claim → because (reason) → for instance (evidence) → it is true that... however... (concession + rebuttal). The classic Toulmin model reduces this to three parts: claim, data (evidence) and the hidden bridge between them, the warrant (the logic of justification). A one-sided, unsupported claim is considered weak in academic writing; real maturity lies in seeing the other side's strongest point and reckoning with it. Argumentation is the heart of academic writing, opened by the thesis statement, forming the backbone of the essay types, and guiding the reader with signposting expressions. IELTS Task 2, TOEFL Independent Writing and university essays are all measured by this skill.

Bir argümanı bir mahkeme gibi düşün: İddia senin savın, kanıt delillerin, warrant jüriye "bu delil neden bu savı destekliyor" diye anlattığın mantık, karşı görüş ise karşı avukatın itirazı. Argumentation is not merely voicing a view, but making it into a persuasive whole by supporting it with reasons and evidence while also taking the other side's objections into account. The aim is to persuade the reader not by force, but through logic and evidence.

Why does it exist? Because in the academic world, whether a claim is accepted depends not on how loudly you say it, but on how well you justify it. Saying "capital punishment is wrong" is an opinion; supporting it with "because it is irreversible and innocent people can be executed; indeed, dozens have been saved from execution in the US through DNA testing" is an argument. The difference between the two is the difference between a sentence and an essay.

Just an opinion

"Capital punishment is wrong." — unsupported, a single sentence.

↔
An argument

It is wrong because it is irreversible and dozens of innocent people have been saved from execution through DNA testing: claim + reason + evidence.

The closest intuition in Turkish is the patterns "çünkü" (because), "örneğin" (for example), "doğru ama" (true, but) and "buna karşılık" (in contrast). When speaking Turkish too, we support our opinion with reasons and examples. However, in Turkish school culture a one-sided, emotional defence that never mentions the counter-view is often considered sufficient. In the English academic tradition, though, ignoring the counter-argument is a great weakness: the mature writer writes knowing the opponent's strongest point.

Why is it hard? Because argumentation is not a word or a single grammar pattern, but an architecture of thinking. You must arrange all the parts — claim, reason, evidence, the warrant (justification bridge), counter-argument, concession and rebuttal — in the right order and with the right connectors. If one part is missing, the argument collapses: an unsupported claim is "empty talk", an argument without a counter-view is "one-sided propaganda", and a concession without a rebuttal is "shooting yourself in the foot".

"Whether a claim is accepted depends not on how loudly you say it, but on how well you justify it.
Key Point
Think of an argument as a courtroom: the claim is your case, the evidence your proofs, the warrant the logic by which you tell the jury "why this proof supports this case", and the counter-argument the opposing lawyer's objection. A good lawyer does not ignore the other side's objection — first they fairly acknowledge it (concession), then refute it (rebuttal). "It is true that... however..." is your closing speech.

Altın Kalıp

Karşı görüşü önce kabul et, sonra çürüt: "It is true that… however…" Bu tek kalıp, tek yanlı bir yazıyı olgun bir argümana çevirir.

  • Argumentation = structure for persuasion: claim + reason + evidence + counter-argument + rebuttal; not just saying "I think".
  • The basic formula: Claim → Reason → Evidence → Counter → Rebuttal (Claim → Because → For instance → True, but → Even so).
  • The Toulmin model: claim + data (evidence) + warrant (the hidden logic linking the two); at an advanced level backing, qualifier, rebuttal are added.
  • An unsupported claim is weak; every main claim must be supported with a reason AND at least one concrete piece of evidence (example, data, expert opinion).
  • Acknowledging and refuting the counter-view is strength, not weakness: the "It is true that... however..." pattern shows maturity.
  • The Turkish school tradition considers a one-sided defence sufficient; the English academic tradition requires a counter-view.
  • Main connectors: reason (because, since), evidence (for instance, according to), concession (although, admittedly), rebuttal (however, nevertheless), conclusion (therefore, thus).
  • This is the essence of IELTS Task 2 and TOEFL Independent Writing; the formula "one claim, one reason, one example per paragraph" is very useful in the exam.
  • Qualifying the claim (in most cases, to some extent) makes it more logically defensible; an absolute claim is easily refuted.
  • The thesis statement, essay type and signposting expressions are the inseparable neighbours of argumentation.
Golden Pattern

First concede, then refute the counter-view: "It is true that… however…" This single pattern turns a one-sided piece into a mature argument.

Tek cümlede ödün + iddia: 'although' yan cümlesi karşı noktayı kabul eder.
Admittedly, free healthcare is expensive to run. Nevertheless, the social cost of leaving people untreated is far higher.
Kabul etmek gerekir ki ücretsiz sağlık hizmetini işletmek pahalıdır. Yine de insanları tedavisiz bırakmanın toplumsal maliyeti çok daha yüksektir.
'Admittedly' güçlü bir ödün işaretidir; 'nevertheless' çürütmeyi taşır.

3) Toulmin köprüsü: Warrant'ı görünür kılmak

Formül: Claim + Data + Warrant (This matters because... / This shows... because...). Warrant, kanıtın iddiayı neden desteklediğini açıklayan gizli mantıktır. Genelde söylenmez ama zayıf argümanı güçlendirmek için açığa çıkarılır.

Traffic deaths fell 30% after the speed limit dropped (data). This shows lower limits save lives (claim), because slower speeds reduce impact force (warrant).
Hız sınırı düşürüldükten sonra trafik ölümleri %30 azaldı (veri). Bu, düşük sınırların hayat kurtardığını gösterir (iddia), çünkü daha yavaş hızlar çarpma kuvvetini azaltır (köprü).
Warrant açıkça yazıldığında argüman çok daha ikna edici olur.
The candidate lacks any management experience (data); therefore she is unfit to lead the team (claim) — the unstated warrant being that leadership requires prior management experience.
Aday hiç yönetim deneyimine sahip değil (veri); dolayısıyla ekibi yönetmeye uygun değil (iddia) — söylenmeyen köprü ise liderliğin önceden yönetim deneyimi gerektirdiğidir.
Warrant tartışmalıysa argüman zayıflar: belki liderlik deneyim gerektirmez. Warrant'ı görmek, argümanın zayıf noktasını bulmaktır.
Yanlış Homework is bad because it is bad.
Doğru Homework can be counterproductive because it reduces the free time children need for rest and play.
Ödev ters etki yaratabilir çünkü çocukların dinlenme ve oyun için ihtiyaç duyduğu boş zamanı azaltır.
"because it is bad" bir gerekçe değil, iddianın tekrarıdır (döngüsel akıl yürütme / circular reasoning). Gerçek bir gerekçe, iddiadan farklı ve onu destekleyen yeni bir bilgi sunmalıdır.
Yanlış Everyone agrees that plastic is harmful, so we should ban it completely.
Doğru There is growing scientific consensus that single-use plastic harms marine life, which strengthens the case for restricting it.
Tek kullanımlık plastiğin deniz yaşamına zarar verdiğine dair büyüyen bir bilimsel görüş birliği vardır; bu da onu sınırlama gerekçesini güçlendirir.
"Everyone agrees" kanıtlanamaz, mutlak ve safsata (bandwagon) kokan bir dayanaktır. Ayrıca "ban it completely" aşırı ve nitelendirilmemiş bir iddiadır; kanıta dayalı ve ölçülü bir ifadeyle değiştirilir.

An argument is not a single sentence pattern but the arrangement of several parts in the right order. Below we see the three basic building blocks and the classic Toulmin model. These are not an argument's "positive / negative / question" forms but its logical layers.

  1. Claim — the claim: what are you defending?
  2. Reason — the reason: because / since.
  3. Evidence — the evidence: for instance / according to.
  4. Counter — the counter-view: it is true that…
  5. Rebuttal — the rebuttal: however / nevertheless.
When a Link Breaks

Every main claim needs a reason AND at least one concrete piece of evidence; if one of the five links breaks, the argument loses its persuasive power.

1) The core chain: Claim → Reason → Evidence (the heart of CREC)

Formula: Claim + because/since (reason) + for instance/according to (evidence). This trio is the smallest complete argument. The claim says "what", the reason says "why", the evidence says "show it with proof".

Cities should invest in cycling lanes because they reduce pollution. For instance, Copenhagen cut emissions sharply after building them.
Cities should invest in cycling lanes because they reduce pollution. For instance, Copenhagen cut emissions sharply after building them.
Claim (invest) + reason (reduce pollution) + evidence (Copenhagen): a complete core chain.
Remote work benefits employees since it saves commuting time. According to a 2021 survey, workers reclaimed over an hour a day.
Remote work benefits employees since it saves commuting time. According to a 2021 survey, workers reclaimed over an hour a day.
"since" carries the reason, "according to" carries the evidence.
Social media harms teenagers as it fuels comparison. A recent study, for example, linked heavy use to lower self-esteem.
Social media harms teenagers as it fuels comparison. A recent study, for example, linked heavy use to lower self-esteem.
"as" = because; the evidence is embedded within the sentence.

2) The counter-view layer: Concession → Rebuttal

Formula: It is true that / Although (concession) ... However / Nevertheless (rebuttal) .... First you grant the other side a point, then you return to your own claim. This is the layer that rescues an argument from one-sidedness.

It is true that exams cause stress. However, they remain the fairest way to measure knowledge objectively.
It is true that exams cause stress. However, they remain the fairest way to measure knowledge objectively.
A concession (stress) is given, then with 'however' you return to the claim (fairest).
Although nuclear power carries risks, it produces far less carbon than fossil fuels.
Although nuclear power carries risks, it produces far less carbon than fossil fuels.
Concession + claim in a single sentence: the 'although' clause acknowledges the counter-point.
Admittedly, free healthcare is expensive to run. Nevertheless, the social cost of leaving people untreated is far higher.
Admittedly, free healthcare is expensive to run. Nevertheless, the social cost of leaving people untreated is far higher.
'Admittedly' is a strong concession marker; 'nevertheless' carries the rebuttal.

3) The Toulmin bridge: Making the warrant visible

Formula: Claim + Data + Warrant (This matters because... / This shows... because...). The warrant is the hidden logic that explains why the evidence supports the claim. It is usually unstated but is brought out to strengthen a weak argument.

Traffic deaths fell 30% after the speed limit dropped (data). This shows lower limits save lives (claim), because slower speeds reduce impact force (warrant).
Traffic deaths fell 30% after the speed limit dropped (data). This shows that lower limits save lives (claim), because slower speeds reduce impact force (warrant).
When the warrant is written out explicitly, the argument becomes far more persuasive.
The candidate lacks any management experience (data); therefore she is unfit to lead the team (claim) — the unstated warrant being that leadership requires prior management experience.
The candidate has no management experience (data); therefore she is unfit to lead the team (claim) — the unstated warrant being that leadership requires prior management experience.
If the warrant is disputable the argument weakens: perhaps leadership does not require experience. Seeing the warrant means finding the argument's weak point.
Wrong Homework is bad because it is bad.
Correct Homework can be counterproductive because it reduces the free time children need for rest and play.
Homework can be counterproductive because it reduces the free time children need for rest and play.
"because it is bad" is not a reason but a repetition of the claim (circular reasoning). A real reason must present new information that is different from the claim and supports it.
Wrong Everyone agrees that plastic is harmful, so we should ban it completely.
Correct There is growing scientific consensus that single-use plastic harms marine life, which strengthens the case for restricting it.
There is growing scientific consensus that single-use plastic harms marine life, which strengthens the case for restricting it.
"Everyone agrees" is an unprovable, absolute ground that smells of a fallacy (bandwagon). Also, "ban it completely" is an extreme and unqualified claim; it is replaced with an evidence-based, measured expression.

Argümantasyonun parçaları farklı işlevler görür. En yaygın kullanımdan daha özele doğru gidelim.

A) İddiayı destekleme (en yaygın: claim + reason + evidence)

Bir görüşü ileri sürüp gerekçe ve kanıtla desteklemek, argümantasyonun temel işidir. Her ana iddia en az bir gerekçe ve bir somut kanıt ister.

University education should be more affordable, because high fees exclude talented students from poorer backgrounds.
Üniversite eğitimi daha uygun fiyatlı olmalı çünkü yüksek harçlar yoksul kesimden yetenekli öğrencileri dışlar.
İddia + toplumsal gerekçe; kanıt bir sonraki cümlede gelir.
Recycling alone cannot solve the waste crisis; the main reason is that most plastic is never actually recycled.
Geri dönüşüm tek başına atık krizini çözemez; asıl neden çoğu plastiğin aslında hiç geri dönüştürülmemesidir.
"the main reason is that" gerekçeyi öne çıkaran güçlü bir işaretçi.
Public libraries deserve funding, as evidenced by the millions who rely on them for free internet access.
Halk kütüphaneleri fon almayı hak eder; nitekim milyonlarca kişi ücretsiz internet erişimi için onlara bağımlıdır.
"as evidenced by" kanıtı doğrudan iddiaya bağlar.
Regular exercise improves academic performance; studies consistently show a link between fitness and concentration.
Düzenli egzersiz akademik başarıyı artırır; çalışmalar tutarlı biçimde form ile konsantrasyon arasında bir bağ gösterir.
"studies consistently show" kanıtı araştırmaya dayandırır.

B) Karşı görüşü öngörüp ele alma (counter + rebuttal)

Olgun argüman, okurun aklına gelecek itirazı önceden karşılar. Bu, argümanı hem güçlendirir hem de yazarı adil gösterir.

Some may argue that zoos protect endangered species. However, most zoo animals are common species kept mainly for entertainment.
Bazıları hayvanat bahçelerinin nesli tükenen türleri koruduğunu savunabilir. Ancak hayvanat bahçesindeki hayvanların çoğu, büyük ölçüde eğlence için tutulan yaygın türlerdir.
Karşı görüş kurulur (protect), sonra veriyle çürütülür (common species).
Critics of remote work claim it weakens teamwork, yet collaboration tools have largely closed that gap.
Uzaktan çalışmanın eleştirmenleri ekip çalışmasını zayıflattığını iddia eder, oysa iş birliği araçları bu açığı büyük ölçüde kapadı.
"yet" kısa ve güçlü bir çürütme bağlacı.
It might be objected that stricter laws limit freedom; in response, one could note that freedom without safety is meaningless.
Daha sıkı yasaların özgürlüğü kısıtladığı itirazı yapılabilir; buna yanıt olarak, güvenlik olmadan özgürlüğün anlamsız olduğu belirtilebilir.
"in response" çürütmeyi açıkça işaretler; C1-C2 tonu.

C) Ödün verip iddiayı korumak (concession)

Karşı tarafın bir noktasına dürüstçe hak vermek, argümanı zayıflatmaz — tam tersine güvenilirliği artırır. Kilit nokta: ödünden sonra mutlaka çürütmeyle kendi iddiana dön.

Granted, technology can be distracting; even so, its benefits for learning far outweigh this drawback.
Kabul, teknoloji dikkat dağıtıcı olabilir; buna rağmen öğrenme için sağladığı yararlar bu sakıncayı fazlasıyla aşar.
"Granted" ödünü, "even so" çürütmeyi taşır.
While it is true that renewable energy is intermittent, storage technology is improving rapidly.
Yenilenebilir enerjinin kesintili olduğu doğru olsa da, depolama teknolojisi hızla gelişmektedir.
"While it is true that" ödün + karşıt geçiş tek cümlede.
There is some merit to the view that tourism damages local culture, but well-managed tourism can also preserve it.
Turizmin yerel kültüre zarar verdiği görüşünde bir doğruluk payı vardır, ancak iyi yönetilen turizm onu koruyabilir de.
"There is some merit to the view that" nazik, dengeli bir ödün kalıbı.

D) İddiayı nitelendirme (qualifier ile ölçme)

Mutlak iddialar ("her zaman", "asla") tek karşı örnekle çöker. Nitelendirici eklemek iddiayı mantıksal olarak daha savunulabilir kılar. Bu, tez cümlesini de daha güçlü yapar.

In most cases, early intervention improves outcomes for struggling readers.
Çoğu durumda erken müdahale, zorlanan okuyucuların sonuçlarını iyileştirir.
"in most cases" istisnalara kapı bırakır; iddiayı kolay çürütülemez kılar.
Advertising influences children to a significant extent, though not entirely.
Reklamlar çocukları önemli ölçüde etkiler, tamamen olmasa da.
"to a significant extent" + "though not entirely": ölçülü ve dürüst.
Fast food is generally less nutritious than home-cooked meals.
Fast food genellikle ev yemeklerinden daha az besleyicidir.
"generally" mutlak iddiadan ("always") korur.

E) Sonucu iddiaya bağlama (concluding the argument)

For these reasons, governments should prioritise public transport over new roads.
Bu nedenlerle hükümetler yeni yollar yerine toplu taşımaya öncelik vermelidir.
"For these reasons" gerekçeleri toparlayıp iddiaya bağlar.
On balance, the advantages of a four-day week appear to outweigh its costs.
Genel olarak bakıldığında, dört günlük çalışma haftasının avantajları maliyetlerini aşıyor gibi görünüyor.
"On balance" iki yanı tarttıktan sonraki dengeli sonuç kalıbı; IELTS'te çok değerli.

The parts of argumentation serve different functions. Let us go from the most common use to the more specific.

A) Supporting a claim (most common: claim + reason + evidence)

Putting forward a view and supporting it with a reason and evidence is the basic job of argumentation. Every main claim needs at least one reason and one concrete piece of evidence.

University education should be more affordable, because high fees exclude talented students from poorer backgrounds.
University education should be more affordable, because high fees exclude talented students from poorer backgrounds.
Claim + a social reason; the evidence comes in the next sentence.
Recycling alone cannot solve the waste crisis; the main reason is that most plastic is never actually recycled.
Recycling alone cannot solve the waste crisis; the main reason is that most plastic is never actually recycled.
"the main reason is that" is a strong marker that foregrounds the reason.
Public libraries deserve funding, as evidenced by the millions who rely on them for free internet access.
Public libraries deserve funding, as evidenced by the millions who rely on them for free internet access.
"as evidenced by" ties the evidence directly to the claim.
Regular exercise improves academic performance; studies consistently show a link between fitness and concentration.
Regular exercise improves academic performance; studies consistently show a link between fitness and concentration.
"studies consistently show" grounds the evidence in research.

B) Anticipating and addressing the counter-view (counter + rebuttal)

A mature argument answers in advance the objection that will come to the reader's mind. This both strengthens the argument and makes the writer appear fair.

Some may argue that zoos protect endangered species. However, most zoo animals are common species kept mainly for entertainment.
Some may argue that zoos protect endangered species. However, most zoo animals are common species kept mainly for entertainment.
The counter-view is set up (protect), then refuted with data (common species).
Critics of remote work claim it weakens teamwork, yet collaboration tools have largely closed that gap.
Critics of remote work claim it weakens teamwork, yet collaboration tools have largely closed that gap.
"yet" is a short, strong rebuttal connector.
It might be objected that stricter laws limit freedom; in response, one could note that freedom without safety is meaningless.
It might be objected that stricter laws limit freedom; in response, one could note that freedom without safety is meaningless.
"in response" clearly marks the rebuttal; a C1-C2 tone.

C) Conceding while protecting the claim (concession)

Honestly granting the other side a point does not weaken the argument — on the contrary, it increases credibility. The key point: after the concession, always return to your own claim with a rebuttal.

Granted, technology can be distracting; even so, its benefits for learning far outweigh this drawback.
Granted, technology can be distracting; even so, its benefits for learning far outweigh this drawback.
"Granted" carries the concession, "even so" the rebuttal.
While it is true that renewable energy is intermittent, storage technology is improving rapidly.
While it is true that renewable energy is intermittent, storage technology is improving rapidly.
"While it is true that" combines concession + a contrastive transition in one sentence.
There is some merit to the view that tourism damages local culture, but well-managed tourism can also preserve it.
There is some merit to the view that tourism damages local culture, but well-managed tourism can also preserve it.
"There is some merit to the view that" is a polite, balanced concession pattern.

D) Qualifying the claim (measuring with a qualifier)

Absolute claims ("always", "never") collapse with a single counter-example. Adding a qualifier makes the claim more logically defensible. This also makes the thesis statement stronger.

In most cases, early intervention improves outcomes for struggling readers.
In most cases, early intervention improves outcomes for struggling readers.
"in most cases" leaves room for exceptions; makes the claim hard to refute.
Advertising influences children to a significant extent, though not entirely.
Advertising influences children to a significant extent, though not entirely.
"to a significant extent" + "though not entirely": measured and honest.
Fast food is generally less nutritious than home-cooked meals.
Fast food is generally less nutritious than home-cooked meals.
"generally" protects from an absolute claim ("always").

E) Tying the conclusion to the claim (concluding the argument)

For these reasons, governments should prioritise public transport over new roads.
For these reasons, governments should prioritise public transport over new roads.
"For these reasons" gathers the reasons and ties them to the claim.
On balance, the advantages of a four-day week appear to outweigh its costs.
On balance, the advantages of a four-day week appear to outweigh its costs.
"On balance" is a balanced conclusion pattern after weighing both sides; very valuable in IELTS.

Argümantasyonun ustalığı dengededir: iddianızın gücü, kanıtınızın gücüyle eşleşmelidir. Ayrıca karşı görüşü nasıl kurduğunuz argümanın dürüstlüğünü belirler. Aynı iddiayı zayıf ya da güçlü savunabilirsiniz — fark, ele aldığınız karşı görüşün gücündedir.

Video games are bad because my cousin plays them and he failed school. (zayıf: tekil örnek + korelasyon)
Video oyunları kötüdür çünkü kuzenim oynuyor ve okulda kaldı. (zayıf: tek örnek + korelasyon)
Tek bir kişisel örnek genelleme için yetersizdir (hasty generalization); ayrıca oyun ile başarısızlık arasında nedensellik kanıtlanmamış.
Excessive gaming may harm academic performance, as several longitudinal studies link over four hours a day to lower grades. (güçlü)
Aşırı oyun akademik başarıya zarar verebilir; nitekim birkaç boylamsal çalışma günde dört saatin üzerini düşük notlarla ilişkilendirir. (güçlü)
"may" nitelendirir, "excessive/over four hours" kapsamı sınırlar, kanıt çoğul çalışmaya dayanır.

Steelman vs straw man: Karşı görüşü çürütürken onu en zayıf hâliyle kurup yıkmak kolaya kaçmaktır ve bir safsatadır (straw man — korkuluk adam). Olgun yazar, karşı görüşün en güçlü hâlini (steelman) kurar ve onu çürütür. "Some naively think X" (küçümseyici) yerine "A serious case can be made that X, because..." (adil) yazmak, argümanınızı daha inandırıcı kılar.

İddianın gücü ile dilin gücü: Elinizde güçlü, tekrarlanmış kanıt varsa kararlı konuşabilirsiniz ("clearly demonstrates"); kanıtınız sınırlıysa iddianızı yumuşatın ("may suggest", "appears to"). Kanıttan güçlü konuşmak (overstatement) argümanı kırılgan kılar: tek karşı örnek onu devirir. Bu incelik, ölçülü dil (hedging) becerisiyle iç içedir.

İleri nüans: örtük warrant'ları avlamak
Her argümanda söylenmeyen bir warrant (köprü) vardır ve argümanın en kırılgan yeri genellikle orada gizlidir. "She studied at Oxford, so she must be brilliant" argümanının örtük warrant'ı "Oxford mezunları zekidir" varsayımıdır — tartışmalı. C2 becerisi, hem kendi warrant'larınızı fark edip gerekçelendirmek (backing eklemek), hem de rakibin örtük warrant'ını görünür kılıp onu hedef almaktır. Bir argümanı çürütmenin en zarif yolu, verisine değil, warrant'ına saldırmaktır.

Mastery of argumentation lies in balance: the strength of your claim must match the strength of your evidence. Moreover, how you set up the counter-view determines the honesty of the argument. You can defend the same claim weakly or strongly — the difference lies in the strength of the counter-view you address.

Video games are bad because my cousin plays them and he failed school. (weak: a single example + correlation)
Video games are bad because my cousin plays them and he failed school. (weak: a single example + correlation)
A single personal example is insufficient for a generalization (hasty generalization); moreover, causation between gaming and failure is not proven.
Excessive gaming may harm academic performance, as several longitudinal studies link over four hours a day to lower grades. (strong)
Excessive gaming may harm academic performance, as several longitudinal studies link over four hours a day to lower grades. (strong)
"may" qualifies, "excessive/over four hours" limits the scope, the evidence rests on multiple studies.

Steelman vs straw man: When refuting the counter-view, setting it up in its weakest form and knocking it down is taking the easy way out and is a fallacy (straw man). The mature writer sets up the strongest form (steelman) of the counter-view and refutes it. Writing "A serious case can be made that X, because..." (fair) instead of "Some naively think X" (dismissive) makes your argument more convincing.

The strength of the claim vs the strength of the language: If you have strong, repeated evidence, you can speak with conviction ("clearly demonstrates"); if your evidence is limited, soften your claim ("may suggest", "appears to"). Speaking more strongly than the evidence (overstatement) makes the argument fragile: a single counter-example topples it. This nuance is intertwined with the skill of measured language (hedging).

Advanced nuance: hunting the implicit warrant
Every argument has an unstated warrant (bridge), and the argument's most fragile point is usually hidden there. The implicit warrant of "She studied at Oxford, so she must be brilliant" is the assumption "Oxford graduates are intelligent" — disputable. The C2 skill is both to notice and justify your own warrants (add backing), and to make the opponent's implicit warrant visible and target it. The most elegant way to refute an argument is to attack not its data but its warrant.

Not every text is an argument, and not every context calls for the same argument structure. A good writer knows when to build a full argument and when to simplify.

  • Definitions, facts and neutral explanation do not require an argument: "Water boils at 100°C" or "The report has three sections" are pieces of information; adding a reason/counter-view to them is unnecessary.
  • A counter-view is not required in every paragraph: generally in an essay the counter-view is devoted to one body paragraph; cramming a counter-argument into every sentence scatters the text.
  • Some essay types can be one-sided: in IELTS "To what extent do you agree?" supports taking a strong side; even so, the strongest essays acknowledge and refute a counter-view.
  • In personal/creative writing the rule loosens: a blog or personal essay may rest on experience rather than the academic evidence standard; but in university and exam writing, evidence is expected.
  • If there is a strong, established consensus, excessive concession is unnecessary: building a long counter-view for "Smoking causes cancer" is artificial; here the evidence is already overwhelming.

Advanced: how much should the concession grant?
When making a concession, balance is critical. Too little concession = you appear one-sided and unfair. Too much concession = you refute your own claim ("It is true that homework is useless, but it is essential" — you shoot yourself in the foot). Rule: the concession must grant a legitimate point of the counter-view, but never surrender the core of your claim. The rebuttal must always be stronger than the concession, so that the paragraph closes in your favour.

  1. (a) We should ban cars because cars are bad. (b) We should limit cars in city centres because they are the main source of urban air pollution.

Where to start
Teach argumentation not as an abstract "persuasion" but as a concrete structure. The most effective start: give students a bare claim ("Homework should be banned") and have them climb the CREC ladder — first "because" (reason), then "for example" (evidence), then "true, but" (concession + rebuttal). This mechanical scaffold gives confidence; nuance (warrant, steelman, fallacy) comes later.

Concept check: Show the student the sentence "Homework is bad because it is bad" and ask "Why is this a weak argument?". If the answer is "because the reason repeats the claim and gives no new information (circular)", they have grasped the most critical distinction. Most students confuse a reason with a repetition of the claim; correcting this early is essential.

A trap specific to the Turkish learner: The Turkish school composition tradition rewards one-sided, emotional, authority-based defence ("herkes bilir ki" / everyone knows, "vicdanı olan karşı çıkar" / anyone with a conscience opposes it). Mark these patterns specifically and teach their alternatives: require building a counter-argument, ask for a source/evidence instead of "everyone knows", and have them build a concrete reason instead of emotional accusation.

Activity suggestion: "Fallacy hunt" — have students bring real arguments from advertisements, social media or newspaper columns; let them hunt ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, ad populum and slippery slope. Then a "steelman tournament": one student defends a view, the other must set up the counter-view in its strongest form (without belittling it). This exercises both critical thinking and fair argument at once. To close, recall the link with the thesis statement and the essay type.

  • Karşı görüşü kabul edip çürütmek güçtür, zayıflık değil: "It is true that... however..." — ama çürütme her zaman ödünden güçlü olmalı.
  • En güçlü karşı görüşü kur (steelman), çarpıtıp yıkma (straw man safsatadır).
  • Mantık hatalarından kaçın: ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, ad populum, slippery slope, circular reasoning.
  • İddiayı nitelendir (in most cases, to some extent); mutlak iddia (always/never) tek karşı örnekle çöker.
  • Türk-öğrenci tuzağı: tek yanlı, duygusal, "herkes bilir ki" tarzı otorite çağrısı — bunlardan kaçın, kanıt ve mantık kur.
  • Tez cümlesi, deneme türleri ve yönlendirici ifadeler ile iç içe; IELTS Task 2 ve TOEFL essay'inin özüdür.
  • Argumentation = structure for persuasion: building a claim by supporting it with a reason and evidence and by acknowledging (concession) and refuting (rebuttal) the counter-argument.
  • The basic formula CREC: Claim → Reason → Evidence → Counter. Climb this ladder in every body paragraph.
  • The Toulmin model: claim + data + warrant (the hidden logic linking evidence to claim); at an advanced level backing, qualifier, rebuttal.
  • An unsupported claim is weak; every main claim needs a reason AND concrete evidence (example, data, source).
  • Acknowledging and refuting the counter-view is strength, not weakness: "It is true that... however..." — but the rebuttal must always be stronger than the concession.
  • Set up the strongest counter-view (steelman), do not distort and knock it down (straw man is a fallacy).
  • Avoid logical fallacies: ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, ad populum, slippery slope, circular reasoning.
  • Qualify the claim (in most cases, to some extent); an absolute claim (always/never) collapses with a single counter-example.
  • The Turkish-learner trap: one-sided, emotional, "everyone knows"-style appeals to authority — avoid these, build evidence and logic.
  • Intertwined with the thesis statement, essay types and signposting expressions; it is the essence of IELTS Task 2 and the TOEFL essay.
