Ünite 2 · Present Perfect Continuous · Bölüm 1/6

Grammar: Present Perfect Continuous

Study the rules, look at the examples, then do the exercises.

An action that is still happening

In Unit 1 you used the Present Perfect Simple (I have worked, she has gone) to talk about experiences and finished results. Now, at B1, you meet a very useful new tense: the Present Perfect Continuous (I have been working, she has been running). It is one of the clearest ways to sound natural in English, because native speakers use it all the time.

Here is the core idea in one line: the Present Perfect Continuous connects an activity that started in the past and is still going on now — or that has just stopped and we can still see the result. The focus is on the activity itself and how long it has continued.

  • I have been studying English for three years.Started in the past, still true now → how long? → Continuous.
  • It has been raining, so the streets are wet.Just stopped, the result is visible now.
  • You look tired. Have you been playing outside all day?The activity and its effect on now.

1. Form (have / has + been + V-ing)

The Present Perfect Continuous is built in three parts: have or has, then been (this part never changes), then the -ing form of the main verb. Only have / has changes for the subject — been is always the same.

SubjectPositiveNegativeQuestion
I / you / we / theyhave been workinghaven't been workingHave you been working?
he / she / ithas been workinghasn't been workingHas she been working?
Key point
  • The middle word been never changes: I have been reading, she has been reading.
  • The main verb always takes -ing: work → working, run → running, write → writing.
  • Do NOT drop been: "She has been waiting." ✓ — "She has waiting." ✗

2. When we use it (the two main uses)

There are two situations where this tense is the perfect choice. Learn both.

UseMeaningExample
1. Unfinished actionstarted in the past, still continuing nowShe has been living in Ankara for a year. (She still lives there.)
2. Recent action with a resultjust finished, but we see or feel the effect nowI'm hot because I have been running. (I have just stopped.)
Golden rule
  • If you can ask "How long?" about an activity still going on, the Present Perfect Continuous is usually right.
  • If a present situation (wet ground, tired eyes, dirty hands) is the result of a recent activity, use it too.

3. How long...? with for and since (süre)

This tense loves the question How long...? and the answers for and since — exactly the two words you met in Unit 1.

WordAnswersExample
fora length of time (a duration)for two hours, for a week, for years
sincea point when it startedsince 9 o'clock, since Monday, since 2020
  • — How long have you been waiting?The typical question for this tense.
  • — I have been waiting for twenty minutes.for + a duration.
  • — We have been walking since six this morning.since + a starting point.

4. Time words that fit this tense (clues)

Certain time expressions point straight to the Present Perfect Continuous. When you see them, think of an activity that has been going on.

Time wordMeaningExample
all day / all morningfor the whole periodI have been drawing all day.
recently / latelyin the near past, around nowShe has been feeling better lately.
for hours / for agesa long time up to nowThey have been talking for hours.
How long...?asking about durationHow long have you been learning it?
Watch out
  • lately and recently often go with this tense to describe a habit around now: "I have been sleeping badly lately."

5. Continuous vs Simple (process vs result)

This is the heart of the unit. Compare the Present Perfect Continuous (the process, how long) with the Present Perfect Simple from Unit 1 (the result, how many / finished). Read every row twice.

IdeaContinuous (process)Simple (result)
The activity vs the finished jobI have been colouring a big picture. (the activity)I have coloured three pictures. (the result)
How long vs how manyShe has been drawing pictures all day.She has drawn ten pictures.
Maybe not finished vs finishedHe has been reading that book. (perhaps not finished)He has read that book. (finished it)
Quick test
  • Focus on how long or on the ongoing activity? → Continuous.
  • Focus on how many or on a completed result? → Simple.

6. State verbs: a classic trap (✗ / ✓)

Some verbs describe states, not activities: know, like, love, want, need, believe, understand, have (for possession). These verbs are not normally used in the continuous, even when we talk about "how long". Use the Present Perfect Simple instead.

Wrong ✗Right ✓Why?
I have been knowing her for years.I have known her for years."know" is a state verb → simple, not continuous.
We have been wanting a bigger bike.We have wanted a bigger bike."want" is a state, not an activity.
She has been liking this song.She has liked this song."like" describes a feeling, not an action.
Remember
  • Activity verbs (work, run, study, wait, rain) → continuous is fine.
  • State verbs (know, like, believe, want, understand) → use the simple form.

7. Putting it together (Model Dialogue)

Two friends meet in the park. Notice how they use the Present Perfect Continuous for ongoing activities, and how know stays in the simple form.

  • — You look exhausted! What have you been doing?Continuous: an activity with a result now (tired).
  • — I have been carrying boxes all morning. We're moving to a new house.all morning + activity → Continuous.
  • — Really? How long have you been living here?How long...? → Continuous.
  • — For six years. I have known this street since I was little."know" is a state verb → simple: have known.
  • — I have been looking for my lost football too, but I haven't found it yet.Continuous for the ongoing search; simple for the result.
Test yourself
  • Find the verb that is NOT in the continuous. Why? (have known — "know" is a state verb.)
  • Which question asks about duration? (How long have you been living here?)
  • Turn "I have been carrying boxes" into a question. (Have you been carrying boxes?)

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Before You Read (Okumadan Önce)

Good readers prepare before they start. Before you read, think about these three questions (you do not need to write the answers):

  • Think about your own week. What is one activity you have been doing a lot lately?
  • The text is about a busy young artist. Which tense do you expect to see many times?
  • Look at the title, "Still Drawing". What does it suggest about the girl — is she stopping or continuing? Guess!
How to read
  • First read: read quickly, without stopping. Get the general idea; you do not need every word.
  • Second read: read the questions, then search the text for the answers. Notice each Present Perfect Continuous ("has been doing") and how long it has lasted.

Words to spot (from the Speaking section): lately · busy · all afternoon · progress · recently · exhausted

Progress you keep making, hour by hour and month by month.
Still Drawing: Mia's Big Comic Book

It is nine o'clock on a Saturday morning in Izmir, and Mia is already awake. She has been sitting at her little desk since eight. Her eyes hurt a little, because she has been staring at her drawing for so long. For most children the day has not started yet; for Mia, work on her comic book has been going on since breakfast.

Mia is nine years old, and lately she has been busier than ever. For the last six months she has been working on a huge comic book called 'The Sky Kingdom', so she rarely has a free weekend. The school art show is close, and when the big day feels near, she draws all afternoon, and by bedtime she is completely exhausted. She has not had a proper, lazy day off for weeks.

Her room is small, but it is full of paper, and piles of drawings have been growing on the floor. These days she has been spending almost all her time at home, because the comic needs her full attention. She has been trying to keep her normal bedtime, but the art show has been getting closer, so recently she has been drawing in the evenings too. She has been rewriting the same page for two weeks, because she wants the dragon on it to look perfect.

Making the comic has not always been easy. However, Mia never complains, and she is proud of every page. Some days she has been making wonderful progress; other days she has been sitting for hours and drawing almost nothing. 'Making a comic is not a race,' she says. 'It is more like gardening: you plant ideas and slowly a whole world grows.'

Mia has always loved drawing. She has been studying comics since she was six years old, and she now fills a new sketchbook almost every month. While she was on her summer holiday, she also started painting with watercolours, because she wanted to colour her skies in a softer way. 'People think I finished learning years ago,' she laughs, 'but the truth is I have never stopped. I have been getting better all year, although I still make lots of mistakes.'

Mia did not always draw comics. When she was smaller, she only drew animals and rainbows. Then, three years ago, her teacher gave her a comic book and said, 'Try making one.' She has been doing this hobby — inventing her own stories — for three years now, and she still loves it more than anything. 'Recently I have been getting closer to my biggest dream, winning first prize at the school art show, but I have not reached it yet,' she says.

Yesterday her friend Deniz came round. They have known each other since they were very little, and he worries that Mia has been working too hard. 'How long have you been drawing today?' he asked. 'Since breakfast,' Mia admitted. 'You have been saying "just one more page" for two weeks!' he laughed. Mia smiled, because he was right.

Since that visit, Mia has been trying to look after herself better. She has been going for a short bike ride every evening. She has not won her prize yet, but she has been getting a little closer every day — and, just like the hero in her comic, she is still going.

Ünite 2 · Present Perfect Continuous · Bölüm 5/6

Writing: What I Have Been Doing Lately

Write and submit your paragraph; your teacher will review it.

Connectors turn sentences into a text (Linking Words)

A list of short, separate sentences is not real writing. What connects your ideas is linking words. At A2 you used and, but, so, because. At B1 you add four more powerful connectors: also, when, while, although and however. These let you show contrast, background events and extra information like a confident writer.

ConnectorUse it to...Example
andadd informationI have been practising hard, and I have been sleeping well.
butshow a small contrastI have been trying, but it is not easy.
soshow a resultI have been running, so I am tired now.
becausegive a reasonMy eyes hurt because I have been drawing all day.
alsoadd an extra pointI have been learning the piano. I have also been practising the guitar.
whensay at what past timeWhen I started, I could draw very little.
whiletwo things at the same timeWhile I was at school, I began drawing comics.
althoughshow a strong contrastAlthough I have been busy, I keep going.
howevercontrast between two sentencesI have been improving. However, I still make mistakes.

See the difference — the same facts, written two ways:

Separate sentences ✗Connected, fluent writing ✓
I have been learning to draw. It is hard. I have been making progress. I still make mistakes. I do not give up. I have been learning to draw, and although it is hard, I have been making progress. However, I still make mistakes, but I do not give up.
Rule
  • Use at least 3 different connectors in your paragraph. Do not write sentence by sentence; link your ideas.
  • When when / while / although / however start a sentence, they are usually followed by a comma: "However, I still make mistakes."

Paragraph structure (Paragraph Structure)

A "what I have been doing" paragraph has three parts. The skeleton stays the same every time — you change the content, not the structure:

PartWhat to writeExample
1. OpeningA general topic sentence in the Present Perfect ContinuousI have been very busy lately, and I have been doing lots of fun things.
2. BodyYour ongoing activities. Say how long with for / since, add a present result, and link with connectors.I have been practising the piano for three months, so I have got much better. Although it is hard, I keep going.
3. ClosingA final comment about progress or a goal you have not reached yetI have not learned my favourite song yet, but I feel closer every week.

Task (Görev)

Write about activities you have been doing lately. Use the Present Perfect Continuous, and say how long.

WHAT TO INCLUDE

  • an opening sentence about how busy you have been (Present Perfect Continuous)
  • two or three activities you have been doing lately (Present Perfect Continuous)

Sample Answer (~165 words) I have been learning to draw for a few years, and it has become my favourite thing to do. When I started at school, I could hardly draw a straight line, but I did not give up. While I was on holiday, I also began painting with watercolours, because I wanted to develop my colouring.
