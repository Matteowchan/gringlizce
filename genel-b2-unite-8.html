Ünite 8 · Advanced Passive · Bölüm 1/7

Grammar: Advanced Passive

Take the passive beyond the basics: modals, perfect forms, infinitives and gerunds.

When the action matters more than the doer

You already know the basic passive: The bread is baked every morning. The B2 skill is using the passive in every tense and structure — with modals, in the perfect, after other verbs as an infinitive or a gerund. We choose the passive when the action is more important than the person who did it, when the doer is obvious or unknown, or when we want to sound formal and objective.

This is the language of reports, rules, science and news. Master it and your written English immediately sounds more professional and precise, because you can put the important information first and keep the doer in the background.

  • This form must be completed in black ink.Modal passive: must + be + V3 → a rule; the doer (you) is obvious.
  • The new bridge has been completed at last.Present perfect passive: has + been + V3 → result now.
  • By June, the road will have been finished.Future perfect passive: will + have + been + V3.
  • I hate being told what to do all the time.Passive gerund: being + V3, after a verb like "hate".

1. Modal passives (modal + be + V3)

To make any modal passive, keep the modal and add be + past participle. The modal never changes; only be links it to the participle. This is how we express rules, ability, possibility and advice in an impersonal way.

ModalPassive formExample
obligationmust / have to + be + V3Helmets must be worn on site.
adviceshould / ought to + be + V3This email should be sent today.
ability / possibilitycan / could + be + V3The whole city can be seen from here.
uncertaintymay / might + be + V3The flight might be delayed by the storm.
Key point
  • The word be is not optional: "It must be checked." ✓ — "It must checked." ✗
  • For the past, use modal + have been + V3: "The parcel should have been sent yesterday."

2. Perfect passives (have / had + been + V3)

Every perfect tense has a passive. The pattern is always the same: take the perfect (have / has / had / will have) and add been + past participle. The tricky word is been — it is easy to forget, but the sentence is wrong without it.

TensePassive formExample
Present Perfecthas / have + been + V3The report has been finished.
Past Perfecthad + been + V3The palace had been built before 1900.
Future Perfectwill have + been + V3By Friday it will have been tested twice.
B2 subtlety — do not lose "been"
  • "The house has been sold." ✓ — "The house has sold." means something different (it sold itself / it went), and "has sold" is active.
  • Compare active and passive: They have built a bridge. (active) → A bridge has been built. (passive).

3. Passive infinitive & passive gerund (to be done / being done)

After many verbs and adjectives we need a passive infinitive (to be + V3) or a passive gerund (being + V3). Choose the infinitive after verbs like need, want, have and after must/should; choose the gerund after prepositions and verbs like hate, enjoy, avoid, remember.

FormUse it after...Example
to be + V3need, want, would like, have toThe engine needs to be checked.
to be + V3it is + adjective (important, easy)This point is easy to be misunderstood.
being + V3hate, enjoy, avoid, rememberShe avoids being interrupted.
being + V3a preposition (of, about, without)I'm tired of being told what to do.
B2 subtlety
  • need + to be + V3 and need + -ing both work: "The car needs to be washed." = "The car needs washing." Both are passive in meaning.
  • Never mix them: "I hate being told." ✓ — "I hate to be telling." ✗

4. "It is said that..." (impersonal passive — a bridge to Unit 9)

When we report what people generally say or believe — without naming who — English uses two impersonal passive patterns. You will study reporting in detail in Unit 9; here is the essential shape.

PatternStructureExample
impersonalIt is said / believed / thought + that + clauseIt is said that the acoustics are perfect.
personalSubject + is said / believed + to + infinitiveThe theatre is said to have perfect acoustics.
Key point
  • Both mean the same. "It is believed that he is rich." = "He is believed to be rich."
  • This is very common in news and formal writing, where the source is unknown or unimportant.

5. Passive signals (when to choose it)

Certain situations almost always call for the passive. Learn to recognise them and the right form will follow.

SituationWhy passive?Example
rules & instructionsthe doer is obvious (you / people)Bags must be left at the door.
news & reportsthe action matters, not the agentThree people have been arrested.
processes & scienceobjective, impersonal toneThe sample is then heated to 100°C.
unknown doerwe do not know who did itMy bike has been stolen.
Watch out — naming the doer
  • If the doer is important, add it with by: "The palace was designed by a famous architect."
  • Do not overuse the passive. If the doer is interesting, the active is often clearer and stronger.

6. Common mistakes (✗ / ✓)

These are the errors B2 students still make with the passive. Check your own writing against every row.

Wrong ✗Right ✓Why?
This form must completed today.This form must be completed today.a modal passive needs "be": modal + be + V3.
The bridge has completed.The bridge has been completed.a perfect passive needs "been": has + been + V3.
The letter should be wrote again.The letter should be written again.use the past participle (written), not the past simple.
I hate to be told what to do.I hate being told what to do."hate" takes the passive gerund: being + V3.
It is said he is very rich. / He is said he is rich.He is said to be very rich.personal passive = subject + is said + to + infinitive.

7. Putting it together (Model Dialogue)

Two managers inspect a building project. Read it twice and watch how the passive works across modals and perfect tenses: a rule with must, a result with the present perfect, an earlier event with the past perfect, and a forecast with the future perfect.

A:Has the main roof been finished yet?Present perfect passive question → has + been + V3.
B:Yes. It was completed last week, and every joint has been checked.Past simple passive + present perfect passive.
A:Good. Remember, all visitors must be given a helmet.Modal passive rule → must + be + V3.
B:Of course. Actually, before we arrived, the safety signs had already been put up.Past perfect passive → had + been + V3.
A:Perfect. By next month the whole site will have been inspected.Future perfect passive → will have + been + V3.
B:And it is said to be the safest project in the region!Personal passive → subject + is said + to + infinitive.
Test yourself
  • Find the future perfect passive. What are its four parts? (will + have + been + inspected)
  • Why is "must be given" passive, not "must give"? (We focus on the visitors receiving a helmet, not on who gives it.)
  • Rewrite "It is said that this is the safest project" as a personal passive. (This is said to be the safest project.)

Speaking: How Things Are Made & Kept

Learn the words, then use them in your own passive sentences about processes and rules.

construct (v)
to build something large (inşa etmek)
Syn: build · Ant: demolish
"The stadium was constructed in only two years."
demolish (v)
to deliberately destroy a building (yıkmak)
Syn: knock down · Ant: construct
"The old factory is going to be demolished."
restore (v)
to bring something back to its original state (restore etmek, aslına döndürmek)
Syn: renovate · Ant: ruin
"The painting has been carefully restored."
preserve (v)
to keep something safe from harm or change (korumak, muhafaza etmek)
Syn: protect · Ant: destroy
"These documents must be preserved for the future."
inspect (v)
to examine something carefully and officially (denetlemek, incelemek)
Syn: examine · Ant: overlook
"Every lift is inspected once a year."
approve (v)
to officially accept a plan or request (onaylamak)
Syn: authorise · Ant: reject
"The design had to be approved by the council."
manufacture (v)
to make goods in large quantities in a factory (imal etmek, üretmek)
Syn: produce · Ant: —
"These parts are manufactured in Germany."
install (v)
to fit equipment ready for use (kurmak, monte etmek)
Syn: set up · Ant: remove
"New cameras have been installed at the entrance."
launch (v)
to introduce a new product or project (piyasaya sürmek, başlatmak)
Syn: release · Ant: withdraw
"The new app will be launched in spring."
recall (v)
to officially ask for a faulty product back (geri çağırmak)
Syn: withdraw · Ant: release
"Thousands of cars have been recalled for repair."
maintain (v)
to keep something in good condition (bakımını yapmak, sürdürmek)
Syn: service · Ant: neglect
"The machines are maintained every month."
neglect (v)
to fail to look after something (ihmal etmek)
Syn: ignore · Ant: maintain
"The building had been neglected for years."
renovate (v)
to repair and improve an old building (yenilemek, tadilat yapmak)
Syn: refurbish · Ant: neglect
"The whole station is being renovated."

Good readers prepare before they start. Before you read, think about these three questions (you do not need to write the answers):

  • The text is about saving a historic theatre. Which passive forms do you expect to see a lot?
  • The title is "Saving the Old Opera House". Do you think the ending will be hopeful or sad? Guess!
How to read
  • First read: read quickly, without stopping. Get the general idea; you do not need every word.
  • Second read: read the questions, then search the text. Notice each passive — a present perfect passive ("has been restored"), a past perfect passive ("had been neglected"), a modal passive ("must be protected") — and ask why the writer chose it.

Words to spot (from the Speaking section): restore · preserve · neglect · renovate · inspect · construct · demolish · maintain

From neglect to new life: a historic theatre that has been given a second chance.
Saving the Old Opera House

The old opera house in the town centre has finally been saved. For nearly thirty years it stood empty, and many people believed that it would eventually be demolished. However, after a long public campaign, the building has been restored to its original beauty, and it will be reopened to the public next spring.

For most of those thirty years, the building had simply been forgotten. Its tall doors were locked, its windows were boarded up, and weeds were allowed to grow between the front steps. Local children invented stories about the 'ghost theatre', and photographs of its faded posters were shared online, whereas older residents still remembered the music that had once poured out of it. Every so often a developer would suggest that the site could be sold and cleared, and each time the idea was quietly dropped, since no one could agree on what should replace such a beloved landmark.

What made the opera house special was not only its size but its history. It had been designed as the cultural heart of the town, a place where ordinary families could listen to great music for the price of a cheap ticket. When it was closed, that shared space was lost, and the town centre felt emptier because of it. Therefore, when a small group of volunteers announced a plan to rescue the building, hundreds of people signed up within a week, and donations were collected in shops, schools and cafés across the region.

The opera house was built in 1890 by a famous local architect. For decades it was admired across the whole region; concerts were performed there every week, whereas today most music is streamed online. Nevertheless, by the 1980s the theatre had been neglected for years. The roof had been damaged by winter storms, and the beautiful interior had been ruined by damp.

The decline had not happened overnight. In its golden age, the hall had been filled to the last seat, and famous singers had been invited from across Europe. Gradually, however, audiences shrank, ticket money dried up, and repairs were postponed year after year. Once the water had been allowed to seep through the roof, the damage spread quickly: plaster crumbled, the gold paint flaked, and the great chandelier had to be taken down before it could fall. By the time the doors were finally locked, the building that had once been the pride of the town was considered too expensive to save.

When the restoration finally began, hundreds of problems had to be solved. The roof had to be rebuilt completely, and every wall needed to be checked for safety. Original paint was discovered under later layers; therefore, experts decided that the colours should be returned to their 1890 design. Although the work was slow and expensive, it was never rushed.

The restoration turned out to be a journey into the past. As the later layers were carefully removed, the workers uncovered delicate patterns that had been hidden for a century, and old letters were even found beneath the stage floor. Skilled craftspeople were brought in to copy the original mouldings by hand, because machine-made copies would not be accepted. Every piece of timber had to be treated against damp, and the enormous chandelier was cleaned crystal by crystal. Wherever possible, the original materials were preserved rather than replaced, so that the building would keep its true character.

None of this could be done cheaply. Grants had to be applied for, and each stage of the work had to be approved by the local council before it could continue. Moreover, strict rules were introduced on the site: hard hats must be worn at all times, and nothing could be touched until it had been inspected. Volunteers helped in other ways too — archives were sorted, fundraising concerts were organised in the town square, and the story of the theatre was told again and again until almost everyone in the region felt part of it.

Since the project was announced, thousands of tickets have been requested. It is said that the acoustics are now better than ever, and the opening night has already been sold out. Moreover, the theatre must be protected for future generations, so a special trust has been created to look after it. The work is not quite finished yet, yet the main hall is ready.

Not everyone had believed the rescue was possible. 'We were told the theatre could not be saved, and that it would have to be pulled down,' remembers one long-time resident who joined the campaign. 'Nevertheless, we refused to give up. Now my granddaughter will sit in the same seats where I first heard an orchestra, and that is something money cannot buy.' Stories like hers have been repeated in newspapers and online, and they have helped to keep public support strong throughout the long years of work.

Plans for the reopening are already being prepared. A season of concerts has been arranged, and local schools will be given free tickets so that a new generation can experience live music in the hall. The building will also be maintained regularly from now on, and a small museum is being created in the foyer to explain how the rescue was carried out. Although a few finishing touches still remain, the trust is confident that everything will have been completed before the first performance.

'A building like this should never be allowed to fall apart,' says the head of the trust. 'It has been given a second life, and it will be enjoyed by our grandchildren.'

For the people who fought to save it, the lesson is simple. When a place is loved and looked after, it can be kept alive for hundreds of years; when it is neglected, even the finest building can be lost in a single generation. The old opera house was very nearly lost, yet it was rescued in time. Now, as the lights are switched on once more, it stands as proof of what a determined community can achieve together.

Writing: Describing a Process or a Place

Write and submit your paragraph; your teacher will review it.

Connectors that sound B2 (Linking Words)

At B1 you linked ideas with and, but, so, because. At B2 you sound noticeably more advanced when you use formal, precise connectors. Four of them are essential this unit — and two familiar ones, although and however, become sharper tools.

ConnectorUse it to...Example
moreoveradd a stronger, extra pointThe roof was repaired. Moreover, new cameras were installed.
thereforeshow a logical resultThe paint was damaged; therefore, the walls had to be restored.
whereascontrast two ideas directlyThe exterior was repaired fast, whereas the interior took years.
neverthelesssay "in spite of that"The work was expensive. Nevertheless, it was never rushed.
althoughconcede a point inside one sentenceAlthough it had been neglected, the building was saved.
howevercontrast between two sentencesIt stood empty for years. However, it has now been restored.

See the difference — the same facts, written two ways:

Basic linking ✗Confident B2 writing ✓
The station is old. It is being renovated. The roof was fixed. The platforms are not finished. It will open next year. The old station is being renovated. Although the roof has already been fixed, the platforms have not been finished yet; therefore, the work will continue. Nevertheless, it will be reopened next year.
Rule
  • Use at least 3 different B2 connectors from the table. Do not write sentence by sentence; connect your ideas.
  • Moreover, Therefore, However, Nevertheless usually start a sentence and take a comma: "However, it has now been restored."

Paragraph structure (Paragraph Structure)

A "process or place" paragraph has three parts. The skeleton stays the same — you change the content, not the structure:

PartWhat to writeExample
1. OpeningA topic sentence introducing the place or processThe old market hall in my town is being completely renovated.
2. BodyDescribe the steps or history with passives — present simple for facts, perfect passives for results, modal passives for rules. Link with B2 connectors.The roof has already been repaired, whereas the floor has not been finished yet.
3. ClosingA final comment about now or the futureWhen it is reopened, the hall will be enjoyed by the whole town.

WHAT TO INCLUDE

Sample Answer (~185 words) The old market hall in the centre of my town is being completely renovated, and the change is remarkable. The hall was built in 1905 and was once admired by everyone, but for many years it had been neglected. The roof had been damaged by rain, and part of the wall had to be rebuilt.

The work has been organised very carefully. First, the whole structure was inspected by engineers; therefore, the weakest sections were repaired at once. Moreover, the original stone was cleaned rather than replaced, because the council insisted that the building should be preserved, not modernised. Although the project is expensive, it has been approved by everyone.

Today the roof has already been finished, whereas the interior is still being decorated. New lighting has been installed, and strict rules now apply: bags must be left at the entrance, and nothing may be touched until the opening.

The hall has not been reopened yet. Nevertheless, when the work is complete, it will be enjoyed by the whole town for generations to come.
