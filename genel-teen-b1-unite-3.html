Ünite 3 · Past Perfect · Bölüm 1/7

Grammar: The Past Perfect

Study the rules, look at the examples, then do the exercises.

A "past before the past"

You already know the Past Simple for finished actions (I arrived, she left, they finished). But real stories rarely happen in a neat line. Sometimes, when you talk about a moment in the past, you also want to point to an even earlier moment. For that "past before the past", English has a special tense: the Past Perfect (had + V3).

Here is the core idea in one line: when two things happened in the past, the Past Perfect marks the one that happened first, and the Past Simple marks the one that happened second.

  • When I arrived at the station, the train had left.First the train left (Past Perfect), then I arrived (Past Simple).
  • She was tired because she had studied all day.First she studied (earlier), then she felt tired.
  • By the time the film started, we had found our seats.Finding the seats came before the film started.

1. The Past Perfect: form (had + V3)

We build the Past Perfect with had (the same for every subject) plus the past participle (the "third form", V3). Regular verbs add -ed (worked, arrived, finished). Irregular verbs keep their special V3 (go → gone, leave → left, see → seen, write → written, break → broken).

SubjectPositiveNegativeQuestion
I / you / we / theyhad lefthadn't leftHad you left?
he / she / ithad lefthadn't leftHad she left?
Key point
  • had never changes for the subject — it is the same for I, you, he, she, we, they. Only the main verb (the V3) carries the meaning.
  • In speech we usually contract it: I had finished → I'd finished; they had gone → they'd gone.

2. When do we use it? (the earlier action)

Use the Past Perfect when you are already talking about the past and you want to "step back" to something that happened before that point. Think of a timeline: the Past Perfect action sits to the left (earlier); the Past Simple action sits to the right (later).

SituationEarlier action (Past Perfect)Later action (Past Simple)
Reason for a past stateI had forgotten my keys,so I waited outside.
A finished action before anotherAfter we had eaten,we watched a film.
Something already true at a past momentBy 2010 they had movedto a new city. (already true by then)
Reporting an earlier pastHe told me he had seen the filmthe week before.
Golden rule
  • If both actions are in the past and you want to show which came first, put that first action in the Past Perfect.
  • If there is only one past action, you do not need the Past Perfect — the Past Simple is enough.

3. Time words that help you (before, after, by the time, already, just)

Certain time words often appear with the Past Perfect because they naturally connect two past moments. Learn them well — they are strong clues in exam questions.

WordUseExample
beforethe earlier action ends before the later oneShe had finished lunch before the class started.
afterthe later action follows the earlier oneAfter he had locked the door, he left.
by the time"not later than this moment" — earlier action already completeBy the time we arrived, the guests had gone.
alreadysooner than expected (goes after "had")When I called, she had already left.
justa very short time before (goes after "had")The train had just left when I reached the platform.
Watch the word order
  • already and just go between had and the V3: I had already eaten, they had just arrived.
  • With by the time, the "by the time" clause is Past Simple and the main clause is Past Perfect: By the time it rained, we had gone inside.

4. The key contrast (Past Perfect vs Past Simple order)

This is the heart of the unit. Look at how one small change of tense changes the whole meaning:

SentenceWhat really happened?
When I arrived, the train had left.First the train left, THEN I arrived. I missed it.
When I arrived, the train left.I arrived, and THEN (right after) the train left. I saw it go.
When she called, I had cooked dinner.Dinner was ready before her call.
When she called, I cooked dinner.Her call came first; then I started cooking.
Remember
  • Past Perfect = the action was already complete at that past moment.
  • Past Simple + Past Simple = the two actions happened one after the other, in the order you say them.

5. Common mistakes (✗ / ✓)

These are the errors B1 students make most often with the Past Perfect. Check your own writing against this list.

Wrong ✗Right ✓Why?
When I arrived, the train has left.When I arrived, the train had left.an earlier past inside a past story → had, not has.
I had ate before they came.I had eaten before they came.use the V3 (past participle), not the past simple.
She had went home early.She had gone home early.the V3 of "go" is "gone", not "went".
I had just already left.I had already left. / I had just left.use one adverb between had and the V3, not two.
Yesterday I had gone to school.Yesterday I went to school.only one past action → Past Simple is enough.

6. Putting it together (Model Dialogue)

Two friends talk about a difficult morning. Read it twice and notice how the Past Perfect (had done) points back to an earlier moment, while the Past Simple (did) carries the main story forward.

  • — You looked stressed this morning. What happened?Main past events → Past Simple.
  • — My alarm didn't ring because the battery had died during the night.Earlier cause → Past Perfect.
  • — Oh no! So by the time you woke up, you had already missed the bus?By the time + already + Past Perfect.
  • — Exactly. When I got to the stop, it had just left.just + Past Perfect = a moment earlier.
  • — How did you get to school, then?Question about the main story → Past Simple.
  • — Luckily my neighbour hadn't left yet, so he gave me a lift.Negative Past Perfect (still not gone), then Past Simple.
Test yourself
  • Find one sentence with by the time. Which tense is in the main clause? (Past Perfect.)
  • "When I got to the stop, it had just left." Why Past Perfect and not Past Simple? (The bus left before I got there.)
  • Change "the battery had died" into a negative. (the battery hadn't died.)

Reading: The Audition She Almost Missed

Read the story, then answer the questions.

Before You Read (Okumadan Önce)

Good readers prepare before they start. Before you read, think about these three questions (you do not need to write the answers):

  • Have you ever had a very stressful morning? What went wrong?
  • The text is a short story about a past event. Which tense do you expect to see for the actions that happened earlier?
  • Look at the title, "The Audition She Almost Missed". Do you think it ends well or badly? Guess!
How to read
  • First read: read quickly, without stopping. Follow the story; you do not need every word.
  • Second read: read the questions, then search the text for the answers. Notice each Past Perfect ("had done") and ask yourself which action happened first.

Words to spot (from the Speaking section): realise · previous · already · suddenly · recognise · prepare · nervous · arrive

A morning that went wrong — step by step along the timeline.
The Audition She Almost Missed

Last spring, Selin had one of the most stressful mornings of her life. She had prepared for weeks for an important drama audition at school, and she did not want anything to go wrong. The previous night, she had ironed her best clothes, learned her lines, and set two alarms.

For Selin, this was not just any ordinary audition. She had joined the school drama club two years earlier, and ever since that first meeting she had dreamed of playing a leading part on the big stage in the main hall. Her best friend, Deniz, had tried out for smaller roles with her many times before, yet Selin had never felt so ready in her life. During the previous month she had watched famous plays online, copied the actors' voices in front of her bedroom mirror, and repeated the same difficult lines until she knew every one of them by heart. Almost everyone in the club had told her that, this year, the main role was finally hers to win.

By the time she climbed into bed, Selin had done everything she could possibly think of. She had packed her school bag, laid out her shoes by the door, and even sent a short message to Deniz: "I've practised so much that I can't wait for tomorrow!" She had also promised herself that she would not open any social media apps in the morning, because she knew how easily a few funny videos could steal a whole hour. Before she finally fell asleep, she had read through her lines one last time, and she had felt completely sure that nothing in the world could stop her now.

However, when she woke up, she realised that something had gone terribly wrong. Her phone was silent because the battery had died during the night, and both alarms had failed. It was already 8:40, and the audition started at nine.

For a few seconds she just stared at the dark, silent screen and could not believe her eyes. She had charged that phone hundreds of times before, and yet the night before, after so much practice, she had been so tired that she had simply forgotten to plug it in. When she finally managed to turn it on, she saw that Deniz had already sent her four worried messages, including "Where are you?" and "The teacher keeps asking for you!" Selin's heart was beating fast, because she had never once been late for something this important.

Selin jumped out of bed. She had never dressed so quickly in her life. She ran to the bus stop, but by the time she got there, the bus had already left. For a moment she wanted to cry, because she had worked so hard for this chance. Then she suddenly remembered that her neighbour, Mr. Kaya, drove past the school every morning.

As she stood alone at the empty bus stop, the whole journey to school suddenly felt impossible. The street was quiet, she could not recognise a single face among the few people walking by, and not one taxi appeared around the corner. Then, almost like a small miracle, she pictured Mr. Kaya's old grey car, which she had seen leaving his driveway at exactly the same time on so many other mornings. Without thinking twice, she turned around and ran straight back towards her own building.

Luckily, he had not left yet. He kindly offered her a lift, and they arrived at the school at 8:58. When Selin walked in, she was still nervous, but the drama teacher smiled warmly. He explained that the previous student had cancelled, so there was no rush after all.

Inside the busy hall, Deniz was waiting nervously by the door and waved the moment she recognised her friend's face. "I honestly thought you had given up on us!" she whispered, and the two girls laughed together with pure relief. Selin had imagined this exact moment for weeks and weeks, and now, at last, it had truly arrived. When the teacher finally called her name and the room went quiet, she stepped onto the stage and calmly began the very lines she had practised so many hundreds of times at home.

Selin took a deep breath, and all her worries disappeared. By the end of the audition, she felt calm and confident. Two days later, the teacher told her the good news: they had chosen her for the main role. She had learned an important lesson — always charge your phone!

That evening, once she was safely back home, Selin posted a short clip from the rehearsal room with the simple caption, "Never give up before you have even started." Within a single hour, her classmates had filled the comments with kind and funny words, and Deniz had proudly shared the post with the whole drama club. Looking back on that crazy morning, Selin realised that one tiny thing — a phone she had forgotten to charge — had almost cost her the biggest opportunity of the entire school year. From that day on, before every important morning, she checked her battery twice, asked her parents to wake her as well, and never again trusted a single alarm on its own.

Writing: A Story With a Twist

Write and submit your story; your teacher will review it.

Connectors that order a story (Time Linkers)

A story is not just a list of actions — it has an order in time. What shows that order is your time linkers. At A2 you used and, but, so, because. At B1, when you write a narrative in the Past Perfect, you add powerful sequencing words: before, after, by the time, already, when and however. They tell the reader clearly what happened first.

LinkerUse it to...Example
beforeshow the earlier actionI had finished my breakfast before the taxi came.
aftershow the later actionAfter I had packed, I called a taxi.
by the timeshow an action was already completeBy the time we arrived, the show had started.
alreadysay it happened sooner than expectedThe shop had already closed.
whenmark the main past momentWhen I got home, they had gone to bed.
thenmove the story forwardI found my keys. Then I opened the door.
howevershow a contrast between sentencesI had planned everything. However, things went wrong.
luckilyintroduce a piece of good fortuneLuckily, my friend had not left yet.

See the difference — the same events, written two ways:

Separate sentences ✗Connected, ordered writing ✓
I got to the cinema. The film started. I did not find my ticket. I looked in my bag. It was at home. By the time I got to the cinema, the film had already started. However, I could not find my ticket, because I had left it at home.
Rule
  • Use at least 3 different connectors in your story. Do not write action by action; order your ideas in time.
  • When by the time / when / however / after start a sentence, they are usually followed by a comma: "However, things went wrong."

Story structure (Story Structure)

A short narrative has three parts. The skeleton stays the same every time — you change the content, not the structure:

PartWhat to writeExample
1. Set the sceneSay when and where it happened, and what you had prepared or planned before.Last summer, I had planned a perfect trip to the coast.
2. The problemSomething goes wrong. Use the Past Perfect to explain the earlier cause, and the Past Simple for the main events.When I reached the station, I realised that I had left my ticket at home.
3. The endingHow it was solved and how you felt.Luckily, a kind man had found it and returned it, so my day was saved.

Sample Answer (~168 words) Last summer, I had planned a perfect day trip to the coast with two friends. The previous evening, I had prepared everything: I had charged my camera, packed my bag, and printed the train tickets. I felt calm and organised, and I went to bed early.

However, the next morning things went wrong. When I arrived at the station, I suddenly realised that I had left the tickets on my desk at home. By the time I understood my mistake, the train had already left, and the next one was two hours later. I felt nervous and a little angry with myself, because I had checked my bag twice the night before.

Luckily, my friend had brought her phone, so we bought new tickets online. After we had calmed down, we laughed about it. In the end, we still reached the beach, and it became one of the best journeys of the summer. I learned to check my pockets before I leave!
