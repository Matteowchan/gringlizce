Derece ve sonuç bildiren yapılar: so + sıfat, such + (a) + sıfat + isim, too (fazla — olumsuz sonuç), enough (yeterli — sıfattan sonra, isimden önce). Türkçedeki "çok / o kadar / fazla / yeterince" ayrımına yakındır.

So, such, too, enough are small but very common words that express degree and result. In English they make the Turkish "çok / o kadar / fazla / yeterince" distinction, but their rules differ from Turkish: so goes before an adjective/adverb (so hot), such goes before a noun phrase (such a hot day), too means "more than necessary — negative" (too hot to sleep), enough, with the meaning "sufficient", comes after an adjective but before a noun (warm enough / enough money). The Turkish learner's biggest trap is confusing too with very and getting the position of enough wrong. This topic is closely connected to Adjective Order and Comparatives & Superlatives.

Bir şeyin derecesini belirtmek — yani "ne kadar" sıcak, "ne kadar" güzel, "ne kadar" yorucu olduğunu söylemek — dilin en temel ihtiyaçlarından biridir. Türkçede bunu tek bir kelimeyle, çok, hallederiz: çok sıcak, çok güzel bir gün, fazla pahalı, yeterince büyük. İngilizce ise bu "çok"u dört farklı kalıba böler: so, such, too ve enough. Hepsi bir dereceyi güçlendirir (bu yüzden bunlara intensifier — güçlendirici denir) ama her biri farklı bir yerde ve farklı bir anlamla kullanılır.

Bu kelimeler neden var? Çünkü İngilizce, "çok"un arkasına ne geldiğine (sıfat mı, isim mi) ve hangi anlamı (sadece yoğunluk mu, yoksa istenmeyen aşırılık mı, yoksa yeterlilik mi) taşıdığına göre ayrı kelime seçer. so ve such tarafsız yoğunluk verir ("o kadar"); too her zaman bir sorun, bir engel ima eder ("gereğinden fazla"); enough ise bir eşiğin karşılandığını söyler ("yeterince").

It's so hot today.
Bugün çok sıcak.
so + sıfat: sadece yoğunluk, olumlu ya da nötr.
It's such a hot day.
Öyle sıcak bir gün ki.
such + a + sıfat + isim: aynı yoğunluk ama isim öbeğiyle.
It's too hot to go outside.
Dışarı çıkılamayacak kadar sıcak.

Türk öğrenci için bu konu neden zor? Üç sebepten. Birincisi, Türkçedeki tek "çok" dört yapıya bölündüğü için hangisini seçeceğimizi düşünmek gerekir. İkincisi, enough Türkçedeki "yeterince"nin aksine sıfattan sonra gelir (good enough, "yeterince iyi" değil kelime sırası olarak "iyi yeterince"). Üçüncüsü, too ile very Türkçede çoğu zaman aynı "çok" ile çevrildiği için karıştırılır — oysa too her zaman bir problem ima eder, very etmez. Bu üç tuzağı bu derste tek tek işaretleyeceğiz.

Expressing the degree of something — that is, saying "how" hot, "how" beautiful, "how" tiring it is — is one of language's most basic needs. In Turkish we handle this with a single word, çok: çok sıcak, çok güzel bir gün, fazla pahalı, yeterince büyük. English, however, splits this "çok" into four different patterns: so, such, too and enough. They all intensify a degree (which is why they are called intensifiers) but each is used in a different place and with a different meaning.

Why do these words exist? Because English chooses a separate word depending on what follows the "çok" (an adjective or a noun) and which meaning it carries (just intensity, or an unwanted excess, or sufficiency). so and such give neutral intensity ("so much"); too always implies a problem, an obstacle ("more than necessary"); enough says that a threshold has been met ("sufficiently").

It's so hot today.
Bugün çok sıcak.
so + adjective: only intensity, positive or neutral.
It's such a hot day.
Öyle sıcak bir gün ki.
such + a + adjective + noun: the same intensity but with a noun phrase.
It's too hot to go outside.
Dışarı çıkılamayacak kadar sıcak.
too: excess + negative result (we can't get out).
It's warm enough to swim.
Yüzülebilecek kadar sıcak / yeterince ılık.
enough: the threshold is met, we can swim.

Why is this topic hard for the Turkish learner? For three reasons. First, because the single Turkish "çok" splits into four structures, we have to think about which one to choose. Second, enough, unlike the Turkish "yeterince", comes after the adjective (good enough, in word order "iyi yeterince", not "yeterince iyi"). Third, too and very are confused because in Turkish they are often both translated with the same "çok" — yet too always implies a problem, while very does not. We will flag these three traps one by one in this lesson.

  • so = sıfat veya zarfın önüne gelir: so tired, so quickly (o kadar / çok). Arkasında isim YOKTUR.
  • such = isim öbeğinin önüne gelir: such a nice person, such good news (öyle / o kadar). Arkasında bir isim VARDIR.
  • Tekil sayılabilir isimde such a/an kullanılır: such a problem. Çoğul ve sayılamayanda a/an yok: such problems, such patience.
  • too = "gereğinden fazla", her zaman olumsuz/istenmeyen: too expensive (fazla pahalı — alamıyoruz).
  • too ile very aynı değildir: very hot nötr (çok sıcak), too hot problemli (fazla sıcak, bir engel var).
  • enough sıfat/zarftan SONRA gelir: tall enough, fast enough (yeterince uzun/hızlı).
  • enough isimden ÖNCE gelir: enough money, enough chairs (yeterli para/sandalye).
  • too...to ve enough...to mastarla birleşir: too tired to walk, strong enough to lift it.
  • Sonuç bildirmek için: so + sıfat + that ve such + isim + that: so cold that..., such a mess that....
  • Miktarla: so many (sayılabilir), so much (sayılamayan): so many people, so much noise.
  • so = goes before an adjective or adverb: so tired, so quickly (so / very). There is NO noun after it.
  • such = goes before a noun phrase: such a nice person, such good news (such / so). There IS a noun after it.
  • With a singular countable noun use such a/an: such a problem. With plurals and uncountables there is no a/an: such problems, such patience.
  • too = "more than necessary", always negative/unwanted: too expensive (too expensive — we can't buy it).
  • too is not the same as very: very hot is neutral (very hot), too hot is problematic (too hot, there is an obstacle).
  • enough comes AFTER an adjective/adverb: tall enough, fast enough (tall/fast enough).
  • enough comes BEFORE a noun: enough money, enough chairs (enough money/chairs).
  • too...to and enough...to combine with an infinitive: too tired to walk, strong enough to lift it.
  • To state a result: so + adjective + that and such + noun + that: so cold that..., such a mess that....
  • With quantity: so many (countable), so much (uncountable): so many people, so much noise.

such — isim öbeğiyle

such bir isim öbeğinin önüne gelir. Tekil sayılabilir isimde araya a/an girer. Formül: such + (a/an) + (sıfat) + isim.

It was such a boring lecture.
O kadar sıkıcı bir dersti ki.
such + a + sıfat + tekil isim.
They are such kind people.
Öyle nazik insanlar ki.
Çoğul isim → a/an yok.
She has such patience.
Öyle bir sabrı var ki.
Sayılamayan isim → a/an yok; sıfat da olmayabilir.

too — aşırılık (olumsuz sonuç)

too sıfat/zarfın önüne gelir ama so'dan farklı olarak "gereğinden fazla" der ve daima bir engel/olumsuz sonuç ima eder. Sık sık to + fiil ya da for + kişi ile tamamlanır. Formül: too + sıfat/zarf (+ to V / + for sb).

This bag is too heavy.
Bu çanta fazla ağır.
Bir sorun var: taşıyamıyoruz.
It's too dark to read here.

The dress was too small for her.
Elbise ona fazla küçüktü.
too + sıfat + for + kişi.

so — with adjectives and adverbs

so comes directly before an adjective or an adverb. A noun never follows it. Formula: so + adjective/adverb.

The exam was so difficult.
Sınav çok zordu / o kadar zordu ki.
so + adjective (difficult).
She spoke so quietly.
Öyle sessiz konuştu ki.
so + adverb (quietly).
I'm so happy for you!
Senin adına çok mutluyum!
Everyday, emotional emphasis.

such — with a noun phrase

such comes before a noun phrase. With a singular countable noun, a/an goes in between. Formula: such + (a/an) + (adjective) + noun.

It was such a boring lecture.
O kadar sıkıcı bir dersti ki.
such + a + adjective + singular noun.
They are such kind people.
Öyle nazik insanlar ki.
Plural noun → no a/an.
She has such patience.
Öyle bir sabrı var ki.
Uncountable noun → no a/an; there may be no adjective either.

too — excess (negative result)

too comes before an adjective/adverb but, unlike so, it means "more than necessary" and always implies an obstacle/negative result. It is often completed with to + verb or for + person. Formula: too + adjective/adverb (+ to V / + for sb).

This bag is too heavy.
Bu çanta fazla ağır.
There is a problem: we can't carry it.
It's too dark to read here.
Burada okumak için fazla karanlık.
too + adjective + to V (result: we can't read it).
The dress was too small for her.
Elbise ona fazla küçüktü.
too + adjective + for + person.

enough — sufficiency

enough's position depends on which word it comes with. It comes AFTER an adjective/adverb, but BEFORE a noun. This is the rule Turkish learners get wrong most often. Formulas: adjective/adverb + enough and enough + noun.

adjective (tall) + enough. 'enough tall' is WRONG.
We have enough chairs.
Yeterli sandalyemiz var.
enough + noun (chairs).

adverb (clearly) + enough + to V.

2) Sonuç bildirmek (so/such ... that)

Bir sebebin doğurduğu sonucu anlatmak için so + sıfat + that veya such + isim + that kurulur. "O kadar ... ki (şöyle oldu)" anlamı. Günlük dilde that sık sık düşer.

The music was so loud that we couldn't talk.
Müzik o kadar yüksekti ki konuşamadık.
so + sıfat + that + sonuç.
It was such a long queue that we gave up.
Öyle uzun bir kuyruktu ki vazgeçtik.
such + isim + that + sonuç.
I was so tired I fell asleep on the sofa.
O kadar yorgundum ki kanepede uyuyakaldım.
Günlük dilde 'that' düştü.
There was such a crowd that we lost each other.
Öyle bir kalabalık vardı ki birbirimizi kaybettik.

THROUGH TURKISH EYES

In Turkish, degree is built with a single word family: çok, o kadar, öyle, fazla, yeterince. These words usually come before the word they modify and make no adjective/noun distinction: çok güzel, çok güzel bir gün. English, however, splits the same "çok" into so and such depending on whether an adjective or a noun follows. Here is the trap: the learner thinking in Turkish assumes both mean "çok/o kadar" and continues with so, wrongly putting so before a noun.

  • The Turkish yeterince iyi order is "yeterince + adjective"; English, conversely, says adjective + enough (word-for-word "iyi yeterince"). This reversed order is the most frequent source of error.
  • The Turkish fazla and çok can often replace each other in the same sentence; in English too (excessive) and very are not interchangeable — they change the meaning.
  • The Turkish "öyle ... ki" and "o kadar ... ki" result pattern maps directly to English so/such ... that — so the logic is familiar, only the word choice differs.

Concept check Answer each question in your own words; then look at the sample answer.
  1. In the sentence 'The coffee is too hot', can we drink the coffee? Why?
    No. 'too' implies an obstacle; the coffee is too hot to drink.
  2. If I say 'The coffee is very hot', is there anything stopping me from drinking it?
    Not necessarily. 'very' only states the degree, it implies no obstacle; we can drink it carefully.
  3. When choosing between 'so' and 'such', what single question do you ask yourself?
    Is there a noun after it? If yes, such; if no, so.
  4. What is the meaning difference between 'She's old enough' and 'She's too old'?
    The first says a threshold has been met (old/big enough); the second says an excess, a problem (too old).
  5. Is 'enough money' or 'money enough' correct, and why?
    'enough money' is correct; when enough is used with a noun it comes before the noun.
  6. On what basis do you choose between 'too many' and 'too much'?
    If the noun is countable, many (too many people); if uncountable, much (too much water).

Where to start?
In the first lesson, reduce the so ↔ such distinction to a single question: "Is there a noun after it?" This is the rule students internalize fastest. The position rule of enough (after the adjective, before the noun) should be practised kinesthetically: put word cards on the board and have students physically order them. Leave the too vs very difference for last; because this semantic difference, if given before the form rules have settled, causes confusion.

A commonly observed problem: Students use too and very interchangeably because they map both to the Turkish "çok". To fix this, the contrast pair technique works: have them say the same adjective first with very then with too and ask "Which one has a problem?". The contrast between very hot (drink carefully) and too hot (you can't drink it) draws a clear line in the mind.

Concept-check question (CCQ) examples: "'It's too cold to swim.' — Are we swimming? (No)" · "'She's tall enough for the ride.' — Can she ride? (Yes)" · "'such a mess' — is there a noun here? (Yes, mess)". Such yes/no CCQs test meaning, not form.

Activity suggestion: "Complaint Chain" — students take turns producing a holiday/restaurant complaint; each sentence must contain too or not ... enough (The room was too small. The food wasn't hot enough.). Then have a round praising the same place with so/such. This reinforces the four structures at opposite poles within one context.

Akılda Tutma — iki kısayol
1) "İsim varsa suCH, sadece sıfatsa SO" — 'suCH' içindeki C'yi 'Countable/isim' diye hatırla. 2) enough sandviçi: sıfat-enough (enough arkada), enough-isim (enough önde). Kural: "enough sıfatı ARKADAN, ismi ÖNDEN kucaklar."
  • so = before an adjective/adverb (NO noun): so cold.
  • such = before a noun phrase (there IS a noun): such a cold day. In the singular don't forget 'a/an'.
  • too = 'more than necessary', always negative/an obstacle: too cold (to swim).
  • too ≠ very: very only gives degree, too implies a problem.
  • enough AFTER an adjective/adverb (cold enough), BEFORE a noun (enough time).
  • For a result: so + adjective + that, such + noun + that. In everyday language 'that' can drop.
  • Quantity: countable → so/too many; uncountable → so/too much.
  • Exception: so many/much/few/little + noun (so, not such).
  • The two most common mistakes: putting enough before the adjective and saying so with a noun.

Keep in Mind — two shortcuts
1) "If there's a noun, suCH; if just an adjective, SO" — remember the C in 'suCH' as 'Countable/noun'. 2) the enough sandwich: adjective-enough (enough behind), enough-noun (enough in front). Rule: "enough hugs the adjective from BEHIND, the noun from the FRONT."
