Ünite 10 · Causative · Bölüm 1/6

Grammar: The Causative

Learn how to say that you arrange for someone else to do something for you.

When someone else does the work

You do not repair your own car, cut your own hair, or rewire your own house — you arrange for a professional to do it. English has a special structure for exactly this idea: the causative. Instead of saying "A mechanic repaired my car," a fluent speaker says "I had my car repaired." The focus moves from the worker to you and the result.

This is one of the most useful everyday structures at B2. It lets you talk naturally about services, repairs, and delegation — and it also describes unpleasant things that happen to you ("I had my phone stolen").

  • I had my car repaired last week.A garage repaired it for me → have + object + past participle.
  • She got her hair cut yesterday.A hairdresser cut it → get + object + past participle (same meaning, more informal).
  • I had the mechanic check the brakes.Naming the person → have + person + base form.
  • I got him to help me move.Naming the person with get → get + person + to-infinitive.

1. The forms at a glance (quick map)

The causative comes in two families: one where you mention the thing (passive causative), and one where you mention the person who does the job.

StructurePatternExample
Passive causative (thing)have + object + past participleWe had the house painted.
Passive causative (informal)get + object + past participleWe got the house painted.
Active (person) with havehave + person + base verbWe had a builder paint it.
Active (person) with getget + person + to-infinitiveWe got a builder to paint it.
Key point
  • When the object is a thing, the verb is a past participle (V3): had the house painted.
  • When you name the person, use the base form after have but the to-infinitive after get: had a builder paint / got a builder to paint.

2. Passive causative: have / get something done

This is the most common form and the one you will use every day. You pay for or arrange a service, and you talk about the result, not the worker.

Ordinary sentenceCausativeWhy it is better
A dentist checked my teeth.I had my teeth checked.The worker is not important; the service is.
Someone will fix the roof.We are getting the roof fixed.Natural way to talk about arranging a job.
A shop is printing her a T-shirt.She is having a T-shirt printed.Works in any tense — just change 'have'.
Any tense — only 'have/get' changes
  • Present: I have my car serviced twice a year.
  • Past: I had my car serviced last month.
  • Future / going to: I am going to have my car serviced.
  • The past participle (serviced, cut, repaired) never changes.

3. Naming the person: have someone do vs get someone to do

Sometimes you want to say who did the job. Both have and get work — but they take a different verb form. This is the classic B2 trap.

VerbFollowed byExample
haveperson + base form (no 'to')I had the mechanic check the engine.
getperson + to-infinitiveI got the mechanic to check the engine.
Remember the difference
  • have + person + base: The teacher had us rewrite the essay. ✓
  • get + person + to: The teacher got us to rewrite the essay. ✓
  • Never mix them: "got us rewrite" ✗ / "had us to rewrite" ✗

4. Close cousins: make and let

Two more verbs follow a similar person + base-verb pattern, but with different meanings. Keep them separate from the causative of service.

VerbMeaningExample
make + person + baseforce someone to do somethingThey made me apologise.
let + person + baseallow someone to do somethingThey let me leave early.
have + person + basearrange / ask (neutral)They had me fill in a form.
Watch the 'to'
  • make and let take the base form in the active: made me wait, let me go — never "made me to wait" ✗
  • Only get uses the to-infinitive for the person.

5. Not always a service: experiences & misfortune

The passive causative also describes something that happened to you — often unpleasant — even though you did not arrange it. The form is identical: have + object + past participle.

  • He had his phone stolen on the bus.Something bad happened to him (he did not arrange it).
  • They had their match cancelled because of the storm.An event affected them → still have + object + V3.
  • She had her bag searched at the concert entrance.The action was done to her.
Two meanings, one form
  • Arranged service: I had my hair cut. (I chose to)
  • Misfortune: I had my hair pulled. (it happened to me)
  • The context, not the grammar, tells you which meaning is intended.

6. Common mistakes (✗ / ✓)

These are the errors B2 students make with the causative. Check your own sentences against every row.

Wrong ✗Right ✓Why?
I had my car repair.I had my car repaired.The object is a thing → past participle, not base.
I got the plumber fix the leak.I got the plumber to fix the leak.get + person needs the to-infinitive.
She had the artist to paint a mural.She had the artist paint a mural.have + person takes the base form, no 'to'.
They made me to sign it.They made me sign it.make + person + base form (no 'to').
I did cut my hair yesterday. (I paid for it)I had my hair cut yesterday.Use the causative when someone did it for you.

7. Putting it together (Model Dialogue)

Two friends talk about a bedroom that one of them is doing up as a little studio. Read it twice and watch the causative forms working together: passive causative for services, and 'get someone to' for people.

A:Your room looks amazing! Did you paint it yourself?A simple question — A assumes B did the work.
B:No way. I had it painted by a proper decorator.Passive causative → have + object + past participle.
A:And the shelves? Those look custom.Following up on another job.
B:They are. I got my uncle to build them.Naming the person with get → to-infinitive.
A:Smart. The old lights must have been a nightmare.Guessing about a hard job.
B:They were faulty, so I had them replaced first. Oh — and last week I had a parcel delivered to the wrong address!Service, then misfortune — same form.
Test yourself
  • Why is it "had it painted", not "had it paint"? (The object 'it' is a thing → past participle.)
  • Rewrite "I got my uncle to build them" with have. (I had my uncle build them.)
  • Is "had a parcel delivered to the wrong address" a service or a misfortune? (A misfortune — it happened to B.)

Good readers prepare before they start. Before you read, think about these three questions (you do not need to write the answers):

  • The text is about setting up a room for a hobby. Which causative forms (have/get something done) do you expect to see?
  • The title is "Getting It Done". Do you think the person does everything herself, or delegates? Guess!
How to read
  • First read: read quickly, without stopping. Get the general idea; you do not need every word.
  • Second read: read the questions, then search the text. Notice each causative — "had it done", "got someone to do" — and ask who did the job.

Getting It Done: How Deniz Set Up Her Studio Room

Deniz always dreamed of running her own photography channel, yet she knew almost nothing about decorating or editing. When her parents finally let her turn the spare room into a small studio last spring, the place was a complete mess. Therefore, instead of trying to do everything herself, she decided to get other people to help.

At school, Deniz was already the person her friends turned to whenever a class project needed to look professional. During her media course the previous year, she had had a dull history presentation completely redesigned by two classmates, and the teacher was so impressed that she had it shown to every other group as a model. That small success planted a bigger idea in her head. She wanted a proper channel, a real space where she could film interviews with students, cover school matches and review the books everyone was arguing about online. However, dreaming about a studio and actually building one, she soon realised, were two very different challenges.

First, she had the walls repainted in soft grey, and she got a carpenter to build the shelves along one side. Although the old lights were faulty, she did not panic; she simply had them replaced by a reliable electrician. Moreover, she got a friend who studies design to create her logo, whereas her cousin, who is good with computers, set up her website for free.

The practical side turned out to be the hardest part of all. Because none of her friends knew much about wiring or measurements, she learned quickly that it was wiser to trust people who genuinely did. She had the floor measured properly before ordering a deep blue rug online, and she got an older student from the school film club to lend her a spare microphone stand. Meanwhile, her younger brother, who is endlessly curious, kept asking to help, so she let him test the lighting while an adult supervised. Slowly the messy spare room started to feel like somewhere a real creator might actually work, and her confidence grew with every finished job.

Not everything went smoothly, however. Two weeks before her first big post, someone broke into the changing rooms at school, and she had her new camera stolen from her locker. Nevertheless, Deniz refused to give up. She had the lock replaced the next morning, and since her family's insurance covered most of the loss, she got another camera delivered within days.

The theft shook her far more than she admitted at first. For a few days she seriously thought about abandoning the whole idea, especially since a couple of classmates teased her, calling a school studio a childish dream. Her best friend, Aylin, disagreed completely. 'You have already got half the school talking about your channel,' she said firmly. 'Do not let one stolen camera decide your future.' Encouraged by those words, Deniz did something braver than simply replacing the equipment: she turned the setback into content. She filmed a short, honest video about the break-in and how she coped, and within a week it had become her most shared post yet.

That video taught her an important lesson about online life. Suddenly hundreds of strangers were leaving comments, and while most were kind, a few were unpleasant and even cruel. Rather than arguing with them at midnight, she had a simple rule agreed with her parents: any nasty message would be screenshotted, reported and then ignored. Moreover, she got the channel's privacy settings checked by her school's IT teacher, so that her home address and timetable could never be worked out from her clips. Managing an audience, she discovered, meant protecting yourself as carefully as you edited your videos, and she was determined to grow up sensibly rather than chase views at any cost.

These days Deniz rarely does the boring jobs herself. She has her photos checked by an online mentor every month, and she gets her equipment serviced twice a year. She still takes every photograph personally, though — that part she will never hand to anyone else.

Since then, her channel has grown in ways she never expected. She now coordinates a small team of volunteers from the debate club and the sports society, and each of them handles a task that suits their strengths. One friend, who plays basketball for the school, films short training clips that Deniz later edits into weekly highlights, whereas another, who loves writing, has her captions checked for spelling before anything goes live. She has even started getting her thumbnails designed by a younger student who is brilliant at drawing. Balancing homework, matches and uploads is genuinely exhausting; nevertheless, she insists that sharing the work with people she trusts is exactly what keeps the whole project alive.

Her teachers have noticed the change in her too. In debate lessons she now speaks with the calm confidence of someone used to a camera, and she recently had one of her interview clips entered into a regional student media competition by her media teacher. Although she did not win first place, the judges praised how clearly she explained complicated ideas, which she said meant more to her than any prize. When younger students ask her for advice, she always gives the same answer. Learn to do the important things yourself, she tells them, but never be too proud to have the rest done by people who are better at it than you are.

'You cannot do everything alone,' she says. 'A good creator knows exactly what to do herself and what to have done by others.'

Writing: A Project You Managed

Write and submit your paragraph; your teacher will review it.

Connectors that sound B2 (Linking Words)

At B1 you linked ideas with and, but, so, because. At B2 you sound noticeably more advanced when you use formal, precise connectors. Four of them are essential this unit — and two familiar ones, although and however, become sharper tools.

ConnectorUse it to...Example
moreoveradd a stronger, extra pointI had my bike fixed. Moreover, I got my room repainted.
thereforeshow a logical resultMy charger was faulty; therefore, I had it replaced.
whereascontrast two ideas directlyI did the painting myself, whereas I had the shelves put up.
neverthelesssay "in spite of that"It was expensive. Nevertheless, I hired a professional.
althoughconcede a point inside one sentenceAlthough I could not do it alone, I managed the project.
howevercontrast between two sentencesI planned to save money. However, I had to hire help.

See the difference — the same facts, written two ways:

Basic linking ✗Confident B2 writing ✓
I renovated my room. It was hard. I could not do it all. I asked people. It looks good now. I decided to renovate my room, but I could not do it all alone. Therefore, I had the walls repainted and got an electrician to install new lights. Although it was hard, the room looks wonderful now.
Rule
  • Use at least 3 different B2 connectors from the table. Do not write sentence by sentence; connect your ideas.
  • Moreover, Therefore, However, Nevertheless usually start a sentence and take a comma: "However, I hired help."

Paragraph structure (Paragraph Structure)

A "project you managed" paragraph has three parts. The skeleton stays the same — you change the content, not the structure:

PartWhat to writeExample
1. OpeningA topic sentence about the projectLast year I decided to do up my old bedroom.
2. BodyTell the story with causative forms. Use "have/get something done" for services and "get someone to do" for people. Link with B2 connectors.I had the walls repainted, and I got a carpenter to build the shelves. Nevertheless, I had a delivery sent to the wrong address.
3. ClosingA final comment about the result or the futureI have not finished decorating yet, therefore I still have plans.
