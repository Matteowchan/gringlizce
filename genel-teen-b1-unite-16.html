Ünite 16 · Determiners & Quantifiers · Bölüm 1/6

Grammar: Determiners & Quantifiers

Study the rules, look at the examples, then do the exercises.

From vague amounts to exact quantities

At A2 you used a few basic words to show amount: a lot of, many, some. That works for simple sentences, but real B1 English needs to be more precise. Determiners and quantifiers are the small words that come before a noun to show which one or how much / how many: all, most, some, any, no, both, each, every, (a) few, (a) little.

The most important skill is agreement. Some quantifiers go only with countable nouns (few, both, each, every), some only with uncountable nouns (little, much), and some with both (all, most, some, any, no). Choosing the wrong one is the most common B1 mistake, so we will focus on it carefully.

  • Most people recycle at home.most + plural countable noun → general amount.
  • I have very little free time.little + uncountable noun (time).
  • Each student has a locker.each + singular noun + singular verb.

1. General quantity (all / most / some / any / no / none)

These words show quantity from 100% down to 0%. They work with both countable (plural) and uncountable nouns. The tricky parts are some vs any and no vs none.

WordMeaning / useExample
all100% of the groupAll children need sleep.
mostthe biggest partMost people like music.
somea part (in positive sentences & offers)I bought some apples.
anyin questions & negativesIs there any milk? / We don't have any bread.
no + nounzero (with a positive verb)There is no time.
nonezero (a pronoun; stands alone)How many are left? None.
Key point
  • Use some in positive sentences and offers; use any in questions and negatives: "We have some sugar." / "We don't have any sugar."
  • no + noun takes a positive verb: "There is no sugar." (NOT "There isn't no sugar.")
  • none replaces the noun and stands alone: "None of them came."

2. A specific group (all / most / some ... of the)

Use a quantifier alone to talk about things in general, but add of the / of my / of these when you mean a specific, known group. Compare: "Most people..." (people in general) and "Most of the people at the party..." (a specific group).

General (no "of")Specific (+ of the)Note
Most students study hard.Most of the students in my class study hard.a known group → add "of the".
Some cheese is expensive.Some of the cheese was old.works the same with uncountable nouns.
All children play.All (of) the children were tired.after all / both, "of" is optional.
The rule that saves marks
  • Before the / my / these / us / them you must use of: "some of the water", "none of us".
  • After all and both, "of" is optional: "all (of) the students".
  • Never say "most of people" ✗ — either "most people" or "most of the people" ✓.

3. Two things (both / either / neither)

Use these three only when you talk about two things or people. The verb form is the key: both is plural, but either and neither are singular.

WordMeaningStructure & example
boththe two togetherBoth restaurants are good. (both + plural + plural verb)
eitherone OR the otherEither day is fine. (either + singular + singular verb)
neithernot one and not the otherNeither answer is correct. (neither + singular + singular verb)
Watch the verb
  • both + plural noun + plural verb: "Both shops are open."
  • either / neither + singular noun + singular verb: "Neither shop is open."
  • Pairs: both...and, either...or, neither...nor: "I like both tea and coffee." / "Neither Ali nor Sam came."

4. Every individual member (each / every)

Both talk about all the members of a group, but seen one by one. They are followed by a singular noun and a singular verb — this surprises many students.

WordFocusExample
eachthe members one by one (2 or more)Each player gets a card.
everyall members as a group (3 or more)Every player was tired.
each of the + pluralone by one, a specific groupEach of the players was ready.
Singular, always
  • each / every + singular noun + singular verb: "Every room has a window." (NOT "Every rooms have")
  • Use each of the / my + plural noun: "Each of my brothers lives abroad."
  • For only two things, use each, not every: "She had a bag in each hand."

5. Small amounts ((a) few / (a) little)

These four are easy to mix up, but two questions decide everything: is the noun countable or uncountable, and is there "a" or not?

WordGoes withMeaning
a fewcountable pluralsome — a small but positive amount
fewcountable pluralnot many — almost none (negative)
a littleuncountablesome — a small but positive amount
littleuncountablenot much — almost none (negative)
The "a" changes the feeling
  • "I have a few friends here." = some friends (positive). "I have few friends here." = almost none (negative).
  • "There is a little milk." = some milk. "There is little milk." = almost no milk.
  • few / a few + countable (books); little / a little + uncountable (water). Never "a few water" ✗ or "a little books" ✗.

6. Common mistakes (✗ / ✓)

Almost every B1 mistake with quantifiers comes from wrong agreement (countable / uncountable) or a missing or extra "of".

Wrong ✗Right ✓Why?
Every students have a book.Every student has a book.every + singular noun + singular verb.
I don't have some money.I don't have any money.use "any" in negatives, not "some".
Most of people like it.Most people like it.no "of" without "the"; or "most of the people".
There isn't no bread.There is no bread."no" already means zero; don't add a second negative.
I have a little friends.I have a few friends.friends is countable → use "a few".
Both my sister play basketball.Both my sisters play basketball.both + plural noun + plural verb.

7. Putting it together (Model Dialogue)

Two friends check the kitchen before they cook dinner together. Read it twice and notice how each quantifier fits its noun — singular after each / every, plural after both / a few, and an uncountable noun after a little.

  • — Do we have any milk? — There's a little, but not much.any in a question; a little + uncountable (milk).
  • — How many eggs? — Only a few. Most of them are gone.a few + countable (eggs); most of + them.
  • — Both shops near us are open, so either one is fine.both + plural verb; either + singular noun.
  • — Let's check each shelf. Every jar here is empty!each / every + singular noun + singular verb.
  • — We need some bread, but we don't need any rice.some in a positive sentence; any in a negative.
  • — None of the vegetables are fresh. Let's buy all of them again.none of the (zero); all of them (100%).
Test yourself
  • Why is it "a little milk" and not "a few milk"? (Milk is uncountable, so we use a little.)
  • Change "Every jar is empty" to talk about a specific group with each of. (Each of the jars is empty.)
  • Why "any rice" in "we don't need any rice"? (It is a negative sentence, so we use any, not some.)

Speaking: How Much We Use, Waste & Share

Learn the words, then use them — with quantifiers — to talk about your habits.

quantity (n)
how much or how many of something there is (miktar)
Syn: amount · Ant: —
"We throw away a large quantity of food."
amount (n)
how much of something (usually uncountable) (miktar)
Syn: quantity · Ant: —
"A small amount of water is enough."
plenty (pron)
a large amount; more than enough (bol, yeterinden fazla)
Syn: a lot · Ant: hardly any
"There is plenty of bread for everyone."
enough (det)
as much or as many as you need (yeterli)
Syn: sufficient · Ant: too little
"Some people do not have enough food."
several (det)
more than two but not very many (birkaç, birçok)
Syn: a few · Ant: none
"I visited several shops before I chose one."
various (adj)
of many different kinds (çeşitli, farklı)
Syn: different · Ant: identical
"The fridge holds various kinds of food."
majority (n)
most of a group; more than half (çoğunluk)
Syn: most · Ant: minority
"The majority of my classmates help out."
spare (adj)
extra; not being used at the moment (fazladan, yedek)
Syn: extra · Ant: needed
"If you have spare food, please share it."
donate (v)
to give something to help others (bağışlamak)
Syn: give · Ant: keep
"A bakery donates some bread every day."
reduce (v)
to make something smaller or less (azaltmak)
Syn: cut down · Ant: increase
"We try to reduce the amount of waste."
waste (v/n)
to use badly or throw away for no reason (israf etmek; atık)
Syn: throw away · Ant: save
"Most students waste a little food each week."
share (v)
to give a part of something to others (paylaşmak)
Syn: split · Ant: keep for yourself
"They share all of the food fairly."
container (n)
a box or jar used to hold or store things (kap, kutu)
Syn: box, jar · Ant: —
"Each container has a date on it."
neighbour (n)
a person who lives near you (komşu)
Syn: local resident · Ant: stranger
"Both of my neighbours grow vegetables."
Talking about quantities: use all, most, some, a few, a little and none.

Production Task (Üretim Görevi)

Write 8 sentences about how much of different things (food, money, time, water...) you use, waste or share. Use at least 8 of the words above.

Grammar requirement — apply this unit's topic
  • Use all / most / some / any / no / none for general amounts, and remember: a few + countable, a little + uncountable.
  • Use each / every with a singular noun and verb, and both / either / neither for two things.
  • Write at least 2 sentences with "of the": one about a specific group. (Örn: "Most of the food in my fridge is fresh. None of my friends waste water.")

Sentence starters (you can begin with these):

"Every week, I ..." "Most of my ..." "I waste only a little ..." "Both of my ..." "None of us ..."

Reading: The Snack Share Shelf

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Before You Read (Okumadan Önce)

Good readers prepare before they start. Before you read, think about these three questions (you do not need to write the answers):

  • Think about how much food you and your friends throw away at school in a week. Could any of it be shared?
  • The title is "The Snack Share Shelf". What do you think a shelf for spare snacks at school is for?
  • This unit is about quantifiers. As you read, underline every all, most, some, each, a few and a little you find.
How to read
  • First read: read quickly, without stopping. Get the general idea; you do not need every word.
  • Second read: read the questions, then search the text for the answers. Notice how each quantifier fits its noun (countable or uncountable).

Words to spot (from the Speaking section): quantity · amount · plenty · spare · donate · reduce · waste · share

From a few spare snacks to a shelf that feeds the whole school.
The Snack Share Shelf at Green Valley School

A few months ago, the students of Green Valley School had a problem. Every day, most students brought too much food and threw a lot of it away, while some of their classmates came to school with no lunch at all. One lunchtime, two friends, Deniz and Ayla, decided that neither of them wanted to watch this waste continue. They shared a simple idea: a shelf where anyone in the school could leave spare snacks and anyone could take them.

At first, few students believed the plan would work. There was very little money for boxes, and none of the teachers wanted to help. Even so, the two friends did not give up. They asked each class to bring a little time or a few snacks. Slowly, both the shelf and the trust between students began to grow.

Once the shelf had a few regular visitors, Deniz and Ayla wanted more of the school to know about it. Neither of them enjoyed speaking in front of a big crowd, so they looked for an easier way to share the news. They designed a small, colourful poster and posted a photo of it in their class group chat online. Within a few days, several older students had shared the same photo with their own friends, and soon most of the school had heard about the plan. A few teachers who had said no at the start slowly began to change their minds, and a little quiet support started to arrive from almost every corner of the building.

Today, the shelf stands next to the library. Every morning, two volunteers check it, and each item is dated to keep the food safe. Most of the fruit comes from students' gardens, and some of the bread arrives from a bakery near the school. If you look at the shelf, you will almost never find it empty; there is always at least a little of something for everyone.

The shelf is now popular with almost everyone in the school. After each football match, players who have spare fruit or an extra bottle of water leave it on the shelf instead of throwing it away. Students from the art club and the music club stop by too, because a quick snack helps them focus during long afternoon practice. On busy days, most of the sandwiches disappear within an hour, but there is nearly always a little fruit or a few biscuits left for anyone who arrives late. Both younger and older students use it, and neither group feels shy about taking what they really need. The school even added a second, smaller shelf near the gym so that no team ever leaves its snacks too far away.

The rules are easy. Take only what you need, and leave some for others. Both giving and taking are welcome, and either action helps the whole school. There is no charge, and no one is ever turned away. Several students now bring any spare food instead of wasting it.

Of course, running a shelf like this is not always easy. On some days, too much food arrives and a little of it goes bad before anyone takes it. On other days there is hardly any, especially just before a long holiday. To solve these problems, the volunteers wrote a few simple extra rules for weekends: no fresh food is left out on Fridays, and every container must show a clear date. A few students even keep a small notebook in which each person can note down which snacks are most needed. Little by little, the whole school has learned that planning ahead saves both food and time.

News of the shelf did not stay inside the school gates. A few neighbours who live near Green Valley heard about it and offered to help. One of them, a baker, now leaves a little extra bread by the door most mornings, and another gives away spare vegetables from her garden every Saturday. At the weekend, some students turn the shelf into a small hobby of their own: they decorate the boxes, draw funny signs, and film short clips for the school social media page. None of this brings in any money, yet each small act makes the shelf feel like it belongs to the whole neighbourhood, not just a few classes.

The project has also changed the way many teenagers see one another. Before the shelf, few students talked to classmates outside their own small group. Now, most of them share a quick smile, a joke or a snack at break time, and a few new friendships have started right next to the library. Some students who were once very shy say they feel braver, because helping others is easier than they expected. Ayla says that giving away spare food feels far better than wasting it, and Deniz adds that even a little kindness can spread across a whole school.

All of the volunteers agree on one thing: none of this would have happened without a few determined students and a lot of patience. The project has reduced the amount of food that Green Valley wastes, and the majority of students say they feel closer than ever before.

Writing: Wasting Less, Sharing More

Write and submit your paragraph; your teacher will review it.

Quantifiers make writing precise (Quantifiers)

Weak B1 writing repeats the same vague words (a lot of, many) or lists short, separate sentences. What makes writing mature is precise, correct quantity. This unit gives you a full set of quantifiers for general amounts, two things, individuals and small amounts. Use them — and remember the golden rule: match the quantifier to its noun.

QuantifierUse it to...Example
all / most / someshow a general amountMost of my friends recycle.
no / noneshow zeroNone of us wastes water on purpose.
both / either / neithertalk about two thingsBoth my parents grow vegetables.
each / everytalk about individualsEvery family can help a little.
(a) few / (a) littleshow a small amountI waste a little food and only a few things.
... of thepoint to a specific groupSome of the food in my fridge is old.

See the difference — the same facts, written two ways:

Vague & separate ✗Precise, fluent writing ✓
I have food. I do not eat it all. I throw food away. My family is the same. Every week my family buys a lot of food, but we do not eat all of it. Most of it is fine, yet a little is wasted, and none of us likes throwing it away.
Rule
  • Use at least 3 different quantifiers from this unit. Do not repeat "a lot of" everywhere.
  • Remember: a few + countable, a little + uncountable; each / every + singular noun and verb; add of the only for a specific group.

Paragraph structure (Paragraph Structure)

A "quantity" paragraph has three parts. The skeleton stays the same every time — you change the content, not the structure:

PartWhat to writeExample
1. OpeningName your topic in one general sentenceEvery week my family uses a large amount of food and water.
2. BodyHow much you use, waste and share. Use general amounts (all / most / some / none), two things (both / either / neither) and small amounts (a few / a little).Most of the vegetables come from our garden, but a little food is still wasted. Both of my parents try to reduce it.
3. ClosingWhat you have learned or want to changeNow none of us wastes as much, and we share any spare food.

Task (Görev)

Write about how much of different things (food, money, time, water...) you and your family use, waste and share. Show general amounts, individuals and small amounts with quantifiers.

WHAT TO INCLUDE

  • an opening sentence that names your topic
  • how much you use or have (all / most / some / plenty)
  • what you waste, using a small amount (a few / a little)
  • what you share, and something about two things (both / either / neither)
  • a closing sentence about what you want to change
  • at least 3 quantifiers: all, most, some, any, no, none, both, either, neither, each, every, a few, a little

Sample Answer (~165 words) Every week, my family uses a large amount of food, water and money, but we do not always use it well. Most of our meals are cooked at home, and some of the vegetables come from my grandmother's garden. Sadly, a little food is still wasted, and a few things go off in the fridge before we eat them.

Both of my parents want to reduce this waste. Neither of them likes throwing food away, so each person in the house now has a job. My mother checks the fridge, and every container has a date on it. None of us buys more than we need, and if we have plenty of spare bread, we donate it to our neighbours.

The majority of our rubbish is now much smaller. I have learned that all of these small changes matter. If each family wastes just a little less, the whole street can share more and throw away almost nothing.
