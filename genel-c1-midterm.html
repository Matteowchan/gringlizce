Sınav Hakkında

Bu ara sınav, Ünite 1-10'da öğrendiğin her şeyi C1 seviyesinde ölçer: advanced tense & aspect; ileri modal anlamları; modal perfect yapıları; inversion (devrik yapı); conditional inversion (devrik koşul); advanced conditionals (but for / provided that / mixed); cleft ve vurgu yapıları (What... / It was... that...); advanced participle clauses; nominalisation (isimleştirme); ve advanced passive & reporting (is said to / is believed to have). Speaking hariç dört beceri test edilir. Toplam süre 100 dakikadır ve tek bir sayaçla ilerler — süreyi bölümler arasında kendin yönetirsin.

Reading (2 metin · 20 soru)

Suggested time: 25 minutes. (Önerilen süre: 25 dakika.) Read both texts, then answer the questions. (İki metni oku, soruları cevapla.)

Text 1 · The Paradox of Choice

For much of the twentieth century, it was taken for granted that greater choice was, without exception, a good thing. The more options consumers were offered, the reasoning went, the more likely they were to find something that suited them precisely. In recent decades, however, this assumption has been called into question by a growing body of psychological research.

In one now-famous experiment, shoppers in a supermarket were presented with a display of jams. On some days, only six varieties were offered; on others, twenty-four. Curiously, although the larger display attracted more interest, it was the smaller one that led to far more actual purchases. Faced with two dozen options, many customers simply walked away empty-handed, apparently overwhelmed.

Psychologists have since argued that an excess of choice can be paradoxically paralysing. When the alternatives are few, we compare them with relative ease. When they multiply, the effort of weighing each against the others becomes exhausting, and the fear of making the wrong decision grows. Some people respond by postponing the decision indefinitely; others choose, but are left dissatisfied, haunted by the options they rejected.

This is not to say that choice is undesirable. Few would wish to return to an age of rigid uniformity, and a reasonable range of options remains essential to any notion of freedom. The point, rather, is that the relationship between choice and satisfaction is not a straight line. Beyond a certain threshold, additional options may add little of value while imposing a real psychological cost.

For businesses, the implications are significant. Rather than overwhelming customers with endless variety, some companies have found that carefully curating a smaller selection can actually increase sales and loyalty. What consumers appear to want is not limitless choice, but choice that has already been made manageable on their behalf.

Text 2 · The Vanishing Art of the Letter

Not so long ago, the arrival of a handwritten letter was an event. The envelope, the familiar handwriting, the particular weight of the paper — all of these carried meaning before a single word had been read. Today, such letters have become a rarity, replaced almost entirely by the instant convenience of the email and the text message.

Something, many argue, has been lost in this transition. A letter demanded time and thought. Because it could not be recalled once posted, the writer chose each sentence with care. A message typed in seconds, by contrast, is easily sent and just as easily forgotten. The very effort that made letters slow was precisely what made them valuable.

There is also the question of permanence. Emails vanish into overflowing inboxes or disappear when an account is closed. A letter, however, can be kept in a drawer for decades, its ink fading but its words intact. Historians have long depended on such correspondence to understand the past; future historians may find our own era strangely silent.

None of this means that older forms of communication were superior in every respect. Letters were slow, unreliable, and sometimes never arrived at all. The telephone and the email have connected people across distances that would once have seemed impossible. Progress, as always, has brought genuine benefits alongside its quieter losses.

Perhaps the answer lies not in rejecting new technology but in remembering what the old methods taught us. To write slowly, to choose our words, to imagine the person who will read them — these habits need not vanish with the paper that once carried them.

C1 Midterm Sınavı · Bölüm 5/6

Writing (2 görev)

Suggested time: 35 minutes — Task 1: 13 min, Task 2: 22 min. (Önerilen süre: 35 dakika — Görev 1: 13 dk, Görev 2: 22 dk.) Öğretmenin değerlendirecek; örnek cevap yoktur.

Task 1 · Opinion Email (120-150 words)

Your city council has proposed turning the main square into a car-free zone, open only to pedestrians and cyclists. A local newspaper has asked readers for their views. Write an email to the newspaper giving your opinion. (Belediyen ana meydanı yayalara ve bisikletlilere açık, araç girişine kapalı bir bölgeye çevirmeyi önerdi. Yerel bir gazete okuyucularının görüşünü soruyor. Görüşünü belirten bir e-posta yaz.)

  • State your opinion clearly at the start. (görüşünü baştan net belirt)
  • Use at least one emphatic or cleft structure ("What the city really needs is..." / "It is the residents who..."). (bir vurgu/cleft yapısı)
  • Use at least one inversion or advanced conditional ("Not only would this..." / "Provided that..."). (bir devrik yapı ya da ileri koşul)
  • Use at least 3 connectors: however, moreover, therefore, although. (en az 3 bağlaç)
  • 120-150 words. (120-150 kelime)
E-POSTA YAPISI
  • Açılış: "Dear Editor," + neden yazdığın ("I am writing to share my view on...").
  • Gövde: görüşün + en az iki gerekçe; bir koşul cümlesiyle sonucu göster.
  • Kapanış: görüşünü özetle + "Yours faithfully," + adın.

Task 2 · For-and-Against Paragraph (160-200 words)

Some people believe that online learning will one day replace traditional universities completely. Others strongly disagree. Discuss the arguments for and against this view, and then give your own opinion. (Bazıları çevrimiçi öğrenimin bir gün geleneksel üniversitelerin yerini tamamen alacağını düşünür; bazıları buna kesinlikle katılmaz. Bu görüşün lehindeki ve aleyhindeki argümanları tartış, sonra kendi görüşünü ver.)

  • Introduce the topic, then give one paragraph FOR and one AGAINST. (biri lehte, biri aleyhte)
  • Use at least one advanced passive/reporting structure ("It is widely believed that..." / "...are said to...") and one mixed or inverted conditional. (bir ileri edilgen/aktarım + bir karma ya da devrik koşul)
  • Use at least 5 connectors: however, although, whereas, moreover, therefore. (en az 5 bağlaç)
  • End with a clear opinion. (net bir görüşle bitir)
  • 160-200 words. (160-200 kelime)
