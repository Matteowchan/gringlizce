IELTS SpeakingIELTS Speaking — Part 1, 2, 3 Rehberi

IELTS Speaking, bir insanla yüz yüze yapılan 11-14 dakikalık bir sohbettir; ezber değil, gerçek iletişim ölçülür. Bu rehber üç bölümü tek tek açar, dört değerlendirme kriterinde band 5 ile band 9 arasındaki farkı somut örneklerle gösterir ve akıcılık, kelime, telaffuz için sınavda işe yarayan kalıpları verir.

IELTS Speaking is an 11–14 minute conversation held face to face with a person; it measures real communication, not memorisation. This guide opens up the three parts one by one, shows the difference between band 5 and band 9 across the four assessment criteria with concrete examples, and gives you patterns for fluency, vocabulary and pronunciation that work in the exam.

1. Sınavın Yapısı: Üç Bölüm, Bir Sohbet

IELTS Speaking, bir examiner (görevli sınav yapıcı) ile birebir, yüz yüze (veya video ile) yapılan canlı bir görüşmedir. Toplam süresi 11-14 dakikadır ve baştan sona ses kaydı alınır. Akademik ve General Training modüllerinde Speaking bölümü tamamen aynıdır. Görevli sizi rahatlatmaya çalışır ama puanı standart bir band descriptor tablosuna göre verir; yani "iyi izlenim" değil, ölçülebilir dil performansı önemlidir.

Zihniyet değişimi

Speaking bir "soru-cevap testi" değil, bir konuşma performansıdır. Görevli sizden cümleleri uzatmanızı, düşüncenizi geliştirmenizi bekler. Tek kelimelik ya da tek cümlelik cevaplar, ne kadar doğru olursa olsun, düşük band getirir çünkü değerlendirilecek yeterince dil ortaya koymazsınız.

"Görevli senin fikrinle değil, o fikri nasıl dile getirdiğinle ilgilenir. Amaç doğru cevabı bulmak değil; değerlendirilecek zengin dil ortaya koymak.

1. The Structure of the Exam: Three Parts, One Conversation

IELTS Speaking is a live, one-to-one, face-to-face (or video) interview with an examiner. Its total length is 11–14 minutes and it is recorded from start to finish. The Speaking section is exactly the same in the Academic and General Training modules. The examiner tries to put you at ease but gives your score according to a standard band descriptor table; that is, it is not a "good impression" but measurable language performance that matters.

PartTimeWhat happens?Purpose
Part 1 — Introduction & Interview4-5 minIdentity check + short questions on familiar topics (home, work, hobbies)Speaking comfortably on everyday topics
Part 2 — Long Turn (Cue Card)3-4 min1 min preparation + 1-2 min of uninterrupted solo speakingDescribing a topic on your own, at length
Part 3 — Discussion4-5 minAbstract, debatable questions linked to the Part 2 topicGenerating ideas, justifying, comparing

A shift in mindset

Speaking is not a "question-answer test" but a speaking performance. The examiner expects you to extend your sentences and develop your thought. One-word or one-sentence answers, however correct, get a low band because you do not produce enough language to be assessed.

"The examiner is interested not in your opinion but in how you put it into words. The aim is not to find the right answer; it is to produce rich language to be assessed.

Puanınız dört kriterin ortalamasıdır ve her biri toplam notun %25'ini oluşturur. Kriterler eşit ağırlıklı olduğu için "kelimem çok iyi ama telaffuzum kötü" demek, o kriterden düşük almak ve ortalamayı aşağı çekmek demektir. Aşağıda her kriter derinlemesine açıklanıp band farkları örneklerle gösterilir.

Kriter 1 — Fluency & Coherence (Akıcılık ve Tutarlılık)

Ne kadar duraksamadan, kendini düzenli fikir akışıyla ifade ettiğinizi ölçer. "Akıcılık" hız demek değildir; uzun ve doğal cümleler kurabilmek, düşünceleri bağlaçlarla birbirine bağlamak demektir. Coherence ise fikirlerinizin mantıklı sıralanması: neden-sonuç, örnekleme, karşılaştırma.

BandFluency & Coherence görünümü
5Sık ve uzun duraksama; tekrar ve kendini düzeltme akışı bozar; basit bağlaçlar (and, but, because) tekrar eder
6Konuşmayı sürdürür ama bazen dil ararken durur; bağlaç kullanır ama bazen yanlış yerde
7Uzun uzun konuşur; duraksamalar dil değil içerik aramaktan; çeşitli bağlaçlar akıcı
8Zahmetsiz, tutarlı; nadiren duraksar; fikirleri esnek biçimde geliştirir ve bağlar
9Tamamen doğal akış; her tür bağlantı kusursuz; duraksama yalnızca vurgu için

Kriter 2 — Lexical Resource (Kelime Dağarcığı)

Ne kadar geniş ve isabetli kelime kullandığınızı, konuya uygun collocation (kelime eşleşmeleri) ve deyimleri doğru biçimde kullanıp kullanmadığınızı ölçer. Anahtar kavram paraphrasing: aynı kelimeyi tekrarlamak yerine eş anlamlı ifadelere geçebilmek.

BandLexical Resource görünümüÖrnek (aynı fikir: "çok yoruldum")
5Sınırlı, günlük kelime; tekrar; konu spesifik kelime azI was very very tired.
6Konu hakkında yeterli kelime; bazı yanlış seçimlerI was really exhausted.
7Esnek kelime; bazı deyimsel dil; iyi collocationI was completely worn out.
8Geniş, kesin kelime; doğal deyim kullanımıI was absolutely drained by the end of it.
9Tam doğallık ve incelik; her nüansı yakalarI was running on empty after such a gruelling day.

Kriter 3 — Grammatical Range & Accuracy (Dilbilgisi Çeşitliliği ve Doğruluğu)

İki şeyi birlikte ölçer: çeşitlilik (basit + karmaşık yapılar, farklı zamanlar, koşul cümleleri, ilgi cümlecikleri) ve doğruluk (hata oranı). Sadece kısa doğru cümleler kurmak band 6'yı geçmez; karmaşık yapıları deneyip çoğunlukla doğru kullanmak gerekir.

BandGrammatical Range & Accuracy görünümü
5Ağırlıkla basit cümleler; karmaşık yapılarda sık hata; hatalar anlamı bazen bozar
6Basit ve karmaşık yapı karışımı; karmaşık cümlelerde hata sık ama anlam korunur
7Çeşitli karmaşık yapılar; birçok hatasız cümle; hatalar seyrek
8Geniş yapı yelpazesi; çoğunlukla hatasız; nadir sistematik olmayan hata
9Tam esneklik ve doğruluk; hatalar ana dili konuşanınkiyle aynı düzeyde nadir
Basit (band 5): I like my job. It is interesting. I work with computers.
Karmaşık (band 7-8): What I find most rewarding about my job is that, even though it can be demanding, I get to solve problems that would otherwise take a whole team days to crack.

Kriter 4 — Pronunciation (Telaffuz)

Sadece sesleri doğru çıkarmak değil; anlaşılırlık, kelime ve cümle vurgusu, tonlama ve bağlı konuşma ölçülür. Aksanınız olması sorun değildir — İngiliz/Amerikan aksanı taklidi gerekmez. Önemli olan görevlinin sizi zorlanmadan anlamasıdır.

BandPronunciation görünümü
5Bazı özellikleri kontrol eder ama tutarsız; zaman zaman anlaşılırlık düşer
6Vurgu/tonlamayı dener; genelde anlaşılır ama bazı hatalı sesler dikkat dağıtır
7Çok çeşitli telaffuz özelliği; genelde iyi kontrol; anlaşılırlık yüksek
8Geniş özellik yelpazesi; nadir anlaşılırlık sorunu; aksan iletişimi engellemez
9Tüm özelliklerde kesin ve zahmetsiz kontrol; her zaman kolay anlaşılır

2. The Four Assessment Criteria (Band 5 → 9)

Your score is the average of four criteria, and each makes up 25% of the total. Because the criteria are equally weighted, saying "my vocabulary is great but my pronunciation is poor" means scoring low on that criterion and dragging the average down. Below, each criterion is explained in depth and the band differences are shown with examples.

25%Fluency & Coherence
25%Lexical Resource
25%Grammar Range & Accuracy
25%Pronunciation

Criterion 1 — Fluency & Coherence

Measures how much you express yourself without hesitation and with an orderly flow of ideas. "Fluency" does not mean speed; it means being able to build long, natural sentences and to connect your thoughts with conjunctions. Coherence is the logical ordering of your ideas: cause-effect, exemplification, comparison.

BandHow Fluency & Coherence looks
5Frequent and long hesitation; repetition and self-correction disrupt the flow; simple conjunctions (and, but, because) repeat
6Keeps the conversation going but sometimes pauses to search for language; uses conjunctions but sometimes in the wrong place
7Speaks at length; hesitations come from searching for content, not language; a variety of conjunctions used fluently
8Effortless, coherent; rarely hesitates; develops and links ideas flexibly
9Fully natural flow; every kind of connection flawless; hesitation only for emphasis

Criterion 2 — Lexical Resource

Measures how wide and precise your vocabulary is, and whether you use topic-appropriate collocations and idioms correctly. The key concept is paraphrasing: being able to switch to synonymous expressions instead of repeating the same word.

BandHow Lexical Resource looksExample (same idea: "I was very tired")
5Limited, everyday vocabulary; repetition; little topic-specific vocabularyI was very very tired.
6Enough vocabulary about the topic; some wrong choicesI was really exhausted.
7Flexible vocabulary; some idiomatic language; good collocationI was completely worn out.
8Wide, precise vocabulary; natural use of idiomI was absolutely drained by the end of it.
9Full naturalness and subtlety; captures every nuanceI was running on empty after such a gruelling day.

Criterion 3 — Grammatical Range & Accuracy

Measures two things together: range (simple + complex structures, different tenses, conditionals, relative clauses) and accuracy (error rate). Building only short correct sentences will not get you past band 6; you need to attempt complex structures and use them mostly correctly.

BandHow Grammatical Range & Accuracy looks
5Mostly simple sentences; frequent errors in complex structures; errors sometimes distort meaning
6A mix of simple and complex structures; errors frequent in complex sentences but meaning is preserved
7A variety of complex structures; many error-free sentences; errors are infrequent
8A wide range of structures; mostly error-free; rare, non-systematic errors
9Full flexibility and accuracy; errors as rare as a native speaker's
Simple (band 5): I like my job. It is interesting. I work with computers.
Complex (band 7-8): What I find most rewarding about my job is that, even though it can be demanding, I get to solve problems that would otherwise take a whole team days to crack.

Criterion 4 — Pronunciation

Not just producing the sounds correctly; intelligibility, word and sentence stress, intonation and connected speech are measured. Having an accent is not a problem — imitating a British/American accent is not required. What matters is that the examiner understands you without effort.

BandHow Pronunciation looks
5Controls some features but inconsistently; intelligibility drops at times
6Attempts stress/intonation; generally intelligible but some faulty sounds distract
7A wide variety of pronunciation features; generally good control; high intelligibility
8A wide range of features; rare intelligibility issues; accent does not impede communication
9Precise, effortless control of all features; always easy to understand

3. Part 1 — Tanıdık Konular: Cevabı Nasıl Genişletirsin?

Part 1'de görevli, hakkınızda tanıdık ve kişisel konularda kısa sorular sorar: yaşadığınız yer, işiniz/okulunuz, hobileriniz, yemek, hava durumu, seyahat. Sorular kolaydır — tuzak, cevabı çok kısa vermektir. Kural: hiçbir soruya tek cümleyle cevap verme.

Altın formül: Cevap + Neden + Örnek/Detay

Her soruyu üç adımla genişlet: (1) doğrudan cevap ver, (2) nedenini ekle, (3) bir örnek, detay veya karşılaştırma ile bitir. Bu, iki-üç cümlelik doğal bir yanıt üretir — ne çok kısa ne de ezber gibi uzun.

Part 1 tipik soruları
Do you work or are you a student? · Where do you live? · Do you like your neighbourhood? · What do you usually do in your free time? · Do you prefer tea or coffee? · How often do you use public transport? · Did you enjoy studying English at school?

Zayıf cevap (tek kelime/cümle — band 5):

Q: Do you like your neighbourhood?
A: Yes, I like it. It is good.

Güçlü cevap (Cevap + Neden + Örnek — band 7+):

Q: Do you like your neighbourhood?
A: Yes, I'm quite fond of it, actually. The main reason is that it's really quiet but still close to everything — there's a lovely park just around the corner. For example, on Sunday mornings I usually go for a run there, and it's the perfect way to clear my head before the week starts.

Part 1 genişletme kalıpları

  • Cevap açma: To be honest… / I'd say… / Well, it depends, but…
  • Neden ekleme: The main reason is that… / mainly because… / That's largely down to…
  • Örnek verme: For instance… / A good example would be… / Take last week — …
  • Sıklık/alışkanlık: I tend to… / I usually end up… / More often than not, I…

3. Part 1 — Familiar Topics: How Do You Extend Your Answer?

In Part 1 the examiner asks short questions on familiar and personal topics about you: where you live, your work/school, your hobbies, food, the weather, travel. The questions are easy — the trap is answering too briefly. The rule: never answer any question in a single sentence.

The golden formula: Answer + Reason + Example/Detail

Extend every question in three steps: (1) give a direct answer, (2) add the reason, (3) finish with an example, detail or comparison. This produces a natural two-to-three-sentence response — neither too short nor rehearsed-sounding and long.

Typical Part 1 questions
Do you work or are you a student? · Where do you live? · Do you like your neighbourhood? · What do you usually do in your free time? · Do you prefer tea or coffee? · How often do you use public transport? · Did you enjoy studying English at school?

Weak answer (one word/sentence — band 5):

Q: Do you like your neighbourhood?
A: Yes, I like it. It is good.

Strong answer (Answer + Reason + Example — band 7+):

Q: Do you like your neighbourhood?
A: Yes, I'm quite fond of it, actually. The main reason is that it's really quiet but still close to everything — there's a lovely park just around the corner. For example, on Sunday mornings I usually go for a run there, and it's the perfect way to clear my head before the week starts.

Part 1 extension patterns

  • Opening the answer: To be honest… / I'd say… / Well, it depends, but…
  • Adding a reason: The main reason is that… / mainly because… / That's largely down to…
  • Giving an example: For instance… / A good example would be… / Take last week — …
  • Frequency/habit: I tend to… / I usually end up… / More often than not, I…

4. Part 2 — Cue Card: 1 Dakika Hazırlık + 2 Dakika Konuşma

Görevli size bir cue card (konu kartı), bir kalem ve kâğıt verir. Bir konu ve altında yönlendirici üç-dört madde vardır. 1 dakika hazırlık süreniz olur; ardından 1-2 dakika kesintisiz tek başınıza konuşursunuz. Görevli araya girmez, sadece dinler ve süreyi tutar.

1 dakikayı doğru kullanma: not alma stratejisi

Cümle yazmaya çalışma — vaktin yetmez. Bunun yerine her maddenin yanına 2-3 anahtar kelime yaz. Amacın "okuyacağın metin" değil, hatırlatıcı harita oluşturmak. En güçlü strateji: soyut yönergeyi somut, gerçek bir örneğe bağlamak (uydurma bir olay bile olsa, anlatması kolay olanı seç).

Örnek Cue Card
Describe a skill you would like to learn.
You should say:
— what the skill is
— why you want to learn it
— how you would learn it
and explain how this skill would change your life.

1 dakikada aldığın notlar (örnek)

  • What: cooking — proper meals, not just pasta
  • Why: health + save money + impress friends
  • How: YouTube channels, evening class, practise weekends
  • Change life: confidence, healthier, host dinners

Konuşma yapısı (yol haritası)

  • Giriş (1 cümle): Konuyu tanıt ve seçimini belirt.
  • Gövde (madde madde): Kartın her maddesini sırayla, örnekle genişlet. Zamanın çoğu burada geçmeli.
  • Kapanış (son madde): "explain…" ile başlayan asıl soruyu geniş tut — süreyi burada doldurursun.

Model 2-dakika cevap (açıklamalar köşeli parantez içinde):

The skill I'd absolutely love to master is cooking — and I don't mean throwing some pasta in a pot, I mean cooking proper, home-made meals from scratch. [Giriş: konu + kişisel dokunuş]

The main reason I want to learn it is threefold, really. First and foremost, it's about health — I've realised that when you cook for yourself, you know exactly what goes into your food. On top of that, it would save me a small fortune, because eating out or ordering in every night is ridiculously expensive. And, I have to admit, I'd love to be able to impress my friends. [Neden maddesi: üç gerekçe, bağlaçlarla]

As for how I'd go about it, I'd probably start with YouTube — there are some brilliant channels that break everything down step by step. But honestly, I think I'd learn far more from a proper evening class, where a chef can correct you on the spot. And of course, there's no substitute for practice, so I'd cook something new every weekend. [Nasıl maddesi: yol + örnek]

In terms of how it would change my life, I genuinely think it would make me more confident and a lot healthier. But more than anything, I'd love to host dinner parties one day — there's something really special about bringing people together around a table you've prepared yourself. [Kapanış: geniş, duygusal, süreyi doldurur]

Süre doldurma teknikleri (2 dakika uzun görünürse)

  • Karşılaştırma ekle: Unlike most people who… / Compared to when I was younger…
  • Duygu/gerekçe derinleştir: What really appeals to me is… / The thing is…
  • Somut örnek anlat: I remember once… / Just the other day…
  • Gelecek tahmini: In the long run, I imagine… / Down the line, it could…

4. Part 2 — Cue Card: 1 Minute Preparation + 2 Minutes Speaking

The examiner gives you a cue card, a pencil and paper. There is a topic with three or four guiding bullet points beneath it. You get 1 minute of preparation; then you speak uninterrupted for 1–2 minutes on your own. The examiner does not interrupt, only listens and keeps time.

Using the 1 minute well: a note-taking strategy

Do not try to write sentences — you won't have time. Instead, write 2–3 keywords next to each bullet. Your aim is not "a text to read" but a reminder map. The strongest strategy: tie the abstract prompt to a concrete, real example (even a made-up event — choose the one that is easy to tell).

Sample Cue Card
Describe a skill you would like to learn.
You should say:
— what the skill is
— why you want to learn it
— how you would learn it
and explain how this skill would change your life.

The notes you take in 1 minute (example)

  • What: cooking — proper meals, not just pasta
  • Why: health + save money + impress friends
  • How: YouTube channels, evening class, practise weekends
  • Change life: confidence, healthier, host dinners

The structure of the talk (a roadmap)

  • Introduction (1 sentence): Introduce the topic and state your choice.
  • Body (bullet by bullet): Expand each bullet of the card in order, with an example. Most of your time should go here.
  • Conclusion (final bullet): Keep the main "explain…" question broad — this is where you fill the time.

A model 2-minute answer (annotations in square brackets):

The skill I'd absolutely love to master is cooking — and I don't mean throwing some pasta in a pot, I mean cooking proper, home-made meals from scratch. [Introduction: topic + personal touch]

The main reason I want to learn it is threefold, really. First and foremost, it's about health — I've realised that when you cook for yourself, you know exactly what goes into your food. On top of that, it would save me a small fortune, because eating out or ordering in every night is ridiculously expensive. And, I have to admit, I'd love to be able to impress my friends. [The reason bullet: three grounds, with conjunctions]

As for how I'd go about it, I'd probably start with YouTube — there are some brilliant channels that break everything down step by step. But honestly, I think I'd learn far more from a proper evening class, where a chef can correct you on the spot. And of course, there's no substitute for practice, so I'd cook something new every weekend. [The how bullet: method + example]

In terms of how it would change my life, I genuinely think it would make me more confident and a lot healthier. But more than anything, I'd love to host dinner parties one day — there's something really special about bringing people together around a table you've prepared yourself. [Conclusion: broad, emotional, fills the time]

Time-filling techniques (if 2 minutes feels long)

  • Add a comparison: Unlike most people who… / Compared to when I was younger…
  • Deepen the feeling/reason: What really appeals to me is… / The thing is…
  • Tell a concrete example: I remember once… / Just the other day…
  • Predict the future: In the long run, I imagine… / Down the line, it could…

5. Part 3 — Soyut Tartışma: Fikir Üret, Gerekçelendir

Part 3, Part 2'nin konusuyla bağlantılı ama soyut ve genel sorular içerir. Artık kendinizden değil, toplumdan, geleceğe dair eğilimlerden, avantaj-dezavantajlardan konuşursunuz. Bu bölüm en çok ayırt eden bölümdür çünkü fikir üretme ve gerekçelendirme yeteneğini ölçer.

İpucu

Karşı görüşü kabul etmek (concession) sofistike düşünme gösterir ve band'i yükseltir. Kendi görüşünde dururken diğer tarafı da tanı: “That said…”, “While it's true that…, I still believe…”.

Altın formül: Görüş + Gerekçe + Örnek + Karşı görüş

(1) Net bir görüş belirt, (2) mantıklı bir gerekçe sun, (3) somut bir örnekle destekle, (4) mümkünse diğer tarafı da kabul et. Karşı görüşü kabul etmek (concession) sofistike düşünme gösterir ve yüksek band getirir.

Part 3 tipik soru tipleri (Part 2 "skill" konusundan)
Why do you think some skills are disappearing nowadays? · Is it better to learn a skill from a teacher or on your own? · Should schools teach more practical skills? · Do you think technology will make certain skills unnecessary in the future?
Q: Is it better to learn a skill from a teacher or on your own?

[Görüş] Personally, I'd argue it really depends on the skill, but for most things a teacher gives you an edge. [Gerekçe] The main advantage is instant feedback — a good teacher spots your mistakes before they become bad habits. [Örnek] Take learning an instrument, for example: you can watch a thousand videos, but you won't realise your technique is wrong until someone points it out. [Karşı görüş] That said, I can see the appeal of self-teaching too — it's cheaper and you can go at your own pace. On balance, though, I think guided learning tends to be more efficient in the long run.

Part 3 tartışma kalıpları

  • Görüş bildirme: I'd argue that… / My view is that… / I'm inclined to think…
  • Gerekçelendirme: This is largely because… / The reason I say this is…
  • Karşı görüş (concession): That said… / Having said that… / While it's true that…, I still believe…
  • Genelleme: By and large… / More often than not… / As a rule…
  • Tahmin: It's highly likely that… / In all probability… / I wouldn't be surprised if…
  • Emin değilken: It's hard to say for certain, but… / I could be wrong, but…

5. Part 3 — Abstract Discussion: Generate Ideas, Justify Them

Part 3 contains questions that are linked to the Part 2 topic but abstract and general. Now you talk not about yourself but about society, future trends and advantages-disadvantages. This is the most discriminating part because it measures your ability to generate and justify ideas.

Tip

Conceding the opposing view (concession) shows sophisticated thinking and raises your band. While standing by your own view, acknowledge the other side too: "That said…", "While it's true that…, I still believe…".

The golden formula: Opinion + Reason + Example + Counter-view

(1) State a clear opinion, (2) give a logical reason, (3) support it with a concrete example, (4) if possible, acknowledge the other side. Conceding the opposing view (concession) shows sophisticated thinking and earns a high band.

Typical Part 3 question types (from the Part 2 "skill" topic)
Why do you think some skills are disappearing nowadays? · Is it better to learn a skill from a teacher or on your own? · Should schools teach more practical skills? · Do you think technology will make certain skills unnecessary in the future?
Q: Is it better to learn a skill from a teacher or on your own?

[Opinion] Personally, I'd argue it really depends on the skill, but for most things a teacher gives you an edge. [Reason] The main advantage is instant feedback — a good teacher spots your mistakes before they become bad habits. [Example] Take learning an instrument, for example: you can watch a thousand videos, but you won't realise your technique is wrong until someone points it out. [Counter-view] That said, I can see the appeal of self-teaching too — it's cheaper and you can go at your own pace. On balance, though, I think guided learning tends to be more efficient in the long run.

Part 3 discussion patterns

  • Stating an opinion: I'd argue that… / My view is that… / I'm inclined to think…
  • Justifying: This is largely because… / The reason I say this is…
  • Counter-view (concession): That said… / Having said that… / While it's true that…, I still believe…
  • Generalising: By and large… / More often than not… / As a rule…
  • Predicting: It's highly likely that… / In all probability… / I wouldn't be surprised if…
  • When unsure: It's hard to say for certain, but… / I could be wrong, but…

6. Akıcılık Teknikleri: Duraksamayı Yönetmek

Akıcılık, hiç duraksamamak değildir — ana dili konuşanlar da duraksar. Önemli olan duraksamayı sessizlikle değil, dille doldurmak. Aşağıdaki üç araç, düşünürken bile konuşmaya devam etmenizi sağlar.

Düşünme kalıpları (thinking phrases)

That's an interesting question… / Let me think for a moment… / Well, I've never really thought about that, but… / I suppose the best way to put it is…

Dolgu ifadeleri (doğal, aşırıya kaçmadan)

you know, … / I mean, … / sort of / kind of / actually / to be honest / at the end of the day

Kendini düzeltme (self-correction)

…or rather, … / what I mean to say is… / sorry, let me rephrase that… / well, not exactly — …

Yap / Yapma — Akıcılık

Yap:

  • Sessiz kalacağına düşünme kalıbı söyle.
  • Küçük hatayı bırak, akışı koru — her hatayı düzeltme.
  • Cümleleri bağlaçlarla uzat (because, so, which means).

Yapma:

  • "Ee…", "ııı…" gibi Türkçe dolgu sesleri çıkarma.
  • Bilmediğin kelimeyi aramak için 5 saniye susma — parafraze et.
  • Her cümleye "and" ile başlama.

6. Fluency Techniques: Managing Hesitation

Fluency is not never hesitating — native speakers hesitate too. What matters is filling the hesitation with language, not silence. The three tools below let you keep talking even while you think.

Thinking phrases

That's an interesting question… / Let me think for a moment… / Well, I've never really thought about that, but… / I suppose the best way to put it is…

Filler expressions (natural, not overdone)

you know, … / I mean, … / sort of / kind of / actually / to be honest / at the end of the day

Self-correction

…or rather, … / what I mean to say is… / sorry, let me rephrase that… / well, not exactly — …

Do / Don't — Fluency

Do:

  • Say a thinking phrase rather than going silent.
  • Let a small error go, keep the flow — don't correct every mistake.
  • Extend sentences with conjunctions (because, so, which means).

Don't:

  • Make Turkish filler sounds like "ee…", "ııı…".
  • Go silent for 5 seconds hunting for a word — paraphrase it.
  • Start every sentence with "and".

7. Kelime Zenginliği: Collocation ve Deyim

Yüksek band, "zor kelime" değil doğru kelimeyi doğru yerde kullanmaktır. En etkili yol, konu-bazlı collocation (birlikte kullanılan kelime grupları) öğrenmek. Tek tek kelime ezberlemek yerine, sık geçen IELTS konularında (iş, eğitim, teknoloji, çevre, sağlık) kalıp öbekler biriktirin.

KonuDoğal collocation'lar (band 7+)

Eğitimto broaden your horizons · hands-on experience · to grasp a concept · a well-rounded education
Teknolojito keep up with the latest trends · at your fingertips · a double-edged sword · to be glued to a screen
Çevreto cut down on waste · a carbon footprint · to raise awareness · environmentally friendly

Deyimleri dikkatli kullan

Deyimler (idioms) doğru kullanılırsa Lexical Resource'u yükseltir, ama zorlama ve yanlış kullanım tam tersini yapar. Kural: yalnızca tam emin olduğun ve doğal aktığı yerde kullan. Güvenli, sık kullanılan konuşma dili deyimleri: once in a blue moon (çok nadir), a piece of cake (çok kolay), to be on the same page (hemfikir), to get the hang of it (kapmak, alışmak). Bir yanıta beş deyim tıkıştırmak ezber izlenimi verir; bir-iki tanesi yeterlidir.

7. Vocabulary Range: Collocation and Idiom

A high band is not using "hard words" but using the right word in the right place. The most effective way is to learn topic-based collocations (groups of words used together). Instead of memorising words one by one, build up ready-made phrases in frequent IELTS topics (work, education, technology, environment, health).

TopicNatural collocations (band 7+)
Work / Careera demanding job · to climb the career ladder · a steep learning curve · to strike a work-life balance
Educationto broaden your horizons · hands-on experience · to grasp a concept · a well-rounded education
Technologyto keep up with the latest trends · at your fingertips · a double-edged sword · to be glued to a screen
Environmentto cut down on waste · a carbon footprint · to raise awareness · environmentally friendly

Use idioms carefully

Idioms raise your Lexical Resource if used correctly, but forcing them and using them wrongly does the opposite. The rule: use one only where you are completely sure and where it flows naturally. Safe, common spoken idioms: once in a blue moon (very rarely), a piece of cake (very easy), to be on the same page (to agree), to get the hang of it (to get used to something). Cramming five idioms into one answer sounds rehearsed; one or two is enough.

8. Telaffuz: Ses Değil, Anlaşılırlık

Telaffuz kriteri dört katmandan oluşur; her birini ayrı çalışmak puanı ölçülebilir biçimde yükseltir. Aksanınızı silmeye değil, anlaşılır ve doğal olmaya odaklanın.

Kelime vurgusu (word stress)

Her çok heceli kelimenin bir vurgulu hecesi vardır; yanlış hece anlamı bozar. PHO-to-graph vs. pho-TO-gra-pher. Yeni kelimeyi vurgusuyla birlikte öğren. Detay: Kelime Vurgusu.

Cümle vurgusu (sentence stress)

İçerik kelimeleri (isim, fiil) vurgulanır; dilbilgisi kelimeleri (of, the, to) zayıflar. Bu ritim İngilizce'yi doğal yapar. Detay: Cümle Vurgusu.

Tonlama (intonation)

Sesin inip çıkması ilgi ve anlam taşır; düz ton sıkıcı ve robotik algılanır. Soru, liste, karşıtlık farklı tonlar ister. Detay: Tonlama.

Bağlı konuşma (connected speech)

Doğal konuşmada kelimeler birbirine bağlanır: "want to" → "wanna", "an apple" → "anapple". Bunu tanımak ve üretmek akıcılık izlenimini artırır. Detay: Bağlı Konuşma.

8. Pronunciation: Not the Sound, but Intelligibility

The Pronunciation criterion has four layers; working on each separately raises your score measurably. Focus not on erasing your accent but on being clear and natural.

Word stress

Every multi-syllable word has one stressed syllable; the wrong one distorts the meaning. PHO-to-graph vs. pho-TO-gra-pher. Learn a new word together with its stress. Detail: Word Stress.

Sentence stress

Content words (nouns, verbs) are stressed; grammar words (of, the, to) weaken. This rhythm makes English natural. Detail: Sentence Stress.

Intonation

The rise and fall of the voice carries interest and meaning; a flat tone reads as dull and robotic. Questions, lists and contrasts need different tones. Detail: Intonation.

Connected speech

In natural speech, words link together: "want to" → "wanna", "an apple" → "anapple". Recognising and producing this improves the impression of fluency. Detail: Connected Speech.

9. Band 6 vs Band 8: Aynı Cevap, İki Dünya

Aynı soruya verilen iki cevap, doğruluk açısından ikisi de "anlaşılır" olabilir; farkı yaratan genişletme, kelime seçimi ve yapı çeşitliliğidir. Aşağıda aynı Part 1 sorusuna band 6 ve band 8 yanıtları karşılaştırılmıştır.

Band 6
Doğru ama düz cümleler listeler; günlük kelime, basit yapı, kişisel dokunuş yok. Fikir gerekçeye bağlanmaz.
↔
Band 8
Fikri gerekçe ve örneğe bağlar; doğal collocation ve deyim, karşıtlık/taviz yapıları, dürüst kişisel ton.
Soru: Do you enjoy cooking?
6 Yes, I enjoy cooking. I cook every day for my family. It is relaxing and I like to try new recipes sometimes. It is a good hobby.
8 I do, yes — although I'd say it's more of a stress-reliever than a hobby for me. Farkı ne yarattı?

  • Genişletme: Band 8 fikri (stres atma), gerekçeye ve örneğe bağladı; band 6 cümleleri listeledi.
  • Kelime: therapeutic, hectic, have a go at, every now and then — doğal collocation ve deyim.
  • Yapı: Band 8 zaman zarfı öbeği, karşıtlık (although) ve taviz (even when…) kullandı.
  • Ton: Kişisel, dürüst dokunuşlar (to be honest, it's still good fun) doğallık kattı.

Two answers to the same question can both be "intelligible" in terms of accuracy; what makes the difference is extension, word choice and structural variety. Below, band 6 and band 8 answers to the same Part 1 question are compared.

Band 6

↔

10. Sık Yapılan Hatalar

Ezber cevap

Görevliler ezberlenmiş yanıtları anında fark eder — akış aniden pürüzsüzleşir, göz teması kaybolur, konu tam oturmaz. Ezber, band düşürür. Kalıp öğren ama içeriği anlık üret.

Tek kelimelik yanıt

"Yes." / "No." / "Sometimes." tek başına ölümcül. Görevli seni genişletmeye zorlamak zorunda kalırsa, değerlendirilecek dilin azalır. Her yanıtı en az iki-üç cümleye çıkar.

Panik sessizliği

Soruyu anlamadığında sus kalma. "Sorry, could you rephrase that?" demek tamamen kabul edilebilir ve puan düşürmez. Sessizlik ise akıcılık puanını doğrudan vurur.

"Doğru cevap" arama

Part 3'te tek doğru görüş yoktur. Görevli fikrinle ilgilenmez; onu nasıl gerekçelendirdiğinle ilgilenir. Rahatça savunabileceğin görüşü seç, "doğru" olanı değil.

Aşırı basit kalma

Sadece hatasız ama basit cümleler band 6'da tavan yapar. Risk al: karmaşık yapı dene. Ara sıra hata, hiç karmaşıklık denememekten daha az cezalandırılır.

Görevliyi taklit etme

Görevlinin aksanını taklit etmeye çalışma. Kendi anlaşılır telaffuzunla konuş; taklit hem doğallığı bozar hem dikkat dağıtır.

10. Common Mistakes

Memorised answers

Examiners spot memorised answers instantly — the flow suddenly becomes smooth, eye contact is lost, the topic does not quite fit. Memorisation lowers your band. Learn patterns but generate the content on the spot.

One-word answers

"Yes." / "No." / "Sometimes." on their own are fatal. If the examiner has to push you to extend, the language to assess shrinks. Take every answer to at least two or three sentences.

Panic silence

Do not go silent when you don't understand the question. Saying "Sorry, could you rephrase that?" is entirely acceptable and does not lower your score. Silence, however, hits your fluency score directly.

Searching for "the right answer"

In Part 3 there is no single correct view. The examiner is not interested in your opinion; they are interested in how you justify it. Choose the view you can defend comfortably, not the "correct" one.

Staying overly simple

Only error-free but simple sentences peak at band 6. Take a risk: attempt complex structures. The odd error is penalised less than never attempting any complexity.

Imitating the examiner

Do not try to imitate the examiner's accent. Speak with your own intelligible pronunciation; imitation breaks naturalness and distracts.

13. Üç Altın Kural: Kampın Konuşma Protokolü

Bu üç kural, Gri kamp materyalinin özüdür. Sınavda panik anında hatırlaman gereken, düşünmeden uygulayacağın refleksler. Ezberle değil, alışkanlık haline getir.

Kaçın

Tek kelimelik cevap, uzun sessizlik ve “I don't know” — bu üçü Speaking'de doğrudan puan öldürür. Aşağıdaki üç kural tam olarak bu üç refleksi tersine çevirir.

Kural 1 — Üç cümle: hiçbir cevap 3 cümleden kısa olamaz

Her cevabın iskeleti aynı: Cevap + Sebep + Örnek. Tek kelimelik yanıt sıfır puandır. "Do you like reading? → Yes." değerlendirilecek dil ortaya koymaz; görevli seni genişletmeye zorlamak zorunda kalır ve akıcılık puanın düşer. Kısa soru bile olsa, refleksin üç cümle olmalı: önce cevabı ver, sonra nedenini ekle, son olarak somut bir örnekle bitir.

Kural 2 — Doldurma ifadeleri: kelime bulamayınca susma

Beynin bir sonraki cümleyi kurarken sessiz kalmak yerine, düşünme boşluğunu dille doldur. Bu ifadeler doğaldır, ana dili konuşanlar da kullanır; sessizlik ise akıcılık puanını doğrudan vurur.

  • Let me think about that for a second.
  • That's an interesting question.
  • It depends, really.
  • Off the top of my head…

Kural 3 — "I don't know" yasak

"I don't know" demek görevliye "değerlendirecek dilim yok" demektir ve konuşmayı öldürür. Bilmesen bile tahmin et, konuyu bildiğin bir alana çek, ama susma. Örneğin uzay teknolojisi hakkında fikrin yoksa: "Honestly, it's not something I know much about, but if I had to guess, I'd say…" diye başla ve kendi deneyimine bağla. Fikir eksikliği değil, sessizlik puan kaybettirir.

13. Three Golden Rules: The Camp's Speaking Protocol

These three rules are the essence of the Gri camp material. They are the reflexes you should remember in a moment of panic in the exam and apply without thinking. Do not just memorise them — turn them into a habit.

Avoid

One-word answers, long silences and "I don't know" — these three kill your score directly in Speaking. The three rules below reverse exactly these three reflexes.

Rule 1 — Three sentences: no answer can be shorter than 3 sentences

The skeleton of every answer is the same: Answer + Reason + Example. A one-word answer is zero points. "Do you like reading? → Yes." produces no language to assess; the examiner has to push you to extend and your fluency score drops. Even for a short question, your reflex should be three sentences: first give the answer, then add the reason, and finish with a concrete example.

Rule 2 — Filler phrases: don't go silent when you can't find a word

Instead of staying silent while your brain builds the next sentence, fill the thinking gap with language. These phrases are natural, native speakers use them too; silence, on the other hand, hits your fluency score directly.

  • Let me think about that for a second.
  • That's an interesting question.
  • It depends, really.
  • Off the top of my head…

Rule 3 — "I don't know" is banned

Saying "I don't know" tells the examiner "I have no language to assess" and kills the conversation. Even if you don't know, guess, pull the topic into an area you know, but don't go silent. For example, if you have no idea about space technology: begin with "Honestly, it's not something I know much about, but if I had to guess, I'd say…" and tie it to your own experience. It is not a lack of ideas but silence that loses points.

14. Part 1 / 2 / 3 — Cevap İskeletleri ve Model Cevaplar

Her Part'ın kendi kalıbı vardır. Kalıbı ezberle, içeriği anlık üret. Aşağıda her bölüm için iskelet ve tam bir model cevap var.

14. Part 1 / 2 / 3 — Answer Skeletons and Model Answers

Each Part has its own pattern. Memorise the pattern, generate the content on the spot. Below is a skeleton and a full model answer for each part.

Part 1 — Answer + Reason + Example

Short, natural, personal. Three sentences in three steps is enough.

Q: Do you enjoy reading?
A: Yes, I do, although I read less than I'd like to. I mostly read novels, and a good story helps me switch off after school. At the moment I'm halfway through a detective novel a friend recommended.

Part 2 — Introduction + Four bullets + Closing evaluation

Introduce the card, expand each bullet in order, and close by evaluating why you like/value it. Skeleton patterns:

Introduction: I'd like to talk about…
Four bullets: It's… · I usually go… · I normally… · The best part is…
Closing: The reason I like it so much is that…

Part 3 — Opinion + Counter-pressure + Your own reasoning

Take a clear opinion, acknowledge the pressure/difficulty of the other side, then stand by your view with your own reasoning.

Q: Should cities have more green spaces?
A: Definitely, and I'd say it's becoming more urgent. As cities grow, the pressure to build on every piece of land is enormous, so parks are the first thing sacrificed. But they give people somewhere to go that costs nothing, which matters in a city where everything requires money.

15. Part 2 Cue Card — How to Fill Two Minutes (Pace Map)

Most students finish the cue card in one minute and fall silent. The reason is not a lack of things to say but not knowing how much time to give each bullet. The distribution below fills the two minutes exactly. Internalise this pace along with your notes.

TimeWhat you are doingHowHow many sentences
0:00 – 0:15IntroductionI'd like to talk about… Introduce the topic. One sentence is enough.1 sentence
0:15 – 0:40Bullet 1Cover the first bullet. One piece of information, one detail.2 sentences
0:40 – 1:05Bullet 2Cover the second bullet. Same pace.2 sentences
1:05 – 1:30Bullet 3Cover the third bullet. Give an example here.2-3 sentences
1:30 – 2:00Final bulletUsually starts with "and explain why". This is the longest part. Add feeling and evaluation.3-4 sentences

Three pace rules

  • The final bullet is the longest. The bullet starting with "and explain why" earns the most points because it requires justification.
  • Finishing early. The examiner will not stop you; if you finish early you fall silent, and silence lowers your Fluency score.
  • Don't leave a sentence half-finished. If the two minutes are up, the examiner will stop you anyway. You just try to finish the sentence.

  • How has technology changed studying?

Model cevap (Free Time setinden, üç cümle):

Q: Do you prefer indoor or outdoor activities?
A: I'd say I lean towards outdoor activities, without a doubt. The main reason is that I sit at a desk all day, so getting out in the fresh air is the perfect way to recharge. For instance, most weekends I'll go hiking with a couple of friends, and I always come back feeling far more relaxed than if I'd stayed in.

Model cevap (Weather setinden, üç cümle):

Just last week, for example, the sun finally came out after days of rain and I suddenly felt like tackling all the jobs I'd been putting off.

17. Örnek Cue Card'lar ve Bir Tam Model 2-Dakika Cevap

Cue card'lar hep aynı yapıdadır: bir konu ve altında yönlendirici maddeler (You should say…). Aşağıda dört tipik kart var. Her biri için 1 dakikada anahtar kelime notu al, sonra tempo haritasına göre iki dakika konuş.

— how often you go there
— what you do there
— and explain why you like it

You should say:
— who the person is
— how you know them
— what they did
— and explain why this help was important to you

Kart 3

You should say:
— what the skill is
— why you want to learn it
— how you would learn it
— and explain how it would change your life

Kart 4

— what it was

— and explain why you enjoyed it

Tam model konuşma (Kart 1 için, tempo haritasına uygun, band 7 seviyesinde — dikkat: good, nice, interesting yerine kesin sıfatlar):

I'd like to talk about a small coastal town called Assos, on the Aegean coast of Turkey. [0:00–0:15 · Giriş]

It's about a two-hour drive from where I live, tucked beneath an ancient hilltop ruin, so the whole place feels frozen in time. The streets are narrow and lined with old stone houses that have been standing for centuries. [0:15–0:40 · Madde 1: where it is]

I don't get to go there nearly as often as I'd like — realistically, only two or three times a year, usually in late spring before the crowds arrive. Whenever exams finish, it's the first place I think of escaping to. [0:40–1:05 · Madde 2: how often]

When I'm there, I keep things wonderfully simple. I'll spend the mornings swimming in the crystal-clear water, then wander up to the ruins in the afternoon and just sit there watching the sun sink into the sea. For example, last summer I took a book up to the old temple and ended up reading for hours without noticing the time. [1:05–1:30 · Madde 3 + örnek]

But if I'm honest, the real reason I love it so much is the sense of calm it gives me. Back home everything is rushed and noisy, whereas in Assos I can finally breathe and switch off completely. There's something deeply restorative about being surrounded by that much history and that much sea at once — it always reminds me what actually matters. [1:30–2:00 · Kapanış: and explain why, en uzun bölüm]

18. Part 3 Örnek Sorular ve Yaklaşım

Part 3 soyut ve toplumsaldır. Kendi hayatından değil, genel eğilimlerden konuşursun. Kalıp: Görüş + Karşı baskı + Kendi gerekçen (ya da genişletilmiş hali: Görüş + Gerekçe + Örnek + Karşı görüş).

Education

  • Should students choose their own subjects at school?
  • Is exam-based assessment fair?
  • How has technology changed teaching?
  • Do children learn better in groups or alone?

Technology

  • Does technology make people less social?
  • Should schools ban mobile phones?
  • Will technology make certain skills unnecessary?
  • Do young people rely too much on the internet?

Environment

  • Whose responsibility is it to protect the environment, governments or individuals?
  • Is public transport the answer to pollution?
  • Are people willing to change their habits for the planet?
  • Should cities have more green spaces?

Model cevap (Environment setinden, görüş–karşı baskı–gerekçe kalıbında):

Q: Whose responsibility is it to protect the environment, governments or individuals?

[Görüş] Honestly, I'd argue it has to be both, but the real weight falls on governments. [Karşı baskı] Of course, individuals matter — if nobody recycles or cuts down on waste, no policy will work, and it's easy to say "it's not my problem." [Kendi gerekçen] But the truth is, one person switching off a light barely makes a dent, whereas a government can regulate entire industries, tax pollution and invest in clean energy overnight. So while personal habits set the tone, only governments have the scale to make a genuine difference.
