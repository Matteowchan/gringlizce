Grammar: Linking & Complex Sentences

Study the rules, look at the examples, then do the exercises.

From simple sentences to complex ideas

At A2 you joined ideas with a few basic words: and, but, so, because. That is enough for short sentences, but real B1 writing and speaking need more. To sound natural, you must show contrast (two ideas that clash), give a reason, and describe a result — all inside longer, connected sentences.

This unit gives you the tools for that. The most important skill is knowing what comes after each linking word: some need a full clause (subject + verb), while others need only a noun or an -ing form. Choosing the wrong one is the most common B1 mistake, so we will focus on it carefully.

  • Although it was raining, we went out.although + clause (it was raining) → contrast.
  • Despite the rain, we went out.despite + noun (the rain) → same meaning, different structure.
  • My schoolbag was so heavy that I could not lift it.so + adjective + that → result.

1. Contrast with a clause (although / even though / though)

Use although, even though or though to connect two ideas that are surprising together. The key rule: each of these is followed by a full clause (a subject and a verb). Even though is a stronger, more emphatic version of although; though is more informal and can also go at the end of a sentence.

LinkerWhat followsExample
althoughsubject + verb (a clause)Although he was tired, he kept doing his homework.
even thoughsubject + verb (stronger contrast)Even though it was late, she finished her drawing.
thoughsubject + verb (informal; also end position)It was a good cartoon. I did not like the ending, though.
Key point
  • The contrast linker can start the sentence or join the middle: "Although she was nervous, she sang well." = "She sang well, although she was nervous."
  • When it starts the sentence, put a comma before the second clause.

2. Contrast with a noun (despite / in spite of)

Despite and in spite of mean exactly the same as although, but the grammar is different: they are followed by a noun, a pronoun, or an -ing form — never a full clause. This is the single most important table in the unit.

StructureCorrect formExample
despite / in spite of + nouna noun phraseDespite the noise, I slept well.
despite / in spite of + -ingverb + -ingIn spite of feeling ill, she came to school.
despite / in spite of + the fact that + clausethe only way to use a clauseDespite the fact that it was late, we kept playing.
The critical difference — learn this pair
  • Although + clause: "Although the weather was bad, we played outside." ✓
  • Despite + noun: "Despite the bad weather, we played outside." ✓
  • Do NOT mix them: "Despite the weather was bad..." ✗ — "Although the bad weather..." ✗

3. Reason (because vs because of)

Both give a reason, and again the difference is only in the grammar. Because is followed by a clause; because of is followed by a noun.

LinkerWhat followsExample
becausesubject + verb (a clause)We stayed in because it was raining.
because ofa noun / noun phraseWe stayed in because of the rain.
  • The match was cancelled because the field was too wet.because + clause (the field was too wet).
  • The match was cancelled because of the heavy rain.because of + noun (the heavy rain).

4. Result (so ... that / such ... that)

To show that something is so extreme that it causes a result, English uses two patterns. The choice depends on whether an adjective stands alone or comes with a noun.

PatternStructureExample
so ... thatso + adjective + thatThe soup was so hot that I burned my mouth.
such ... thatsuch + (a/an) + adjective + noun + thatIt was such a hot day that we stayed inside.
Quick test
  • Is there a noun after the adjective? Use such: such a long cartoon that...
  • Is the adjective alone? Use so: so long that...
  • Never write "so long cartoon" ✗ or "such long that" ✗.

5. Linking two sentences (however)

However is a connecting adverb. Unlike but, it usually joins two separate sentences and shows a contrast between them. Punctuation matters: put a full stop (or a semicolon) before it, and a comma after it.

WordPosition & punctuationExample
butjoins two clauses inside one sentenceThe book was short, but it was very good.
howeverstarts a new sentence + commaThe book was short. However, it was very good.
Watch out
  • Do NOT join two sentences with only a comma before however: "The book was short, however, it was good." ✗ Use a full stop or semicolon: "The book was short. However, it was good." ✓

6. Common mistakes (✗ / ✓)

These are the errors B1 students make most often with linking words. Almost all of them come from putting the wrong thing (a clause or a noun) after the linker.

Wrong ✗Right ✓Why?
Although the bad weather, we went out.Despite the bad weather, we went out.a noun follows → use despite / in spite of.
Despite it was raining, we went out.Although it was raining, we went out.a clause follows → use although / even though.
The match stopped because of it rained.The match stopped because it rained.a clause follows → use because, not because of.
It was so nice weather that we walked.It was such nice weather that we walked.an adjective + noun follows → use such.
It was cheap, however it was good.It was cheap. However, it was good.however needs a full stop before it and a comma after.

7. Putting it together (Model Dialogue)

Two friends talk about a school sports-day race. Read it twice and notice how each linking word is followed by the right structure — a clause after although / because, a noun after despite / because of, and the so/such ... that pattern for results.

  • — You won the race! Although you hurt your knee, you didn't stop.although + clause (you hurt your knee).
  • — Thanks. Despite the pain, I kept going.despite + noun (the pain).
  • — Why did you almost give up at the end?Wh- question with the Past Simple.
  • — Because of the hot sun. It was so hot that I felt dizzy.because of + noun; so + adjective + that.
  • — But you kept running because you had practised for weeks.because + clause (you had practised).
  • — Exactly. It was such a hard race that I cried at the finish. However, I would do it again!such + (a) + adj + noun + that; However + comma.
Test yourself
  • Find the sentence with despite. What kind of word follows it? (A noun: "the pain".)
  • Rewrite "Despite the pain, I kept going" with although. (Although I was in pain, I kept going.)
  • Why is it "such a hard race" and not "so a hard race"? (There is a noun after the adjective, so we use such.)

Speaking: Challenges & How We Overcome Them

Learn the words, then use them — with linking words — to talk about a real challenge.

challenge (n)
a difficult task that tests you (zorluk, meydan okuma)
Syn: difficulty · Ant: ease
"Learning to ride a bike was a real challenge."
overcome (v)
to succeed in dealing with a problem (üstesinden gelmek)
Syn: beat, master · Ant: surrender to
"Despite the fear, she overcame the problem."
effort (n)
the energy you use to do something hard (çaba)
Syn: hard work · Ant: ease
"It took a lot of effort, but I finished."
patience (n)
the ability to wait or stay calm (sabır)
Syn: calmness · Ant: impatience
"Learning a language needs patience."
determined (adj)
wanting to do something very much and not stopping (kararlı)
Syn: firm · Ant: hesitant
"She was determined to win the school race."
succeed (v)
to get the result you wanted (başarmak)
Syn: manage · Ant: fail
"Although it was hard, he succeeded."
give up (phr v)
to stop trying to do something (pes etmek, vazgeçmek)
Syn: quit · Ant: keep going
"It was hard, but I did not give up."
difficulty (n)
a problem or something hard to do (güçlük, sorun)
Syn: problem · Ant: ease
"In spite of the difficulty, we continued."
opportunity (n)
a chance to do something good (fırsat)
Syn: chance · Ant: missed chance
"The school trip was a great opportunity."
reliable (adj)
that you can trust and depend on (güvenilir)
Syn: dependable · Ant: unreliable
"My old bike is slow but reliable."
improve (v)
to make or become better (geliştirmek, ilerlemek)
Syn: get better · Ant: worsen
"My English has improved a lot."
achieve (v)
to succeed in doing something after trying (başarmak, elde etmek)
Syn: accomplish · Ant: fail
"She achieved her goal in the end."
despite (prep)
used to show contrast (+ noun/-ing) (-e rağmen)
Syn: in spite of · Ant: because of
"Despite the rain, we kept walking."
however (adv)
used to link two contrasting sentences (ancak, bununla birlikte)
Syn: nevertheless · Ant: therefore
"It was hard. However, I finished it."
Talking about challenges: use although, despite, because and so ... that.

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Before You Read (Okumadan Önce)

Good readers prepare before they start. Before you read, think about these three questions (you do not need to write the answers):

  • Think about a person you know who succeeded after many difficulties. What helped them keep going?
  • The title is "Against the Odds". What kind of story do you expect — easy success or a hard struggle?
  • This unit is about linking words. As you read, underline every although, despite, because and so ... that you find.
How to read
  • First read: read quickly, without stopping. Get the general idea; you do not need every word.
  • Second read: read the questions, then search the text for the answers. Notice how each linking word is followed by a clause or a noun.

Words to spot (from the Speaking section): challenge · overcome · effort · patience · determined · succeed · difficulty · improve

A garden built against the odds: from a few small pots to a whole school garden.
Against the Odds: Lila's Garden Club

Lila runs the most popular club at her school, but it did not start easily. Although she always loved growing plants, she never had a big garden, because her family lived in a small flat. Despite this difficult start, she never gave up on her dream.

Lila grew up in a small flat near the edge of her town. Her parents worked very long hours, so she often spent her afternoons on the balcony with her grandmother. Together they grew tomatoes, beans and bright flowers in old pots and empty cans. Her grandmother used to say that good plants do not need expensive things; they only need care and time. Lila listened carefully and remembered every word. Even as a little girl, she loved the smell of wet soil after rain and the sight of a new green leaf opening in the sun. She dreamed of a real garden full of vegetables, butterflies and busy bees, where all her friends could come and help. She did not know yet how she would make it, but the dream stayed with her for many years.

When she was ten, Lila joined the school garden helpers. The work was so hard that she often wanted to quit. However, she stayed because she wanted to learn everything about plants. In spite of the long afternoons, she grew new seeds at home every evening, while her friends were out playing.

During those first weeks, Lila did almost every job. She dug the soil, pulled out weeds and carried heavy bags of earth. At first, the old school gardener, Mr Kaya, did not notice her, but he slowly saw that she was always the first to arrive and the last to leave. "You work harder than anyone here," he said one day. "If you keep learning, one day you will have your own garden." Lila never forgot those words, and she wrote everything he taught her in a small notebook.

After two years, Lila decided to start her own garden club. It was such a big risk that her friends were worried. The first month was very difficult. Because of a slow start, almost no one joined, and she nearly gave up. Even though the club was nearly empty for weeks, Lila kept improving her little garden with patience and effort.

Those first weeks were the hardest of all. Some afternoons nobody came at all, so Lila gave away small pots of flowers to children who walked past and asked them what she could do better. Slowly, a few children began to come back, and each new face gave her a little more hope.

Then things changed. The school newspaper wrote about her fresh vegetables and bright flowers, and more children arrived. Although she was still tired every day, she felt proud, because she had overcome so many difficulties. Her club became so well known that children from other schools now come to visit.

Today, Lila still works hard in the garden, but she is very happy. She often tells younger children that talent is important; however, hard work and patience matter even more. Despite all the challenges, she believes that anyone can succeed if they stay determined and never stop trying.

Writing: A Challenge I Overcame

Write and submit your paragraph; your teacher will review it.

Linking words build complex sentences (Linking Words)

A list of short, separate sentences is not strong B1 writing. What makes writing mature is the connections between ideas. This unit gives you a full set of linkers for contrast, reason and result. Use them — and remember the golden rule: put the right structure after each one.

ConnectorUse it to...Example
although / even thoughshow contrast (+ clause)Although it was hard, I finished.
despite / in spite ofshow contrast (+ noun / -ing)Despite the difficulty, I finished.
becausegive a reason (+ clause)I stayed because I wanted to learn.
because ofgive a reason (+ noun)We stopped because of the rain.
so ... thatshow a result (+ adjective)It was so hard that I nearly quit.
such ... thatshow a result (+ noun phrase)It was such a challenge that I grew stronger.
howevercontrast between two sentencesI failed once. However, I tried again.

See the difference — the same facts, written two ways:

Separate sentences ✗Connected, fluent writing ✓
I wanted to learn to ride a bike. I was nervous. I fell on my first try. I did not give up. I rode well later. Although I wanted to learn to ride a bike, I was very nervous. Because of my nerves, I fell on my first try. However, I did not give up, and I rode well later.
Rule
  • Use at least 3 different connectors from this unit. Do not write sentence by sentence; link your ideas.
  • Remember: although + clause, despite + noun. When a linker starts a sentence, use a comma: "However, I tried again."

Paragraph structure (Paragraph Structure)

A "challenge" paragraph has three parts. The skeleton stays the same every time — you change the content, not the structure:

PartWhat to writeExample
1. OpeningName the challenge in one general sentenceLearning to ride a bike was one of the biggest challenges of my life.
2. BodyThe difficulties and how you dealt with them. Use contrast (although / despite), reason (because / because of) and result (so / such ... that).Although I was nervous, I practised every weekend. Because of my effort, I slowly improved.
3. ClosingWhat you learned, or how you feel nowHowever, I never gave up, and today I feel proud.

Task (Görev)

Write about a challenge you have faced and how you dealt with it. Show contrast, give reasons and describe results with linking words.

WHAT TO INCLUDE

  • an opening sentence that names the challenge
  • the main difficulties you faced (contrast with although / despite)
  • the reason you kept going (because / because of)
  • a result you remember (so ... that / such ... that)
  • a closing sentence about what you learned or how you feel now
  • at least 3 connectors: although, even though, despite, in spite of, because, because of, so ... that, such ... that, however

Sample Answer (~165 words) Learning to ride a bike was one of the biggest challenges of my life. Although I really wanted to ride like my friends, I was very afraid of falling. Because I was so nervous, I fell many times and hurt my knees.

In spite of those falls, I did not give up. I practised every weekend in the park, even though I was often tired. My first tries were so difficult that I almost stopped, but my dad helped me. Because of his patience, I slowly improved, and riding became such a fun game that I wanted to do it all day.

I could finally ride alone last summer. However, the real challenge was riding to school for the first time. Despite my fear, I rode all the way with a big smile. I made a few small mistakes, but I felt proud. This challenge taught me that effort and patience can help you overcome almost any difficulty.
