Sınav Hakkında

Bu ara sınav, Ünite 1-10'da öğrendiğin her şeyi C2 (native-yakını incelik) seviyesinde ölçer: register ve üslup aralığı; öne çekme (fronting) ve tematik konum; end-weight ve end-focus; cleft yapılarının ustalığı; retorik tercih olarak devrik yapılar (inversion); hedging, boosting ve stance (duruş); nominalizasyon ve dilbilgisel eğretileme; bağdaşıklık (gönderim/yerine koyma/eksilti); söylem belirteçleri ve metin örgütlenmesi; ve modalitede ince ayrım. Speaking hariç dört beceri test edilir; konular okul, arkadaşlar, spor takımı, sınavlar ve dijital hayat etrafında döner. Toplam süre 100 dakikadır ve tek bir sayaçla ilerler — süreyi bölümler arasında kendin yönetirsin.

Reading (2 metin · 20 soru)

Text 1 · The Trouble with Multitasking

Ask almost any teenager how they revise for an exam, and the answer is likely to involve several things happening at once: a textbook open on the desk, a playlist in the background, and a phone glowing quietly beside the keyboard. Doing everything at the same time feels efficient, even impressive. Yet a growing body of research suggests that what we proudly call multitasking is, for the most part, an illusion.

The brain, it turns out, cannot truly attend to two demanding tasks simultaneously. What actually happens is 'task-switching': attention darts back and forth between activities, and each switch carries a small hidden cost. A message is checked, a reply is typed, and by the time the eyes return to the page, the thread of the argument has been lost and must be picked up again from the beginning.

These costs are easy to overlook precisely because each one is tiny. A few seconds here, a moment of confusion there — none of it seems to matter. Over the course of an evening, however, the losses accumulate, and a task that should have taken an hour stretches comfortably into two. Worse still, work produced in this fragmented state tends to be shallower, riddled with small errors that a more focused mind would have caught.

It would be unfair, though, to blame young people alone for this habit. The devices themselves are engineered to interrupt: every buzz and banner is designed to pull attention away from whatever came before. Resisting such a steady stream of invitations requires a kind of discipline that even adults struggle to muster, and it is hardly surprising that students, surrounded by the same technology, find it no easier.

The good news is that concentration, like a muscle, can be trained. Those who set aside their phones, work in short focused bursts and take deliberate breaks report not only better results but, curiously, less exhaustion at the end. The problem was never the amount of work, it seems, but the constant switching — and that, at least, is something within our control.

Text 2 · The Last Match

Elif had promised herself she would not cry, and for most of the afternoon she managed it. It was the final match of her last season, and although the score no longer mattered — her team had already lost the league weeks before — the changing room that morning had felt unusually quiet, as though everyone understood that something was ending.

For six years the old sports hall had been the fixed point of her week. She knew every squeak of its floor, every draught from its high windows, the particular smell of dust and polish that greeted her at the door. Other things in her life had come and gone, but this place, and the friends she had made in it, had stayed reliably the same.

The match itself passed in a blur. She remembered, oddly, not the goals but the small things: the captain's shout across the court, the coach folding his arms in the way he always did, the sunlight falling in long stripes through the windows. Memory, she would later think, keeps not what we expect but what it chooses.

When the final whistle blew, nobody rushed to leave. The players lingered, half-dressed, telling the same old jokes they had told a hundred times, reluctant to admit that there would be no next week. Elif looked around the hall one last time and found, to her surprise, that she felt no sadness — only a quiet gratitude that it had all happened at all.

On the bus home that evening, her kit bag heavy on her lap, she realised that the team had not really ended. Its matches were over, certainly, but everything it had taught her — how to lose well, how to trust others, how to keep going when tired — she would carry with her, quietly, for a long time to come.

Writing (2 görev)

Suggested time: 35 minutes — Task 1: 13 min, Task 2: 22 min. (Önerilen süre: 35 dakika — Görev 1: 13 dk, Görev 2: 22 dk.) Öğretmenin değerlendirecek; örnek cevap yoktur.

Task 1 · Opinion Email (120-150 words)

Your school council has proposed turning the main courtyard into a phone-free zone during breaks, where students may only talk, read or play games. The school magazine has asked students for their views. Write a formal email to the magazine giving your opinion. (Okul meclisin ana avluyu teneffüslerde telefonsuz bir bölgeye çevirmeyi önerdi; öğrenciler yalnızca sohbet edebilecek, okuyabilecek ya da oyun oynayabilecek. Okul dergisi öğrencilerin görüşünü soruyor. Görüşünü belirten resmî bir e-posta yaz.)

  • Maintain a consistent formal register throughout. (baştan sona tutarlı resmî register)
  • Use at least one cleft or inversion for emphasis ("It is the constant scrolling that..."; "Not only would this..."). (vurgu için en az bir cleft ya da devrik yapı)
  • Hedge or boost at least one claim ("The proposal may well..."; "There is no doubt that..."). (en az bir iddiada hedging/boosting)
  • Use at least 3 discourse markers: however, nevertheless, moreover, therefore. (en az 3 söylem belirteci)
  • 120-150 words. (120-150 kelime)
E-POSTA YAPISI
  • Açılış: "Dear Editor," + neden yazdığın ("I am writing to share my view on...").
  • Gövde: görüşün + en az iki gerekçe; bir cleft/devrik yapıyla vurgu ver.
  • Kapanış: görüşünü özetle + "Yours faithfully," + adın.
0 kelime

Task 2 · For-and-Against Paragraph (160-200 words)

Some people believe that online lessons will one day replace ordinary schools completely. Others strongly disagree. Discuss the arguments for and against this view, and then give your own opinion. (Bazıları çevrimiçi derslerin bir gün sıradan okulların yerini tamamen alacağını düşünür; bazıları buna kesinlikle katılmaz. Bu görüşün lehindeki ve aleyhindeki argümanları tartış, sonra kendi görüşünü ver.)

  • Introduce the topic, then give one paragraph FOR and one AGAINST. (biri lehte, biri aleyhte)
  • Use at least one nominalisation ("the rapid rise in...") and one cleft/inversion. (bir nominalizasyon + bir cleft/devrik)
  • Signal your stance with hedging or boosting ("It could be argued..."; "There is little doubt..."). (hedging/boosting ile duruş)
  • Use at least 5 discourse markers: however, whereas, nevertheless, moreover, therefore. (en az 5 söylem belirteci)
  • End with a clear opinion. (net bir görüşle bitir)
  • 160-200 words. (160-200 kelime)
