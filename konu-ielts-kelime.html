1. Kelime IELTS'te nasıl puanlanır? (Lexical Resource)

Writing ve Speaking'in dört ölçütünden biri Lexical Resource (LR) — kelime dağarcığı. Ama LR "zor kelime bilmek" değildir. Buradan çıkan strateji

1. How is vocabulary scored in IELTS? (Lexical Resource)

One of the four criteria in Writing and Speaking is Lexical Resource (LR) — vocabulary. But LR is not about "knowing hard words". The examiner looks at three things: range (not repeating the same word), precision (choosing the exactly right word) and naturalness (collocation — the correct partner of words). A fancy word used in the wrong place scores lower than a plain word used correctly.

"Lexical Resource measures not "hard words" but precision and naturalness: a fancy word in the wrong place cannot beat a plain word in the right one.
BandVocabulary profile
5Limited and repetitive; basic words are correct but vague terms like good, bad, important, thing; collocation errors make meaning harder to follow.
6Adequate for the topic; some less common words are attempted but with errors; collocation occasionally wrong (do a mistake).
7Flexible use; awareness of less common words and collocation; occasional word-choice/spelling error but meaning is clear.
8Wide and fluent vocabulary; uses rare words and idiomatic language with ease; catches subtle shades of meaning (connotation); errors are very rare and minor.
9Natural and subtle; rare words perfectly placed; flawless collocation; almost no errors.

The difference between the bands in this table is not, as most students think, "how many words you know". Between Band 5 and Band 7 the number of words known matters less than you might expect; the real difference is how precisely and naturally a known word is placed. Someone writing at Band 5 might also know the word detrimental; the difference is whether they can use it inside a correct phrase like a detrimental effect on health, in the right context. So set your goal not as "memorising more words" but as "placing the words you know flawlessly".

If you are at band 5-6, your goal

Systematically hunt down vague words (good, bad, thing, a lot) and replace them with precise equivalents. Close the basic collocation errors (make/do, heavy/strong). This alone takes you to 6.5.

The climb to band 6.5-7

For each theme, know 8-10 core collocations not from memory but from production. Make your paraphrasing meaningful, not mechanical. Separate register (Writing vs Speaking). These three steps are the threshold of 7.

Band 7.5+ fine-tuning

Use idiomatic language and subtle shades of meaning (connotation) correctly; avoid the show-off word. Combine two themes naturally in a single sentence. A few perfectly precise rare words beat many half-right ones.

The strategy that follows from this

The key to moving from 6 to 7 is not "harder words" but collocation + precision. This page gives not a vocabulary list but a method for using vocabulary correctly. For the raw list (tiered 1000/3000/5000), we point you to the Word Bank and IELTS Vocabulary practice at the end of the page.

2. Kelime öğrenmenin doğru yolu — üretim testi

Bir kelimeyi "tanımak" (okuyunca anlamak) ile "üretmek" (kendi cümlende doğru kullanmak) farklı şeylerdir. IELTS üretimi ölçer. Kural: bir kelime, ancak kendi cümlende doğru kullandığında senin olur.

Bunun arkasındaki ayrım pasif ve aktif kelime dağarcığıdır. Pasif dağarcık okuyunca/duyunca tanıdığın kelimelerdir; aktif dağarcık ise kendi cümlende rahatça ürettiklerindir. Çoğu öğrencinin pasif dağarcığı aktifinden kat kat büyüktür — ve IELTS Writing ile Speaking yalnızca aktif dağarcığı ölçer. Bu yüzden "yeni kelime tanımak" hedefi yanıltıcıdır; asıl iş, pasif bildiğin kelimeleri tekrar tekrar üreterek aktife taşımaktır. İki yüz kelimeyi pasif tanımaktansa elli kelimeyi aktif kullanabilmek sınavda çok daha yüksek puan getirir.

Yanlış yöntem
Kelime + Türkçe karşılığını ezberleyip geçmek. Sınavda kelime aklına gelir ama hangi kelimeyle kullanıldığını (collocation) ve hangi tonu taşıdığını bilemezsin.

Doğru yöntem
Kelimeyi öbek (chunk) olarak öğren: a sedentary lifestyle, to allocate funds. Bir örnek cümlede gör, sonra kendi cümleni kur. Konuya göre grupla (aşağıdaki 7 tema gibi).

Günlük ritim (kanıtlı)

  • Günde 8–10 kelime, öbek hâlinde. Fazlası akılda kalmaz.
  • Her kelime için bir kendi cümlen. Yazmadıysan öğrenmemişsindir.
  • Haftada bir aktif hatırlama: Türkçesine bakıp İngilizce öbeği üret (tersi değil).
  • Konu bazlı çalış: bir hafta Environment, bir hafta Education. Sınavda tema tema hazır gelirsin.

2. The right way to learn vocabulary — the production test

"Recognising" a word (understanding it when you read it) and "producing" it (using it correctly in your own sentence) are different things. IELTS measures production. The rule: a word is yours only once you have used it correctly in your own sentence.

The distinction behind this is between passive and active vocabulary. Passive vocabulary is the words you recognise when you read/hear them; active vocabulary is what you can comfortably produce in your own sentence. Most students' passive vocabulary is many times larger than their active — and IELTS Writing and Speaking measure only the active. So the goal of "recognising new words" is misleading; the real work is moving words you know passively into the active store by producing them again and again. Being able to actively use fifty words scores far higher on the exam than passively recognising two hundred.

The wrong method
Memorising a word + its Turkish equivalent and moving on. In the exam the word comes to mind but you don't know which word it is used with (collocation) or what tone it carries.
↔
The right method
Learn the word as a chunk: a sedentary lifestyle, to allocate funds. See it in an example sentence, then build your own sentence. Group by theme (like the 7 themes below).

Daily rhythm (evidence-based)

  • 8–10 words a day, as chunks. More than that will not stick.
  • One sentence of your own for each word. If you didn't write it, you haven't learned it.
  • Once a week, active recall: look at the Turkish and produce the English chunk (not the other way round).
  • Study by theme: one week Environment, one week Education. In the exam you arrive ready theme by theme.

3.1 · Collocation türleri — hangi kelime hangiyle eşleşir

Collocation rastgele değildir; belli dilbilgisel kalıplarda oluşur. Bu kalıpları tanırsan yeni bir kelime gördüğünde "bunun tipik eşi ne olur?" diye doğru soruyu sorarsın. IELTS'te en çok işine yarayacak dört tür şunlardır.

Sıfat + İsimniteleyen + isima heated debate, a common misconception, a viable alternative, a compelling argument, adverse effects, a pressing concern, ample opportunity

Bu dört türü ezberden geçmek yerine birkaç girdiyi derinlemesine anlamak, kalıbı içselleştirmeni sağlar. Fiil+isim türünde raise awareness öbeğine bak: Türkçe düşünen öğrenci "farkındalığı artırmak" dediği için otomatik olarak increase awareness yazar; bu dilbilgisel olarak anlaşılır ama İngilizcede bilinci yükseltmenin doğal fiili raise'dir (raise awareness / raise a concern / raise the standard). Küçük görünen bu seçim, değerlendiriciye kelimeyi bağlamıyla öğrendiğini gösterir ve tam da Band 6 ile 7'yi ayıran "collocation farkındalığı"dır. Sıfat+isim türünde adverse effects öbeği bir başka tipik hatayı düzeltir: öğrenci "kötü etkiler" demek isteyince bad effects yazar, oysa akademik yazıda olumsuz sonucun doğal eşi adverse ya da detrimental'dır ve bunlar yalnızca effects, consequences, impact gibi belirli isimlerle oturur. Son olarak isim+isim türündeki life expectancy (yaşam süresi değil "beklenen ömür") tek kelimelik bir kavramı iki isimle karşılamanın nasıl daha sıkı ve akademik durduğunu gösterir; bu kalıbı tanıyan öğrenci crime rate, job satisfaction, peer pressure gibi onlarca öbeği aynı mantıkla üretebilir.

İPUCU — zarf+sıfat en hızlı yükseltir

Öğrencilerin en çok ihmal ettiği tür zarf+sıfattır ama en hızlı puan getirenlerden biridir. very important yerine critically important, very common yerine increasingly common yazmak tek bir kelimeyle cümleyi Band 5 tonundan Band 7 tonuna taşır. very kelimesini denemenden çıkarmayı bir alıştırma olarak dene.

"Zayıf" ile "güçlü" collocation farkı

Bir kelimenin çok yaygın, "zayıf" bir eşi (a big problem) ile daha isabetli, "güçlü" bir eşi (a pressing problem) vardır. İkisi de dilbilgisel olarak doğrudur; ama güçlü collocation Lexical Resource puanını yukarı çeker çünkü kelime seçiminde bilinç gösterir. Aşağıdaki üç örnek aynı fikri zayıftan güçlüye taşıyor:

a big difference → a marked difference · a large number of people → a substantial proportion of the population · a hard decision → a difficult trade-off

3. Collocation — the heart of natural vocabulary

A collocation is a pair of words used together. English says heavy rain, not strong rain; it says make a decision, not do a decision. Because Turkish students translate word by word, they lose the most marks here. Memorise collocations; do not translate them one by one.

✗ Word-for-word translation error✓ Correct collocation
strong rainheavy rain
do a decision / do a mistakemake a decision / make a mistake
say your opinionexpress / give your opinion
big problem (weak)a serious / pressing problem
gain moneyearn money / make money
high knowledgeextensive / in-depth knowledge
make a sportdo / play a sport

Rule

When you learn a new noun, note down which verb/adjective it takes as well. In the dictionary, look not at the word but at its partner in the example sentence. Band 7+ comes with collocation awareness.

3.1 · Types of collocation — which word pairs with which

Collocation is not random; it forms in certain grammatical patterns. If you recognise these patterns, then when you see a new word you ask the right question: "what is its typical partner?" The four types most useful to you in IELTS are these.

TypePatternExamples
Verb + Nounaction + objectraise awareness, meet a deadline, pose a threat, tackle a problem, draw a conclusion, conduct research, achieve a goal, address an issue
Adjective + Nounmodifier + nouna heated debate, a common misconception, a viable alternative, a compelling argument, adverse effects, a pressing concern, ample opportunity
Noun + Nountwo nouns combinedcrime rate, poverty line, job satisfaction, life expectancy, traffic congestion, peer pressure, climate change
Adverb + Adjectiveintensifier + adjectivedeeply concerned, widely accepted, highly effective, entirely dependent, closely linked, readily available, increasingly popular

Rather than skimming through these four types, understanding a few entries in depth lets you internalise the pattern. In the verb+noun type, look at the phrase raise awareness: because the Turkish-thinking student says "increase awareness", they automatically write increase awareness; this is grammatically understandable but the natural verb for lifting awareness in English is raise (raise awareness / raise a concern / raise the standard). This small-seeming choice shows the examiner that you learned the word with its context, and it is exactly the "collocation awareness" that separates Band 6 from 7. In the adjective+noun type, the phrase adverse effects corrects another typical error: when the student wants to say "bad effects" they write bad effects, whereas in academic writing the natural partner of a negative outcome is adverse or detrimental, and these sit only with certain nouns like effects, consequences, impact. Finally, the noun+noun item life expectancy shows how covering a one-word concept with two nouns stands tighter and more academic; a student who recognises this pattern can produce dozens of phrases like crime rate, job satisfaction, peer pressure by the same logic.

TIP — adverb+adjective raises you fastest

The type students most neglect is adverb+adjective, yet it is one of the fastest to earn marks. Writing critically important instead of very important, or increasingly common instead of very common, lifts a sentence from a Band 5 tone to a Band 7 tone with a single word. Try removing the word very from your essay as an exercise.

The difference between a "weak" and a "strong" collocation

A word has a very common, "weak" partner (a big problem) and a more apt, "strong" partner (a pressing problem). Both are grammatically correct; but the strong collocation raises the Lexical Resource score because it shows conscious word choice. The three examples below take the same idea from weak to strong:

a big difference → a marked difference · a large number of people → a substantial proportion of the population · a hard decision → a difficult trade-off

4. Paraphrase ve eş anlamlı üretimi

IELTS aynı kelimeyi tekrar etmeni cezalandırır. Hem soruyu parafraz etmen (kopyalamamak) hem de yazı boyunca eş anlamlıları döndürmen gerekir. Aşağıdaki tablo en çok tekrar edilen kelimelerin akademik alternatiflerini verir.

cause (fiil)lead to, result in, give rise to, trigger, bring about

Bu listeyi güvenle kullanmanın sırrı, eş anlamlıların birebir değiştirilebilir olmadığını görmektir; her alternatif kendi bağlamını ister. increase satırına bak: rise ve grow nötr ve her yerde geçerlidir, ama surge yalnızca ani ve büyük bir sıçramayı anlatır — prices surged doğaldır, oysa yavaş bir değişimi surged ile anlatmak abartı kaçar ve isabeti bozar. Aynı şekilde cause satırındaki give rise to ile trigger arasında ton farkı vardır: trigger ani, tek bir tetikleyici olayı ima eder (the decision triggered protests), give rise to ise yavaş yavaş bir sonuç doğurmayı anlatır. En kritik satır good/bad'tir: burada amaç yalnızca "daha havalı kelime" değil, belirsiz bir değer yargısını ölçülebilir bir yargıya çevirmektir — a bad effect yerine a detrimental effect yazmak, cümleye hem akademik ton hem netlik katar. Türk öğrencinin en sık hatası, bu alternatiflerden rastgele birini seçip yanlış bağlama sokmaktır; doğru yöntem, her yeni eş anlamlıyı bir örnek cümleyle görüp hangi durumda oturduğunu not etmektir.

Dikkat — aşırı parafraz tuzağı

Her kelimeyi eş anlamlısıyla değiştirmeye çalışma; teknik terimler (temperature, percentage, unemployment) korunur. Zorlama eş anlamlı (jobless situation gibi) doğallığı bozar ve LR'ı düşürür. Amaç doğal çeşitlilik, gösteriş değil.

4.1 · Dört paraphrase tekniği

Parafraz sadece "eş anlamlı bul" değildir. Usta yazar aynı fikri dört ayrı kaldıraçla yeniden söyler ve bunları birleştirir. Eş anlamlı bulamadığında diğer üçünü kullanırsın — bu yüzden gerçek parafraz gücü kelime hazinesinden çok bu tekniklere hâkimiyettir.

  1. Eş anlamlı değişimi: anahtar kelimeyi isabetli bir eşiyle değiştir. children → youngsters, cars → private vehicles. En bilinen ama en sınırlı teknik; tek başına yetmez.
  2. Sözcük türü değiştirme (word form): fiili isme, sıfatı isme çevirerek cümle iskeletini değiştir. Prices rose sharply → There was a sharp rise in prices. People are increasingly reliant on… → People's growing reliance on…
  3. Aktif ↔ Pasif çevirimi: özne-nesne yerini değiştir. The government funds the programme → The programme is funded by the government. Özellikle Task 1 harita/süreç ve neden-sonuç cümlelerinde işe yarar.
  4. Genelleme / özelleştirme: dar kavramı üst kategoriyle, geniş kavramı bir örnekle karşıla. Facebook and Instagram → social media platforms; unhealthy food → processed food high in sugar and fat.
Task 1 uygulaması
Soru cümlesi: The chart shows the number of visitors to three museums. Parafraz: The bar chart illustrates how many people visited three different museums (eş anlamlı shows→illustrates + sözcük türü number of visitors→how many people visited).
↔
Task 2 uygulaması
Soru cümlesi: Some people think children should start school at a very early age. Parafraz: It is argued by some that formal education ought to begin in early childhood (aktif→pasif people think→it is argued + genelleme start school→begin formal education).
Kural — soruyu asla aynen kopyalama

Giriş cümlende soru metnini birebir kopyalarsan o kelimeler kelime sayına sayılmaz ve değerlendirici parafraz beceresini göremez. İlk cümlede en az iki tekniği birlikte kullanmayı alışkanlık yap: bir eş anlamlı + bir sözcük türü değişimi çoğu soruyu güvenle karşılar.

4. Paraphrase and producing synonyms

IELTS penalises repeating the same word. You must both paraphrase the question (not copy it) and rotate synonyms throughout your writing. The table below gives academic alternatives for the most repeated words.

Commonly usedAcademic alternatives
importantsignificant, crucial, vital, key, essential
increase (verb)rise, grow, climb, surge, expand
reducecut, lower, curb, decrease, diminish
cause (verb)lead to, result in, give rise to, trigger, bring about
problemissue, drawback, obstacle, challenge, setback
good / bad (vague)beneficial / detrimental, positive / adverse
a lot ofnumerous, a great deal of, a considerable amount of
showillustrate, demonstrate, reveal, indicate

The secret to using this list safely is seeing that synonyms are not interchangeable; each alternative demands its own context. Look at the increase row: rise and grow are neutral and valid everywhere, but surge describes only a sudden, large jump — prices surged is natural, whereas describing a slow change with surged is an exaggeration and breaks precision. Likewise, in the cause row there is a tone difference between give rise to and trigger: trigger implies a sudden, single triggering event (the decision triggered protests), while give rise to describes producing a result gradually. The most critical row is good/bad: here the aim is not merely "a cooler word" but turning a vague value judgement into a measurable one — writing a detrimental effect instead of a bad effect adds both academic tone and clarity to the sentence. The Turkish student's most frequent error is picking one of these alternatives at random and dropping it into the wrong context; the right method is to see each new synonym in an example sentence and note when it fits.

Caution — the over-paraphrase trap

Do not try to replace every word with a synonym; technical terms (temperature, percentage, unemployment) are kept. A forced synonym (like jobless situation) breaks naturalness and lowers LR. The aim is natural variety, not show.

4.1 · Four paraphrase techniques

Paraphrase is not just "find a synonym". A skilled writer restates the same idea with four different levers and combines them. When you can't find a synonym, you use the other three — which is why real paraphrasing power comes less from vocabulary than from mastery of these techniques.

  1. Synonym substitution: replace the key word with an apt equivalent. children → youngsters, cars → private vehicles. The best-known but most limited technique; not enough on its own.
  2. Changing word form: change the sentence skeleton by turning a verb into a noun, an adjective into a noun. Prices rose sharply → There was a sharp rise in prices. People are increasingly reliant on… → People's growing reliance on…
  3. Active ↔ Passive conversion: swap subject and object. The government funds the programme → The programme is funded by the government. Especially useful in Task 1 map/process and cause-effect sentences.
  4. Generalisation / specification: cover a narrow concept with a superordinate category, or a broad concept with an example. Facebook and Instagram → social media platforms; unhealthy food → processed food high in sugar and fat.
Task 1 application
Question sentence: The chart shows the number of visitors to three museums. Paraphrase: The bar chart illustrates how many people visited three different museums (synonym shows→illustrates + word form number of visitors→how many people visited).
↔
Task 2 application
Question sentence: Some people think children should start school at a very early age. Paraphrase: It is argued by some that formal education ought to begin in early childhood (active→passive people think→it is argued + generalisation start school→begin formal education).
Rule — never copy the question verbatim

If you copy the question text word for word in your introduction, those words do not count towards your word count and the examiner cannot see your paraphrasing skill. Make it a habit to use at least two techniques together in the first sentence: one synonym + one word-form change safely handles most questions.

5. Task 1 veri dili bankası

Task 1 görseli betimlemenin dili büyük ölçüde hareket fiili + derece ve karşılaştırma/oran ifadelerine dayanır. Bu iki eksene hâkim olmak, aslında Task 1'in kelime işinin yarısından fazlasını çözer: veri ya zaman içinde değişir (o zaman hareket fiili istersin) ya da başka bir veriyle kıyaslanır (o zaman karşılaştırma dili istersin). Türk öğrencinin buradaki en büyük tuzağı, aynı increase ya da the graph shows kalıbını cümle başına tekrarlayıp durmaktır; bu tekrar, değerlendiriciye kelime dağarcığının sınırlı olduğunu doğrudan gösterir ve Lexical Resource'u Band 5-6'da tutar. Kural basit: aynı fiili iki kez kullanma, aynı yapıyı üst üste bindirme — aşağıdaki iki kalıbı ve dereceleri döndürerek her cümleyi tazele.

Bu öbekleri ezberlemek yeterli değil; her grafik türünün kendi "gramerini" tanımak gerekir. Pie chart'ın kalbi oran fiilleridir (account for, make up, constitute) ve buradaki en yaygın hata ratio ile proportion karışıklığıdır: grafikte yalnızca yüzdeler varsa proportion ya da share denir, ratio ancak iki değer birbirine "X'e Y" biçiminde oranlandığında doğrudur. Aşağıdaki örnekler bu türleri bağlamda gösteriyor:

5. Task 1 data language bank

The language of describing a Task 1 visual rests largely on movement verb + degree and comparison/proportion expressions. Mastering these two axes actually solves more than half of the vocabulary work in Task 1: data either changes over time (then you need a movement verb) or is compared with other data (then you need comparison language). The Turkish student's biggest trap here is repeating the same increase or the graph shows pattern at the start of every sentence; this repetition directly signals to the examiner that your vocabulary is limited and keeps Lexical Resource at Band 5-6. The rule is simple: do not use the same verb twice, do not stack the same structure — refresh every sentence by rotating the two patterns and degrees below.

5.1 · Movement verbs and their degrees

DirectionVerb / nounDegree (adverb · adjective)
Increaserise, climb, surge, soar; a rise, growth, an upturnsharply/sharp, dramatically/dramatic, steeply/steep (large); steadily/steady, gradually/gradual (moderate); slightly/slight, marginally/marginal (small)
Decreasefall, decline, plunge, plummet, dip; a drop, a slump
Stable/fluctuatinglevel off, plateau, hold steady; fluctuate, vary; peak at, bottom out

Two patterns, rotate them: (1) verb+adverb → Unemployment fell sharply. (2) there+be+adjective+noun → There was a sharp fall in unemployment.

The power of these verbs emerges when they are matched with their degrees; being able to describe the same direction at different intensities shows in Task 1 that you "read the data correctly". Each line below shows a phrase in context, so you learn the word not alone but with its use:

Increase: Car ownership climbed steadily throughout the 1990s. · The population soared dramatically, more than doubling in a decade.
Decrease: Sales plummeted in the final quarter, hitting their lowest point in years. · Unemployment dipped slightly before recovering.
Stable/fluctuating: After a turbulent start, prices levelled off at around £50. · Attendance fluctuated throughout the year, peaking in summer.

A typical error to watch for is attaching strong verbs like plummet, soar, plateau to small changes: describing a two-per-cent decline with plummeted misrepresents the data and lowers both Lexical Resource and Task Achievement. The rule is simple — the intensity of the verb must match the intensity of the change in the graph.

5.2 · Core language by graph type

  • Overview: Overall, it is clear that…; The most striking feature is that…; The general trend was one of…
  • Bar (comparison): considerably higher than, far greater than, by far the largest, the second most common, roughly twice as high as
  • Pie (proportion): accounts for, makes up, constitutes, represents; the majority, a significant minority, just under a third (note: if there is no true ratio, do not say ratio, say proportion/share)
  • Map (passive): was built, was demolished, was converted into, was replaced by, was cleared to make way for; on the outskirts, adjacent to
  • Process (passive + sequence): Initially, Subsequently, Once this is complete, Ultimately; is collected, is heated, is filtered, is distributed
  • Approximation: approximately, roughly, just under, just over, well over, nearly
Model body: After peaking at around 80,000 units in 2008, sales plummeted dramatically over the subsequent two years before levelling off at roughly 30,000.

Memorising these phrases is not enough; you must recognise the "grammar" of each graph type. The heart of a pie chart is the proportion verbs (account for, make up, constitute) and the most common error here is confusing ratio with proportion: if the graph has only percentages, you say proportion or share; ratio is only correct when two values are given as "X to Y". The shared secret of map and process graphs is the passive voice: on a map, because the agent of the change is unimportant, passive patterns like a car park was built, the woodland was cleared are natural; in a process, the passive the beans are roasted, the mixture is filtered is repeated. The examples below show these types in context:

Overview: Overall, it is clear that renewable sources gained ground while coal declined steadily over the period.
Bar: Spending on housing was by far the largest category, roughly twice as high as that on transport.
Pie: Private cars accounted for just under half of all journeys, while public transport made up a significant minority.
Map: The open fields to the north were cleared to make way for a residential estate, and a small harbour was built on the coast.
Process: Initially, the raw clay is extracted; subsequently, it is shaped and left to dry before being fired in a kiln.

The second half of Task 1 is positioning the data relative to each other: which is higher, how many times, the highest/lowest, in what proportion. Reading numbers alone is Band 5; building relationships in words is Band 7+.

6. Task 2 işlevsel dil bankası

Task 2'de kelime işleve göre gruplanır: fikir bildirme, sıralama, neden-sonuç, zıtlık, hedging, sonuç. Task 1'de kelimeyi ne gördüğün (grafik) belirlerken, Task 2'de kelimeyi ne yaptığın (argüman kurmak, karşı çıkmak, örnek vermek) belirler; bu yüzden temaya değil işleve göre öbek biriktirmek çok daha verimlidir — çünkü hangi konu çıkarsa çıksın bir görüş bildirmen, bir neden göstermen ve bir sonuca varman gerekir. Türk öğrencinin tipik zaafı, bu işlevsel öbekleri ya hiç kullanmayıp cümleleri kuru bırakmak ya da tam tersine her cümlenin başına Moreover, Furthermore yapıştırıp yapaylaştırmaktır; ikisi de tonu düşürür. Doğru kullanıldığında bu öbekler fikirlerinin omurgasıdır: okuru bir düşünceden ötekine sorunsuz taşır ve denemene Band 7'nin istediği "mantıksal akış" hissini verir.

Görüş bildirmeIn my view; I firmly believe that; I am convinced that; While I acknowledge…, I would argue…
Sıralama/eklemeTo begin with; First and foremost; Furthermore; Moreover; Equally important

Bu öbekleri gerçek cümlelere yerleştirmeden ezberlemek en yaygın Task 2 hatasıdır; öbek yalnızca fikirlerin arasındaki mantık köprüsüdür, kendisi bir fikir değildir. Neden-sonuç sırasındaki stem from ile lead to ayrımına dikkat et: stem from bir sorunun kökenini geriye doğru işaret eder (this problem stems from poverty), lead to ise ileriye, sonuca bakar. Türk öğrencinin bir başka tipik hatası, görüş bildirirken her cümleye I think koymaktır; oysa While I acknowledge…, I would argue… kalıbı hem karşı görüşü tanıdığını hem kendi konumunu koruduğunu gösterir ve bu "dengeli argüman" tam da Band 7 Task Response ölçütünün istediğidir. Aşağıdaki örnekler her işlevi bağlamda gösteriyor:

Görüş + ödün: While I acknowledge that stricter laws can help, I would argue that education is the more sustainable solution.
Neden-sonuç: Traffic congestion largely stems from poor urban planning and, in turn, gives rise to higher levels of air pollution.
Örnek + sonuç: Take, for example, the case of Scandinavian countries, where investment in public transport has, to some extent, curbed car dependency.

Hedging neden puan getirir?

Değerlendiriciler mutlak iddia (This will solve the problem) yerine ölçülü iddiayı (This may, to some extent, reduce the problem) ödüllendirir. Hedging hem daha akademiktir hem aşırı genellemeyi önler. Türk öğrencilerin çoğu tam tersini yapar: Everyone knows that…, It is a fact that…, All people… gibi mutlak ifadeler kullanır. Bu ifadeler hem kanıtsız hem savunulamaz olduğu için puan düşürür. Many people, in most cases, tends to, is likely to gibi ölçülü kalıplarla değiştir.

İşlev öbeğini "boş" bırakma

İşlevsel öbek yalnızca bir bağlantıdır; asıl puan onun ardından gelen somut fikirdan gelir. Furthermore, it is important deyip cümleyi orada bırakmak boş bir kalıp yığınıdır. Öbeği her zaman gerçek bir gerekçe, örnek veya sonuçla doldur: Furthermore, subsidising public transport would ease congestion by giving commuters a viable alternative to driving. Değerlendirici kalıbı değil, kalıbın taşıdığı fikri ödüllendirir.

Essay türüne özel öbekler

  • Opinion: I largely agree, with some reservations; The strongest argument in favour is…; This argument carries little weight.
  • Discussion: supporters contend that; conversely, opponents argue; having considered both sides; while both arguments have merit.
  • Adv/Disadv: one major benefit is; a significant drawback is; this is offset by; the benefits far outweigh the drawbacks.
  • Problem/Solution: a pressing issue is; this problem is rooted in; one effective measure would be to; a viable solution lies in.
  • Two-part: Turning to the first question…; As for the second issue…; with regard to the latter point…

6. Task 2 functional language bank

In Task 2, vocabulary is grouped by function: expressing an opinion, sequencing, cause-effect, contrast, hedging, conclusion. While in Task 1 the vocabulary is determined by what you see (the graph), in Task 2 it is determined by what you do (build an argument, object, give an example); so collecting phrases by function rather than by theme is far more efficient — because whatever topic comes up, you have to state an opinion, give a reason and reach a conclusion. The Turkish student's typical weakness is either not using these functional phrases at all and leaving sentences dry, or the opposite — sticking a Moreover, Furthermore at the start of every sentence and making it artificial; both lower the tone. Used well, these phrases are the backbone of your ideas: they carry the reader smoothly from one thought to the next and give your essay the "logical flow" Band 7 demands.

FunctionPhrases (English)
Expressing opinionIn my view; I firmly believe that; I am convinced that; While I acknowledge…, I would argue…
Sequencing/additionTo begin with; First and foremost; Furthermore; Moreover; Equally important
Cause-effectgives rise to; leads to; stems from; owing to; as a result / consequently
Giving examplesFor instance; To illustrate; A case in point is; Take, for example, the case of…
Contrast/concessionHowever; Nevertheless; Admittedly; It could be argued that; Despite this
Hedging (moderation)tend to; are likely to; may; to some extent; arguably; in many cases
ConclusionIn conclusion; On balance; Weighing up both sides; The evidence suggests that

Memorising these phrases without placing them in real sentences is the most common Task 2 error; a phrase is only a logical bridge between ideas, not an idea in itself. Note the distinction between stem from and lead to in the cause-effect row: stem from points backwards to the origin of a problem (this problem stems from poverty), while lead to looks forward to a result. Another typical Turkish-student error is putting I think in every sentence when expressing an opinion; whereas the template While I acknowledge…, I would argue… shows both that you recognise the opposing view and that you hold your own position, and this "balanced argument" is exactly what the Band 7 Task Response criterion wants. The examples below show each function in context:

Opinion + concession: While I acknowledge that stricter laws can help, I would argue that education is the more sustainable solution.
Cause-effect: Traffic congestion largely stems from poor urban planning and, in turn, gives rise to higher levels of air pollution.
Example + conclusion: Take, for example, the case of Scandinavian countries, where investment in public transport has, to some extent, curbed car dependency.

Why does hedging earn marks?

Examiners reward a measured claim (This may, to some extent, reduce the problem) over an absolute claim (This will solve the problem). Hedging is both more academic and prevents over-generalisation. Most Turkish students do the opposite: they use absolute expressions like Everyone knows that…, It is a fact that…, All people…. These lower marks because they are both unsupported and indefensible. Replace them with measured patterns like Many people, in most cases, tends to, is likely to.

Don't leave the functional phrase "empty"

A functional phrase is only a link; the real marks come from the concrete idea that follows it. Saying Furthermore, it is important and stopping there is a heap of empty template. Always fill the phrase with a real reason, example or result: Furthermore, subsidising public transport would ease congestion by giving commuters a viable alternative to driving. The examiner rewards not the phrase but the idea it carries.

Phrases specific to essay type

  • Opinion: I largely agree, with some reservations; The strongest argument in favour is…; This argument carries little weight.
  • Discussion: supporters contend that; conversely, opponents argue; having considered both sides; while both arguments have merit.
  • Adv/Disadv: one major benefit is; a significant drawback is; this is offset by; the benefits far outweigh the drawbacks.
  • Problem/Solution: a pressing issue is; this problem is rooted in; one effective measure would be to; a viable solution lies in.
  • Two-part: Turning to the first question…; As for the second issue…; with regard to the latter point…

8. Register — the tone of vocabulary across the four skills

Register is the "formality tone" of a word: of two words with the same meaning, one is formal/academic, the other everyday/conversational. The most common hidden error in IELTS is putting a Speaking word into Writing (too casual) or speaking bookishly in Speaking (too artificial). The right word is not only "the right meaning" but the right tone for that skill.

Writing — academic tone
Impersonal, measured, fully formed. children (not kids), a great deal of (not a lot of), do not / cannot (no contractions), Furthermore, Consequently. Use idioms and phrasal verbs sparingly and selectively.
↔
Speaking — natural tone
Fluent, personal, idiomatic. Contractions (I'd, it's), phrasal verbs (get by, look into), natural fillers (to be honest, I guess) and idioms are a plus here. A bookish sentence sounds artificial.

Common register errors

In your essay, contractions like don't, isn't, addressing the reader with you, starting a sentence with And / But, and everyday words like a lot of, kids, get lower the tone. In Writing, tune up (to a formal tone); in Speaking, tune to natural; in Reading and Listening, use tone as a reading clue. You carry the same vocabulary but change its tone in each skill — that is flexibility.

10. Band 5 → 7 → 9: the same idea, raised through vocabulary

How is a single idea — "cars cause pollution" — written at three bands?

BandSentence
5Cars make a lot of pollution and this is a big bad problem for cities.
7Private cars produce significant pollution, which has become a serious problem in many urban areas.
9Heavy reliance on private vehicles gives rise to considerable air pollution, a pressing issue that increasingly threatens the liveability of major cities.

What changed?

  • 5→7: the vague words are gone (big bad → serious); correct collocation (produce pollution); a relative clause.
  • 7→9: strong phrases (heavy reliance, gives rise to, a pressing issue); even a single apt rare word; fluent, no forcing.

A second example — the idea: "technology isolates people":

5Technology makes people alone and this is a bad thing for society.
7Excessive use of technology can make people feel isolated, which may weaken social bonds.
9An over-reliance on digital communication risks eroding face-to-face interaction, gradually undermining the social bonds that hold communities together.

What changed? (second example)

  • 5→7: the vague makes people alone becomes the apt make people feel isolated (alone is physical solitude, isolated is emotional disconnection); a valueless phrase like a bad thing is removed entirely; a concrete result is added with weaken social bonds.
  • 7→9: the subject rises to a phrase (an over-reliance on digital communication); two strong verbs enter — erode (slow wearing away) and undermine (weakening from the base) layer the same idea in two different tones; the adverb gradually times the process; the relative clause the social bonds that hold communities together completes the idea, none of it for show.

Notice: the Band 9 sentence is high not because it is longer or "harder" but because every word is perfectly placed. erode, undermine, over-reliance all sit with the right collocation and the right tone. This is "placing words", not "piling words" — and the difference between the two determines the whole band. The shared lesson of the two examples: the climb comes not from adding more words to a sentence but from replacing the words that carry the idea with more apt ones. Moving from Band 5 to 7 is usually "drop the vague word, put in the right collocation"; moving from 7 to 9 is "replace the ordinarily-correct word with the one that sits most subtly in that exact context". So when you want to raise a sentence you've written, think first not about lengthening it but about whether each word passes the question "is this the most apt one?"

10.1 · Subtle shades of meaning (connotation)

The invisible weapon of Band 8-9 is connotation — the positive/negative emotional tone a word carries alongside its dictionary meaning. Words that look synonymous carry different connotations; a wrongly-toned synonym, even if technically "correct", breaks the sentence.

Neutral/positiveNegative connotationDifference

TIP — learn connotation from examples

You sense a word's connotation not from the dictionary definition but from the example sentences it appears in. When learning a new synonym, ask yourself: "If I use this word for a person, am I praising them or criticising them?" If unsure, prefer the known word with a neutral connotation — precision comes before show once again.

11. Common vocabulary errors (Turkish students)

1) Word-for-word translation

strong rain, do a decision. Learn as a chunk, don't translate word by word.

2) Vague words

good, bad, important, thing, a lot. These are Band 5 markers; replace with a precise word.

3) Adding -s to uncountables

informations, advices, researches are wrong. These are uncountable; see the Grammatical Accuracy page.

4) The show-off word

Using a memorised rare word in the wrong context. Don't use a word you're unsure of; precision matters more than show.

5) Register (formality)

kids, stuff, a lot of, don't are not appropriate in an academic essay: children, aspects, many, do not.

6) Overusing connectors

Putting Moreover/Furthermore at the start of every sentence becomes artificial. Use a connector for a genuine logical transition.

The common root of these errors is the same: learning a word out of context. The solution is also common — record every new word not on its own but together with a sentence it naturally appears in and its typical partners. Read an essay you've written a day later "like a foreign reader" and ask these three questions: Is this word really the most apt, or is there a more concrete equivalent? Does the register fit the tone of the essay? Does this connector mark a genuine logical relationship? A text that passes these three filters almost always earns a higher Lexical Resource score than one full of memorised, showy words. Remember: the exam rewards not the hardest word you know, but the word that carries the idea most aptly.

"Precision comes before show. Using a fancy word you're unsure of is riskier than using a plain word in the right place.

12. Making vocabulary stick — how to study

A vocabulary is not built overnight; and once built, it needs maintenance. For your brain to carry a word from "I've seen it" to "I can use it", two things are needed: spaced repetition (seeing the word again just as you begin to forget it) and production (building your own sentence with it). The rhythm below combines the two.

  1. Collect (as chunks): when a word comes up while reading/listening, put it in your notebook not alone but with the chunk it appeared in: a growing reliance on, to pose a serious threat. Write its Turkish meaning and its typical partner.
  2. Produce (your own sentence): build your own sentence for each word immediately. A word you don't write does not stick; production turns recognition into knowledge.
  3. Space your repetition: see the word again on day 1, day 3, day 7 and day 21. Reviewing at these intervals moves the word from short-term into long-term memory. A flashcard system calculates these intervals for you.
  4. Group by theme: pick a weekly theme (Environment, Health…). Attach all that week's new words to that theme; in the exam they come ready theme by theme.
  5. Use in an essay and audit: use at least five new chunks deliberately in each essay; a day later, audit yourself with the "precision / register / naturalness" trio.
