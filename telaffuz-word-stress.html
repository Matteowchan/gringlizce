Kelime vurgusu (word stress), çok-heceli bir İngilizce kelimede hangi hecenin daha güçlü, daha uzun ve daha net söyleneceğini belirler: PHOtograph ilk heceden, phoTOGraphy ikinci heceden vurgulanır. Türkçede hece vurgusu görece düz ve genelde sona yakın olduğu için, Türk öğrenciler yanlış heceyi vurgulamaya çok yatkındır; bu da anlaşılırlığı en çok bozan telaffuz hatalarından biridir. Vurgu bazen sözcük türünü ve anlamı değiştirir (REcord = kayıt/isim, reCORD = kaydetmek/fiil). Bu bölüm vurgunun ne olduğunu, kurallarını, ek-vurgu ilişkilerini, sık hataları ve pratik akılda-tutma yollarını ele alır. Cümle düzeyindeki vurgu ve ritim için bkz. Cümle Vurgusu; vurgusuz hecelerin zayıflaması için bkz. Zayıf Biçimler.

Word stress determines which syllable in a multi-syllable English word is said stronger, longer and clearer: PHOtograph is stressed on the first syllable, phoTOGraphy on the second. Because Turkish syllable stress is relatively flat and usually near the end, Turkish learners are very prone to stressing the wrong syllable; and this is one of the pronunciation mistakes that most damages intelligibility. Stress sometimes changes the word type and meaning (REcord = a record/noun, reCORD = to record/verb). This section covers what stress is, its rules, suffix-stress relationships, common mistakes and practical ways to remember. For stress and rhythm at sentence level, see Sentence Stress; for the weakening of unstressed syllables, see Weak Forms.

Örneğin banana kelimesinde üç hece vardır (ba-na-na) ama biz onu baNAna diye söyleriz: ortadaki hece patlar, diğer ikisi zayıflar.

Daha güçlü

Vurgulu hece daha fazla nefes ve enerjiyle söylenir; kelimenin duyulan “vuruşu” odur.

Daha uzun

Ünlüsü belirgin biçimde uzar: baNAAna derken ortadaki hece gibi.

Daha net

Ünlü tam ve berrak kalır; vurgusuz heceler ise çoğu zaman belirsiz schwa /ə/ sesine kayar.

Peki bu neden var? İngilizce vurgu-zamanlı (stress-timed) bir dildir: konuşma ritmi hecelerin sayısına değil, vurgulu hecelerin aralığına göre akar. Vurgulu heceler ritmin 'vuruşlarıdır'; aralarındaki vurgusuz heceler sıkışıp zayıflar (bkz. Zayıf Biçimler). Yani vurgu, İngilizcenin müziğini kuran temel unsurdur. Vurguyu yanlış yere koyduğunuzda, dil bilgisi ve kelimeleriniz doğru olsa bile karşı taraf sizi anlamakta zorlanır.

Vurgu bazen sadece 'müzik' değildir; anlamı da değiştirir. REcord (isim: plak/kayıt) ile reCORD (fiil: kaydetmek) aynı harflerle yazılır ama farklı hecede vurgulanır ve farklı işlev görür. Bu yüzden vurgu, hem anlaşılırlık hem de anlam için hayatidir.

"Vurguyu yanlış heceye koyduğunuzda, dil bilginiz ve kelimeleriniz kusursuz olsa bile karşınızdaki sizi anlamakta zorlanır: vurgu, İngilizcenin duyulmayan iskeletidir.

Türkçedeki benzeri ve neden zor?

Türkçede de vurgu vardır ama çok daha düz ve öngörülebilirdir: kural olarak vurgu genellikle son heceye yakındır (kaLEM, arkaDAŞ, İstanBUL yer adlarında istisnalar olsa da). Türkçe konuşanlar heceleri neredeyse eşit uzunlukta söyler; İngilizcedeki gibi bir heceyi patlatıp diğerlerini yutma alışkanlığı yoktur. İşte tam da bu yüzden Türk öğrenci İngilizce kelimeyi 'düz' okur: PHOtograph yerine her heceyi eşit basar veya yanlış heceyi vurgular. Bu, en sık ve en fark edilen telaffuz hatasıdır.

Puf Noktası
Vurguyu 'ses yüksekliği' sanmayın. Vurgulu hece daha yüksek olabilir ama asıl fark uzunluk + netliktir: vurgulu hecenin ünlüsü tam, uzun ve berrak; vurgusuz hecenin ünlüsü kısa ve çoğu zaman /ə/ (schwa) sesine kayar. baNAAna: ortadaki 'a' uzun ve net, baştaki ve sondaki 'a' kısa /ə/.

In English, in every word of two or more syllables one syllable is said stronger, longer and clearer than the others. This syllable is called the stressed syllable, and the phenomenon is called word stress. For example the word banana has three syllables (ba-na-na) but we say it as baNAna: the middle syllable bursts, the other two weaken.

Stronger

The stressed syllable is said with more breath and energy; it is the audible "beat" of the word.

Longer

Its vowel clearly lengthens: baNAAna like the middle syllable when saying it.

Clearer

The vowel stays full and crisp; unstressed syllables, on the other hand, often drop to the vague schwa /ə/ sound.

So why does it exist? English is a stress-timed language: the rhythm of speech flows by the interval between stressed syllables, not by the number of syllables. Stressed syllables are the 'beats' of the rhythm; the unstressed syllables between them compress and weaken (see Weak Forms). So stress is the fundamental element that builds the music of English. When you put the stress in the wrong place, even if your grammar and words are correct, the other person struggles to understand you.

Sometimes stress is not just 'music'; it also changes meaning. REcord (noun: a record) and reCORD (verb: to record) are spelled with the same letters but stressed on different syllables and function differently. That is why stress is vital for both intelligibility and meaning.

"When you put the stress on the wrong syllable, even if your grammar and words are flawless, the person opposite you struggles to understand you: stress is the unheard skeleton of English.

The Turkish parallel and why it's hard

Turkish has stress too, but it is much flatter and more predictable: as a rule the stress is usually near the last syllable (kaLEM, arkaDAŞ, though there are exceptions in place names like İstanBUL). Turkish speakers say syllables at almost equal length; there is no habit, as in English, of bursting one syllable and swallowing the others. This is exactly why a Turkish learner reads an English word 'flat': instead of PHOtograph, they stress every syllable equally or stress the wrong one. This is the most common and most noticeable pronunciation mistake.

Key Point
Don't mistake stress for 'loudness'. The stressed syllable may be louder, but the real difference is length + clarity: the stressed syllable's vowel is full, long and crisp; the unstressed syllable's vowel is short and often drops to the /ə/ (schwa) sound. baNAAna: the middle 'a' is long and clear, the initial and final 'a' are short /ə/.

İpucu

Yeni bir kelime öğrenirken hangi hecenin patladığını işaretleyerek not alın (örn. baNAna). Vurguyu kelimeyle birlikte ezberlemek, sonradan düzeltmekten çok daha kolaydır.

  • Every multi-syllable English word has one main stressed syllable; we say it strong, long and clear.
  • Unstressed syllables weaken and their vowels usually drop to the /ə/ (schwa) sound.
  • In a dictionary the stress is shown by the upright tick mark ˈ that comes right before the stressed syllable: not /ˈbæn.ə.nə/ but /bəˈnɑː.nə/.
  • Stress sometimes changes the word type: REcord (noun) / reCORD (verb), PREsent / preSENT.
  • Some suffixes reliably pull the stress: -tion, -sion, -ic, -ity, -ical → stress on the syllable right before the suffix.
  • Some suffixes put the stress on the end: -ee (employee), -eer (engineer), -ese (Japanese), -ette.
  • In a word family the stress can shift: PHOtograph → phoTOGraphy → photoGRAPHic.
  • In compound nouns the stress is usually on the first word: a GREENhouse, a BLACKboard.
  • Because of the Turkish habit of reading flat, Turkish learners often stress the wrong syllable; this is the mistake that most damages intelligibility.
  • Stress does not work alone; together with sentence rhythm (Sentence Stress) and weak forms (Weak Forms) it builds natural pronunciation.
Tip

When learning a new word, note it by marking which syllable bursts (e.g. baNAna). Memorising the stress together with the word is far easier than fixing it later.

Word stress has no 'positive/negative/question' inflection; instead, by 'structure' we mean the patterns that determine which syllable the stress falls on. There are three practical steps to find the stress.

  1. Count the syllables. Split the word by its vowel nuclei: com-pu-ter = 3 syllables.
  2. Burst the main syllable. Say one syllable long and clear, weaken and swallow the others.
  3. Verify in the dictionary. ˈ marks the syllable right after it as the main stress.

Step 1 — Count the syllables

First split the word into its syllables. Each syllable has one vowel sound. com-pu-ter = 3 syllables, im-POR-tant = 3 syllables, beau-ti-ful = 3 syllables. Single-syllable words have no stress problem (dog, run, big).

1) Stress changes the type, but not every pair

The Turkish and English stress systems are fundamentally different. Turkish is close to syllable-timed: syllables are said at relatively equal length and the stress is relatively light, mostly near the last syllable. English, on the other hand, is stress-timed: stressed syllables burst, and the syllables between compress and weaken. This difference is the root cause of Turkish learners reading English 'flat'.

Turkish

Syllables are relatively equal in length; the stress is light and mostly near the end. Even an unstressed syllable is said clearly.

↔
English

The stressed syllable bursts; the syllables between compress and are swallowed into a schwa. The rhythm flows in beats.

  • In Turkish the stress is light; in English the stressed syllable is clearly long and strong.
  • In Turkish the unstressed syllable is also said clearly; in English the unstressed syllable is swallowed, drifting to a schwa (/ə/).
  • Turkish stress is usually near the end; in English the stress can be at the start, middle or end depending on the word.
  • In Turkish stress does not change the word type; in English it can (REcord/reCORD).
  • Turkish learners tend to press every syllable equally; this is the most noticeable signal of a 'foreign accent'.
Caution

Pressing every syllable equally makes your speech instantly sound "foreign", even if your grammar is flawless. Aim first to say a single syllable strongly, then to weaken the others.

Word stress plays a decisive role in academic communication, yet it is often overlooked by learners who focus solely on grammar and vocabulary. When a speaker presents a complex argument, the audience relies on rhythm to follow the structure of long, technical words. Consider a presentation on economic policy: terms such as 'economic', 'analysis', 'significant', and 'development' appear repeatedly. If the presenter stresses the wrong syllable in even one of these, listeners momentarily lose focus while their brains try to reconstruct the intended word. This small delay, repeated across a talk, quietly undermines the speaker's authority. Correct stress, by contrast, signals fluency and control. It tells the audience that the speaker owns the material. For this reason, advanced learners preparing for IELTS, TOEFL, or academic conferences are advised to check the stress of every new technical term in a dictionary and to practise it aloud, treating pronunciation as seriously as spelling. Over time, correct stress becomes automatic, and the speaker's ideas — not their accent — take centre stage.

Where to start?
Instead of explaining stress abstractly, make it physical: tell students to tap the table, rise slightly from the chair, or stretch a rubber band while saying the stressed syllable. Exaggerate the stressed syllable when saying 'baNAAAna'. A Turkish learner usually feels 'I'm exaggerating too much' — but that is exactly the right amount. Start with familiar two-syllable words (hotel, coffee, table), then move on to the -tion/-ic suffix rules.

Concept-check (CCQ) examples: When you say 'REcord', is this a noun or a verb? (noun) In the word 'education', is -tion or CA strong? (CA) Is 'green HOUSE' a greenhouse or a green house? (a green house). These questions test understanding, not the rule.

Activity ideas: (1) Stress dictation: the teacher says a word, students write only the stressed syllable in capitals. (2) Word family ladder: PHOtograph → phoTOGrapher → photoGRAPHic is written on the board, students mark the stress. (3) Minimal stress pairs: a noun-verb game with REcord/reCORD cards. (4) Tapping: tapping long words to the rhythm. Be sure to practise the weak syllables drifting to schwa (Weak Forms) together as well; otherwise the stress stays 'half-done'.

Common classroom trap
Students find the stressed syllable but say the unstressed ones clearly too; the result is a 'robotic' pronunciation. The fix: instead of enlarging the stressed syllable, focus on shrinking the unstressed ones. Not 'com-PU-ter', but 'kƏm-PYU-tƏ' — melt the edges.
  • Çok-heceli her İngilizce kelimede tek bir ana vurgulu hece vardır; onu uzun, güçlü ve net söyleriz.
  • Vurgusuz heceler zayıflar; ünlüleri genelde schwa /ə/'ye kayar. 'Net ünlü = vurgulu, bulanık ünlü = vurgusuz.'
  • Sözlükte ˈ işaretinden hemen sonraki hece ana vurguludur (/bəˈnɑː.nə/ → baNAna).
  • Vurgu bazen sözcük türünü/anlamı değiştirir: REcord/reCORD, PREsent/preSENT, INcrease/inCREASE.
  • Güvenilir ek kuralları: -tion/-sion/-ic/-ical/-ity/-ious → vurgu ekten önceki hecede; -ee/-eer/-ese/-ette → vurgu ekin kendisinde (sonda).
  • Bir kelime ailesinde vurgu kayar: PHOtograph → phoTOGraphy → photoGRAPHic. Aileyi bir bütün öğrenin.
  • Bileşik isimlerde vurgu ilk kelimede (GREENhouse); bu, öbekten (green HOUSE) ayırır.
  • Türkçe düz-okuma alışkanlığı yüzünden yanlış hece vurgulamak ve her heceyi eşit basmak en sık hatalardır.
  • İstisnalar (hotel, police, politics/political) tek tek, sözlükle öğrenilir; ek kuralları 'iki heceli isim/fiil' eğiliminden daha güvenilirdir.
  • Vurgu yalnız çalışmaz: cümle ritmi (Cümle Vurgusu) ve zayıf biçimlerle (Zayıf Biçimler) birlikte doğal telaffuzu kurar.
  • Every multi-syllable English word has one main stressed syllable; we say it long, strong and clear.
  • Unstressed syllables weaken; their vowels usually drift to schwa /ə/. 'Clear vowel = stressed, blurry vowel = unstressed.'
  • In a dictionary the syllable right after the ˈ mark is the main stress (/bəˈnɑː.nə/ → baNAna).
  • Stress sometimes changes the word type/meaning: REcord/reCORD, PREsent/preSENT, INcrease/inCREASE.
  • Reliable suffix rules: -tion/-sion/-ic/-ical/-ity/-ious → stress on the syllable before the suffix; -ee/-eer/-ese/-ette → stress on the suffix itself (at the end).
  • In a word family the stress shifts: PHOtograph → phoTOGraphy → photoGRAPHic. Learn the family as a whole.
  • In compound nouns the stress is on the first word (GREENhouse); this distinguishes it from the phrase (green HOUSE).
  • Because of the Turkish flat-reading habit, stressing the wrong syllable and pressing every syllable equally are the most common mistakes.
  • Exceptions (hotel, police, politics/political) are learned one by one with a dictionary; the suffix rules are more reliable than the 'two-syllable noun/verb' tendency.
  • Stress does not work alone: together with sentence rhythm (Sentence Stress) and weak forms (Weak Forms) it builds natural pronunciation.
