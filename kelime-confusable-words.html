Confusable words are pairs and triples of words in English that are constantly mixed up because their meaning or use is close: make/do, say/tell, lend/borrow, affect/effect, its/it's, fun/funny, travel/trip/journey. For a Turkish learner the real difficulty is this: most of these words correspond to a single word in Turkish (say/tell = söylemek, travel/trip/journey = seyahat/yolculuk), so which one goes where cannot be worked out by thinking in Turkish. The distinction is resolved by meaning, grammar (whether it takes an object, countability) and fixed patterns (collocation). This section gives each pair with its rule + reason + plenty of examples; it links the related patterns to Collocations and word derivation to the Word Formation topic.

İngilizcede bazı kelimeler o kadar benzer görünür ya da anlamları o kadar yakındır ki, öğrenciler onları sürekli birbirinin yerine kullanır. Bunlara confusable words (karıştırılan kelimeler) denir. Bu bölüm, en sık karıştırılan çiftleri ve üçlüleri tek tek ele alır: make/do, say/tell, lend/borrow, affect/effect, its/it's, fun/funny ve travel/trip/journey.

Bu kelimeler neden var? Çünkü İngilizce, Türkçenin tek bir kelimeyle ifade ettiği bir kavramı çoğu zaman ince farklarla birden fazla kelimeye bölmüştür. Türkçe "söylemek" der ve geçer; İngilizce ise sözün kendisine odaklanıyorsa say, o sözü bir kişiye aktarıyorsan tell kullanmanı ister. Yani fark, dilin daha ayrıntılı düşünmeni istemesinden doğar.

“Kelimeyi çevirme, işlevini düşün: kim veriyor kim alıyor, yeni bir şey mi üretiliyor, fiil mi isim mi? Doğru soruyu soran öğrenci doğru kelimeyi zaten bulur.

Türkçedeki benzeri

Türkçede de karıştırılan kelimeler vardır: "yalnız/yanlız", "herkes/herkez", "da/de ayrı mı bitişik mi". Ama Türk öğrencinin İngilizcede yaşadığı asıl sorun yazım değil, anlam-eşleştirme sorunudur: bir Türkçe kelime birden çok İngilizce kelimeye karşılık geldiği için beynimiz hangisini seçeceğini bilemez. "Ödünç" kelimesi hem lend (vermek) hem borrow (almak) demektir; "seyahat" hem travel hem trip hem journey olabilir.

Neden zor?

  • Bir Türkçe kelime birden çok İngilizce kelimeye bölünür (söylemek → say/tell).
  • Ayrım çoğu zaman anlamda değil, dilbilgisinde gizlidir: tell nesne (kişi) ister, say istemez.
  • Bazıları neredeyse aynı yazılır/okunur (affect/effect, its/it's) — küçük bir harf anlamı değiştirir.
  • Doğru seçim çoğu zaman ezberlenen sabit kalıplara (collocation) bağlıdır: make a mistake doğru, do a mistake yanlış.
  • Türkçeden birebir çeviri neredeyse her zaman yanlış kelimeyi seçtirir.
Bakış açısı
Bu bölümü "kural ezberi" gibi değil, "her kelimenin bir işi var" mantığıyla oku. Her çiftin bir ayırıcı sorusu vardır (Kim veriyor kim alıyor? Yeni bir şey mi üretiliyor yoksa iş mi yapılıyor?). Doğru soruyu sorarsan doğru kelime kendiliğinden gelir.

In English some words look so similar or their meanings are so close that learners constantly use one in place of the other. These are called confusable words. This section takes the most frequently confused pairs and triples one by one: make/do, say/tell, lend/borrow, affect/effect, its/it's, fun/funny and travel/trip/journey.

Why do these words exist? Because English has often split a concept that Turkish expresses with a single word into several words with fine differences. Turkish says "söylemek" and that's that; English wants you to use say if you are focusing on the words themselves, and if you are conveying those words to a person, tell. So the difference arises from the language asking you to think in more detail.

“Don't translate the word, think about its function: who is giving and who is receiving, is something new being produced, is it a verb or a noun? A learner who asks the right question already finds the right word.

The equivalent in Turkish

Turkish also has confusable words: "yalnız/yanlız", "herkes/herkez", whether "da/de" is written separately or joined. But the Turkish learner's real problem in English is not spelling, it is a meaning-matching problem: because one Turkish word corresponds to several English words, our brain cannot decide which to choose. The word "ödünç" means both lend (to give) and borrow (to take); "seyahat" can be travel, trip or journey.

Why is it hard?

  • One Turkish word splits into several English words (söylemek → say/tell).
  • The distinction is often hidden not in meaning but in grammar: tell wants an object (a person), say does not.
  • Some are written/pronounced almost the same (affect/effect, its/it's) — one small letter changes the meaning.
  • The right choice often depends on memorised fixed patterns (collocation): make a mistake is correct, do a mistake is wrong.
  • A word-for-word translation from Turkish almost always makes you pick the wrong word.
Point of view
Read this section not as 'rule memorisation' but with the logic 'every word has a job'. Each pair has a distinguishing question (Who is giving and who is receiving? Is something new being produced, or is a task being done?). If you ask the right question, the right word comes by itself.

Dilbilgisi ayırır

say / tell, lend / borrow — fark anlamda değil, cümledeki dizilişte: tell me ama say to me; veren lend, alan borrow.

Tek harf değiştirir

affect / effect, its / it's — neredeyse aynı yazılır ve okunur; fiil mi isim mi, kesme var mı yok mu diye bakılır.

Anlam nüansı seçer

make / do, fun / funny, travel / trip / journey — üretme mi iş mi, keyif mi gülme mi, genel mi belirli mi?

  • make = yeni bir şey üretmek/ortaya çıkarmak (make a cake, make a decision); do = bir işi/görevi gerçekleştirmek (do homework, do the dishes).
  • say = söze odaklanır, kişi zorunlu değildir (say something); tell = birine bir şey aktarır, kişi genelde zorunludur (tell me).
  • lend = ödünç vermek (sen verirsin); borrow = ödünç almak (sen alırsın).
  • affect = etkilemek (fiil, A→Action); effect = etki (isim, E→End result).
  • its = onun (iyelik, kesme YOK); it's = it is / it has (kesme VAR).
  • fun = eğlence/keyifli (have fun); funny = komik ya da tuhaf.
  • travel = seyahat etmek/genel kavram; trip = belirli kısa gezi; journey = bir yerden bir yere uzun yolculuk.
  • Çoğu hata Türkçeden birebir çeviriden doğar; kelimeyi çevirme, işlevini düşün.
  • Doğru seçim genelde sabit kalıba bağlıdır — kalıpları Eş Dizimler (Collocations) ile birlikte ezberle.
  • Şüphedeysen her çiftin ayırıcı sorusunu sor: Kim yapıyor? Kime? Yeni bir şey mi üretiliyor?

Grammar separates them

say / tell, lend / borrow — the difference is not in meaning but in the word order of the sentence: tell me but say to me; the giver lends, the receiver borrows.

One letter changes it

affect / effect, its / it's — written and pronounced almost the same; you check whether it is a verb or a noun, whether there is an apostrophe or not.

Meaning nuance chooses

make / do, fun / funny, travel / trip / journey — produce or do, enjoyment or laughter, general or specific?

  • make = to produce/bring about something new (make a cake, make a decision); do = to carry out a job/task (do homework, do the dishes).
  • say = focuses on the words, a person is not required (say something); tell = conveys something to a person, a person is usually required (tell me).
  • lend = to give on loan (you give); borrow = to take on loan (you take).
  • affect = to affect (verb, A→Action); effect = an effect (noun, E→End result).
  • its = its (possessive, NO apostrophe); it's = it is / it has (apostrophe YES).
  • fun = enjoyment/enjoyable (have fun); funny = amusing or strange.
  • travel = to travel/the general concept; trip = a specific short trip; journey = a long journey from one place to another.
  • Most mistakes come from a word-for-word translation from Turkish; don't translate the word, think about its function.
  • The right choice usually depends on a fixed pattern — memorise the patterns together with Collocations.
  • When in doubt, ask each pair's distinguishing question: Who is doing it? To whom? Is something new being produced?

With confusable words, "structure" means knowing in which word order each word stands in the sentence. The most critical structural differences are between say/tell, lend/borrow and affect/effect. Below you see the affirmative, negative and question patterns of each.

say vs tell — object order

Formula: say (+ that) + words  |  tell + PERSON + thing. Right after 'tell' comes the person you told; with 'say', if you want to state the person you add to (say to me).

She said (that) she was busy.
Meşgul olduğunu söyledi.
say + words; no person, correct.
She told me (that) she was busy.
Bana meşgul olduğunu söyledi.
tell + PERSON (me) + words; correct.

Negative: didn't + say.

Question: did + tell + you.
Wrong She said me that she was busy.
Correct She told me that she was busy. / She said to me that she was busy.
Bana meşgul olduğunu söyledi.
A person (me) cannot come immediately after 'say'. You either say 'tell me' or 'say TO me'.

lend vs borrow — direction order

Formula: lend + (person) + thing or lend + thing + to + person (the giving party)  |  borrow + thing + from + person (the receiving party). The source/target prepositions differ: to with lend, from with borrow.

This whole section is really a Turkish-English matching problem. Because Turkish uses a single word, when translating it you have to choose the English equivalent according to meaning.

TRAP

Translating Turkish word-for-word almost always makes you pick the wrong word with these words: “bana söyledi” → said me (wrong), “ödünç ver” → borrow me (wrong). First drop the Turkish, think about the English function.

  • "Söylemek" → say (to the words) or tell (to a person): 'Bana söyle' = tell me, 'Bir şey söyledi' = said something.
  • "Ödünç" → lend (to give) or borrow (to take): direction comes from context in Turkish, from the word in English.
  • "Yapmak" → make (to produce) or do (to do a task): 'hata yapmak' = make a mistake, 'ödev yapmak' = do homework.
  • "Etkilemek/etki" → affect (verb) or effect (noun): the root is the same in Turkish, the word class changes in English.
  • "Seyahat/yolculuk/gezi" → travel / trip / journey: Turkish uses these almost synonymously, English separates them.
  • "Eğlenceli/komik" → fun (enjoyable) or funny (amusing): the line is blurred in Turkish, clear in English.

concept check Answer by thinking about the meaning.
  1. What is the basic grammatical difference between 'say' and 'tell'?
    'tell' usually wants a person (an object) right after it (tell me); 'say' does not, and 'to' is needed to state the person (say to me).
  2. You give your pen to a person. 'lend' or 'borrow'?
    lend — you are the giving party (I lend you my pen).
  3. Which is a verb, 'affect' or 'effect', and how do you remember?
    affect is the verb (A = Action); effect is the noun (E = End result).
  4. 'The dog wagged ___ tail.' — its or it's? Why?
    its — possessive (its tail), takes no apostrophe. 'it is tail' would be meaningless.
  5. A party was enjoyable but didn't make you laugh. 'fun' or 'funny'?
    fun — something enjoyable but not necessarily amusing is 'fun'; 'funny' means amusing or strange.
  6. In the sentence 'Going from Izmir to Van took 20 hours', which of travel/trip/journey is natural?
    journey — the duration/process of going from one place to another is emphasised (The journey took 20 hours).

En yüksek getirili çiftlerle başla: say/tell ve make/do günlük konuşmada en sık karıştırılanlardır. Önce say/tell'in dilbilgisi farkını (tell + kişi) net oturt; make/do'yu ise kural yerine kalıp listeleriyle (Eş Dizimler (Collocations)) öğret çünkü tam kural yoktur.

Kavram kontrolü için tercüme yaptırma; bunun yerine "Bu cümlede kim veriyor kim alıyor?" (lend/borrow), "Bu bir fiil mi isim mi?" (affect/effect) gibi ayırıcı sorular sor. Öğrenci doğru kelimeyi kuralı ezberleyerek değil, doğru soruyu sorarak bulmalı.

Etkinlik önerisi: "Odd one out" oyunu — tahtaya karışık cümleler yaz (bazıları hatalı), öğrenciler hatalıyı bulup düzeltsin ve nedenini tam cümleyle açıklasın. Bir başka etkinlik: kartlarda Türkçe cümleler ('Bana söyledi', 'Ödünç ver', 'Bir karar ver'), öğrenciler doğru İngilizce çifti seçsin. affect/effect ve its/it's için kısa dikte pratiği çok işe yarar çünkü bunlar yazı hatalarıdır.

En yaygın tek hata
Tüm gruplarda bir numaralı hata 'say me' (doğrusu 'tell me') ve 'borrow me' (doğrusu 'lend me')dir — ikisi de Türkçeden birebir çeviriden doğar. Bu ikisini erken ve tekrar tekrar düzeltmek en büyük ilerlemeyi sağlar.
Where to start?
Start with the highest-yield pairs: say/tell and make/do are the most commonly confused in everyday speech. First firmly establish the grammatical difference of say/tell (tell + person); teach make/do with lists of fixed phrases (Collocations) rather than a rule, because there is no exact rule.

For concept checking, don't have them translate; instead ask distinguishing questions like 'Who is giving and who is receiving in this sentence?' (lend/borrow), 'Is this a verb or a noun?' (affect/effect). The student should find the right word by asking the right question, not by memorising a rule.

Activity idea: an 'Odd one out' game — write mixed sentences on the board (some with errors), have students find and fix the wrong one and explain the reason in a full sentence. Another activity: cards with Turkish sentences ('Bana söyledi', 'Ödünç ver', 'Bir karar ver'), and students choose the correct English pair. For affect/effect and its/it's, short dictation practice works very well because these are writing errors.

The single most common mistake
In all groups the number-one mistake is 'say me' (correctly 'tell me') and 'borrow me' (correctly 'lend me') — both come from a word-for-word translation of Turkish. Correcting these two early and repeatedly brings the biggest progress.
  • make yeni bir şey üretir, do bir görevi yapar — emin değilsen kalıba bak (Eş Dizimler (Collocations)).
  • tell genelde bir kişi (nesne) ister (tell me); say istemez (say to me).
  • lend = ödünç vermek (veren sen), borrow = ödünç almak (alan sen, from ile).
  • affect fiildir (A→Action), effect isimdir (E→End result); effect'in nadir fiil hâli = gerçekleştirmek.
  • its iyelik (kesme yok), it's = it is/it has (kesme var) — 'it is' diyebiliyorsan it's.
  • fun keyif verir, funny güldürür ya da tuhaftır.
  • travel genel kavram, trip belirli (kısa) gezi, journey bir yerden bir yere gidiş süreci.
  • Türkçeden birebir çeviri bu kelimelerde neredeyse hep yanlış seçtirir — kelimeyi değil işlevini düşün.
  • İpucu kelimeleri (from, an ... on, own...) yardımcı olur ama garanti değildir; anlamı doğrula.

  • make produces something new, do performs a task — if unsure, look at the pattern (Collocations).
  • tell usually wants a person (an object) (tell me); say does not (say to me).
  • lend = to lend (you give), borrow = to borrow (you take, with from).
  • affect is a verb (A→Action), effect is a noun (E→End result); the rare verb form of effect = to bring about.
  • its possessive (no apostrophe), it's = it is/it has (with apostrophe) — if you can say 'it is', it's.
  • fun is enjoyable, funny is amusing or strange.
  • travel the general concept, trip a specific (short) outing, journey the process of going from one place to another.
  • A word-for-word translation from Turkish almost always makes you pick wrong with these words — think about the function, not the word.
  • Clue words (from, an ... on, own...) help but are not a guarantee; confirm the meaning.
