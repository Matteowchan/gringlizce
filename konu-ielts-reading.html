IELTS Reading bir okuma testi değil, bir zaman ve strateji testidir. 60 dakikada 3 uzun akademik pasaj ve 40 soru; başarı, metni baştan sona okumaktan değil, doğru cevabı doğru yerde hızlı bulmaktan geçer. Bu rehber testin yapısını, her soru tipinin özel stratejisini, en çok hata yapılan tuzakları ve band 6.5 ile band 8 arasındaki gerçek farkı örneklerle anlatır.

IELTS Reading is not a reading test but a test of time and strategy. In 60 minutes, 3 long academic passages and 40 questions; success comes not from reading the text end to end but from finding the right answer in the right place, fast. This guide explains the structure of the test, the specific strategy for each question type, the traps that cause the most errors, and the real difference between band 6.5 and band 8, with examples.

1. Test Structure: 3 Passages, 40 Questions, 60 Minutes

The IELTS Academic Reading module lasts 60 minutes and consists of three separate passages. The passages are authentic (real) texts taken from newspapers, magazines, books and academic sources; the difficulty increases as you progress. The total word count is roughly 2,150–2,750. Unlike the Listening module, there is no extra transfer time: you must write your answers on the answer sheet within the 60 minutes.

3. Üç Temel Okuma Becerisi: Skimming, Scanning, Detailed Reading

IELTS Reading'de tek bir okuma hızı yoktur; hangi soruyu çözdüğüne göre vites değiştirirsin. Üç beceriyi doğru anda kullanmak, testin en temel becerisidir.

Skimming (hızlı tarama)

Metnin genel fikrini ve her paragrafın konusunu yakalamak için gözü hızla gezdirmek. Başlıkları, ilk/son cümleleri, tekrar eden kelimeleri oku. Ne zaman: pasaja ilk bakışta (1–2 dk) ve Matching Headings'te.

Scanning (nokta arama)

Belirli bir bilgiyi (isim, tarih, sayı, anahtar kelime) bulmak için metni tarayıp geri kalanını okumamak. Ne zaman: Completion, Short Answer, isim/tarih içeren tüm sorularda.

Detailed reading (yakın okuma)

Doğru yeri bulduktan sonra o 2–3 cümleyi kelime kelime okuyup anlamı ve nüansı çözmek. Ne zaman: True/False/Not Given, Multiple Choice ve cevabı doğrulama anında.

Doğru iş akışı

1) Pasajı 1–2 dk skim et → yapıyı çıkar. 2) Soruyu oku, anahtar kelimeyi belirle. 3) O kelimeyi/eş anlamını scan ederek bul. 4) İlgili cümleleri detailed oku → cevabı ver ve doğrula. Bütün pasajı baştan sona okumak (band 5 alışkanlığı) neredeyse her zaman zaman kaybettirir.

3. Three Core Reading Skills: Skimming, Scanning, Detailed Reading

There is no single reading speed in IELTS Reading; you change gear depending on which question you are solving. Using the three skills at the right moment is the most fundamental skill of the test.

Skimming (quick overview)

Running your eye quickly over the text to catch the general idea and the topic of each paragraph. Read the headings, first/last sentences and repeated words. When: on your first look at the passage (1–2 min) and in Matching Headings.

Scanning (spotting a point)

Sweeping the text to find a specific piece of information (a name, date, number, keyword) without reading the rest. When: Completion, Short Answer, and any question containing a name/date.

Detailed reading (close reading)

Once you have found the right place, reading those 2–3 sentences word by word to work out the meaning and nuance. When: True/False/Not Given, Multiple Choice and when confirming an answer.

The right workflow

1) Skim the passage for 1–2 min → work out its structure. 2) Read the question, identify the keyword. 3) Find that word/its synonym by scanning. 4) Read the relevant sentences in detail → answer and confirm. Reading the whole passage from start to finish (a band 5 habit) almost always wastes time.

5. Multiple Choice (Çoktan Seçmeli)

Genellikle A–D dört seçenek verilir ve tek doğru cevap istenir (bazen "iki tanesini seç" varyantı olur). Sorular metnin sırasını takip eder: 12. soru genelde 11. sorudan sonra gelir. En büyük tehlike, "kulağa doğru gelen" ama pasajda söylenmeyen çeldiricilerdir.

Strateji

  • Önce soru kökünü oku, seçeneklere sonra bak (seçenekler seni yanıltmadan önce metinde ne aradığını bil).
  • İlgili bölümü bul ve her seçeneği ayrı ayrı ele: neden yanlış olduğunu söyleyebilmelisin.
  • Çeldirici türleri: pasajdaki kelimeleri aynen kullanan ama anlamı değiştiren; doğru ama soruyu yanıtlamayan; aşırı genelleyen ("all", "always").

Mini pasaj: "Although solar panels were once prohibitively expensive, manufacturing advances have cut prices by nearly 80% since 2010, making them competitive with fossil fuels in many regions — though not yet in areas with limited sunlight."

Soru: According to the passage, solar panels are now…
A) cheaper than fossil fuels everywhere
B) competitively priced in many but not all places
C) still too expensive for most regions
D) unaffected by manufacturing changes

Çözüm: Doğru cevap B. A "everywhere" ile aşırı genelliyor (pasaj "not yet in areas with limited sunlight" diyor). C, fiyatların %80 düştüğü bilgisiyle çelişiyor. D pasaja tamamen aykırı. B, "competitive in many regions" + istisna nüansını tam yansıtıyor. Ders: aşırı genelleyen seçenek genelde tuzaktır.

5. Multiple Choice

Usually four options A–D are given and one correct answer is required (sometimes there is a "choose two" variant). The questions follow the order of the text: question 12 usually comes after question 11. The biggest danger is distractors that "sound right" but are not stated in the passage.

Strategy

  • Read the question stem first, look at the options afterwards (know what you are looking for in the text before the options mislead you).
  • Find the relevant section and take each option separately: you should be able to say why it is wrong.
  • Types of distractor: using the exact words from the passage but changing the meaning; true but not answering the question; over-generalising ("all", "always").

Mini passage: "Although solar panels were once prohibitively expensive, manufacturing advances have cut prices by nearly 80% since 2010, making them competitive with fossil fuels in many regions — though not yet in areas with limited sunlight."

Question: According to the passage, solar panels are now…
A) cheaper than fossil fuels everywhere
B) competitively priced in many but not all places
C) still too expensive for most regions
D) unaffected by manufacturing changes

Solution: The correct answer is B. A over-generalises with "everywhere" (the passage says "not yet in areas with limited sunlight"). C contradicts the fact that prices dropped 80%. D is entirely contrary to the passage. B captures "competitive in many regions" + the exception nuance exactly. Lesson: the over-generalising option is usually the trap.

Bu tip, adaylar için tek başına en kritik puan kaybı kaynağıdır çünkü Not Given kavramı sezgiye aykırıdır. Bir ifadenin metinle ilişkisini üç kategoriden birine sokman istenir:

FALSE, metnin ifadenin tersini aktif olarak söylemesidir. NOT GIVEN, metnin o konuda hiçbir şey söylememesidir. Türk adayların en sık hatası: "Pasajda böyle bir şey görmedim, demek ki FALSE." Yanlış! Görmediysen ve tersi de yazmıyorsa cevap NG'dir. FALSE için metinden çelişen bir cümle gösterebilmen gerekir.

Mini pasaj: "The city library, built in 1902, was funded entirely by a local merchant. It now holds over two million books and remains open seven days a week."

  • "The library was funded by public taxes." → FALSE (metin "funded entirely by a local merchant" diyor — çelişki var).
  • "The library is open every day of the week." → TRUE ("open seven days a week" = paraphrase eşleşmesi).
  • "The library is the largest in the country." → NOT GIVEN (ülke çapında karşılaştırma metinde hiç yok — ne doğrulanıyor ne çürütülüyor).

Yes / No / Not Given ile farkı

TFNG olgulara/bilgilere bakar (metinde yazan gerçekler). YNNG ise yazarın görüş, iddia veya inancına bakar. YNNG'de soru "Yazar buna katılır mı?" demektir; bu yüzden görüş bildiren fiillere dikkat et: argues, believes, claims, suggests, doubts. Mekanik aynıdır (eşleşme/çelişki/sessizlik) ama YNNG'de kanıtı yazarın tutumunda ararsın, kuru bilgide değil.

Yap / Yapma

  • Yap: İfadeyi parçalara böl — her parça (özne + eylem + koşul) ayrı ayrı metinle eşleşmeli. Bir parça bile uymuyorsa TRUE olamaz.
  • Yap: Sorular metnin sırasını takip eder; bir sonraki cevabı daha aşağıda ara.
  • Yapma: Kendi genel kültürünü kullanma — sadece metinde yazanı esas al.
  • Yapma: "Muhtemelen doğrudur" diye TRUE deme; kesin kanıt yoksa NG.

6. True / False / Not Given (The Most Error-Prone Type)

This type is on its own the single most critical source of lost points for candidates, because the concept of Not Given is counter-intuitive. You are asked to place the relation between a statement and the text into one of three categories:

AnswerMeaningEvidence status
TRUEThe statement matches / is confirmed by the information in the textThe text says this
FALSEThe statement contradicts the information in the textThe text says the opposite
NOT GIVENThere is no information in the text to confirm or refute thisThe text is silent on it

The logic of NG — lock this in

FALSE means the text actively says the opposite of the statement. NOT GIVEN means the text says nothing about it. Turkish candidates' most common mistake: "I didn't see anything like this in the passage, so it must be FALSE." Wrong! If you did not see it and the opposite is not written either, the answer is NG. For FALSE you must be able to point to a contradicting sentence in the text.

Mini passage: "The city library, built in 1902, was funded entirely by a local merchant. It now holds over two million books and remains open seven days a week."

  • "The library was funded by public taxes." → FALSE (the text says "funded entirely by a local merchant" — there is a contradiction).
  • "The library is open every day of the week." → TRUE ("open seven days a week" = a paraphrase match).
  • "The library is the largest in the country." → NOT GIVEN (there is no nationwide comparison in the text — it is neither confirmed nor refuted).

The difference from Yes / No / Not Given

TFNG looks at facts/information (the facts stated in the text). YNNG looks at the writer's opinion, claim or belief. In YNNG the question means "Would the writer agree with this?"; so watch for opinion verbs: argues, believes, claims, suggests, doubts. The mechanics are the same (match/contradiction/silence) but in YNNG you look for the evidence in the writer's attitude, not in dry facts.

Do / Don't

  • Do: Break the statement into parts — each part (subject + action + condition) must match the text separately. If even one part does not fit, it cannot be TRUE.
  • Do: The questions follow the order of the text; look for the next answer further down.
  • Don't: Use your own general knowledge — base your answer only on what is written in the text.
  • Don't: Say TRUE because "it's probably true"; if there is no definite evidence, it is NG.

7. Matching Headings (Paragraf–Başlık Eşleme)

Sana bir başlık listesi (i, ii, iii…) verilir ve her paragrafa (A, B, C…) en uygun başlığı eşlemen istenir. Başlık sayısı paragraf sayısından fazladır (çeldirici başlıklar). Bu tip ana fikir testidir; detay değil. Sırayı takip etmez.

Strateji

  • Her paragrafı skim et ve kendi kelimelerinle "bu paragraf ne hakkında?" diye özetle — sonra eşleşen başlığı ara.
  • İlk/son cümleler genelde ana fikri taşır; ama tek bir cümledeki detaya kanma.
  • En kolay/en emin olduğun paragraftan başla; kullandığın başlığı listeden çiz.
  • Tuzak: paragraftaki bir kelimeyi aynen içeren başlık genelde yanlıştır (kelime tuzağı). Ana fikri değil, tek detayı yakalar.

Başlık listesi: i) The cost of migration · ii) How birds navigate · iii) Threats from climate change

Paragraf: "Migrating birds rely on a remarkable set of internal tools. Some sense the Earth's magnetic field; others memorise star patterns or track the position of the setting sun. Together these systems guide them across thousands of kilometres with astonishing accuracy."

Çözüm: Doğru başlık ii) How birds navigate. Paragraf boyunca konu "yön bulma araçları" (magnetic field, star patterns, sun). "Cost" ve "climate change" hiç geçmiyor. Ders: ana fikri özetle, sonra eşle — tek kelimeye değil bütüne bak.

7. Matching Headings

You are given a list of headings (i, ii, iii…) and asked to match the most suitable heading to each paragraph (A, B, C…). There are more headings than paragraphs (distractor headings). This type is a test of main idea, not detail. It does not follow the order.

Strategy

  • Skim each paragraph and summarise in your own words "what is this paragraph about?" — then look for the matching heading.
  • First/last sentences usually carry the main idea; but do not be fooled by a detail in a single sentence.
  • Start with the easiest/most certain paragraph; cross the heading you use off the list.
  • Trap: a heading that contains a word exactly as it appears in the paragraph is usually wrong (the word trap). It catches a single detail, not the main idea.

Heading list: i) The cost of migration · ii) How birds navigate · iii) Threats from climate change

Paragraph: "Migrating birds rely on a remarkable set of internal tools. Some sense the Earth's magnetic field; others memorise star patterns or track the position of the setting sun. Together these systems guide them across thousands of kilometres with astonishing accuracy."

Solution: The correct heading is ii) How birds navigate. Throughout the paragraph the topic is "navigation tools" (magnetic field, star patterns, sun). "Cost" and "climate change" do not appear at all. Lesson: summarise the main idea, then match — look at the whole, not a single word.

8. Matching Information / Features (Bilgi ve Özellik Eşleme)

İki yakın akraba tip: Matching Information belirli bir bilgiyi (örnek, tanım, neden) hangi paragrafta geçtiğini sorar. Matching Features ise ifadeleri kişi/teori/kategori gibi bir listeyle eşler (ör. "Bu görüşü kim savundu?"). İkisi de sırayı takip etmez ve saf scanning işidir.

Strateji

  • Matching Information zordur çünkü tüm paragraflara bakman gerekebilir; bunu genelde en sona bırak.
  • Matching Features'ta önce isimleri/kategorileri metinde bul ve işaretle (isimler scan'lemesi kolaydır), sonra çevrelerindeki iddiaları oku.
  • Bir paragraf birden çok soruya cevap olabilir; bir kişiye birden çok özellik eşlenebilir. "Her seçenek bir kez kullanılır" yazmıyorsa varsayma.

Metin (özet): Researcher Lin argued that early cities grew around trade routes. Ortega, by contrast, claimed defence was the primary driver, pointing to hilltop settlements. Novak suggested both factors mattered but were secondary to reliable water access.

Soru: Who believed access to water was the most important factor? → Novak ("secondary to reliable water access" = su erişimini en öne koyuyor). Ders: isimleri çapa yap, sonra iddiayı oku — paraphrase'i çöz ("most important" ↔ "primary/above others").

8. Matching Information / Features

Two close relatives: Matching Information asks which paragraph a specific piece of information (an example, definition, reason) appears in. Matching Features matches statements to a list such as people/theories/categories (e.g. "Who held this view?"). Neither follows the order, and both are pure scanning work.

Strategy

  • Matching Information is hard because you may need to look at all the paragraphs; usually leave it for last.
  • In Matching Features, first find and mark the names/categories in the text (names are easy to scan), then read the claims around them.
  • One paragraph may answer several questions; one person may be matched with several features. Do not assume "each option is used once" unless it says so.

Text (summary): Researcher Lin argued that early cities grew around trade routes. Ortega, by contrast, claimed defence was the primary driver, pointing to hilltop settlements. Novak suggested both factors mattered but were secondary to reliable water access.

Question: Who believed access to water was the most important factor? → Novak ("secondary to reliable water access" = puts water access above all others). Lesson: use names as anchors, then read the claim — decode the paraphrase ("most important" ↔ "primary/above others").

9. Sentence / Summary / Note / Table Completion (Tamamlama)

Bir cümlenin, özetin, notun veya tablonun boşluklarını metinden alınan kelimelerle doldurursun. En kritik kural: kelime sınırı. "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" gibi bir talimat verilir; bunu aşarsan cevap yanlış sayılır, doğru bilgi olsa bile.

Strateji

  • Boşluğun etrafındaki dilbilgisine bak: gelen kelime isim mi, fiil mi, sıfat mı olmalı? Bu, doğru cevabı daraltır.
  • Cümledeki anahtar kelimeyi metinde scan et; cevap genelde o kelimenin eş anlamının yakınındadır.
  • Summary Completion'da bazen bir kelime kutusu (A–H) verilir — o zaman metinden kopyalamazsın, kutudan seçersin.
  • Kelimeyi metinden birebir kopyala; kendi kelimenle değiştirme, çekimini bozma.
  • Kelime sınırını say: kısaltma ve tireli kelimeler (ör. well-known) genelde tek kelime sayılır; sayı bir "word" değildir.

Metin: "The invention spread rapidly because it required no special training and could be produced from cheap, locally available materials."

Soru (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS): The invention spread quickly partly because it was made from ______ that were easy to find nearby.

Çözüm: Cevap cheap materials (veya sadece "materials" boşluğun grameriyle uyumluysa). "spread quickly" ↔ "spread rapidly", "easy to find nearby" ↔ "locally available" paraphrase'leri seni doğru cümleye götürür. Ders: soru pasajın kelimelerini değil, eş anlamlarını kullanır.

9. Sentence / Summary / Note / Table Completion

You fill the gaps in a sentence, summary, note or table with words taken from the text. The most critical rule: the word limit. An instruction such as "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" is given; if you exceed it, the answer is marked wrong even if the information is correct.

Strategy

  • Look at the grammar around the gap: should the incoming word be a noun, verb or adjective? This narrows down the right answer.
  • Scan the text for the keyword in the sentence; the answer is usually near the synonym of that word.
  • In Summary Completion, sometimes a word box (A–H) is given — then you do not copy from the text, you choose from the box.
  • Copy the word from the text exactly; do not swap it for your own word or change its form.
  • Count the word limit: abbreviations and hyphenated words (e.g. well-known) usually count as one word; a number is not a "word".

Text: "The invention spread rapidly because it required no special training and could be produced from cheap, locally available materials."

Question (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS): The invention spread quickly partly because it was made from ______ that were easy to find nearby.

Solution: The answer is cheap materials (or just "materials" if the grammar of the gap allows it). The paraphrases "spread quickly" ↔ "spread rapidly" and "easy to find nearby" ↔ "locally available" lead you to the right sentence. Lesson: the question uses synonyms of the passage words, not the words themselves.

10. Diagram / Flowchart Labelling (Görsel ve Süreç Etiketleme)

Bir diyagramı (makine parçası, döngü, harita) veya bir akış şemasını (süreç adımları) metinden alınan kelimelerle etiketlersin. Bilgi genelde metnin tek bir bölümünde yoğunlaşır — o bölümü bulmak yarı zaferdir.

Strateji

  • Önce diyagramı/şemayı incele: zaten etiketli olan parçalar sana metindeki yeri bulduran çapalardır.
  • Flowchart'ta yön/sıra kelimelerini izle: first, then, next, after this, finally. Süreç genelde metinde de aynı sırada anlatılır.
  • Yine kelime sınırına uy ve kelimeleri metinden birebir al.
  • Diyagramın mantığını kullan: bir parça "sıvının girdiği yer" ise, cevap bir sıvı/giriş ismi olmalı — anlamsız kelimeyi ele.

Metin: "Cold water enters through the inlet valve at the base. A heating coil warms it, and the resulting steam rises into the upper chamber, where it is captured."

Flowchart: Cold water → enters via the ______ (1) → heated by a ______ (2) → steam collects in the ______ (3)

Çözüm: 1) inlet valve, 2) heating coil, 3) upper chamber. Süreç sırası metinle bire bir eşleşiyor; sadece doğru isimleri çek. Ders: akış yönü kelimelerini takip et, çapa parçaları kullan.

10. Diagram / Flowchart Labelling

You label a diagram (a machine part, a cycle, a map) or a flowchart (process steps) with words taken from the text. The information is usually concentrated in a single section of the text — finding that section is half the battle.

Strategy

  • First examine the diagram/chart: the parts that are already labelled are the anchors that help you find your place in the text.
  • In a flowchart, follow the direction/order words: first, then, next, after this, finally. The process is usually described in the same order in the text.
  • Again, obey the word limit and take the words exactly from the text.
  • Use the logic of the diagram: if a part is "where the liquid enters", the answer must be a liquid/inlet name — rule out any word that makes no sense.

Text: "Cold water enters through the inlet valve at the base. A heating coil warms it, and the resulting steam rises into the upper chamber, where it is captured."

Flowchart: Cold water → enters via the ______ (1) → heated by a ______ (2) → steam collects in the ______ (3)

Solution: 1) inlet valve, 2) heating coil, 3) upper chamber. The order of the process matches the text exactly; just pull out the right names. Lesson: follow the flow-direction words, use the anchor parts.

11. Short Answer Questions (Kısa Cevap)

Metne dayalı doğrudan sorular sorulur ("What…? Where…? How many…?") ve kısa cevap istenir — yine kelime sınırıyla. En kolay tiplerden biridir çünkü sorular metnin sırasını takip eder ve cevaplar çoğunlukla somut (isim, sayı, yer) olur.

Strateji

  • Soru kelimesine (what/where/who/how many) bakıp ne tür bir cevap aradığını belirle: yer mi, sayı mı, isim mi?
  • Anahtar kelimeyi scan et, çevresini oku, kelime sınırına uyan en kısa doğru ifadeyi al.
  • Tam cümle yazma; sadece istenen bilgi yeter. Gereksiz kelime, sınırı aşırıp cevabı yanlışa çevirebilir.

Metin: "The expedition, led by Captain Rourke, reached the summit in 1978 after three failed attempts."

Soru (ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER): In which year did the expedition reach the summit? → 1978. Sadece yıl; "in 1978" yazmak bile gereksiz — istenen tek bilgi sayıdır.

11. Short Answer Questions

Direct questions based on the text are asked ("What…? Where…? How many…?") and a short answer is required — again with a word limit. It is one of the easiest types because the questions follow the order of the text and the answers are mostly concrete (a name, number, place).

Strategy

  • Look at the question word (what/where/who/how many) to decide what kind of answer you are looking for: a place, a number, a name?
  • Scan for the keyword, read around it, take the shortest correct expression that fits the word limit.
  • Do not write a full sentence; just the required information is enough. An unnecessary word can exceed the limit and turn the answer wrong.

Text: "The expedition, led by Captain Rourke, reached the summit in 1978 after three failed attempts."

Question (ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER): In which year did the expedition reach the summit? → 1978. Just the year; even writing "in 1978" is unnecessary — the only thing required is the number.

13. Paraphrase Awareness: The Question ≠ the Passage Words

The hidden engine of IELTS Reading is paraphrase. The sentence in the question is almost never the passage sentence written with the same words; it is restated with synonyms and different grammar. A candidate who searches for the word literally will not find the answer; the one who searches for the meaning will.

Golden rule

Hunt for meaning, not words. Do not search for the verbs and adjectives in the question literally in the passage — a synonym will come. Search only for the things that do not change (nouns, dates, numbers); they lead you to the right sentence.

14. Bilinmeyen Kelimeyi Bağlamdan Tahmin Etme

Akademik pasajlarda tanımadığın kelimeler çıkacak — bu normaldir ve her kelimeyi bilmen gerekmez. Cevap için gerçekten önemli olan kelimenin anlamını bağlamdan tahmin etmeyi öğren.

Bağlam ipuçları

  • Cümle mantığı: zıtlık bağlaçları (but, however, although) kelimenin çevresindekinin tersi olduğunu söyler.
  • Örnek/tanım: such as, for example, i.e. ardından gelen örnekler anlamı açar.
  • Kök/ek analizi: -less (yoksunluk), re- (tekrar), un-/in- (olumsuz) gibi ekler ipucu verir.
  • Duygu/yön: kelimenin olumlu mu olumsuz mu olduğunu çözmek çoğu zaman soruyu çözmeye yeter.

"The soil was so arid that almost nothing could grow, and farmers abandoned the land."

Tahmin: "arid" kelimesini bilmesen de "hiçbir şey yetişmiyor + çiftçiler terk etti" bağlamı sana kelimenin çok kuru / verimsiz demek olduğunu söyler. Cevap için bu kadarı çoğu zaman yeterli.

14. Guessing an Unknown Word from Context

Words you do not recognise will come up in academic passages — this is normal, and you do not need to know every word. Learn to guess from context the meaning of the words that genuinely matter for the answer.

Context clues

  • Sentence logic: contrast conjunctions (but, however, although) tell you the word is the opposite of what is around it.
  • Example/definition: examples following such as, for example, i.e. unlock the meaning.
  • Root/affix analysis: affixes such as -less (lacking), re- (again), un-/in- (negative) give a clue.
  • Feeling/direction: working out whether a word is positive or negative is often enough to solve the question.

"The soil was so arid that almost nothing could grow, and farmers abandoned the land."

Guess: Even if you do not know "arid", the context "almost nothing grows + farmers abandoned it" tells you the word means very dry / infertile. That is usually enough for the answer.

16. Common Mistakes (Specific to Turkish Candidates)

Mistaking NG for FALSE

The most expensive mistake. If the text is silent, it's NG; only if there is a contradiction is it FALSE. For every FALSE you must be able to point to the refuting sentence.

Over-reading

Reading every sentence fully and running out of time. Use skim + scan; apply detailed reading only to the answer zone.

Searching for words literally

Looking for the question word letter for letter. The answer is almost always inside the paraphrase.

Exceeding the word limit

Writing three words when it says "TWO WORDS" = wrong. Re-read the instruction for every Completion question.

Bringing in general knowledge

Answering with information from outside the text ("I know that…"). Only what is written in the passage counts.

Leaving a box blank

There is no penalty for a wrong answer; write an answer for every question. Even when unsure, a sensible guess always beats a blank.

Careless spelling

Writing the right word wrongly (e.g. enviroment) is zero points. Check the letters as you copy.

Transferring answers late

Forgetting there is no transfer time and trying to write them all up at the last minute. Write the answer on the sheet as you solve it.

18. Not Given Kararı — 4 Adımlı Test

True/False/Not Given, Reading'de tek başına en çok puan kaybettiren noktadır; çünkü Not Given kavramı sezgiye aykırıdır. Her ifadeyi aşağıdaki dört adımlı testten geçir. Kararı duygunla değil, bu mekanik zincirle ver — hangi adımda durursan cevabın odur.

Dört adım

  • 1. Konu metinde geçiyor mu? Geçmiyorsa karar bitti: cevap NOT GIVEN.
  • 2. Metin ifadenin aynısını mı söylüyor? Evet ise cevap TRUE.
  • 3. Metin ifadenin tersini mi söylüyor? Evet ise cevap FALSE.
  • 4. Konu geçiyor ama bu özel iddiaya girilmiyor mu? O zaman cevap NOT GIVEN.

FALSE ile NOT GIVEN farkı — burada kilitlen

FALSE, metnin ifadenin tersini aktif olarak söylemesidir: bir karşıtlık vardır ve o çürüten cümleyi parmağınla gösterebilmelisin. NOT GIVEN, metnin o konuda hiçbir şey söylememesidir: ne doğrulayan ne çürüten bir cümle vardır. En sık yapılan hata şudur: "Pasajda böyle bir şey görmedim, demek ki FALSE." Yanlış — görmediysen ve tersi de yazmıyorsa cevap NOT GIVEN'dır. Kendine tek soru sor: bu ifadenin tersini söyleyen cümle hangisi? Gösteremiyorsan FALSE diyemezsin.

İki mini kural, çok puan

  • some ≠ all. Metin some (bazı) diyorsa ve ifade all (hepsi) diyorsa cevap FALSE'tur — Not Given değil. "Hepsi" iddiası "bazısı" bilgisiyle çelişir; bu bir karşıtlıktır.
  • may ≠ will. Metin may (olabilir) diyorsa ve ifade will (olacak) diyorsa cevap FALSE'tur — kesinlik derecesi değişince anlam çelişir.

18. The Not Given Decision — A 4-Step Test

True/False/Not Given loses more points than anything else in Reading, because the Not Given concept is counter-intuitive. Put every statement through the four-step test below. Make the decision not with your gut but with this mechanical chain — whichever step you stop at is your answer.

The four steps

  • 1. Is the topic mentioned in the text? If not, the decision is over: the answer is NOT GIVEN.
  • 2. Does the text say the same thing as the statement? If yes, the answer is TRUE.
  • 3. Does the text say the opposite of the statement? If yes, the answer is FALSE.
  • 4. Is the topic mentioned but this specific claim is not addressed? Then the answer is NOT GIVEN.

The difference between FALSE and NOT GIVEN — lock this in

FALSE is when the text actively says the opposite of the statement: there is a contradiction and you must be able to point to the refuting sentence. NOT GIVEN is when the text says nothing about it: there is neither a confirming nor a refuting sentence. The most common mistake is this: "I didn't see anything like this in the passage, so it must be FALSE." Wrong — if you did not see it and the opposite is not written either, the answer is NOT GIVEN. Ask yourself one question: which sentence says the opposite of this statement? If you cannot show it, you cannot say FALSE.

Two mini rules, lots of points

  • some ≠ all. If the text says some and the statement says all, the answer is FALSE — not Not Given. The "all" claim contradicts the "some" information; that is a contradiction.
  • may ≠ will. If the text says may and the statement says will, the answer is FALSE — when the degree of certainty changes, the meaning contradicts.

Lesson: Students usually mistake numbers 5 and 6 for NOT GIVEN. Both are FALSE, because the text says the opposite of the statement. The real NOT GIVENs are 4 and 7: the topic appears but that specific claim is nowhere to be found.

22. Tam Çözümlü Passage: The Return of the Beaver

Şimdi gerçek bir sınav pasajını baştan sona, üç soru tipiyle birlikte çözelim: True/False/Not Given, Sentence Completion ve Short Answer. Önce metni oku, soruları kendi başına çöz, sonra her cevabın gerekçesini ve paragraf referansını incele. Sınav koşulunda bu pasaja yaklaşık 17 dakika ayrılır.

The European beaver was once found in almost every river system on the continent. By the beginning of the twentieth century, however, fewer than 1,200 animals remained, scattered across eight isolated populations. The species had been hunted relentlessly, and not chiefly for its meat. What made it valuable was its dense waterproof fur and castoreum, a bitter substance produced in glands near the tail, which was used in perfume and in traditional medicine. In Britain the animal vanished altogether, probably during the sixteenth century.

Reintroduction began quietly. Small groups were released in Sweden in the 1920s, and further releases followed in Russia, Latvia and Poland. These early projects were driven mainly by a wish to restore a lost species rather than by any expectation of practical gain. It was only much later, when ecologists began to measure the animals' effect on the landscape, that a second argument for reintroduction emerged.

Beavers are what biologists call ecosystem engineers. They fell trees, dig canals and build dams, and in doing so they turn a fast, narrow stream into a slow mosaic of pools, wet meadows and standing dead wood. A single family can flood several hectares. The structures they build are not permanent. Once the willow and aspen within easy reach have been used up, the animals abandon the site and move upstream, and the pond silts up gradually into wet grassland.

The consequences of this activity are significant. Water that would otherwise rush downstream is held back, which lowers the height of flood peaks in the valleys below. In one study on a small Devon catchment, a single family reduced the peak flow leaving the site by roughly thirty per cent. The ponds also trap sediment and agricultural chemicals, so the water leaving a beaver wetland is measurably cleaner than the water entering it. Fish, amphibians and water birds increase in both number and variety.

Not everyone welcomes them. Farmers whose land lies beside a river may find their fields waterlogged and their drainage ditches blocked. Foresters lose trees. In Bavaria, where beavers were reintroduced in the 1960s and now number more than 20,000, the regional government employs a team of specialists whose job is to negotiate between the animals and the landowners. Their standard toolkit includes the flow device, a pipe pushed through a dam that lowers the water level without destroying the structure, and, where nothing else works, permits to remove individual animals.

The British case has been unusually contentious. A population appeared on the River Otter in Devon around 2008, almost certainly escaped from captivity rather than deliberately released. The government's first response was to catch and remove them, on the grounds that the animals might be carrying disease. A public campaign followed, and the beavers were allowed to remain under licence while a five-year monitoring project ran. The project's final report, published in 2020, concluded that the benefits outweighed the costs, and the animals were given permission to stay. Scotland had already granted the beaver protected status two years earlier.

Whether the species will now spread quickly across Britain is far from certain. Beavers disperse slowly, rarely more than a few kilometres in a year, and they cannot cross wide stretches of dry land. Much will depend on whether further releases are licensed and on whether the compensation schemes now under discussion are generous enough to make the animals tolerable to the people who must live alongside them.
