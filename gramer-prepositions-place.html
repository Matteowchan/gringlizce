Prepositions of place tell you where something is. In Turkish, a single -de/-da suffix handles every position, but English splits them into separate prepositions: for a wide area's inside → in, for a surface's top → on, for a single point → at. In addition, between (between two things), among (among a multitude), under (underneath), behind (behind) and in front of (in front of) also come into play as position prepositions. This topic, like the movement-marking Prepositions of Movement and time-marking Prepositions of Time prepositions, uses the same words (in/on/at), but its function is different. A learner who masters place also fixes the most common mistake they make while speaking.

Prepositions of place are the small but very powerful words that describe where a person or object is: in, on, at, between, under, behind, in front of and more. In English you can hardly build any everyday sentence without saying where something is; that is why using these three or four words in the right place is one of the most fundamental building blocks of fluent speech.

"Prepositions of place are small but decisive: in the box, on the box, at the box describe three completely different positions for the same object.

So why does this topic exist, and why is it hard for Turkish learners? The answer lies directly in the structure of Turkish. In Turkish, location is usually expressed with a single suffix, -de / -da / -te / -ta for example: kutuda, masada, durakta. The same suffix covers all three cases. English, however, divides the same idea according to the type of the location into three separate prepositions.

The Mental-Picture Rule
Before building a place sentence, pause for half a second and picture the place in your mind. If you see its inside, in; if you see it touching a surface, on; if you see just a pinhead on the map, at. This image is far more reliable than memorizing the word.

in, bir şeyin üç boyutlu, etrafı sınırlı bir hacmin içinde olduğunu söyler. Kutular, odalar, binalar, araçlar (küçük, içine girilen), şehirler, ülkeler ve sıvılar hep 'in' ister. Zihinsel resim: bir şeyin içine kapanmış olması.

Below you'll find the basic use, logic and plenty of examples for each place preposition. We start with the most common trio (in / on / at) and move on to more specific prepositions.

in

Something inside an enclosed, bounded area: in the box, in London, in the room.

on

In contact with a surface : on the table, on the wall, on the floor.

at

A specific point or address: at the door, at the bus stop, at home.

in — inside an enclosed / bounded area

in, says that something is inside a three-dimensional, enclosed volume. Boxes, rooms, buildings, small vehicles you get into, cities, countries and liquids all take 'in'. Mental image: being shut inside something.

There's some money in my wallet.
Cüzdanımın içinde biraz para var.
A wallet is an enclosed space.
We live in a small town near Bursa.
Bursa yakınında küçük bir kasabada yaşıyoruz.
Settlements (city/town/country) take 'in'.
She's in the kitchen making tea.
Mutfakta çay yapıyor.
Room = enclosed space.
The fish are swimming in the lake.
Balıklar gölde yüzüyor.
A liquid/body of water also takes 'in'.

on — contact with a surface

on, says that something touches a surface . The surface can be horizontal (table, floor) or vertical (wall, ceiling); what matters is contact. Also, on public transport (bus, train, plane) 'on' is used, because you're thought of as standing on a platform/floor.

There's a stain on your shirt.
Gömleğinde bir leke var.
The stain touches the surface.
The clock is on the wall.
Saat duvarda.
A vertical surface also takes 'on'.
We met an interesting man on the train.
Trende ilginç bir adamla tanıştık.
Large public transport = on (but a car takes 'in').
Put your name on the top of the page.
Adını sayfanın en üstüne yaz.
The surface of the page.

at — a specific point / place

at, thinks of a place not by its size but as a point on the map. Meeting places, addresses, event locations, a single position usually take 'at'. Also, places where a person is for an event/institution (at school, at work, at the party) require 'at'.

I'll wait for you at the entrance.
Seni girişte bekleyeceğim.
Entrance = meeting point.
She lives at 42 Green Street.
42 Green Street'te oturuyor.
Full (numbered) address = at; a street alone can be 'in/on'.
There were hundreds of people at the concert.
Konserde yüzlerce insan vardı.
Event = at.
Turn right at the traffic lights.
Trafik ışıklarında sağa dön.
A point in directions = at.

between / among — in between

between describes the space between exactly two (or a few clearly distinguishable) things; among describes being within an indefinite crowd/group . Rule: if there are separate objects you can count, between; if there's a multitude you 'get lost in', among.

The ball rolled between the two cars.
Top iki arabanın arasına yuvarlandı.
Two specific objects = between.
He found the letter among the old papers.
Mektubu eski kâğıtların arasında buldu.
Indefinite multitude = among.
Norway lies between Sweden and the North Sea.
Norveç, İsveç ile Kuzey Denizi arasında yer alır.
Two references = between.
She felt safe among her friends.
Arkadaşlarının arasında güvende hissetti.
Within a group = among.

under / below / over / above — below and above

under = directly below, often with a sense of covering/contact. below = a more general 'at a lower level'. over = above/over (usually without contact, may cover). above = a more general 'at a higher level'. In everyday speech, under and over are the most common.

The cat is hiding under the bed.
Kedi yatağın altında saklanıyor.
Directly below = under.
There's a lovely picture above the fireplace.
Şöminenin üstünde güzel bir tablo var.
Higher up, no contact = above.

Uçak dağların üzerinden geçti.
Movement across the top, no contact = over.
The temperature is below zero today.
Bugün sıcaklık sıfırın altında.
Abstract scale; below is preferred.

These are position prepositions that depend on point of view. behind = at the back, in front of = in front, next to / beside = right beside, opposite = directly across from. They're very useful in giving directions and describing a room.

In English, preposition choice often reflects not reality but the speaker's mental framing . 'in the sofa' (sunk into the sofa) and 'on the sofa' (sitting on top of it) paint different images. İpucu

Pratik eşleme: geniş alan / şehir / ülke → in; bir yüzey → on; tek bir nokta ya da adres → at. Türkçedeki tek "-de/-da" ekini bu üçe bölmeyi alışkanlık hâline getir.

Türkçe konum sistemi İngilizceye göre çok daha 'toplayıcıdır': bir tek -de/-da hâl eki (bulunma hâli) İngilizcedeki in, on ve at'in üçünü birden karşılar. Ayrıca Türkçe konumu genellikle bir iyelik + ilgeç yapısıyla verir: 'masanın üstünde', 'kutunun içinde'. İngilizce ise tek bir edatla halleder.

Tip

Practical mapping: wide area / city / country → in; a surface → on; a single point or address → at. Make it a habit to split the single Turkish "-de/-da" suffix into these three.

The Turkish location system is much more 'consolidating' than English: a single -de/-da case suffix (the locative) covers all three of English's in, on and at. Turkish also usually gives location with a possessive + postposition structure: 'masanın üstünde', 'kutunun içinde'. In English, a single preposition handles it.

  • Turkish '-de/-da' → English in / on / at (choose by location).
  • Turkish 'X'in üstünde' → English on X (you don't say the word 'üst' separately).
  • Turkish 'X'in içinde' → English in X (you don't say the word 'iç' separately).
  • Turkish 'X'in arasında' (also used for a single object) → English either between (two) or among (many).
  • Turkish usually puts the preposition at the end of the noun as a suffix; English puts it before the noun as a separate word.

concept check Picture each situation in your mind and choose the correct preposition; then state the reason in your own words.
  1. A pencil fell right inside a box. 'in' or 'on'? Why?
    in — because the pencil fell into the enclosed inside of the box, not onto its surface.
  2. You'll meet your friend at a street corner. 'in the corner' or 'at the corner'?
    at the corner — because a street corner is an outdoor meeting point, not the inner corner of a room.
  3. There are three people in a photo. Which preposition says they are in the photo's content?
    in the photo — a picture's content takes 'in'; 'on the photo' means the photo's physical surface.
  4. You're sitting among a group of people (an indefinite multitude). 'between' or 'among'?
    among — for an indefinite/large number of people, among; between is only for two (or a few countable) objects.
  5. You've lain down in your bed to sleep. 'in bed' or 'on the bed'?
    in bed — lying down to sleep is a fixed expression; 'on the bed' just means being on top of the bed.

Where to start?
Begin the in/on/at trio in a concrete and visual way: practise with real objects in the classroom (pen on the desk, book in the bag, student at the door). The learner should first build the mental image ; memorizing a list of rules is the last step. Show the Turkish '-de/-da' bridge clearly so the learner understands why three separate words are needed.

Concept-checking (CCQ): For the sentence 'The cat is on the box', ask 'Is the cat inside the box? (No) — Does the cat touch the box? (Yes)'. For 'in the box' it's the opposite. These two questions help the learner internalize the surface/volume distinction.

Activity suggestion: 'I'm describing my room' game — one learner describes a room/picture and the others listen and draw. Then compare the drawings. A wrong preposition leads to a wrong drawing; this makes the mistake very visible. Second activity: 'Where's the object?' — the teacher hides an object and learners try to find it using only place prepositions (Is it under…? Is it behind…?).

The Mistake the Teacher Should Watch For
The most persistent mistake is 'in the table / in the wall' (meaning: on top). Whenever you see it, correct it with a single question: 'Do you mean the inside?' If the learner says 'no', they reach the correct preposition (on) themselves. Prompting this question instead of stating the rule makes the correction stick.
