Data/trend description language (IELTS Task 1, graphs/tables): rise/increase/grow/climb, fall/decline/drop, peak, plateau, fluctuate; sharpness adverbs (sharply, gradually, steadily); the noun+verb pair (a sharp rise / rose sharply). Time prepositions (in 2020, from...to, between). The Turkish student often confuses "increase" as verb/noun and makes preposition (increase OF/IN/BY) errors.

Bir grafiği, tabloyu ya da eğilimi İngilizce betimlemek, sadece kelime bilmek değil; fiil mi isim mi kullanacağını, hangi zarfla hızını vereceğini ve hangi edatla sayıya bağlayacağını doğru seçmektir. Bu bölüm rise/fall/peak/plateau/fluctuate ailesini, sharp/gradual/steady zarflarını, increase of/in/by ayrımını ve in 2020 / from... to / between zaman kalıplarını tek tek işler. Karşılaştırma yaparken Karşılaştırma Dili diline, veriyi tanıtırken Yönlendirme Dili kalıplarına bağlanır.

Describing a graph, table or trend in English is not just knowing the words; it is choosing correctly whether to use a verb or a noun, which adverb gives its speed, and which preposition links it to a number. This section works through the rise/fall/peak/plateau/fluctuate family, the sharp/gradual/steady adverbs, the increase of/in/by distinction and the in 2020 / from... to / between time patterns one by one. When making comparisons it connects to Comparison Language, and when introducing data to the Signposting Language patterns.

Bir çizgi grafik, sütun grafik ya da tablo gördüğünde İngilizcede yaptığın şey aslında tek bir cümlelik değil: ne oldu (yükseldi/düştü/sabit kaldı), ne kadar hızlı (keskin/kademeli/durağan) ve ne kadar (%15, 40 milyona) sorularına aynı anda cevap verirsin. 'Describing data & trends' bu üç katmanı düzgün bir cümlede birleştirme becerisidir. IELTS Academic Task 1'in, üniversite raporlarının, iş sunumlarının ve gazete ekonomi haberlerinin dili budur.

Neden ayrı bir 'konu' olarak var? Çünkü Türkçede biz çoğu değişimi tek bir esnek yapıyla anlatırız: arttı, çoğaldı, yükseldi neredeyse birbirinin yerine geçer ve hepsi aynı edatla (%15 arttı) çalışır. İngilizcede ise her fiilin kendi edatı, kendi isim hali ve kendi zarfı vardır. increase hem fiil hem isimdir; hangisini kullandığına göre etrafındaki kelimeler değişir. İşte bu yüzden ayrı ve dikkat isteyen bir alandır.

Türkçe benzeri: bir hava durumu spikerinin 'sıcaklıklar 5 derece yükselecek, sonra sabit seyredecek' demesi gibi düşün. Türkçede 'yükselecek' ve 'sabit seyredecek' derken hız ve miktarı ayrı kelimelere yüklersin. İngilizce de aynı mantığı kullanır ama parçaları çok daha katı bir dilbilgisiyle birbirine bağlar.

When you see a line chart, bar chart or table, what you do in English is not really a single sentence: you answer at the same time the questions what happened (rose/fell/stayed stable), how fast (sharp/gradual/steady) and how much (15%, to 40 million). 'Describing data & trends' is the skill of combining these three layers in a well-formed sentence. This is the language of IELTS Academic Task 1, university reports, business presentations and newspaper economics stories.

Why does it exist as a separate 'topic'? Because in Turkish we describe most changes with a single flexible structure: arttı, çoğaldı, yükseldi are almost interchangeable and all work with the same suffix (%15 arttı). In English, however, each verb has its own preposition, its own noun form and its own adverb. increase is both a verb and a noun; the words around it change depending on which you use. That is precisely why it is a separate area that demands care.

The Turkish parallel: think of a weather presenter saying 'temperatures will rise 5 degrees, then hold steady'. In Turkish, when you say 'will rise' and 'hold steady' you load the speed and amount onto separate words. English uses the same logic but links the parts with a much stricter grammar.

Why is it hard? There are three reasons — each a separate threshold that trips you up when moving from Turkish to English:

Verb/noun mix-up

The Turkish student tends to say There was increased instead of There was an increase.

Preposition choice

increase by, to or in? Where a single suffix does the job in Turkish, English has three separate meanings.

Adverb placement

sharply increased or increased sharply? The position of the adverb determines the fluency of the sentence.

This section resolves all three one by one. For comparison sentences, Comparison Language, and for patterns that introduce data, Signposting Language are the siblings of this section.

Two main patterns: the verb route and the noun route

There are two equivalent ways to describe a trend. Knowing both lets you vary your sentences without falling into repetition — and that is exactly what earns the 'lexical resource' score in IELTS.

↔

Keep in Mind: the BY - TO - AT preposition trio

The questions below test not the rule but the meaning. Check that you grasp what the sentence says.

meaning check Say what each sentence actually expresses.
  1. 'Sales rose to 40%.' — How much did sales rise, or to which level did they rise?
    It says which level they rose to: they reached the LEVEL of 40%. It does NOT say how much they rose (the amount). For the amount, 'by' would be needed.
  2. 'Prices fluctuated between $10 and $15.' — Did the price rise continuously?
    No. 'fluctuate' means irregular up-and-down; there is no particular direction. The price moved between $10 and $15, it did not rise steadily.
  3. 'The figure remained stable.' — Did the value change?
    No, it did not change; it stayed at the same level. 'remain stable' = stability.
  4. 'There was a slight rise.' vs 'There was a sharp rise.' — What is the difference?
    'slight' = a small, minor rise; 'sharp' = a large, fast rise. The difference is in speed/size.
  5. 'Renewables overtook coal in 2018.' — What happened in 2018?
    Renewable energy overtook coal that year; that is, the value of renewables became higher than that of coal. The two curves crossed.

"Describe the same trend once with a verb (rose sharply) and once with a noun (a sharp rise) — this is the single reflex that doubles your score.

  • Three critical prepositions: by = amount (rose by 20%), to = level (rose to 40m), at = peak level (peaked at 12%).

  • In academic writing use approximation (roughly, just over) and measured adjectives (marked, substantial); not 'huge/crazy'.
