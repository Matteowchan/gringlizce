Grammar: Gerunds & Infinitives

Study the rules, look at the examples, then do the exercises.

When one verb follows another

Very often in English, one verb is followed by a second verb: I want to go, I enjoy going. The big question at B1 is simple to ask but hard to master: does the second verb take the -ing form (the gerund) or the to form (the to-infinitive)? English does not choose randomly — the first verb decides.

Here is the core idea in one line: some verbs are always followed by a gerund (enjoy, avoid, finish), other verbs are always followed by a to-infinitive (want, decide, hope), and after a preposition we always use the gerund.

  • I enjoy cooking at the weekend.enjoy + gerund (-ing).
  • I decided to cook something new.decide + to-infinitive.
  • I'm good at cooking pasta.preposition (at) + gerund.

1. The two forms (gerund vs to-infinitive)

The gerund is the base verb + -ing; it behaves like a noun. The to-infinitive is to + the base verb. Both let you put a second verb into your sentence, but they are not interchangeable — the first verb (or a preposition) chooses one of them.

FormHow to build itExample
Gerundverb + -ing (acts like a noun)swimming, reading, going, being
To-infinitiveto + base verbto swim, to read, to go, to be
Key point
  • The gerund can be the subject of a sentence: "Swimming is good for you." ✓
  • Never put two full verbs together without one of these forms: "I want go." ✗ — "I want to go." ✓

2. Verbs followed by a gerund (verb + -ing)

These common verbs are always followed by the -ing form. Learn them as a group and the choice becomes automatic.

VerbExampleMeaning
enjoyI enjoy reading at night.keyif almak
avoidShe avoids driving in the city.kaçınmak
finishWe finished painting the room.bitirmek
mindDo you mind waiting here?sakınca görmek
suggestHe suggested taking a break.önermek
keepThey kept talking during the film.-meye devam etmek
Also in this group
  • practise, imagine, miss, admit, spend time also take the gerund: "I practise speaking every day."

3. Verbs followed by a to-infinitive (verb + to)

This second group is always followed by to + base verb. Many of them talk about plans, wishes and promises.

VerbExampleMeaning
wantI want to travel more.istemek
decideShe decided to study law.karar vermek
hopeWe hope to see you soon.ummak
promiseHe promised to help me.söz vermek
agreeThey agreed to meet at six.kabul etmek
learnI learned to swim at eight.öğrenmek
Also in this group
  • plan, offer, need, would like, manage, refuse also take the to-infinitive: "We plan to leave early."

4. After a preposition, always a gerund (at / in / about + -ing)

This is one of the most useful rules in the whole unit: when a verb comes straight after a preposition (at, in, of, about, on, for...), it must be the gerund. Adjectives and nouns are often linked to a fixed preposition, so learn them together.

ExpressionExampleMeaning
good at + -ingShe is good at drawing.-de iyi olmak
interested in + -ingI'm interested in learning Spanish.-e ilgi duymak
think about + -ingWe're thinking about moving house.-i düşünmek
before / after + -ingWash your hands before eating.-den önce/sonra
Watch out
  • The word to is sometimes a preposition, not part of an infinitive. After look forward to and be used to, use the gerund: "I look forward to hearing from you." ✓

5. The to-infinitive of purpose (why? → to ...)

When you want to explain why you do something, use to + verb. It answers the question "What for?" — and it is much more natural than "for + -ing" here.

  • I went out to buy some bread.Why did I go out? To buy bread.
  • She called me to say thank you.Purpose → to + verb.
Common error
  • Do not use for + verb to give a purpose: "I went out for buy bread." ✗ — "I went out to buy bread." ✓
  • Use for + noun only: "I went out for bread." ✓

6. Verbs that change meaning (stop / remember / forget)

A few verbs can take either form — but the meaning changes completely. These are classic B1 exam traps, so read each row carefully.

Verb+ gerund+ to-infinitive
stopstop doing = end the activity (She stopped smoking.)stop to do = pause in order to do (She stopped to smoke.)
rememberremember doing = a memory (I remember locking it.)remember to do = don't forget (Remember to lock it!)
forgetforget doing = forget a memory (I'll never forget seeing it.)forget to do = fail to do it (I forgot to call.)
Quick test
  • Is it a memory of the past? Use the gerund. Is it a task not to forget? Use the to-infinitive.
  • "On the way home I stopped to buy milk." (I paused for that purpose) vs "I stopped buying milk." (I no longer buy it).

7. Common mistakes (✗ / ✓)

These are the errors B1 students make most often with gerunds and infinitives. Check your own writing against this list.

Wrong ✗Right ✓Why?
I enjoy to read books.I enjoy reading books.enjoy always takes the gerund.
She avoided to answer.She avoided answering.avoid always takes the gerund.
I'm interested in to learn art.I'm interested in learning art.after a preposition, use the gerund.
We decided going home.We decided to go home.decide always takes the to-infinitive.
I went there for buy a ticket.I went there to buy a ticket.purpose = to + verb, not "for + verb".

8. Putting it together (Model Dialogue)

Two friends talk about a new hobby. Read it twice and notice how the second verb changes form after each first verb or preposition.

A:I've decided to take up a new hobby this year.decide + to-infinitive.
B:Nice! Have you thought about learning the guitar?preposition (about) + gerund.
A:Actually, I really enjoy painting, so I want to try that.enjoy + gerund; want + to-infinitive.
B:Good idea. You should keep practising — and don't forget to buy some brushes!keep + gerund; forget to do = a task.
A:I went to the shop yesterday to get some. I couldn't stop buying colours!to get = purpose; stop + gerund = end the activity.
Test yourself
  • Find one gerund after a preposition. (about learning.)
  • "I went to the shop to get some." Why to get? (It gives the purpose — why she went.)
  • What is the difference between "stop buying" and "stop to buy"? (stop buying = no longer buy; stop to buy = pause in order to buy.)

Speaking: Habits, Hobbies & Goals

Learn the words, then use them in your own sentences about your life.

habit (n)
something you do regularly, often without thinking (alışkanlık)
Syn: routine · Ant: —
"I have a habit of reading before bed."
hobby (n)
an activity you do for pleasure in your free time (hobi)
Syn: pastime · Ant: chore
"Painting is my favourite hobby."
routine (n)
a fixed, regular way of doing things (rutin, günlük düzen)
Syn: schedule · Ant: chaos
"Exercise is part of my daily routine."
enjoy (v)
to get pleasure from something (keyif almak)
Syn: love · Ant: dislike
"I enjoy cooking at the weekend."
avoid (v)
to try not to do or meet something (kaçınmak)
Syn: dodge · Ant: seek
"I avoid eating late at night."
give up (phr v)
to stop doing something for good (bırakmak, vazgeçmek)
Syn: quit · Ant: continue
"She gave up smoking last year."
take up (phr v)
to start a new activity or hobby (yeni bir uğraşa başlamak)
Syn: begin · Ant: drop
"He took up running in spring."
practise (v)
to do something regularly to get better at it (pratik yapmak)
Syn: rehearse · Ant: neglect
"I practise speaking English every day."
improve (v)
to make or become better (geliştirmek, ilerlemek)
Syn: get better · Ant: worsen
"My cooking has improved a lot."
decision (n)
a choice you make after thinking (karar)
Syn: choice · Ant: hesitation
"It was a difficult decision to make."
goal (n)
something you want to achieve (hedef)
Syn: aim, target · Ant: —
"My goal is to run a marathon."
plan (v)
to decide to do something in the future (planlamak)
Syn: intend · Ant: improvise
"We plan to travel next summer."
manage (v)
to succeed in doing something difficult (başarmak, üstesinden gelmek)
Syn: succeed · Ant: fail
"She managed to finish on time."
prefer (v)
to like one thing more than another (tercih etmek)
Syn: favour · Ant: —
"I prefer walking to driving."
Talking about habits and goals: mix gerunds (enjoy doing) and to-infinitives (decide to do).

Reading: A New Habit

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Before You Read (Okumadan Önce)

Good readers prepare before they start. Before you read, think about these three questions (you do not need to write the answers):

  • Think about your own life. Is there a habit you would like to give up, or a hobby you would like to take up?
  • The text is about a person who changed his lifestyle. Which verb patterns do you expect — enjoy doing, decide to do?
  • Look at the title, "A New Habit". Do you think the change will be easy or difficult for the person? Guess!
How to read
  • First read: read quickly, without stopping. Get the general idea; you do not need every word.
  • Second read: read the questions, then search the text for the answers. Notice each gerund ("enjoy doing") and each to-infinitive ("decided to do").

Words to spot (from the Speaking section): habit · routine · avoid · give up · take up · improve · goal · manage

From an unhealthy habit to a new routine: one small decision at a time.
A New Habit: Emre's Story

Emre is thirty-two years old, and he works in an office in the centre of a big city. For most of his twenties, he was too busy to think about his health. He spent long hours sitting in front of a computer, and after work he only wanted to relax. Cooking felt like too much effort, so ordering food became a daily habit. At the weekend, he preferred staying at home to going out, and little by little his routine became completely inactive.

It was not always like this. When Emre was a child, he loved playing football with his friends after school, and he was rarely still for a moment. As he grew older, however, studying and then working slowly took over his time. He stopped playing sport, and he forgot how good it felt to be active. By the time he started his office job, exercising had almost disappeared from his life, and he could hardly remember the energetic boy he used to be.

Two years ago, Emre was not a healthy person. He spent every evening watching television, and he never thought about exercising. He enjoyed eating fast food, but he avoided walking anywhere if he could take the bus. His friends often suggested joining a gym; however, he always kept finding excuses.

His days followed the same pattern for years. In the morning he woke up feeling tired, and he often skipped breakfast to save time. At lunch he chose the easiest option, which usually meant a burger or a pizza near the office. By the evening he had no energy left for anything except the sofa. Whenever a friend mentioned a gym, the idea of exercising after such a long day always seemed impossible. Deep down, he knew that his lifestyle was not good for him, yet changing it felt far too difficult to even begin.

When winter came, Emre got ill. The doctor advised him to change his lifestyle, so he finally decided to do something. Although he wanted to give up after the first week, he promised himself to continue for one month, because he was tired of feeling weak.

The visit to the doctor was a real shock for Emre. The doctor looked at his results and explained that he needed to move more and to eat better food. 'You are still young,' she said, 'but you cannot keep ignoring your body. Try to build small, healthy habits instead of dreaming about big changes.' Emre listened carefully and agreed to try. On his way home, he decided to write a simple plan. He was afraid of failing again, however, so he promised to take just one small step at a time rather than trying to change everything at once.

Emre started small. Every morning he went out to run for twenty minutes before work. He also stopped eating sugar, which was the most difficult change of all. While he was getting fitter, he discovered that he was quite good at swimming, so he began going to the pool twice a week.

The first two weeks were not easy at all. Getting out of bed early was hard, and his legs hurt after every run. He often felt like stopping, but he kept remembering how weak he had felt before. Giving up sugar was even harder than running, because he was used to drinking sweet coffee and eating chocolate every afternoon. To stay motivated, he started writing down everything he managed to do, and seeing his progress on paper helped him to continue. When he tried swimming for the first time in years, he was surprised to find that he really enjoyed being in the water. Swimming did not hurt his knees, so he looked forward to going to the pool every week.

Now, Emre is a different man. He loves exercising, and he cannot imagine returning to his old habits. He still remembers feeling tired and unhealthy, and he never wants to feel like that again. Last month he even managed to run a half-marathon with a colleague.

Training for the half-marathon taught Emre a lot about himself. He learned to set clear goals and to be patient when progress was slow. A colleague from his office, who also enjoyed running, agreed to train with him, and together they practised every weekend. On the day of the race, Emre was nervous, but he refused to give up, even when he felt tired near the end. Crossing the finish line was one of the proudest moments of his life. His friends, who had once watched him make excuses, could not believe how much he had changed. Some of them even asked him to help them start exercising too.

'How do you manage to stay so motivated?' his colleague asked him one morning. 'I just try not to think about the whole race,' Emre replied. 'I only think about finishing today's run. If you focus on enjoying each small session, the big goal slowly takes care of itself.'

'The hardest part was starting,' Emre says. 'I kept telling myself that I would begin tomorrow. However, once you start, you learn to enjoy it. Now I exercise not to lose weight, but because it makes me happy.'

These days, Emre often talks to other people who want to change their habits but do not know how to begin. He always gives them the same simple advice. 'Do not wait for the perfect moment,' he says, 'because that moment never comes. Choose one small habit, and keep practising it until it becomes natural. It is better to run for ten minutes every day than to plan to run for an hour and never start.' He also reminds them to be kind to themselves. Everyone makes mistakes, and missing one day does not mean failing. What matters is deciding to begin again the next morning. For Emre, the most important lesson was learning that a healthy life is not about being perfect; it is about enjoying the small steps and refusing to give up.

Now You Speak (Konuşma Sırası Sende)

Answer these questions out loud in full sentences. There is no single correct answer — the goal is to use the right verb pattern each time. (Sesli cevap ver; önemli olan doğru yapıyı kurman.)

  • Name one thing you have decided to do this year. (decide + to)

  • Why do you study English? Answer with "to ...". (purpose: to + verb)
Tip — to speak accurately
  • After enjoy, avoid, keep, finish use -ing: "I enjoy painting."
  • After want, decide, hope, plan use to: "I plan to travel."
  • To give a reason, use to + verb: "I study English to get a better job."

Writing: My Habits and Goals

Write and submit your paragraph; your teacher will review it.

Connectors turn sentences into a text (Linking Words)

A list of short, separate sentences is not real writing. What connects your ideas is linking words. At A2 you used and, but, so, because. At B1 you add four more powerful connectors: also, when, while, although and however. These let you show contrast, background events and extra information like a confident writer.

ConnectorUse it to...Example
andadd informationI enjoy running, and I have started swimming too.
butshow a small contrastI wanted to give up, but I kept going.
soshow a resultI'm good at cooking, so I decided to take a class.
becausegive a reasonI exercise because it makes me happy.
alsoadd an extra pointI have learned to swim. I have also taken up cycling.
whensay at what past timeWhen I was a child, I avoided doing sport.
whiletwo things at the same timeWhile I was studying, I decided to get fit.
althoughshow a strong contrastAlthough it was hard, I managed to continue.
howevercontrast between two sentencesI planned to stop. However, I kept practising.

See the difference — the same facts, written two ways:

Separate sentences ✗Connected, fluent writing ✓
I decided to get fit. I enjoyed watching television. It was hard. I kept going. I run every day now. I decided to get fit, although I enjoyed watching television. It was hard, but I kept going, so now I run every day.
Rule
  • Use at least 3 different connectors in your paragraph. Do not write sentence by sentence; link your ideas.
  • When when / while / although / however start a sentence, they are usually followed by a comma: "However, I kept practising."

Paragraph structure (Paragraph Structure)

A "habits and goals" paragraph has three parts. The skeleton stays the same every time — you change the content, not the structure:

PartWhat to writeExample
1. OpeningA general topic sentence about what you enjoy doingI enjoy keeping active, and I have several hobbies.
2. BodyHabits and decisions. Use gerunds after enjoy/avoid/keep, to-infinitives after decide/want/plan, and link with connectors.Last year I decided to take up running. Although it was hard, I kept training.
3. ClosingA goal for the future, with a to-infinitiveIn the future, I hope to run a marathon.
